## Notes
- 形声: semantic [[Radical 015|冫]] ("ice") + phonetic [[互]] — the modern Japanese glyph's literal right-hand component is 牙 ("tooth"), but zh.Wiktionary explains 互 and 牙 were phonetically similar enough in Old Chinese to be used interchangeably, and 冴 is itself a further Japanese-standardized descendant of that lineage (冱 → 𠖱 → 冴); the vault's `graphemic_classification: 互` tracks the genuine ancestral phonetic, not the literal modern shape. "To freeze, to stop up" (matching the parent glyph 冱 and `korean_native: 얼` "ice"), with a distinct, extremely common modern-Japanese extended sense, "clear, sharp, brilliant" (冴える, used of a clear mind, bright moonlight, or vivid color) — also the source of the Jinmeiyō nanori さえ (Sae). Genuine variant: 冱 (no independent page).
