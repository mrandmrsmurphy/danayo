## Notes
- 形声 (OC *moːŋ): semantic [[木]] ("tree") + phonetic [[蒙]] (OC *moːŋ) — an archaic botanical term for a scholar-tree-like tree, now obsolete; the character's near-sole modern use is as the bound second half of [[words/檸檬|檸檬]] "lemon" (a rare independent use, 檬果, a historical/archaic alternate spelling for "mango" now superseded by 芒果, has no page or citation anywhere in this vault).
- [SKIP-1-4-14](Lookup/SKIP/SKIP-1/SKIP-1-4-14.md) ([Stroke 18](Lookup/Stroke/Stroke%2018.md))
- Confirmed absent from the Classical Chinese corpus (`mc_id: 0`, checked against all four `Lookup/CC/CC *.md` mirror files — only the phonetic component [[蒙]] itself appears, at rank 782 on `CC 0000.md`). Ancient [[Lookup/CC/initials/聲 明|m]] + [[Lookup/CC/finals/韻 東一|uŋ]] → [ㄇㄛㄋ](syllables/ㄇㄛㄋ.md)
- Reading overridden (2026-07-12) from the MC-derived ㄇㄛㄫ to **ㄇㄛㄋ**, alongside 檸's ㄋ⼶ㄫ→ㄌㄝ, so that [[words/檸檬|檸檬]] spells "lemon" directly: `mc_id: 0` confirms this pair is unattested in Classical Chinese, so the MC initial/final are borrowed-by-analogy, not real inheritance, and the daughter languages show no shared inherited reading either — Japanese レモン and Korean 레몬 both borrow the Western word directly, and Vietnamese uses the native word chanh.
