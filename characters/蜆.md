## Notes
- 形声 (OC *qʰeːnʔ): semantic [[Radical 142|虫]] ("insect, creeping creature") + phonetic [[見 (char)|見]] (OC *keːns) — the basket clam (Corbicula, the small brackish and freshwater clams eaten in East Asia; Japanese しじみ), also the chrysalis of a butterfly in the second Mandarin reading xiàn; it survives in the Chinese butterfly family names such as 蜆蝶科. The dictionaries (English Wiktionary, Zhengzhang's reconstruction) give the clam reading a Middle Chinese 曉 x + 先開 en (xenX, rising tone), the same initial and final as 顕, so the Dan'a'yo sound is the same ㄏㄝㄋ; it does not follow the glide-bearing ㄏ⼶ㄋ of 現 (匣 + 先開), which would have been the sound of the related 峴. The second Mandarin reading xiàn (henX / khenH in Middle Chinese, the chrysalis sense) is not separately represented in Dan'a'yo.
- Homophones (注音 ㄏㄝㄋ): [[顕]] "prominent", [[弦 (char)|弦]] "bowstring" (see also the word [[弦]]), [[賢]] "wise", [[憲]] "constitution", [[献]] "offer", [[舷]] "side of a boat", [[軒]] "flats".
