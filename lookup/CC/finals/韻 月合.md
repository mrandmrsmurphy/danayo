> **Final 月合** fractures four ways, tracking initial type and voicing (see below)

## CJKV Evolution
月合 [ʉɐt] fractures into 4 groups, with the Vowels table's documented value (`et/wet`, row 068 of 文法 - 99韻図) covering its two largest groups: **ㄝㄊ** (4: 閥, 罰, 襪, 伐), **⼔ㄊ** (4: 越, 曰, 月, 粤, all null-initial in Dan'a'yo), **ㄚㄊ** (2: 髪, 発), and **⺢ㄊ** (1: 闕).

The cleanest pattern is a vowel split inside the labiodental subset that follows the daughters: 伐 (voiced v-initial; Mandarin *fá*, Cantonese *fat6*, Korean 벌, Vietnamese *phạt*) lands on ㄈㄝㄊ, while 髪 and 発 (voiceless f-initial; *fà*/*fā*, *faat3*, Korean 발, Vietnamese *phát*) land on ㄈㄚㄊ, so Cantonese's long `aa` and Korean's `a` against the short `a` and Korean `eo` of 伐 track voicing and vowel together. This is an initial-conditioned rule supported by the daughter readings, not homophony avoidance. 閥, 罰 and 伐 share the same readings in every daughter (Mandarin *fá*, Cantonese *fat6*, Korean 벌) and the same v initial, and differ only in the Dan'a'yo initial letter (ㄅ for 閥 and 罰, ㄈ for 伐), which belongs to the 奉 initial page, not to this final.

The four null-initial characters 越, 曰, 月 and 粤 (Middle Chinese ø, ŋ and ɣ) all take ⼔ㄊ, matching the w-glide of a 合 final; their daughters are Cantonese *jyut6*/*joek6*, Korean 월/왈 and Vietnamese *việt*/*viết*/*nguyệt*.
