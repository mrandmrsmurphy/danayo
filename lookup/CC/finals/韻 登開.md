> [Classical Chinese](../Classical%20Chinese.md)
> **Final 登開** genuinely three-way splits, matching its own ambiguous documented D value

## CJKV Evolution
登開 [əŋ] is one of the few finals whose Vowels-table D value is itself listed as ambiguous (`ǝ/o/ung`), and the corpus bears that out directly: of 22 characters, 11 land on **ㄨㄫ** (the ung reading), 10 land on **ㄜㄫ** (the ǝ reading), and 1 (**硼**) lands on **ㄛㄫ** (the o reading).

The split is partly conditioned by initial. Every sibilant or affricate initial (s, ts, dz: **僧 層 贈 曽 憎 増**) and n (**能**) lands on ㄜㄫ, while every labial, velar, glottal and liquid initial (b, p, k, kʰ, ɣ, l: **朋 崩 亙 肯 恒 稜**) lands on ㄨㄫ. The dental stops are where it gets less clean. The t-initial characters **登 灯 等 橙** and the d-initial **鄧** all take ㄉㄨㄫ, but three other d-initial characters, **騰 藤 謄**, take ㄉㄜㄫ. The daughters show an aspiration difference that tracks this: 騰/藤/謄 are aspirated in Mandarin and Cantonese (*téng*, *tang4*), while 鄧 is unaspirated (*dèng*, *dang6*), so the two groups do not share an input, even though the corpus gives them the same MC initial label. ㄉㄜㄫ holds no other crowding member, so this is not a crowding escape.

**硼** is the only ㄛㄫ character: its expected slot ㄅㄨㄫ is already held by **朋** and **崩** (both Mandarin *bēng/péng*, Cantonese *bang1/pang4*), so it dodges by a vowel shift, a crowding-driven escape. Because the three outcomes are the three values the Vowels table itself lists, this reads as a genuine three-way historical split rather than a single dominant outcome with motivated exceptions.
