> [Classical Chinese](../Classical%20Chinese.md)
> **Final 諄** splits into ㄨㄋ vs ⼜ㄋ by initial type, with four characters scattering to four different alternate codas (see below)

## CJKV Evolution
諄 [iuɪn] splits fairly evenly by initial type: 16 characters (mostly real-consonant, non-sibilant initials) land on **ㄨㄋ**, while 14 (null-initial plus most sibilants) land on **⼜ㄋ**, and the Vowels table's documented value (`un/yun`, row 047 of 文法 - 99韻図) names both.

The sibilant-initial (ㄙ) cohort is 16 characters: 淳 and 醇 sit on ㄨㄋ, ten (循, 詢, 峻, 巡, 旬, 殉, 洵, 瞬, 迅, 順) on ⼜ㄋ, and the remaining four scatter to **four different codas**. All sixteen have an -n coda in Mandarin, Cantonese and Korean alike (the four escapees read *sǔn*/*sǔn*/*shùn*/*xún* with Cantonese *zeon2*/*seon2*/*seon3*/*seon4* and Korean 준/순/순/순), so no daughter supports the changed coda:

- **隼** → **⼜ㄫ** (ŋ-coda)
- **筍** → **⼜ㄊ** (t-coda, a stop — the coda's manner changes entirely, not just its place)
- **舜** → **⼜ㄇ** (m-coda)
- **馴** → **⼜ㄎ** (k-coda, also a stop)

The four don't converge on one escape route; they spread across four mutually distinct codas. 隼 and 筍 share Mandarin *sǔn*, 舜 shares *shùn* and Korean 순 with 瞬 and 順 on ⼜ㄋ, and 馴 shares *xún* and Korean 순 with 循 and 詢, so each escapee has a homophone or near-homophone on the crowded ⼜ㄋ slot.

A smaller, more ordinary exception: **錀** dodges a modest 2-member ㄌㄨㄋ (輪, 倫, both l-initial) via a plain vowel shift to **ㄛㄋ**.
