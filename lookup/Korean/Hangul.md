# Hangul and 注音 (諺文 ↔ 注音)

Dan'a'yo's 諺文 field is not a record of how a character is pronounced in Korean. It is a regular hangul spelling of the character's Dan'a'yo 注音 reading, so every 注音 syllable has exactly one 諺文 form, and the mapping runs both ways. This page gives that mapping, and then says how it differs from an actual Korean reading. It parallels [[Kana]] for Japanese. The sound inventory is in [[文法 - 02音韻論]] and the symbols in [[Bopomofo]]. The table below was checked against the 諺文 and 注音 of every character page that has both (905 distinct syllable pairs). The tables give the regular value; a few character pages carry one-off exceptions (a ㄅ written ㅃ, a ㄙ written ㅈ, and a few ㅗ written ㅜ), which look like typos and are not rules.

ㄈ is written ㅃ, not ㅍ, so Dan'a'yo ㅃ means /f/ and is not the Korean tense p.

## Finals

| 注音 | 諺文 | 注音 | 諺文 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ㄇ | ㅁ | ㄎ | ㄱ |
| ㄋ | ㄴ | ㄊ | ㄷ |
| ㄫ | ㅇ | ㄆ | ㅂ |

The off-glide finals (w) are all written with ㅅ on the preceding vowel: ㄚㄨ is 앗, ㄛㄨ is 옷, ㄨㄛ is 웃, ⼄ㄨ is 욧, ⼘ㄨ is 얏, ⼜ㄛ is 윳, ⺢ㄨ is 왓, and ㄜㄧ is 읫. These ㅅ finals are not Korean sibilant finals.

## How it differs from a Korean reading

The 諺文 form is a spelling of the Dan'a'yo reading, which is derived from Middle Chinese in [[Character Creation]], so it can differ from the Sino-Korean reading in the `korean` field. The names on [[Korea]] show this:

| Character | Korean (`korean`) | 諺文 | 注音 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 安 | 안 | 안 | ㄚㄋ |
| 重 | 중 | 총 | ㄑㄛㄫ |
| 根 | 근 | 근 | ㄍㄜㄋ |
| 九 | 구 | 큐 | ㄎ⼜ |
| 国 | 국 | 곡 | ㄍㄛㄎ |
| 日 | 일 | 닏 | ㄋㄧㄊ |
| 朝 | 조 | 잣 | ㄐㄚㄨ |
| 鮮 | 선 | 셤 | ㄙ⼶ㄇ |

So the Dan'a'yo form of 安重根 is ㄚㄋ ㄑㄛㄫ ㄍㄜㄋ, which is not a transliteration of the Korean 안중근. The rule for names is to use the Dan'a'yo form, and to give the Korean hangul beside it, labelled, as on the lists. That `korean` field is the North Korean reading, and for `kwin` a final ㄹ counts the same as a final ㄷ.

## Not covered

- Tense consonants (ㄲ ㄸ ㅆ ㅉ) and the Korean ㅃ have no 注音 letters, since ㅃ is taken for ㄈ.
- Korean does not separate ㅐ from ㅔ, but Dan'a'yo does (ㄚㄧ and ㄝㄧ), so a Korean reading cannot be turned back into 注音 without the character. Use the character page.
- Words that are Korean only, with no character page, need a decision; none has come up yet.
