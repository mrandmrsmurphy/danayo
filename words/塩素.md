## Notes
- [塩 (char)](characters/塩%20(char).md) "salt" + [素](characters/素.md) "element" — chlorine, so named for its role in table salt (鹽/塩化ナトリウム, sodium chloride)
- We avoid the novel Chinese character invented just for this element, 氯/㲶, in favor of the Japanese-style compositional name 塩素 (literally "salt element"), matching Japanese えんそ directly.

塩素 (Cl, atomic number 17) takes its name from its central role in common salt, sodium chloride. Japanese 塩素 (えんそ, enso) is the real, established Japanese word for the element; Korean 염소 (yeomso) is the compositional Sino-Korean equivalent (염 "salt" + 소 "element"). Vietnamese clo is a direct phonetic loan from the international/Latin name, the real Vietnamese term for the element, rather than a Sino-Vietnamese compositional reading.

**Content removed/corrected**: `mandarin`/`cantonese` previously stored `lǜ`/`luk6` — the reading of the single-character name 氯 that this word deliberately avoids, not a reading of 塩素 itself — corrected to the compositional `yánsù`/`jim4 sou3` (matching `characters/塩 (char).md`'s own `yán`/`jim4` and `characters/素.md`'s own `sù`/`sou3`), consistent with how the rest of this page already treats 塩素 as a real two-character compound rather than a stand-in for 氯.
