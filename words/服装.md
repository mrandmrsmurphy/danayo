## Notes
- [服](../characters/服.md) "serve, submit" + [装 (char)](../characters/装%20(char).md) "dress up, clothe" — the "clothes one submits to/wears," i.e. one's dress, outfit, or costume.

服装 differs in nuance from the simpler [[衣服]] ("clothes"): it emphasizes the manner or style of being dressed — a uniform, costume, or outfit as a whole — rather than garments as bare objects. Mandarin fúzhuāng, Cantonese fuk6 zong1, and Japanese ふくそう (fukusō) are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 服's and 装's own stored citations exactly. Korean 복장 (bokjang) is likewise compositional (복 + 장) and carries the same "manner of dress, attire" nuance as the Mandarin/Japanese forms — more formal than the plain word for "clothes."

Vietnamese trang phục (verified, a common and standard term) reverses the character order found in Mandarin/Japanese/Korean — built from 装's own trang and 服's own phục, but in trang-phục rather than phục-trang order; this order-reversal is attested and standard, not an error. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 북좡 diverges from Sino-Korean 복장 in the second syllable's coda. No homophones (注音 ㄅㄨㄎㄐ⺢ㄫ unique among both words and characters).
