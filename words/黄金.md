**Content corrected**: `japanese` was `おーごん`, mixing the katakana long-vowel bar (ー) into a hiragana string — corrected to `おうごん` (ōgon), the standard hiragana spelling. Also corrected `kwin` from `false` to `true`: both [[黄]] (char) and [[金]] (char) are individually kwin, so the AND-rule was actually satisfied.

黄金 (Au, atomic number 79) is a real, established compositional word, not an invented calque. Mandarin huángjīn, Cantonese wong4 gam1, Japanese おうごん (ōgon), and Korean 황금 (hwanggeum) all follow the same direct compositional pattern — though in Japanese, おうごん sits in a more formal/figurative register (黄金時代, ōgon jidai, "golden age"; 黄金比, ōgon-hi, "golden ratio") above the everyday きん (kin, the default word for the metal). Vietnamese vàng is the everyday word for both the metal "gold" and the color "yellow/gold"; the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form hoàng kim is also attested but, like Japanese おうごん, skews literary/figurative (thời hoàng kim, "a golden age") rather than serving as the everyday noun.
