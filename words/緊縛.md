## Notes
- [緊](../characters/緊.md) "tense, tight, firm" + [縛](../characters/縛.md) "tie, bind" — no stand-in relationship applies (緊's own stand-in is [[緊張]]; 縛's own is [[束縛]]).

緊縛 means bondage — literally "tight binding."  緊 contributes tightness and 縛 the tying up, so the word covers any tight tying, with the sexual and artistic sense of rope bondage as its best-known use today.  Neither character is legitimized here, since 緊's stand-in is 緊張 and 縛's is 束縛.

Japanese きんばく (kinbaku) is the real, famous, independently attested word: the specific rope-bondage art form (also known as shibari) that this compound names internationally, well beyond just this vault's own compositional derivation. Mandarin jǐnfù and Cantonese gan2 bok3 are understood compositionally and used in coverage of Japanese kinbaku culture, though the more native, independent Chinese word for "bind tightly, bondage" generally is 綁縛 (bǎngfù) — an equivalent term this vault cannot yet create, since its first character 綁 has no character page here. Korean 긴박 is a defensible compositional reading, though real spoken Korean has no independent attestation of this exact term in the sexual sense (and, coincidentally, 긴박 is already the real Korean reading of the unrelated word 緊迫, "urgent, pressing" — a real-world homophone between two different hanja compounds, not a Dan'a'yo-internal collision). Vietnamese khẩn phọc is likewise a defensible compositional derivation rather than an independently attested fixed term. `kwin` is false per the AND-rule: 縛 is individually `kwin: false`, regardless of 緊's own `kwin: true`.
