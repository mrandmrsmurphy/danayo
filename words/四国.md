## Notes
- [四 (char)](../characters/四%20(char).md) "four" + [国](../characters/国.md) "country; nation"

四国 has two senses. As a proper noun it is Shikoku, the smallest of the four main islands of Japan, home of Ehime, Kagawa, Tokushima and Kōchi prefectures; the name means "four provinces" for the four old provinces of the island. In its literary and figurative sense it is "the world; everything under the sky; all under heaven," the four quarters and so the whole world. According to Wiktionary, Chinese 四國 (sìguó, Cantonese sei3 gwok3) has both the place name and the literary sense, and Japanese しこく is Shikoku. Korean 사국 and Vietnamese tứ quốc are compositional readings, not independently checked. The island sense is used for places such as [愛媛](愛媛.md).

In the 1945 surrender broadcast ([玉音放送](../translation/玉音放送.md)), 美英中蘇四国 is read literally, "the four countries America, Britain, China and the Soviet Union," so there the compositional reading and the word coincide.
