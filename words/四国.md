## Notes
- [四 (char)](../characters/四%20(char).md) "four" + [国](../characters/国.md) "country; nation"

四国 is "the world; everything under the sky; all under heaven," the literary and figurative sense of the compound: the four quarters, and so the whole world. According to Wiktionary, Chinese 四國 (sìguó, Cantonese sei3 gwok3) has this literary figurative sense, and is also the name of the island Shikoku, which is how Japanese しこく uses the spelling; Dan'a'yo takes the literary sense, and the island is a separate use of the same spelling. Korean 사국 and Vietnamese tứ quốc are compositional readings, not independently checked.

In the 1945 surrender broadcast on [Japan](../lookup/Japanese/Japan.md), 美英中蘇四国 is read literally, "the four countries America, Britain, China and the Soviet Union," so there the compositional reading and the word coincide.
