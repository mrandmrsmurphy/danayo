## Notes
- [流](../characters/流.md) "flow" + [亡](../characters/亡.md) "die, flee"

流亡 is the general verb for exile — to be exiled, to wander or flee in exile, typically for political reasons — as distinct from [亡命](亡命.md) "to be forced into exile" (the more specifically compelled, flight-for-one's-life sense) and [放逐](放逐.md) "to send someone into exile" (the transitive act done *to* someone else). Vietnamese lưu vong is real and very well attested, especially in reference to the post-1975 Vietnamese diaspora (chính phủ lưu vong, "government in exile"). Mandarin liúwáng is likewise standard and common.

Japanese るぼう and Korean 류망 are compositional; both languages more often reach for [亡命](亡命.md) to cover this whole conceptual space rather than keeping a separate general/compelled distinction the way Mandarin and Vietnamese do.
