## Notes
- [流](../characters/流.md) "flow" + [亡](../characters/亡.md) "die, flee" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (流's own is [[流動]]; 亡's own is [[死亡]]), so 流亡 is an independent compound. `kwin: true` per the AND-rule: `characters/流.md` and `characters/亡.md` are both individually `kwin: true`.

流亡 is the general verb for exile — to be exiled, to wander or flee in exile, typically for political reasons — as distinct from [[亡命]] "to be forced into exile" (the more specifically compelled, flight-for-one's-life sense) and [[放逐]] "to send someone into exile" (the transitive act done *to* someone else). Vietnamese lưu vong is real and very well attested, especially in reference to the post-1975 Vietnamese diaspora (chính phủ lưu vong, "government in exile"). Mandarin liúwáng is likewise standard and common.

Japanese るぼう and Korean 류망 are compositional; both languages more often reach for [[亡命]] to cover this whole conceptual space rather than keeping a separate general/compelled distinction the way Mandarin and Vietnamese do.
