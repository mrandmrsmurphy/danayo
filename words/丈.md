## Notes
- [丈 (char)](../characters/丈%20(char).md) "zhang; gentleman, husband"

丈 is a 会意 (ideogrammic compound) character combining 十 ("ten") and 又 ("hand"), originally depicting ten *chi* (尺, "foot") measured out by hand — one *zhang* (丈) equals ten *chi*, about 3.3 meters. The unit sense survives in set phrases like [[波乱万丈]] ("agitated by ten-thousand-foot waves," a wildly dramatic turn of events) and in an honorific extension to "an adult male of full stature," which broadened to "gentleman, elder, husband" — the sense behind [[丈夫]] ("husband"), [[丈人]]/[[丈母]] (father-in-law/mother-in-law), and [[姑丈]] (father's sister's husband, "uncle-in-law").

Japanese ちょう (chō) and Korean 장 (jang) both preserve the length-unit sense mainly in historical/literary contexts, since Japan and Korea use metric units in modern life; Vietnamese trượng likewise survives chiefly in classical/historical measurement contexts rather than everyday speech. The "husband/in-law" sense is comparatively more Mandarin-specific — Japanese and Korean each reach for different native or compound vocabulary for "husband" (夫/おっと, 남편) rather than 丈 alone.

1. An ancient unit of length, about 3.3m — [[波乱万丈]] "full of dramatic ups and downs" (lit. "agitated from a 10,000-foot wave")
2. Gentleman, husband (in compounds) — [[丈夫]] "husband," [[丈人]] "father-in-law," [[丈母]] "mother-in-law," [[姑丈]] "father's sister's husband"
