## Notes
- [客](../characters/客.md) "guest, traveller" + [気 (char)](../characters/気%20(char).md) "feeling" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (客's is 客人, 気's is bare 気). `kwin: false` per the AND-rule, since both are individually `kwin: false`.

>[!caution] A genuine Sinosphere auto-antonym — use with care
>客気/客氣 is not merely ambiguous, it is structurally self-contradictory, and this vault generally advises against reaching for it in Dan'a'yo composition (it cannot be formally forbidden the way a character can, since that mechanism doesn't apply to words — this is guidance, not a rule).

**Why it splits, not just that it does**: 客 ("guest, outsider") + 気 ("disposition, energy") literally names the psychological position of *not* being the grounded, authoritative host. That position is inherently unstable, and the Sinosphere resolved the instability in two opposite directions rather than one:
- **Deferential restraint** — shrinking the self to respect the host's boundaries. Mandarin 客气/Cantonese haak3 hei3 ("polite, reserved, standing on ceremony," e.g. 别客气 "don't be so polite" = "you're welcome") and Vietnamese khách khí (formal, ceremonious, holding back) both develop this pole. Verified via search on both.
- **Ungrounded bravado** — puffing up the self to mask the outsider's lack of substance. Japanese かっき (also read きゃくき/きゃっき, glossed directly in a kanji dictionary as "ものにはやる心," a mind that rushes hastily into things, i.e. 血気/vigor) and Korean 객기 (living, dominant sense per standard dictionaries: 객쩍게 부리는 혈기나 용기, "reckless bravado, foolhardy courage," e.g. 객기 부리다 "to act with misplaced bravado," 객기 빼다 "to drop the bravado and act sensibly") both develop this opposite pole instead. Verified via search on both, including the specific idiom.

Same two characters, same root instability, two structurally opposite behavioral compensations — a real auto-antonym across the macro-family, not a simple polysemy or a translation error on either side.

**If a disambiguated form is ever genuinely needed**, the two poles could be split with a prefix rather than reusing the bare compound: something like 拘客氣 ("constrained guestness," 拘 "restrain, confine" — the deferential-restraint pole) vs. 浮客氣 ("floating guestness," 浮 "float, superficial" — the ungrounded-bravado pole). Not coined as of this writing; noted here as the documented path if the need arises.
