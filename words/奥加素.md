## Notes
- [奥 (char)](../characters/奥%20(char).md) "deep" + [加](../characters/加.md) "increase" + [素](../characters/素.md) "element" — a transliteration of *Oganessian*, after physicist Yuri Oganessian. 奥加 renders the first two syllables of his name, truncated to keep the coinage compact rather than transliterating the name in full the way Mandarin's borrowed-character system would. Japanese and Korean instead borrow the IUPAC name as a loanword (オガネソン, 오가네손).

Oganesson is element 118, the heaviest element named to date, synthesized at the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Dubna and named in 2016 for the Armenian-Russian nuclear physicist Yuri Oganessian (b. 1933) — one of only two elements named after a person still living at the time of naming (the other being seaborgium). It sits at the bottom of the noble-gas column, though relativistic effects are predicted to make it far more reactive than its lighter congeners, possibly even solid at room temperature.

The real Chinese element name is the single character 鿫 (U+9FEB), coined by the 全国科学技术名词审定委员会 in 2017: a phono-semantic compound of 气 (the gas-class semantic used for all gaseous elements) and 奥 as phonetic. Per this series' established convention (per [[丹金]], [[加州金]]), the `mandarin`/`cantonese` fields hold the avoided real element character's reading — here `ào`/`ou3`, which by a neat coincidence are identical to [[奥 (char)|奥]]'s own readings, since 鿫's phonetic component is 奥 itself. Vietnamese uses *Oganesson*, the attested term from Vietnamese Wikipedia.
