## Notes
- [即 (char)](../characters/即%20(char).md) "namely; in other words" (stand-in for 即)

即 is a discourse particle meaning "namely," "in other words," or "that is to say" — used to introduce a restatement, clarification, or precise equivalent of what has just been said, rather than to add new information. Etymologically it is 会意 (semantic-semantic compound): 皀 ("an ancient round-mouthed food vessel") + 卩 (a kneeling figure), originally depicting a person kneeling to approach food — "to come near, to approach" — from which the modern sense of immediate identity or equivalence ("this is precisely that") developed by extension: 即 marks that two expressions are being brought into direct correspondence.

Cross-linguistically, 即 functions somewhat differently in each daughter language's register. In Mandarin it remains a formal, literary connective (即, 亦即), more common in writing than in casual speech, where 也就是說 or 就是 would be used instead. In Japanese, 即ち (sunawachi) is the real, commonly used native reading for this function — the on'yomi そく appears mainly bound in compounds (即位, 即日) rather than standing alone as a connective. Korean 즉 is fully current as an everyday connective meaning "namely, in other words," used constantly in both speech and writing without any register restriction. Vietnamese tức (as in tức là) likewise functions as an ordinary, unmarked way of saying "that is to say."
