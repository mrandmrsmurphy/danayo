## Notes
- [峨](../characters/峨.md) "lofty; towering" (doubled) — this word is the stand-in for [峨](../characters/峨.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

峨峨 is a reduplicated intensifier — 峨 alone already means "lofty, towering," and doubling it emphasizes the sheer, steep majesty of mountains or crags rising sharply into view, too steep to easily climb. This character-reduplication pattern for intensified description (堂堂, 洋洋, and others) is common in classical Chinese poetic register.

Japanese がが (used attributively with たる/と, as in 峨峨たる稜線, "a rugged ridgeline") is a real, if literary, term. Korean 아아 and Vietnamese nga nga follow the same reduplicated pattern. No homophones (注音 ㄚㄚ unique across both words and characters).
