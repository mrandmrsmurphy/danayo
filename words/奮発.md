## Notes
- [奮](../characters/奮.md) "stirred up" + [発 (char)](../characters/発%20(char).md) "discharge"
- This word is the viable form of [奮](../characters/奮.md): the character's meaning (to stir up, rouse) is identical, but 奮 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 奮発 is its `stand_in` compound.

奮 depicts a bird beating its wings to fly up, hence rousing oneself to vigorous action; 発 adds "to send forth, set off." Together 奮発 is to rouse one's spirit and launch into a determined effort, the sense kept in Mandarin fènfā, Cantonese fan5 faat3 and Korean 분발. (Mandarin more often uses the near-synonym 奮發 in the written register, and the traditional-form 發 is the parent of 発.)

Japanese ふんぱつ shows real semantic drift: besides "exerting oneself," 奮発する developed the everyday sense of splurging, spending generously on something special (奮発して寿司を食べる "treat oneself to sushi"), as if one "exerts" one's wallet. That colloquial sense is Japan-specific and not shared by the other languages. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared core sense, "exert oneself, make a special effort," and does not adopt the Japanese splurge sense.
