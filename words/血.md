## Notes
- [血 (char)](../characters/血%20(char).md) "blood" — stand-in for [[血 (char)|血]], which cannot appear independently

血 is 会意: 一 ("a drop") over [[皿 (char)|皿]] ("a sacrificial vessel") — a drop of blood inside a ritual chalice, the character page's own Notes tying it directly to ancestral blood-sacrifice practice rather than to the substance in the abstract. Beyond the plain medical/biological sense (血液 "blood," 輸血 "transfuse blood," 出血 "bleed, hemorrhage"), the character carries into folklore (吸血鬼 "vampire," a modern loan-concept phrased with entirely native characters) and into ritual/covenant language directly continuous with its own etymology — 血誓盟約 ("a covenant in blood") makes the sacrificial-vessel origin explicit again at the idiom level, a rare case in this sweep where a character's oldest attested meaning and its modern figurative use point at the same underlying image rather than having drifted apart.

Japanese carries a single on'yomi けつ (KETSU, 血液 ketsueki, 出血 shukketsu), alongside the fully productive native 訓読み ち (chi), the everyday word for blood itself. Vietnamese distinguishes two living registers on the character's own page: huyết (used here, the formal/medical register — huyết áp "blood pressure," huyết thống "bloodline") and tiết, a colloquial/culinary-register word for blood as a food ingredient (as in tiết canh, a traditional blood-based dish) — both genuinely attested, unlike the character's third stored candidate hoét, which shows no comparable independent life. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 훧 (hwed, from Middle Chinese 曉母 x + 屑合韻 wet) diverges from Sino-Korean 혈 in both onset rounding and coda. No homophones (注音 ㄏ⼔ㄊ shared only by [[穴]], a character with its own separate `stand_in`, 洞穴, not itself).
