## Notes
- [嘱](../characters/嘱.md) "order, instruct" + [託](../characters/託.md) "entrust, rely on, commission"
- This word is the stand-in for [嘱](../characters/嘱.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

To formally commission or entrust a task to someone — a handover of responsibility. In Japanese, しょくたく (shokutaku) is an employment and administrative term for a special-contract worker or commissioned person outside the regular staff — 嘱託社員 "contract/commissioned employee." The word combines the sense of 嘱 "to charge someone with a duty" and 託 "to entrust." In Chinese, 嘱托 (zhǔtuō) means "to ask someone to do something on your behalf; to entrust a matter to," appearing in contexts of last wishes (临终嘱托 "deathbed request") and delegated tasks. Korean 촉탁 (囑託) is used in bureaucratic and legal contexts for a commissioned or outsourced function.

It is close to [委託](委託.md) "to entrust, consign" and [依頼](依頼.md) "to request," but 嘱託 is the more formal of the three and carries the sense of a charge laid on someone (嘱 "to enjoin") that is then entrusted (託), whereas 委託 is the plain commercial or legal handing-over (a consignment) and 依頼 a request that may be refused. The Government page places the word for its administrative use, and Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "commission, entrust," with the Japanese employment sense (a contract worker outside the regular staff) as a regional extension, not a separate Dan'a'yo meaning. The Sino-Vietnamese reading is chúc thác; the alias forms 囑託 and 嘱托 are the traditional and simplified-Chinese spellings.
