## Notes
- [重](../characters/重%20(char).md) "heavy; double" + [慶](../characters/慶%20(char).md) "celebrate" — no stand-in relationship applies (both characters are their own stand-ins). 庆 is the alias of 慶, so the filename uses 慶; 重庆 is kept as the word's alias.

重慶 (Chongqing) is a municipality in southwest China, one of the four direct-administered municipalities (直轄市, "municipality directly under the central government") with 北京, 上海 and 天津, and the only one inland (see [China](../lexipedia/China.md) for the other three). Per Wiktionary, the name is literally "double celebration": in 1189 Emperor Guangzong of Song was enfeoffed as a king and then crowned emperor, and the prefecture of 恭州 was raised to 重慶府 in honour of it. The city was the wartime capital of the Republic of China from about 1938 until about 1946 (dates from memory, not re-checked).

The reading follows [重](../characters/重%20(char).md), whose page gives 注音 ㄑㄛㄫ for the heavy sense (Mandarin zhòng). The city name itself is Mandarin Chóngqìng, with 重 read chóng ("again, double"). The two Mandarin readings of 重 descend from the same Middle Chinese initial and final (澄 + 鍾), so the Dan'a'yo syllable ㄑㄛㄫ stands for both and the sound is the same either way; Mandarin tone is not kept in 注音. The Japanese field holds the Sino-Japanese reading じゅうけい (Wiktionary's Japanese entry gives 重(じゅう)慶(けい) for the city); the usual spoken Japanese name today is チョンチン, borrowed from Mandarin. Korean 중경 is the North Korean reading of the characters; the usual Korean name today is 충칭 (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Trùng Khánh is the Sino-Vietnamese name (also a district in Cao Bằng province).

Attestation: the Chongqing sense is found on the English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries, and on the Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wikipedias. The two ordinary senses below are in English, Chinese and Japanese Wiktionary (literary, now rare). No homophones (注音 ㄑㄛㄫㄎ⼶ㄫ unique across both words and characters).

1. (literary) the state of having both parents and grandparents still alive; a double celebration.
2. (literary, verb) to celebrate again.
3. Chongqing, the municipality.
