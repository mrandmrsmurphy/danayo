## Notes
- [清](../characters/清.md) "clear; pure; clean" + [澈](../characters/澈.md) "limpid; transparent (of water)" — stand-in for [澈](../characters/澈.md), which cannot appear independently
- A near-synonym compound describing water, sky, or sound so clear that one can see straight through it — 清 supplying the broad sense of purity and cleanliness, 澈 narrowing it to the specific visual transparency of clear water (its radical 氵 marks it as a water-word from the start). Mandarin qīngchè and Cantonese cing1 cit3 use it everywhere from rivers to eyes to voices; the Sino-Korean reading 청철 (cheongcheol) survives mainly in literary and name-用 contexts (e.g. the old musical idiom 청철장려, "clear and stirring," describing the bright 우조 mode), while Japanese has no native loanword for the compound and instead renders the same idea with phrases like 澄み切っている — 澈 itself appearing far more often barred to compounds like this one than standing as an everyday word. (The Korean idiom 청철장려 淸澈壯勵 is attested in Korean dictionaries as a description of the high, bold feel of the 우조 mode; the same Korean syllables 청철 also name an unrelated copper alloy, a coincidence of sound only.)

The Water page ranks it as one of three progressively narrower near-synonyms for clarity: [清潔](清潔.md) is the everyday "clean," [澄清](澄清.md) is clarity or limpidity in general, and 清澈 is clarity specifically of water, marked by the water radical of 澈. The Valuation page lists it, with 澄清, among the words for "clear" that it excludes as a false match, since they mean transparent liquid and not mental clarity, so it belongs only to the Water sense. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "clear, limpid," with the water-specific emphasis, and with extended use to other transparent things (an eye, a voice, a sky) understood as figurative.
