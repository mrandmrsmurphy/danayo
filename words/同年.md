## Notes
- [同](../characters/同.md) "same" + [年 (char)](../characters/年%20(char).md) "year"

同年 most directly means "the same year" — two events, births, or terms of office falling in the identical calendar year: 同年出生 ("born in the same year"), 同年当選 ("elected in the same year"). By extension it names a person who shares that year with the speaker: a peer of the same age, or (in its most historically specific sense) a fellow candidate who passed the imperial civil service examination — [[及第]] — in the same sitting. Examination-mate 同年 relationships were treated as a significant lifelong social bond in traditional China, Korea, and Vietnam, comparable to modern classmates but carrying additional weight since passing together could mean decades of overlapping official careers.

Vietnamese đồng niên preserves this "peer of the same age" sense as everyday vocabulary — bạn đồng niên ("a friend of the same age") is common and unremarkable, more alive in ordinary speech than the Mandarin or Japanese cognates, which lean more literary/formal. Japanese どうねん and Korean 동년 are both standard compositional Sino-readings, used more for the literal "same year" sense than for the peer-relationship sense.
