## Notes
- [吉 (char)](../characters/吉%20(char).md) "lucky" + [林 (char)](../characters/林%20(char).md) "grove" — short for 吉林烏拉, from Manchu girin ula. No stand-in relationship applies (both characters are their own stand-ins).

The name is both a city and a province. Jilin, the city, is a prefecture-level city of the province (English and Chinese Wiktionary list it as a separate sense); Jilin, the province, is in northeast China and its capital is [[長春]], not the city Jilin (from memory, not re-checked). English Wiktionary says the name is shortened from 吉林烏拉 (Jilin Ula), which was borrowed from Manchu girin ula, so the two characters are used for their sound and the literal reading "lucky grove" is incidental.

The Japanese field holds きつりん (English Wiktionary Japanese). The Korean field holds the Sino-Korean 길림; the usual South Korean name today is 지린 (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Cát Lâm is the Sino-Vietnamese name. Cantonese gat1 lam4 is the Jyutping on English Wiktionary. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄧㄊㄌㄧㄇ unique across both words and characters).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English and Chinese Wiktionary (Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese sections) list the senses above.

1. Jilin, the city in Jilin province.
2. Jilin, the province of northeast China.
