## Notes
- [治](../characters/治.md) "govern, manage" + [癒](../characters/癒.md) "get well, recover" — to cure, to heal; healing. Functions as both verb and verbal noun. Attested across Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, and Korean; no Vietnamese attestation was found, so the Vietnamese field holds the compositional reading.
The blank `vietnamese` field now holds the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading of the two characters, trị dũ (治 trị, 癒 dũ), following the vault's convention for these fields. The Korean 치유 matches the Dan'a'yo 諺文 치유 exactly, so `kwin` is true. The Life page lists the word as "to heal, cure," and Dan'a'yo keeps that sense, with the healing of a wound or illness as its core. It is the outcome-focused word, set apart from [治療](治療.md) "medical treatment," which names the process of treating, and from [回復](回復.md) "recovery, restoration," which is broader than illness. The readings are Mandarin zhìyù, Cantonese zi6 jyu6 and Japanese chiyu (ちゆ). The treatment-versus-outcome contrast is from memory and has not been checked against a source.
