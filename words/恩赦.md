## Notes
- [恩](../characters/恩.md) "kindness; mercy" + [赦](../characters/赦.md) "pardon"

恩赦 is a pardon or amnesty granted by the state: an act of the sovereign, the legislature or the executive that forgives an offence, reducing or removing the sentence. Wiktionary glosses Japanese おんしゃ as "a legislative or executive act by which a state forgives a person for a criminal offense resulting in a reduced or exempted sentence; a pardon", and Mandarin ēnshè as "imperial pardon". Vietnamese ân xá is recorded as "to grant amnesty; to pardon", with the same formal register.

The word is the official act, from above, of mercy shown by authority, as the 恩 "kindness, mercy" in it shows. It is distinct from [赦免](赦免.md), the general word for pardon, amnesty and absolution, which is also used for forgiveness outside a legal act and is housed on the Sin page.
