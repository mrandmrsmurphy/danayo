## Notes
- [現](../characters/現%20(char).md) "present; current" + [用](../characters/用.md) "use" — "in use at present".

現用 describes what is in use right now, as opposed to what is retired, obsolete, or merely available: currently adopted, active. It is a modifier that attaches to nouns of things that exist in sets and get replaced over time: 現用字庫, "the active inventory" of characters in the writing-system chapter ([[文法 - 03文字法]]), is the set of characters presently used, as against characters that have fallen out of use or are not yet admitted. Japanese げんよう is the textbook case ("currently adopted, in actual use"), as in 現用言語 "a language still in use" and, in technical and military contexts, 現用機 "aircraft currently in service" and 現用品.

Mandarin xiànyòng and Korean 현용 are formed on the same elements; Chinese use is mostly technical and administrative (现用名 "present name", 现用汉字), and ordinary speech more often says 现行 or 目前使用的. Compare the series of 用-compounds in the vault: [[常用]] "commonly used" describes frequency of use, [[専用]] "dedicated, exclusive use" restricts it to one user, [[慣用]] "customary use" rests on convention, and [[使用]] "to use" is the act itself. 現用 alone fixes the time of use to the present, and so partners with [[現在]] "now" and the bound [[現]]; its natural opposite is a past-use word such as a former or obsolete status. [[用字]] "characters in use" names the same domain at the level of the whole usage. The Dan'a'yo sense is the shared CJKV one.

Readings: Japanese げんよう was checked against Japanese dictionaries (Weblio, JLearn: "currently adopted, currently in use"). Wiktionary has no 現用 entry, and hvdic.thivien.net does not list the compound, so no `vietnamese` field is given. The Mandarin xiànyòng, Cantonese jin6 jung6 and Korean 현용 are compositional readings from 現 and 用 and were not separately verified as attested entries for this exact compound, and the Chinese usage description above is from general knowledge. 諺文 현용 equals the Korean 현용, so `kwin` is true. No homophones (注音 ㄏ⼶ㄋ·⼄ㄫ is unique among words and chengyu). The dot marks the genuine fork ㄏ⼶ㄋ·⼄ㄫ versus ㄏ⼶·ㄋ⼄ㄫ, as in [[慣用]] and [[専用]].
