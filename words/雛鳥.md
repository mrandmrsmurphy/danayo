## Notes
- [雛](../characters/雛.md) "chick, doll" + [鳥 (char)](../characters/鳥%20(char).md) "bird" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes 雛 as a bound character (鳥's own stand-in is [[鳥]] itself; transitivity fails, so no `#cranberry`).

雛鳥 means a chick or baby bird generally, extending metaphorically to a trainee or beginner (e.g. a trainee pilot). Mandarin 雛鳥/雏鸟 (chúniǎo), Cantonese 雛鳥 (co1niu5), Japanese 雛鳥 (ひなどり, built from the native readings of both characters — distinct from ひよこ, which specifically means "baby chicken" rather than "baby bird" generally), and Sino-Vietnamese sồ điểu all preserve this meaning.

**Found and fixed several real bugs**: `羅馬字` had jucou, mismatched with 雛's own correctly-stored cuo (諺文 춧 and 注音 ㄑㄨㄛ were both already correct) — corrected to cuocou. `cantonese` had co4 nui5 — a wrong tone plus a letter transposition — corrected to co1 niu5 (matching 雛/鳥's own stored readings exactly). `japanese` held ひよこ, the reading of a related but distinct word specifically meaning "baby chicken" — corrected to the real compound reading ひなどり. `korean` held 병아리, 雛's own native gloss (not a Sino-Korean reading at all) — corrected to the compositional 추조.
