## Notes
- [唖](../characters/唖.md) "mute, unable to speak" + [口 (char)](../characters/口%20(char).md) "mouth" — stand-in for [唖](../characters/唖.md), which cannot appear independently outside this compound.

唖口 means mute — unable to speak, whether from a physical/congenital inability or from being struck speechless (啞口無言, "left speechless, at a loss for words," is the compound's most common living use in Mandarin, more idiomatic than a literal medical "mute" today). The character 唖 is 形聲: semantic [口](../characters/口%20(char).md) ("mouth") + phonetic [亜](../characters/亜.md), and carries a secondary sense of "hoarse" alongside "mute."

Mandarin yǎkǒu, Cantonese aa2 hau2, and Japanese あこう (akō) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Korean 아구 is likewise compositional, though the everyday Korean word for "mute" is the native 벙어리 (beongeori) instead — already documented on [唖](../characters/唖.md)'s own character page as its `korean_native` gloss. Vietnamese á khẩu is a real, attested Sino-Vietnamese compound.
