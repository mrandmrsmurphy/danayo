## Notes
- [平](../characters/平.md) "level; flat" + [勒](../characters/勒.md) "to rein in; horizontal stroke"

**平勒** is the *level horizontal*, the horizontal stroke ([[横]], classically 勒, the second of the eight principles of the 永字八法 on the [[Stroke]] page) written flat, in contrast to the slanting horizontal 斜勒. Its best-known appearance is in rule 30 of Huang Ziyuan's *Jiànjià jiégòu zhāiyào jiǔshí'èr fǎ* (《間架結構摘要九十二法》, "Ninety-Two Rules for Character Structure"): a 平勒 written slanted spoils the character's composure, as in 云, 去, 且 and 旦. The preceding rule, 29, gives the opposite instruction for the slanting 斜勒, which must not be written flat (as in 七, 也 and 此).

The name is built on the classical stroke name 勒, a verb "to rein in" (a horse), which the principle of the horizontal stroke takes from the image of holding back the brush as it moves. 勒 and 平 are both bound characters, so the word is a compound of two bound pieces rather than of existing words.

The Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and North Korean forms are compositional, built from the constituent characters. No Vietnamese form is given.
