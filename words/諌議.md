## Notes
- [諌](../characters/諌.md) "to admonish, to remonstrate" + [議](../characters/議.md) "to discuss, to comment" — to remonstrate with and advise a superior, especially a ruler.

諌議 (jiànyì) pairs 諌 (aliased to the more common 諫/谏) — "to admonish, to speak frankly against wrongdoing" — with 議, "to discuss, to deliberate" (cf. [[議員]], [[議定]]). As a verb, it means to candidly point out a ruler's faults and urge correction; as a noun, it is best known as the shortened form of **諫議大夫** (jiàn yì dà fū), the title of an imperial remonstrance official whose formal duty was to criticize the emperor's conduct and policy without fear of reprisal. According to the Chinese Text Project's entry, which cites the 《後漢書·百官志》, the office was called 諫大夫 from the Han (created in 118 BCE under Emperor Wu) and renamed 諫議大夫 in the Eastern Han.

The verbal sense is the one used in **《周禮·地官司徒·保氏》**, whose text (as on Wikisource) reads "保氏：掌諫王惡，而養國子以道" — "the Protector is in charge of remonstrating against the king's faults, and of nurturing the sons of the state in the Way." The vault's own translation of this line (translation/周礼 (book), item 113) renders 諫 as 諌議, giving "主掌諌議王之悪，而養育国子道以," so this is the passage in which Dan'a'yo uses [[主掌]] and 諌議 for the original 掌諫. The compound 諫議/諌議 thus generalizes the specific act of royal admonishment into a single term, later crystallizing into the official title.

The Sino-Japanese かんぎ, Sino-Korean 간의, and Sino-Vietnamese gián nghị readings are straightforward compositional renderings. Simplified 谏议 and traditional 諫議 are recorded as aliases alongside the Dan'a'yo form 諌議, which uses [[諌]] (Dan'a'yo's form of 諫).

The Government page lists it with 輔佐 and 輔弼 as words for acting toward a ruler: 諌議 to remonstrate or admonish, 輔佐 and 輔弼 to assist. Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "to remonstrate, to admonish a ruler."
