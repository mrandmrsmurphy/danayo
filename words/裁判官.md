## Notes
- [裁](../characters/裁.md) "cut; judge, adjudicate" + [判](../characters/判.md) "judge, decide" + [官](../characters/官.md) "official"

裁判官 is the judge, the person who presides over a trial: [裁判](裁判.md) + 官 "official". Wiktionary gives Japanese さいばんかん as "judge", and lists Mandarin cáipànguān as "umpire; referee; judge", with Hong Kong legal usage "magistrate". Korean 재판관 matches the Japanese form. Mandarin and Vietnamese speakers more often say 法官, and Korean and Japanese also use 判事, so this is the one pan-regional form that is transparent from its parts.

[審査](審査.md) is the verb "to judge, review a case"; 裁判官 is the person who does it. The word pairs with [検察官](検察官.md) for the prosecutor.
