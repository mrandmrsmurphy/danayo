## Notes
- [土 (char)](../characters/土%20(char).md) "earth" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 土 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

土 means earth, ground, soil. Mandarin tǔ, Cantonese tou2, Korean 토, and Vietnamese thổ are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese carries two on'yomi in complementary distribution: と (TO, used here, as in 土地 tochi "land") and ど (DO, as in 土曜日 doyōbi "Saturday," 土木 doboku "civil engineering") — while the native 訓読み つち (tsuchi) remains the everyday word for "dirt, soil" itself, the same Sino-Japanese/native split documented throughout this sweep. Within this vault's own periodic-table naming convention, 土 additionally serves as the abbreviation prefix for tellurium via [[土素]].

`kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 토 (to, from Middle Chinese 透母 tʰ + 模韻 uo) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 토. 土 shares its Dan'a'yo reading with two other standalone words, [[兎]] ("rabbit; hare") and [[吐]] ("spit") — a three-way coincidental convergence, unrelated etymologically to any of them.
