## Notes
- [羽](../characters/羽.md) "feather; plume" + [翼](../characters/翼.md) "wing; fin" — stand-in for [[翼]], which is never used on its own

羽翼 names a bird's wing literally, and by extension — the wing as what lifts and supports flight — "support, assistance," or the person providing it (a right-hand man); the historical chengyu 羽翼已成/已豐 ("the wings have already grown/filled out") describes someone who has become fully capable and independent.

The Japanese うよく (uyoku) and Korean 우익 readings are exact homophones of the unrelated, far more common 右翼 ("right wing," politically or militarily; also うよく/우익), since 羽 and 右 share the same Sino-Japanese and Sino-Korean readings. Both languages keep the two as a live homophone pair. Mandarin yǔyì and Vietnamese vũ dực are compositional readings carrying the same literal and figurative senses. These cross-linguistic remarks are from memory, not re-checked.

In Dan'a'yo, the 注音 string ㄨㄧㄎ also appears as the single syllable of [[域]] "domain", but 羽翼 is two syllables (ㄨ + ㄧㄎ), so it is not a true homophone, as 域's own page notes (checked 2026-10-13).
