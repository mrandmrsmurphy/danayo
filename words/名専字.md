## Notes
- [名 (char)](../characters/名%20(char).md) "name" + [専](../characters/専.md) "exclusive; special" + [字 (char)](../characters/字%20(char).md) "character; glyph"

A 名専字 is a character that is used only in proper names: it carries no semantic load of its own, and even educated native readers recognise it only in one specific name (a surname, a place name, a deity). In Dan'a'yo it is the value of the `stand_in` field for such characters, so the character cannot stand in for a word of its own and has no `(char)` suffix on its file, because no word file shares its spelling. The 名専字 are listed on [[List of 名専字]], and a character with this status belongs to the grade 名 (see [[Grade Name]]), though a few characters of other grades also carry it. It contrasts with the 代用字 ([[代用字]]), a common character written in place of a rare one, and with the cranberry-like 借代字, a character bound to a specific compound.

The term is a Dan'a'yo coinage, modelled on the Japanese 人名用漢字 (*jinmeiyō kanji*, "kanji for use in personal names"; compare the Jinmeiyō list), but stricter: the characters are reserved for names alone, hence 専 ("exclusive") in place of 用 ("use"). Its compounds are built from the constituents' own readings: Mandarin míngzhuānzì, Cantonese ming4 zyun1 zi6, Japanese めいせんじ and Korean 명전자 are the compositional readings, with no attestation of the specific compound found. `vietnamese: danh chuyên tự` is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading in the same spirit as that of [[代用字]]. `kwin` is false: the Dan'a'yo 諺文 밍줜지 diverges from the Sino-Korean 명전자.
