## Notes
- [山](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain" + [羊](../characters/羊.md) "sheep, goat" — literally "mountain-goat/sheep," the domestic goat, paired with [[綿羊]] ("wool-sheep") as the sheep/goat half of the traditional [[六畜|Six Domestic Animals]] livestock set.

Mandarin shānyáng and Cantonese saan1 joeng4 are both standard, fully compositional readings, matching 山's and 羊's own stored citations exactly, and 山羊 is itself the ordinary word for domestic goat in both languages. Japanese 山羊 is read やぎ (yagi) as a whole-word native reading (熟字訓) rather than compositionally from 山's and 羊's own on'yomi — but it is likewise the real, standard Japanese word for domestic goat.

**Korean and Vietnamese are a genuine false-friend trap, not a simple compositional match.** The compositional Sino-Korean reading of these exact characters, 산양, is a real Korean word — but it names the long-tailed goral (Naemorhedus caudatus), a wild mountain-dwelling goat-antelope native to Korea, not the domestic goat. The everyday Korean word for domestic goat is instead 염소, a native word unrelated to 山/羊's own citations, stored here since this word specifically means the domesticated animal. The same split happens in Vietnamese: sơn dương (the direct Sino-Vietnamese reading of 山羊) names the serow, a related wild goat-antelope, while the everyday word for domestic goat is dê, likewise stored here as the genuine referent.

`kwin` is true in the narrow phonological sense — Dan'a'yo's own 산양 coincides exactly with the real Sino-Korean reading 산양, since both 山 and 羊 independently carry matching readings on their own character pages — but this is precisely the false-friend case above: the matching reading denotes a different animal in real Korean. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄚㄋ⼘ㄫ unique among both words and characters).
