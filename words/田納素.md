## Notes
- [田](../characters/田.md) "field" + [納 (char)](../characters/納%20(char).md) "receive; admit" + [素](../characters/素.md) "element" — a transliteration of *Tennessee*, after Oak Ridge National Laboratory and the other Tennessee institutions that co-discovered the element. 田納 is the first two syllables of 田納西, the standard Mandarin transliteration of "Tennessee," truncated rather than spelled out in full. Japanese and Korean instead borrow the IUPAC name as a loanword (テネシン, 테네신).

Tennessine is element 117, a superheavy synthetic halogen first announced in 2010 by a joint Russian-American team at Dubna and named by IUPAC in November 2016 after the state of Tennessee, home of Oak Ridge National Laboratory, Vanderbilt University, and the University of Tennessee, all of which contributed to its discovery. Its longest-lived known isotope, ²⁹⁴Ts, has a half-life of only about 51 milliseconds.

The real Chinese element name is the single character 鿬 (U+9FEC, ⿰石田), coined by the 全国科学技术名词审定委员会 in 2017: 石 (the solid nonmetal class-semantic, as in 碘 iodine, 硒 selenium) + 田 as phonetic. Per this series' established convention (per [[丹金]], [[加州金]], [[奥加素]]), the `mandarin`/`cantonese` fields hold the avoided real element character's reading — here `tián`/`tin4`, which are identical to [[田]]'s own readings, since 鿬's phonetic component is 田 itself (the same coincidence seen with 鿫/奥 in [[奥加素]]). Vietnamese uses *Tennessi*, the form attested in Vietnamese science press (consistent with this series' "-i" pattern: *Californi*, *Livermori*, *Meitneri*).
