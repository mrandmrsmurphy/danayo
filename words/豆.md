## Notes
- [豆 (char)](../characters/豆%20(char).md) "bean" — stand-in for [[豆 (char)|豆]], which cannot appear independently

豆 is 象形 of some kind of vessel or container — the character page's own Notes are explicit that this pictograph was purely borrowed phonetically for the unrelated plant name, displacing the genuine Old Chinese word for "bean," 菽 (shū), which survives today only in classical/literary registers. The character page also flags a real false-friend risk worth repeating here: 豆 is unrelated to the visually similar bottom portion of [[豊]], despite the temptation to see a shared component. The modern compound family stays close to the food sense (豆腐 "tofu," this vault's own paired word), while the character's phonetic descendants (頭 "head," 痘 "pox," 豎 "vertical line," 逗 "pause," 短 "short") show no trace of either the container or the bean sense — a purely phonetic family, not a semantic one.

Japanese keeps both on'yomi genuinely productive rather than one dominating: とう (TOU, used here — 豆腐 tōfu) and ず (ZU, equally alive in 大豆 daizu "soybean") — a real two-way split rather than a rare/common asymmetry, unlike most on'yomi pairs covered elsewhere in this sweep — alongside the native 訓読み まめ (mame), the ordinary everyday word for a bean. Vietnamese đậu is the standard, thoroughly modern reading (đậu phụ/đậu hũ "tofu," đậu nành "soybean," both directly paralleling this vault's own compounds) — picked over the character's other stored reading dấu, which is actually the unrelated Vietnamese word for "mark, sign," the same kind of homophone-field contamination already documented repeatedly in this sweep. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 돗 (dou, from Middle Chinese 定母 d + 侯韻 əu) diverges from Sino-Korean 두 in the coda, Dan'a'yo retaining a final consonant Korean lacks — the same divergence pattern already shared with this word's own homophones [[斗]] and [[投]].
