## Notes
- [妓](../characters/妓.md) "female entertainer, prostitute" + [院](../characters/院.md) "institution, courtyard, public building" — literally "prostitute-institution," a brothel.

This word resolves a genuine gap found while auditing [[Buildings]]: none of the four source languages' *everyday* modern words for "brothel" agree with one another (Mandarin colloquially prefers other terms, Japanese historically used 遊郭/yūkaku, Korean and Vietnamese lean on entirely different constructions) — so rather than force a choice among mutually exclusive modern colloquialisms, Dan'a'yo falls back on a classical, pan-Sinitic literary register term instead, following this vault's standing practice of using an older or more formal form to resolve a cross-linguistic disagreement rather than picking one daughter language's everyday word over the others.

Mandarin jìyuàn and Cantonese gei6 jyun2 are both standard, fully compositional, and genuinely in real use (jìyuàn remains a common written/formal Mandarin word for brothel, not purely archaic). Japanese ぎいん (giin) is a real but rare dictionary-attested reading, seldom used in everyday speech — Japanese more commonly reaches for 遊郭 (yūkaku, a historical licensed red-light district) or other native/period-specific terms. Korean 기원 and Vietnamese kỹ viện are the standard compositional readings of these same characters, matching 妓's and 院's own stored citations exactly, though — like the Japanese form — they are not the first word a modern speaker would reach for.

**A related classical term worth noting**: 青樓 ("blue/green tower," originally referring to a luxurious aristocratic residence before narrowing to "brothel" in Tang/Song usage) is, if anything, an even more widely and evenly attested classical pan-CJKV term for this concept — real in Japanese 青楼 (seirō), Korean 청루 (cheongnu), and Vietnamese thanh lâu, alongside Chinese. 妓院 was chosen here regardless, as a direct, transparent compound (prostitute + institution) rather than 青樓's more oblique, metaphor-based construction.

`kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 기원 coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 기원, since both 妓 and 院 independently carry matching readings on their own character pages. **Real homophone**: 注音/諺文/羅馬字 ㄍㄧ⼔ㄋ/기원/gi'wen is shared exactly with [[紀元]] ("era, A.D./C.E."), an unrelated coincidence.
