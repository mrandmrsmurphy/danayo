## Notes
- [鉄 (char)](../characters/鉄%20(char).md) "iron" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

鉄 (Fe, atomic number 26) is one of the oldest metals known to the CJKV sphere, foundational enough to give its name to the Iron Age across every one of these cultures. As a periodic-table entry it also serves as this vault's abbreviation prefix for iron compounds, e.g. 鉄〔養軽〕₃ = Fe(OH)₃ (iron hydroxide).

Mandarin tiě, Cantonese tit3, and Korean 철 (cheol) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese てつ (tetsu) is the Sino-Japanese on'yomi, productive in compounds (鉄道 tetsudō, "railway"; 鉄砲 teppō, "firearm"); the native kun'yomi くろがね (kurogane, literally "black metal") survives mainly in literary or archaic register, with てつ itself doing double duty as the everyday word. Vietnamese has two genuinely distinct attested readings: sắt is the everyday native/Nôm word for the metal itself, while thiết is the literary Sino-Vietnamese reading, productive in compounds like cương thiết (鋼鉄, "steel") — kept as a real two-item list rather than collapsed to one, since both are independently attested and neither displaces the other. **Real bug corrected 2026-09-21**: the constituent character page had also listed thiếc among 鉄's Vietnamese readings — but thiếc means *tin* (Sn, 錫), a wholly different element, not a reading of iron at all; removed there.

No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄊㄝㄊ unique among both words and characters).
