## Notes
- [丙 (char)](../characters/丙%20(char).md) "third class; tertiary"

丙 is the third of the Ten Heavenly Stems (甲, 乙, 丙...), an ancient Chinese calendrical/ordinal system predating and running parallel to the later sexagenary calendar; its original pictographic meaning is lost — proposed origins (a fish's tail, a person's shoulders, a small pedestal) have no settled consensus. From its position as "third" in the stem sequence, 丙 came to mean "third class, tertiary" generally, the way English grading uses A/B/C/D — paralleled directly by [[甲]]/[[乙]] for "first/second class."

Mandarin bǐng, Cantonese bing2, Japanese へい/ひょう (hei/hyō, on'yomi) alongside native ひのえ (hinoe, its name as a stem in the traditional Japanese calendar), and Korean 병 (byeong) all preserve the stem/grade sense. Vietnamese bính is the standard reading, most familiar today from the sexagenary-cycle year names of the Vietnamese calendar (e.g. Bính Dần, Bính Thân) — the same stem-and-branch calendrical role it plays across the whole sphere.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (byeng/병/ㄅ⼶ㄫ) with two other words still awaiting their own turn in this sweep, [[坪]] ("level ground; a unit of area") and [[柄]] ("handle; design") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed on each when it comes up.
