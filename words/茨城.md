## Notes
- [茨](../characters/茨.md) "thorn" + [城](../characters/城.md) "castle"

茨城 is Ibaraki, written "thorn castle", and is explained by an old story from the Hitachi fudoki in which a fort built of thorns (茨) was used against a local people, though this derivation is traditional and uncertain (from memory, not re-checked). The Japanese reading いばらき is native: 茨 (いばら, "thorn") plus き for 城, the old word for a fortification.

Ibaraki lies on the Pacific coast north-east of Tokyo, with its capital Mito, home of the Tokugawa Mito branch and of the garden Kairaku-en. It is known for natto, the lake Kasumigaura, and the science city of Tsukuba.

In Dan'a'yo 茨城 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 이바라키 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
