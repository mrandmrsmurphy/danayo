## Notes
- [風 (char)](../characters/風%20(char).md) "wind" + [狂](../characters/狂.md) "crazy, wild" — 風狂 is the `stand_in` that legitimizes 狂's own independent Dan'a'yo usage; 狂 does not stand alone and only otherwise appears bound inside compounds like 羊狂, 狂想曲, and 狂風.

風狂 (alongside its orthographic variants 瘋狂/疯狂) means "crazy, insane, to be a lunatic" — a plain stative descriptor of a deranged mental state. It is not a homophone of bare 狂 (注音 ㄍ⺢ㄫ) or of 狂風 (注音 ㄍ⺢ㄫㄈㄨㄫ, the same two characters reversed) — the resemblance to 狂風 is a false-friend trap for learners, not a real pronunciation collision: 狂風 "gale, violent wind" puts 風 as the head noun being modified by 狂, while 風狂 puts the mental-state character 狂 last as the actual predicate, with 風 doing something else entirely — not "wind" in the literal sense, but drawing on 風's older Traditional Chinese Medicine sense of "affliction, disorder" (as in 中風 "stroke," 羊癲風 "epilepsy"), where 風 names a category of disturbance of the body or mind rather than moving air. 瘋 was later coined by adding the 疒 "sickness" radical to disambiguate this specialized affliction sense from ordinary "wind," with 疯 as its PRC-simplified form — 風狂 is the older, radical-less spelling of the same idea.

In Japanese, 風狂 (ふうきょう, fūkyō) survives as a loanword, but with a far narrower and more literary sense than Dan'a'yo's everyday "crazy, insane": it names a specific aesthetic of artistic eccentricity and wandering unconventionality associated with Zen-influenced poets and hermits (Bashō, Ikkyū, and the broader tradition of 風狂の徒, "men of fūkyō") — closer to "divine madness" or bohemian nonconformity than to clinical insanity. Dan'a'yo does not follow this narrow literary sense, so the `japanese` field instead gives 狂う (くるう, kuruu), the ordinary native verb "to go mad, to go insane," as the closer functional match — the same way `korean: 미치다` (michida, "to go crazy") gives a native functional verb rather than a Sino-Korean reading of the compound itself.
