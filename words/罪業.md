## Notes
- [罪](../characters/罪%20(char).md) "sin" + [業](../characters/業%20(char).md) "deed" — "sin-deed," a sin as an act that carries weight. Their own `stand_in` values: 罪's is itself; 業's is itself; none points here, so 罪業 is an independent compound.

罪業 is a sin considered as an act: the wrong done and the weight it leaves. Mandarin zuìyè, Cantonese zeoi6 jip6, Japanese ざいごう, Korean 죄업 and Vietnamese tội nghiệp all use it. It is a Buddhist term for wrongdoing and its karmic load. Dan'a'yo takes the term over from its older religious home, on the vault's policy of adopting other religions' words when they carry the right feeling.

**Hebrew**: it best translates *ḥēṭ* (חֵטְא) and *ḥaṭṭāʾt* (חַטָּאת), "sin," the missing of the mark, the sinful act as a thing a person carries and must answer for ("the soul who sins shall die," Ezekiel 18:20). The Greek is *hamartia*. The bare [[罪]] is the general word, and the best fit for *ʿăvērāh* (עֲבֵירָה) "transgression," a crossing of the line, and for crime. Compare [[罪悪]] (*ʿāwōn*), [[業報]] (the result) and [[大罪]] (a grave sin).
