## Notes
- [左](../characters/左.md) "left" + [向 (char)](../characters/向%20(char).md) "toward; facing" + [鉤 (char)](../characters/鉤%20(char).md) "hook"

**左向鉤** is the *left-facing hook*, a hook ([[鉤]], the fourth of the eight principles of the 永字八法 on the [[Stroke]] page) whose tip turns to the left, as at the foot of the vertical stroke in 可. It is the Dan'a'yo rendering of the Chinese 左鉤 (*zuǒgōu*), spelled out with 向 "facing" so the first piece reads as a direction rather than the bound character 左 alone. Chinese instruction distinguishes the hooks of vertical strokes by direction, a left-facing and a right-facing form, besides the horizontal hook, the dagger-axe hook ([[戈鉤]]) and the lying hook ([[臥鉤]]).

On the [[Stroke]] page it is the first of the three varieties of hook, beside the [[戈鉤]] and the [[臥鉤]], where the hook "goes left" in contrast to the hooks that turn down or to the right.

The Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and North Korean forms are compositional, built from the constituent words and characters. No Vietnamese form is given.
