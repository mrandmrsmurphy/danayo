## Notes
- [文](../characters/文.md) "culture; pattern; writing" — a bound character; －文 is its fully productive suffix form.

－文 means "the patterned or written form of X". It is one idea that the Sinosphere spreads across several English words: 文 began as a *pattern* (the character is a person with markings on the chest, and the derived [[紋]] "mark, line" is the same word with 糸 added), widened to *ornament, refinement, and writing* (the civil half of 文武, [[文武]]), and from there to *text, composition, culture*. Dan'a'yo keeps the unified sense, so －文 attached to X yields whichever of these X calls for:

1. **The patterns of X** — [[天文]], "the patterns of the heavens", the old term for astronomy.
2. **Writing or culture in the language or tradition X** — 英文 (English writing), 日文 (Japanese writing), [[漢文]] (Chinese writing), [[中文]] (Chinese language, writing and culture), 国文 (national, i.e. Japanese, literature). Here －文 contrasts with －語: [[中国語]] is Chinese as a system of speech, 中文 is Chinese as a written and cultural whole.
3. **A sentence or text of kind X** — 疑問文 "a question sentence", 陳述文 "a statement". The sentence-type usage is standard in Japanese (疑問文 gimonbun) and Korean (의문문 uimunmun); Mandarin prefers 句 here (疑问句) and Vietnamese câu, so this sense rests on Dan'a'yo's extension of the suffix rather than on a majority of the source languages.

Senses 1 and 2 are the heart of the productivity claim. Mandarin wén, Cantonese man4, Japanese ぶん (BUN; the go-on reading MON survives in 呪文 jumon and 文字 moji), Korean 문 (mun) and Vietnamese văn (Anh văn "English", Hán văn "Chinese writing") all take it for "writing in a language", which is why －文 clears the simple-majority bar for full productivity — all five source languages in sense 2.

Already-enumerated derivatives stay valid and keep their own pages: [[天文]], [[漢文]], [[古文]] "Classical Chinese; ancient text", [[序文]] "preface", [[銘文]] "inscription", [[榜文]] "official proclamation", [[呪文]] "spell", [[諺文]] "Hangul", [[斯文]] "refined, genteel", [[中文]]. Words where 文 is the *head* rather than the suffix are separate compounds and are not －文 derivations: [[文化]] "culture" (the stand-in word that legitimizes the bare character), [[文学]], [[文字]], [[文法]], [[文章]], [[文脈]], [[文末]], [[文言]]. Because the sense is broad, a new －文 coinage should still earn its place on practical usefulness, not merely on being parseable.

Four words share this syllable in Dan'a'yo (mun, ㄇㄨㄋ): 文 itself and the words [[紋]], [[聞]], [[蚊]]; the homophone callout cross-references them, and the grammatical position of a bound suffix keeps it from being confused with any of the standalone words.

The page filename uses the fullwidth hyphen `－` (U+FF0D) to mark a word that cannot stand alone but is fully productive; the ASCII form `-文` is kept as an alias.
