## Notes
- [四 (char)](../characters/四%20(char).md) "four"

四 is the numeral "four." The character's own page traces an unusual history: originally a pictograph of exhaling breath (the original form of 呬), later borrowed purely for its sound to write the unrelated numeral "four" — the character's shape drifted over time from four simple lines to the modern 口+八 form. Before the Western Zhou it was written as four stacked strokes (亖), later standardized to 四 specifically to avoid confusion with 二/三 in vertical writing.

Mandarin 四 (sì). Cantonese 四 (sei3). Japanese shows a real, well-known avoidance pattern: the on'yomi し (shi) is a near-homophone of 死 (shi, "death"), so the native-origin よん (yon) is often preferred instead in contexts where the ambiguity would be unwelcome — hospital rooms, hotel floors, and gift quantities frequently skip 四 for exactly this reason, alongside the parallel avoidance of 九 (ku, homophonous with 苦 "suffering"). Korean's Sino-Korean reading is 사 (sa), which is identical to 死 (also sa), so the same death association exists there. Dan'a'yo does not avoid it: 四 and 死 are genuine homophones (both ㄙㄧㄜ), as the character page notes, which preserves the traditional East Asian taboo linking "four" and "death." (The Japanese and Korean avoidance customs are from memory, not re-checked.) Vietnamese tứ is the standard Sino-Vietnamese reading, used in compounds and formal counting (tứ phương, "the four directions"), with everyday counting instead using the native bốn.

Among words, 四 shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (ㄙㄧㄜ/siǝ/싀) with [[矢]] "arrow" and [[視]] "to look at, inspect" (no other word file has this 注音, checked 2026-10-13); among characters it also shares it with 死, 師, 氏, 獅, 私, 肆 and 諡.
