## Notes
- [山 (char)](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain" + [桃](../characters/桃.md) "peach" — "the mountain peach." No stand-in relationship applies (山's own stand-in is itself; 桃's is [[桃子]]).

山桃 is the Chinese wild peach, *Prunus davidiana* (David's peach): a small wild tree of the hills of northern China, a close relative of the cultivated peach [[桃子]] that blossoms early in spring and bears a small, hard, sour fruit (from memory, not re-checked). It is a peach, not a bayberry.

**Not the Japanese yamamomo.** Japanese writes 山桃 too, but reads it やまもも and means a different tree, the bayberry *Myrica rubra*, a red-fruited evergreen that Chinese calls 楊梅. Wiktionary records both senses separately: Mandarin shāntáo for *Prunus davidiana* and Japanese yamamomo for *Myrica rubra*. Dan'a'yo follows the Chinese sense for 山桃, and the bayberry is [[楊梅]], so the two plants never share a name.

Wiktionary gives only the Mandarin reading (shāntáo) and the Chinese definition. The other fields hold the compositional Sino-readings (Cantonese saan1 tou4, Japanese さんとう, Korean 산도, Vietnamese sơn đào), which were not checked against a dictionary. Everyday Korean uses a native word for the tree (from memory, 산복숭아).
