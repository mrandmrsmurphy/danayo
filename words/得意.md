>[!caution] The senses split — "proud, triumphant" against "skilled", "smug" and "cute"
>得意 means proud, triumphant success in Japanese (first sense), Korean and, often pejoratively, Mandarin ("smug"). Japanese also uses it for "skilled, one's strong suit", and Cantonese for "cute; interesting". The Dan'a'yo sense is the shared "proud, triumphant". Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md); for "skilled" prefer a 巧 or 上手 compound.

## Notes
- [得](../characters/得.md) "obtain" + [意](../characters/意.md) "intention; mood"

得意 is proud and elated: pleased with oneself at a success, in Dan'a'yo the triumphant pride after winning. Wiktionary glosses Mandarin déyì as "successful; smug; proud; self-satisfied" (often pejorative) and Japanese とくい first as "triumphant, proud", then "skilled; one's strong suit" and "regular customer". Korean 득의 is "satisfaction, pride", and Cantonese dak1 ji3 adds "cute, interesting". The shared core is proud, pleased success.

The word is listed on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md) as a Split. It is pride that has just won, lighter than [自負](自負.md), pride in oneself, and more elated than [満足](満足.md), satisfaction. Its vain, show-loving form is [虚栄](虚栄.md).
