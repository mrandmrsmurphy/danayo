## Notes
- [一 (char)](../characters/一%20(char).md) "one" + [打](../characters/打.md) "hit" — here 打 is not its usual sense at all, but a Mandarin/Cantonese *phonetic* loanword: 打 (dǎ/daa2) was adopted purely for its sound, echoing the first syllable of English "dozen," giving 一打 (yīdǎ/jat1 daa2) "a dozen," the same pattern already seen in loans like 苏打 (sūdá, "soda") and 西打 (xīdá, "cider"). Japanese and Korean instead borrow the English word directly as a phonetic loan in their own scripts — ダース (dāsu) and 다스 (daseu), the latter itself entering Korean via Japanese — rather than following the Chinese 打-based loan. No Sino-Vietnamese or loanword form for "dozen" as a fixed lexical unit is attested, so no Vietnamese reading is recorded; Vietnamese instead says *một tá*, using its own count word for the concept.
