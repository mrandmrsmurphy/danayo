## Notes
- [冒](../characters/冒.md) "take a risk" + [険 (char)](../characters/険%20(char).md) "precipitous" — stand-in for [[冒]], which cannot appear independently

**冒険** (*màoxiǎn*) is to venture into danger or the unknown, and by extension an adventure, a risky undertaking. The compound is transparent: 冒 is to brave or push through (as in 冒雨, "to go out braving the rain"), and 険 is a steep, dangerous place, so the word pictures going out into hazard on purpose. It differs from plain [[危険]] (danger), which names the hazard itself and carries no sense of choosing to face it.

In Japanese ぼうけん is the ordinary word for an adventure and has a strongly positive, romantic colour: 冒険家 (explorer, adventurer), 冒険小説 (adventure novel), 冒険心 (the spirit of adventure). In Chinese 冒險 is more often about the risk than the romance, as in 冒險投資 (a risky investment) or 不要冒險 ("don't take the risk"), and the classical phrase 冒險犯難 describes braving peril and hardship. Korean 모험 is used in both senses, with 모험심 for the spirit of adventure, and the Korean-language word has the same two faces of reckless risk and bold exploration.

In Dan'a'yo 冒険 covers both the adventure and the risk-taking, as the English gloss "adventure, risk" says, and keeps the shared CJKV sense.
