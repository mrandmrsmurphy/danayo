## Notes
- [叫 (char)](../characters/叫%20(char).md) "shout" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the character 叫 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

叫/규/kêu diverge along an interesting axis: Mandarin jiào and Cantonese giu3 both carry a second, extremely common everyday sense that has nothing to do with shouting — "to be named/called" (他叫王明, "his name is Wang Ming") and "to order/hire" (叫車, "to hail/order a car") — uses so frequent they arguably outweigh the literal "cry out" sense in daily speech. Korean 규 (from Sino root 叫) stays confined to the literal shouting/crying-out sense, mostly bound in compounds like 절규 (絶叫, a scream) and 규환 (叫喚, shouting) rather than standing alone. Vietnamese kêu keeps the "shout, cry out" core (kêu la, "to shout/scream"; kêu cứu, "to cry for help") and extends it to animal sounds (a rooster's crow, a dog's bark) much like Mandarin, but the more literary Sino-Vietnamese reading khiếu is reserved for classical/poetic register — kêu is the word used in ordinary speech.

English Wiktionary (character 叫, a 形声 of 口 "mouth" and phonetic 丩) lists many Chinese senses, in this order: "to be called" (你叫什麼名字？, "what is your name?"), "to call; to name", "to call (someone)" (叫警察, "to call the police"), "to yell; to shout" (狗叫, "a dog barks"), "to hail; to greet", the ditransitive "to ask; to order" (醫生叫他休息幾天, "the doctor told him to rest for a few days"), "to hire; to call (a taxi); to order (in a restaurant)", a dialectal "to cry", and a passive-sentence marker introducing the agent, "by". The Speech page uses the literal "shout" sense.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄍ⼘ (checked 2026-10-12); the character pages 叫 and 伽 share it.
