## Notes
- [一 (char)](../characters/一%20(char).md) "one" + [体](../characters/体.md) "body" — literally "one body," an organic whole in which every part functions as a unity rather than a mere assemblage.

一体 covers a spread of closely related senses radiating from this "one body" image: the concrete "an organic/unified whole" (a system whose parts work as one), the abstract "unity, oneness" (a state of being undivided), and, distinctively in Japanese, an emphatic interrogative intensifier — いったい何 (ittai nani, "what on earth," "what in the world") — where "one body['s worth of]" reinforces a question the way English piles on "on earth" or "the heck." This last sense has no real parallel in Mandarin/Cantonese, where 一体/一體 stays within the literal unity/wholeness range.

Japanese いったい shows the expected sokuon assimilation of いち (ICHI, 一's primary on'yomi) before た- to give いっ-, the same gemination pattern seen whenever 一 combines with a following unvoiced-obstruent-initial reading. Vietnamese nhất thể is a real attested Hán Việt compound (nhất thể hóa, "unification, integration," a common term in Vietnamese political/administrative writing) — directly compositional from 一's own stored nhất and 体's own stored thể, both already present on their respective character pages.
