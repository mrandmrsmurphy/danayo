## Notes
- [天](../characters/天%20(char).md) "heaven" + [罰](../characters/罰.md) "punish" — "heaven's punishment." Their own `stand_in` values: 天's is itself; 罰's is [[懲罰]]; none points here, so 天罰 is an independent compound.

天罰 is punishment that comes from heaven: the penalty God, and not a court, imposes. Mandarin tiānfá, Japanese てんばつ and Korean 천벌 all use it. Dan'a'yo takes the term over from its older religious home, on the vault's policy of adopting other religions' words when they carry the right feeling.

**Hebrew**: it best translates *nĕqāmāh* (נְקָמָה) "vengeance," as in "vengeance is mine" (Deuteronomy 32:35), and the "visiting" (*pāqad*, פָּקַד) of iniquity in Exodus 20:5. It is not "wrath": the anger is [[憤怒]], and 天罰 is what that anger does. Compare [[業報]] (the result a sin carries in itself), [[刑罰]] (a human penalty) and [[審判]] (judgment).
