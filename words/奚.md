## Notes
- [奚 (char)](../characters/奚%20(char).md) "how" — stand-in for [[奚 (char)|奚]], which cannot appear independently

奚 is a 象形 character with a striking origin: it depicts a person (大) with a rope (幺) around the neck held by a hand (爫) — a captured slave — and its oldest meaning is "slave, servant," surviving in compounds like 奚僮 "slave boy" and 小奚 "young servant" (Vietnamese tiểu hề). The interrogative use — "how, why, what, where" — is a phonetic loan that became so dominant in Classical Chinese that the original sense was all but forgotten: 子奚不為政? ("Why do you not take part in government?", Analects), 衛君待子而為政,子將奚先? ("If the Lord of Wei were to employ you in government, what would you do first?", Analects), 彼且奚適也? ("Where is he off to?", Zhuangzi).

As an interrogative it patterns with [[何]] (what) and [[如何]] (how), but skews adverbial — asking why, how, or whence rather than naming a thing — and it belongs to a distinctly classical register; no living CJKV language uses it in speech. Japanese reads it ケイ (kei) or natively なんぞ (nanzo, literary "why; what"), Korean 해 (hae) in hanmun recitation with native 어찌 ("how, why") as the everyday equivalent, and Vietnamese hề preserves an unusually rich fossil record in fixed classical compounds: hề vị (奚為) "why," hề cố (奚故) "for what reason," hề dĩ (奚以) "by what means," hề như (奚如) "in what state." `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 헤 (hei, with ㅔ) differs from Sino-Korean 해 (with ㅐ).
