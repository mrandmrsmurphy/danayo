## Notes
- [古](../characters/古.md) "ancient, old" + [菌](../characters/菌.md) "fungus, germ" (stand-in for [[細菌]], reused here) — literally "ancient germ," Archaea, the domain of single-celled prokaryotic microorganisms distinguished from Bacteria in 1990 (originally "archaebacteria," an older name reflected in the character choice here).

Mandarin gǔjūn and Vietnamese cổ khuẩn are both real, standard, fully compositional terms, matching 古's and 菌's own stored citations exactly — 古菌 has become the preferred modern Chinese term, displacing the older 古細菌. Cantonese gu2 kwan2 is likewise compositional, mirroring the written Mandarin term. Korean 고균 is also real and compositional, and has similarly overtaken the older 고세균 as the preferred modern term — a rare case in this domain where Dan'a'yo's own compositional coinage lines up with the *currently preferred* term in more than one source language, not just an older or coincidental one. Japanese is the outlier: 古菌 alone is not standard Japanese usage (it is described as primarily a Chinese-sphere term), so the real Japanese word stored here is 古細菌 (kosaikin, "ancient bacteria") — the standard academic term, still preferred over the modern loanword アーキア in most textbooks.

`kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo's own 고균 coincides exactly with the real, currently-preferred Sino-Korean term 고균, since both 古 and 菌 independently carry matching readings on their own character pages. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄛㄍ⼜ㄋ unique among both words and characters).
