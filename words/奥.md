## Notes
- [奥 (char)](../characters/奥%20(char).md) "deep" — stand-in for [[奥 (char)|奥]], which cannot appear independently

奥's oldest sense is concrete and architectural: the innermost, southwest corner of a house — the dim recess where offerings were placed (the character shows hands setting something down inside 宀 "roof"). From "the deepest corner of the room" it generalized to "deep, profound, abstruse," the sense Dan'a'yo carries: 奧秘 "profound mystery," 奧妙 "subtle and marvelous," 深奧 "deep and recondite."

Across the sphere the word stays recognizably the same morpheme but with different center of gravity. Japanese keeps the spatial sense vividly alive in its native reading おく — 奥 "the interior, depths," 奥さん "wife" (literally "the person of the interior"), 奥義 (おうぎ) "innermost secrets (of an art)." Korean 오 (奧) is largely confined to learned compounds like 오묘하다 (奧妙하다) "to be profound," and Vietnamese áo likewise appears chiefly in compounds such as áo diệu (奧妙) "marvelous." Mandarin additionally drafts the character into pure phonetic service in two high-profile loans: 奧林匹克 "Olympic" (whence 奧運 "the Olympics") and 奧地利 "Austria" (compare [[双鷹国]], this vault's symbolic name for that country). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 욱 ('ug, from MC ʔ + ɨuk, preserving the checked -k coda) differs from Sino-Korean 오, which lost the coda entirely.
