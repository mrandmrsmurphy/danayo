## Notes
- [弒](../characters/弒.md) "assassinate" + [君 (char)](../characters/君%20(char).md) "lord; ruler"
- This word is the viable form of [弒](../characters/弒.md): the character's meaning (to assassinate a superior) is identical, but 弒 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 弒君 is its `stand_in` compound.

弒 is not plain "kill" (殺) but the specific term for killing a person to whom one owes loyalty: a ruler or a parent. The distinction is a moral one, and the Confucian tradition of the *Chunqiu* (春秋) uses 弒 to record such a killing as a crime against the order of ruler and subject, even when the victim was a bad ruler. 君 "lord, ruler" names the victim, so 弒君 is "regicide," with the loyalty-violation already built into the verb.

The word is literary and historical, found in discussions of dynastic politics and in the old debate over whether killing a tyrant counts as 弒 at all (Mencius's reply was that it did not). The reading is Mandarin shìjūn, Cantonese si3 gwan1 and Korean 시군. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense "regicide, assassinate a ruler" with no divergence. It is placed under [Government](../lexipedia/Government.md).
