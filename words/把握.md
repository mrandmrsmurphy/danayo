## Notes
- [把](../characters/把.md) "grip, handle" + [握](../characters/握.md) "grasp, hold" — two near-synonymous grasping-verbs compounded together for emphasis; this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the bound character [握](../characters/握.md), which cannot appear independently (把's own stand-in is 把手).

把握 covers both the literal sense (to grasp something firmly) and, more commonly in modern usage, the figurative one: to grasp a concept, to have a firm handle on a situation, or — as a noun — the confidence that comes from that grip (有把握, "to be confident, to have it in hand"). This figurative extension is consistent across Chinese, Japanese (把握する, "to comprehend, get a grip on"), and Korean (파악하다, identical usage), all built on the same double-grasp logic.

Vietnamese breaks from the direct Sino-Vietnamese reading entirely. The character-by-character Hán-Việt would be something like *bả ác*, but that form is not actually used — everyday Vietnamese instead reaches for *nắm bắt*, a fully native compound (nắm "to grip," bắt "to catch") that independently recreates the same double-grasp logic as 把握, rather than borrowing the Chinese compound as a loanword the way Japanese and Korean did.

English Wiktionary gives the Chinese verb several senses: "to grasp; to control; to seize", "to have a firm hold on a situation; to be sure of something", "to grasp; to know well" (with 了解 as the synonym), and three archaic ones, "to grasp with one's hands", "to join hands" and "to shake hands". The noun is "assurance; certainty", and the derived terms are 有把握 and 無把握. Japanese 把握 (はあく) is a noun ("control"; "grasp, in the sense of understanding") and a suru-verb ("to have under control"; "to grasp, to understand"). The Efforts page lists it beside 掌握, which is a separate word in the vault.

Mandarin bǎwò and Cantonese baa2 ngaak1 are as at English Wiktionary. The Cantonese field holds the second of the two readings it lists.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄅㄚㄚㄎ (checked 2026-10-12).
