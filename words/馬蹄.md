## Notes
- [馬 (char)](../characters/馬%20(char).md) "horse" + [蹄 (char)](../characters/蹄%20(char).md) "hoof" — no `#cranberry`: both 馬's and 蹄's own `stand_in` point to themselves; neither points here.

Mandarin mǎtí, Cantonese maa5tai4, and Japanese ばてい (batei) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Korean uses the native compound 말굽 (malgup, 말 "horse" + 굽 "hoof") rather than the Sino-Korean reading 마제 — 말굽 is the real, everyday Korean word, with 마제 largely confined to technical/scientific compounds like 마제형 ("horseshoe-shaped"). Sino-Vietnamese mã đề has undergone a semantic narrowing: rather than "horse hoof" literally, it names *Plantago major* (plantain), an unrelated medicinal herb whose broad leaves were likened to a horse's hoofprint.
