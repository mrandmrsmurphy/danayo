## Notes
- [白 (char)](../characters/白%20(char).md) "white" + [領](../characters/領.md) "collar" — literally "white collar," referring to the crisp white dress shirts of office and professional workers, as opposed to [[藍領]] "blue-collar."

白領 is a 20th-century sociological loan-translation of the English "white-collar," describing office, managerial, and professional workers as distinct from manual laborers. Mandarin báilǐng and Cantonese baak6 leng5 render it as a native Sino compound, in step with how they normally absorb such neologisms. Japanese and Korean instead borrow the English term wholesale as ホワイトカラー (howaito karaa) and 화이트칼라 (hwaiteukalla) rather than calquing it — the more usual Sino-compound path, seen for example in Mandarin, was passed over here. Vietnamese cổ cồn trắng ("white collar-neck") is a direct calque, following the English original's literal imagery rather than compositional Sino-Vietnamese.
