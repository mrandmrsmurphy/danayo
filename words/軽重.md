## Notes
- [軽 (char)](../characters/軽%20(char).md) "light" + [重 (char)](../characters/重%20(char).md) "heavy" — literally "lightness and heaviness"; by extension, the relative importance of things, or the proper weighing of priorities against one another.

軽重 functions as a noun naming the relationship between things of differing significance: 軽重を知る (to know what matters more), 軽重をつける (to assign relative priority), 軽重を誤る (to misjudge the relative importance). The compound is embedded in classical Chinese economic thought: the *Guanzi* (管子), traditionally attributed to the statesman Guan Zhong, has a group of chapters titled 輕重 which treat the balancing of economic and political forces — supply and demand, fiscal policy, the weight of commodities against currency — as a science of statecraft. This classical resonance gives 軽重 a richer connotation than simple "importance"; it invokes the image of a scale, and the wisdom to read it correctly.

Korean 경중 is the standard equivalent and appears in the same contexts: 경중을 따지다 (to weigh relative importance), 경중에 따라 (according to relative gravity). Vietnamese *khinh trọng* follows the Sino-Vietnamese compound form, though everyday Vietnamese more often uses analytic constructions.

Neither character is legitimized here: both 軽's and 重's `stand_in` are themselves.  Mandarin qīngzhòng, Cantonese hing1 zung6, Japanese けいちょう, Korean 경중 and Vietnamese khinh trọng are the same compound, and the alias 輕重 is the traditional form.  The Cantonese field gives zung6, the reading used in this compound, while 重's character file lists the colloquial cung5 for "heavy".  `kwin` is false because the Dan'a'yo 킹총 differs from the Korean 경중.  The 注音 needs no syllable dot, and no other word shares it.
