## Notes
- [派](../characters/派.md) "branch; derive" + [生](../characters/生.md) "birth; produce" + [辞](../characters/辞.md) "word, expression" — "an element that produces words": the derivational affix. [派生](派生.md) "derive" is itself a word; 辞 is bound and appears only in compounds such as [[接辞]] "affix" and [[接尾辞]] "suffix".

派生辞 is the Dan'a'yo part of speech for a **bound element that derives a new lexical item**: －化, －者, －論, －文, －性. Each is a fully productive affix with its own page, named with the fullwidth hyphen `－`, and each adds a meaning of its own ("-ification", "one who", "the theory of", "the written form of", "the nature of") to a stem to make a word that did not exist before. That is what separates it from the other bound words in the grammar. [[関詞]] — case markers, the restrictive suffixes 只/且/就, the passive 被 — mark a *relation*: they take a phrase and fix its role in the sentence, and they create no new lexical item. A 派生辞 takes a *stem* and returns another stem.

The practical test is stacking. The output of a 派生辞 is an ordinary word and can be the input to the next: 進化 "evolve" → 進化論 "evolutionary theory" → 進化論者 "evolutionist"; 近代 → 近代化 → 近代化論. Each step has to make sense, since an affix chooses the kind of stem it attaches to and the kind of word it returns, but nothing in the system limits the depth. A case marker does not work this way: it closes off the noun phrase for its role, so it does not feed another round of derivation. A 派生辞 is also the only kind of bound element that can license new vocabulary: a transparent stem plus a 派生辞 is valid Dan'a'yo without its own page, and a derivative earns a page only when its meaning is lexicalized (文化, 化学).

Only fully productive affixes are 派生辞. A *semi-productive* affix (子, 語, 音, 学, 家, 的 and others) has no affix word at all, only a note on its character page, because new coinages with it are not licensed without authority review. A word that is *both* free and an affix, such as 物 and 事, which are also the nominalizers of the 名詞化 section, is an open case: its affix function may need an affix page of its own, as 化 and 者 now have.

派生辞 is a Dan'a'yo coinage, built on the model of 関詞 from the existing words 派生 and 辞, so its `mandarin`, `cantonese`, `japanese`, `korean` and `vietnamese` fields give the literal reading of its own characters rather than a pre-existing native term. Two cautions follow. 辞 rather than 詞 is deliberate: an affix is not a free word (詞), and 辞 is already the second element of 接辞 and 接尾辞. And in Mandarin, 派生词 (pàishēngcí, with 词 for "word") names the *derived word* — the product — where 派生辞 here is the affix that does the deriving, so the traditional-character spelling is what keeps them apart. 接辞 remains the umbrella term for every affix, including the grammatical ones in [[文法 - 05形態]]; 派生辞 is the narrower subclass, and position is a separate matter, so a 派生辞 may be a 接尾辞 or, later, a 接頭辞.
