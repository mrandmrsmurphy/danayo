## Notes
- [有 (char)](../characters/有%20(char).md) "to have" + [趣](../characters/趣.md) "interest" — literally "to possess interest," i.e. interesting.

有趣 is real, standard, high-frequency Mandarin vocabulary (yǒuqù, HSK 4) and is likewise attested in Cantonese (jau5 ceoi3). It is deliberately included despite not being a pan-CJKV word: Japanese and Korean instead reach for native or different Sino-Xenic vocabulary for "interesting" (面白い omoshiroi in Japanese; 재미있다 jaemiitda in Korean, itself a native-plus-Sino-Korean hybrid). The compositional transparency of 有("have") + 趣("interest") was judged worth the trade-off — chosen over the mismatched [[関心]] ("to be concerned about, to have interest in," the experiencer noun/state, not the stimulus adjective "interesting").

`japanese` ゆうしゅ and `korean` 유취 are compositional Sino-Xenic readings only, not independently attested as live words for "interesting" in either language. Note a real Korean homophone collision: 유취 already exists as 乳臭 ("milk-smell"; used in the idiom 口尚乳臭, "the smell of milk still on one's breath," meaning "immature, childish") — an unrelated word sharing the same reading. `vietnamese` hữu thú is compositional (有's own hữu + 趣's own thú), not independently confirmed as an attested Sino-Vietnamese compound. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 유추 diverges from Sino-Korean 유취 in the second syllable. No homophones within Dan'a'yo itself (注音 ⼜ㄑㄨ unique among both words and characters).
