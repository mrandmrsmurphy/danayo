## Notes
- [岸 (char)](../characters/岸%20(char).md) "bank; shore" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; standalone form, 岸 is sufficiently free in Classical usage to serve as a Dan'a'yo word without a compound.

岸 names a riverbank or shore. It shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (an/안/ㄚㄋ) with two other words, [[贋]] ("counterfeit, fake") and [[雁]] ("wild goose") — a genuine three-way homophone group in Dan'a'yo, cross-referenced on all three pages, though the three are unrelated both semantically and in their real-world Mandarin/Cantonese pronunciations (àn vs. yàn vs. yàn — 岸 diverges from the other two even in the source languages).

Mandarin àn, Cantonese ngon6, and Korean 안 (an) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese きし (kishi, on'yomi GAN reserved for compounds, already 岸's own stored `japanese_native` value) is the everyday standalone word. Vietnamese ngạn is the standard Hán Việt reading, one of three attested candidates on 岸's own page (alongside ngàn and ngan), well attested in compounds like hà ngạn ("riverbank"). `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 안 matches Sino-Korean 안 exactly.
