## Notes
- [尺 (char)](../characters/尺%20(char).md) "foot (distance)" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

尺 depicts a person's leg marked at a point ten 寸 ("cun," a smaller unit) above the foot — the traditional East Asian unit of length, historically around 30-33cm (varying by era and country), analogous in role (though not exact size) to the Western foot. It remains productive in the modern metric-derived compound [公尺](公尺.md) ("meter," literally "public/official chi") and in [尺蠖](尺蠖.md) ("inchworm," a caterpillar that moves by arching its body the length of a 尺 at a time).

Mandarin chǐ, Cantonese cek3, and Korean 척 (cheok) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese has two attested on'yomi, しゃく (shaku, the everyday reading, used as the actual unit name in traditional Japanese measurement) and せき (seki, rarer); しゃく is correctly the one stored here. The native kun'yomi さし (sashi, 尺's own stored `japanese_native` value) names the measuring tool, "ruler," rather than the unit itself. Vietnamese xích is the standard Hán Việt reading, still used as the name of the traditional Vietnamese unit of length.
