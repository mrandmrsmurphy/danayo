## Notes
- [尺 (char)](../characters/尺%20(char).md) "foot (distance)" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

尺 depicts a person's leg marked at a point ten 寸 ("cun," a smaller unit) above the foot — the traditional East Asian unit of length, historically around 30-33cm (varying by era and country), analogous in role (though not exact size) to the Western foot. It remains productive in the modern metric-derived compound [[公尺]] ("meter," literally "public/official chi") and in [[尺蠖]] ("inchworm," a caterpillar that moves by arching its body the length of a 尺 at a time).

Mandarin chǐ, Cantonese cek3, and Korean 척 (cheok) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese has two attested on'yomi, しゃく (shaku, the everyday reading, used as the actual unit name in traditional Japanese measurement) and せき (seki, rarer); しゃく is correctly the one stored here. The native kun'yomi さし/ものさし (sashi/monosashi, "ruler, measuring stick," already 尺's own stored `japanese_native` values) name the measuring tool rather than the unit itself. Vietnamese xích is the standard Hán Việt reading, still used as the name of the traditional Vietnamese unit of length. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 척 matches Sino-Korean 척 exactly. **Real gap found and fixed**: this word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (ceg/척/ㄑㄝㄎ) with [[赤]] ("red") — a genuine word-level homophone that had no Homophones callout on either page; added reciprocally to both.
