## Notes
- [加](../characters/加.md) + [州 (char)](../characters/州%20(char).md) "state" + [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "metal" — *Californium* is named directly after the state of California (and the University of California, Berkeley), where the element was discovered. 加州 is the common Mandarin abbreviation of 加利福尼亞州 "California," used as a calque rather than a phonetic transliteration, in the same spirit as 美洲金 (Americium). Japanese and Korean instead borrow the IUPAC name as a loanword (カリホルニウム, 칼리포르늄).

**Content added**: `mandarin`/`cantonese` were both blank; filled with `kāi`/`hoi1`, the readings of the real standard mainland Chinese element character 锎/鐦 — following this series' established convention (per [[丹金]], another toponymic/semantic calque) of using the avoided single-character element name's reading even when the coinage itself is a calque rather than a phonetic transliteration. A separate character, 鉲 (kǎ), is a Taiwan-specific alternate name for the same element, not used here. `vietnamese` was likewise blank; filled with `Californi`, the attested term from Vietnamese Wikipedia.
