## Notes
- [改 (char)](../characters/改%20(char).md) "change, reform" + [元](../characters/元.md) "origin, era"

改元 names the act of changing an era name (年号/年號) mid-reign or at the start of a new one — distinct from 建元 ("to establish an era name" for the first time) and related to [[元年]] ("the first year" of whichever era is currently in force). Japan is the only country that still officially practices this era-name system for civil dating today (most recently 平成→令和, Heisei to Reiwa, in 2019), making 改元 a live, actively-used term in Japanese rather than a purely historical one; in China, Korea, and Vietnam it now describes only a historical practice, since all three abandoned era-name dating in favor of the Gregorian calendar (and, for China, also the continuous PRC-founding-based dating) in the 20th century.

Japanese かいげん and Korean 개원 are both standard compositional Sino-readings, and `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 개원 matches Sino-Korean 개원 exactly. Vietnamese cải nguyên is likewise standard, contrasting with kiến nguyên ("to establish an era name" for the first time). No specific HSK level applies to a term this historically specialized. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄚㄧ⼔ㄋ unique across both words and characters).
