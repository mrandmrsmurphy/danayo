## Notes
- [改 (char)](../characters/改%20(char).md) "change, reform" + [元](../characters/元.md) "origin, era"

改元 is the change of an era name: starting a new era name, with its first year, in place of the one in force. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 改元 (Mandarin gǎiyuán, Cantonese goi2 jyun4), labelled historical, as "to start over with the first year of a new Chinese era name; to change the title of an imperial reign", and Japanese かいげん as "change in the Imperial era name". It has no Korean or Vietnamese entry, so Korean 개원 and Vietnamese cải nguyên here are built from the characters.

English Wikipedia says era names began in 140 BCE under the Emperor Wu of Han, that the Chinese system was the official way of numbering years until the Republic of China in 1912, and that Korea, Vietnam and Japan adopted the concept from China, the Japanese system being independent of the others. The latest Japanese change, to Reiwa, took effect on 1 May 2019, the day Naruhito was enthroned, ending the Heisei era. The era names themselves are the [[元号]], and the first year of an era is the [[元年]].
