## Notes
- [聴 (char)](../characters/聴%20(char).md) "listen" + [衆](../characters/衆.md) "crowd" — literally "listening crowd."

聴衆 means an audience in the ordinary modern sense (Mandarin tīngzhòng, Cantonese ting1 zung3, Japanese ちょうしゅう, Korean 청중 — all standard, fully compositional readings), but it carries a specifically Buddhist origin worth noting. In Buddhist sutra literature, 聴衆 denotes the assembly gathered to hear a dharma teaching — not a passive crowd but active receivers of the Teaching. The act of listening (聴) is itself the first of the Three Wisdoms of Buddhist practice: 聞慧 (wisdom through hearing), 思慧 (wisdom through reflection), 修慧 (wisdom through meditation) — so 聴衆 in a sutra context implies the listeners are engaged in a spiritual act, not merely attending an event. The word appears constantly in sutra preambles, naming the assembly present when the Buddha speaks — the crowd at Vulture Peak, so to speak.
