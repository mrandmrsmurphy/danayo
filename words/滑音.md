## Notes
- [滑 (char)](../characters/滑%20(char).md) "slippery, to slide" + [音](../characters/音.md) "sound" — no stand-in relationship applies (滑 is its own stand-in; 音's is [[音楽]]).

滑音 is the "gliding sound": the glide, a sound that slides into or out of a vowel instead of holding steady, in Dan'a'yo the two glides y and w (ㄧ /j/, ㄨ /w/). It is the general term, and it covers both positions the syllable template allows: the **on-glide** between the onset and the vowel, which the grammar calls the [[介音]] ("medial") and writes G in (C)(G)V(C), and the **off-glide** after the vowel, in the coda, where the page on phonology speaks of the 後続滑音, the following glide, and where 高母音後続 names the same thing from the other side. The alternative term the page uses for a glide on its own, 半母音 (semivowel, 半 + [[母音]]), describes the same class by its resemblance to a vowel.

The term is attested for glides in more than one source language, though not in all of them. Chinese has a linguistic 滑音 separate from the musical one (Mandarin huáyīn, Cantonese waat6 jam1, where the same word is the musical glissando), and Korean 활음, written with the hanja 滑音, is the term for a glide beside 반모음 "semivowel". Japanese phonetics calls the glide わたり音 (渡り音) rather than 滑音, so the `japanese` field holds the compositional on'yomi reading かつおん and not the native term, and no Vietnamese entry was found, so that field is omitted. The musical sense in Chinese does not carry over: in Dan'a'yo 滑音 is the phonetic term.

No homophones (注音 ㄏ⺢ㄊ·ㄨㄇ unique across both words and characters). The dot marks the genuine fork at ㄊ|ㄨㄇ: the string splits legally either as ㄏ⺢ㄊ + ㄨㄇ or as ㄏ⺢ + ㄊㄨㄇ.
