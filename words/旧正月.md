## Notes
- [旧 (char)](../characters/旧%20(char).md) "old, former" + [正 (char)](../characters/正%20(char).md) "principal" + [月 (char)](../characters/月%20(char).md) "month" — literally "the old principal month," i.e. the lunar new year under the calendar Japan no longer officially uses.

旧正月 is specifically a Japanese framing: after Japan adopted the Gregorian calendar in 1873 (see [[太陽暦]]), 正月 ("New Year") came to mean January 1st by default, leaving 旧正月 ("old New Year") as the term for the lunar new year still observed regionally and traditionally, functionally equivalent to [[春節]] and synonymous with [[陰暦年]]. China, Korea, and Vietnam never made this same split — they either kept the lunar new year as their primary new year celebration (as Vietnam's Tết Nguyên Đán still is) or use 春節 without needing an "old" qualifier, since no competing Gregorian-based "new" new year ever displaced it as the culturally primary one.

Japanese きゅうしょうがつ (kyūshōgatsu), where 正月 fuses as しょうがつ (shōgatsu), and Korean 구정월 are both standard compositional readings. **`vietnamese` intentionally left blank**: the "old vs. new calendar" framing this word encodes doesn't map cleanly onto Vietnamese, which simply calls the holiday Tết Nguyên Đán or Tết Âm lịch without a displaced "new" new year to contrast against; a literal compositional "cựu chính nguyệt" risks being misread as "the previous lunar year's first month" rather than this word's intended sense. No specific HSK level applies to a term this culturally specific. No homophones (注音 ㄍ⼜ㄛㄐㄧㄫ⼔ㄊ unique across both words and characters).
