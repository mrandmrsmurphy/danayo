## Notes
- [蹴](characters/蹴%20(char).md) "kick" + [球](characters/球%20(char).md) "ball"

There is a similar division to Asia as exists in the West, between terms like 'soccer' and 'football'. J/K use 蹴球 (lt. kick ball) while C uses 'foot ball'/足球. All these terms should be considered as ambiguous as English "football" in an international context, and should probably be followed by ㄙㄚㄎㄎㄚ_sakka_ or ㄆㄨㄊㄅㄛㄌㄜ_putbolü_.

Japanese しゅうきゅう (shūkyū) and Korean 축구 (chukgu) are both real, everyday words for the sport in their respective languages. Mandarin cùqiú and Cantonese cuk1 kau4 are direct compositional readings, but the term Chinese speakers actually use is the parallel calque 足球 ("foot-ball," zúqiú), not 蹴球.

No `vietnamese` field is given: Vietnamese does not have an attested 蹴球-shaped term at all. It does, however, have an exact structural parallel to the *Chinese* calque — túc cầu (足球, "foot-ball"), a real, dictionary-attested literary/formal synonym for the sport (Vietnamese Wikipedia's own article opens "Bóng đá hay túc cầu là một môn thể thao...") — alongside the everyday native compound bóng đá ("kick-ball," bóng "ball" + đá "to kick"). A separate term, xúc cúc (蹴鞠), exists only for the ancient historical Chinese kick-ball game (cuju), not the modern sport. A hypothetical "xúc cầu" combining 蹴's own Hán Việt reading xúc with cầu is phonologically plausible but simply was never lexicalized.
