## Notes
- [安](../characters/安.md) "peaceful" + [心 (char)](../characters/心%20(char).md) "heart" — "a peaceful heart".

安心 is "peace of mind; to be at ease; to be relieved": the settled state of a heart that has nothing to fear. English Wiktionary lists it as a Chinese verb ("to be relieved; to feel at ease"; also, rarely, "to keep one's mind on something", and, in older texts such as *Xiyouji*, "to harbour malicious intentions"), and as an adjective ("relieved; at ease"), with 放心 as the synonym for the first sense (not a word in the vault) and 安心立命 as a derived term. Japanese あんしん (anshin) is a na-adjective ("safe; unworrying"), a noun ("relief; peace of mind") and a suru-verb ("to feel easy"), with derived terms such as 安心感 ("sense of security") and 不安心 ("uneasiness"). Japanese also has the Buddhist あんじん (anjin), "equanimity". Its antonym in the vault is [[不安]] (unease); the neighbouring words [[安全]] (safe) and [[安定]] (stable) describe a situation, while 安心 describes the feeling it produces.

Mandarin ānxīn and Cantonese on1 sam1 are as at English Wiktionary. Korean 안심 and Vietnamese an tâm are the Sino-Korean and Sino-Vietnamese readings of the same two characters, and English Wiktionary lists both as descendants of the Chinese word.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄚㄋㄙㄧㄇ (checked 2026-10-12).
