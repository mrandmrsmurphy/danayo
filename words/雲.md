## Notes
- [雲 (char)](../characters/雲%20(char).md) "cloud" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 雲 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

雲 means cloud. Mandarin yún, Cantonese wan4, Korean 운, and Vietnamese vân are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese うん (UN) is the on'yomi used in compounds like 雲海 (unkai, "sea of clouds"), while the native 訓読み くも (kumo) remains the everyday word for "cloud" itself — the same Sino-Japanese/native split documented throughout this sweep.

The character's own Notes trace 雲 back to the simpler original form 云, with the weather radical 雨 added once 云 alone came to be associated with the unrelated sense "speak" — a disambiguating semantic component added to resolve the collision, rather than a purely phonetic derivation. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 운 (from Middle Chinese 云母 ø + 文韻 ɨun) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 운. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄨㄋ unique across both words and characters).
