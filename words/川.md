## Notes
- [川 (char)](../characters/川%20(char).md) "river" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 川 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

川 means river, and was picked as the Dan'a'yo word for "river" specifically because the choice among the sphere's rival candidates was otherwise a deadlock: Korean defaults to [江](../characters/江.md) even though many Korean place names contain 川; in Japan nearly every river is simply called 川; Mandarin instead favors [河](../characters/河.md) as the everyday word; and Vietnamese has its own native word for "river," with 江 only preserved as an old, learned spelling. 川 broke the tie on practical grounds — it is graphically simple (three strokes, among the vault's `#hapax` simplest characters) — while 江 was set aside because in Japanese it has drifted to mean "cove, inlet" rather than "river."

Mandarin chuān, Cantonese cyun1, Korean 천, Japanese せん, and Vietnamese xuyên are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 川's own stored citations exactly. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 촨 (cwan) and Sino-Korean 천 are not the same string — 촨 preserves the Middle Chinese medial glide (final *iuᴇn) that Sino-Korean's own regular sound-change process dropped.
