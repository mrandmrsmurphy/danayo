## Notes
- [禿](../characters/禿%20(char).md) "bald" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 禿 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

**Completed the reciprocal homophone callout** already anticipated on [[啄]]'s own page: both share the reading tog/톡/ㄊㄛㄎ.

禿 means bald or bare, describing hair loss but also extending to bare mountains, trees stripped of leaves, or a pen worn down to a nub (禿筆). Its 会意 etymology (毛 "hair," later corrupted into 禾, + 儿 "person") pictures a person with no hair.

Mandarin tū, Cantonese tuk1, and Korean 독 (dok) are standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese とく (toku, on'yomi) is comparatively rare in isolation — the character's own native reading かむろ (kamuro) specifically denotes a bowl-cut child's or attendant's hairstyle rather than baldness generally, so it doesn't directly correspond to this sense; the everyday Japanese word for "bald" is instead the native はげ (hage), not derived from this character's own stored readings. Vietnamese offers two candidates: thốc and ngốc, both stored on the character page.
