## Notes
- [繃](../characters/繃.md) "bandage" + [帯 (char)](../characters/帯%20(char).md) "sash; belt" — this word is 繃's own `stand_in`: the character's meaning (bandage) is identical, but 繃 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word, so the compound pairs it with 帯 (the thing a bandage physically is) to make it usable.

Mandarin bēngdài, Cantonese bang1 daai3, Japanese ほうたい, and Korean 붕대 are all standard, fully compositional readings across the CJKV sphere.

Wiktionary glosses Mandarin bēngdài (绷带, 繃帶) as "bandage", a semantic-phonetic loan, and notes an obsolete literary sense of swaddling clothes for a baby. Cantonese bang1 daai3 matches. The Japanese word is usually written 包帯 (ほうたい), with 包 "wrap" in place of 繃, and Korean 붕대 keeps the 繃 form; the reading ほうたい is the same either way, since ほう is also the on'yomi of 繃.

In Dan'a'yo the word is the general term for a bandage, as placed on the [Body](../lexipedia/Body.md) page.
