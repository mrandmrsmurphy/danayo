## Notes
- [臨](../characters/臨.md) "draw near; approach" + [時 (char)](../characters/時%20(char).md) "time; when"

**臨時** ("facing the time") describes something that exists or is done for the immediate occasion only — not permanent, not planned in advance. The literal sense of the compound is apt: 臨 "to face; to be present at" + 時 "the time" — something arranged for right now, to meet this moment. It applies to provisional governments, temporary staff, emergency measures, and stopgap arrangements of all kinds.

The word is widely used across the CJKV sphere with consistent meaning: Mandarin 臨時 (*línshí*), Japanese 臨時 (りんじ), Korean 임시 (the form with the 두음법칙 shift; the vault's `korean` field keeps the North Korean 림시), and Vietnamese lâm thời all carry the same sense of the provisional and the ad hoc.

The word is the standard prefix-like modifier for the provisional: 臨時政府 (provisional government), 臨時総統 / 臨時大總統 (provisional president — the title Sun Yat-sen held in 1912, before the post became 總統, see [[総統]]), 臨時会議 (extraordinary session), 臨時雇い (casual hire). The best-known Korean instance is the 大韓民国臨時政府 (Provisional Government of the Republic of Korea, 1919–1948; for its head see [[国務領]]), which kept the word 臨時 in its name through decades of exile.

臨時 differs from [[暫時]] (for the time being, a short duration) in what it marks: 暫時 stresses that the time is short, while 臨時 stresses that the arrangement is made for the occasion and not planned in advance. Both are opposed to the permanent, as in [[永久]]. In Dan'a'yo 臨時 keeps the shared CJKV sense.
