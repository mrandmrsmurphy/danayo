## Notes
- [四](../characters/四%20(char).md) "four" + [水](../characters/水%20(char).md) "water" — "four waters"; no stand-in relationship applies (四's own stand-in is itself; 水's is the single-character word [[水]]). The alias 泗水 is the standard Chinese spelling: the character 泗 is itself an alias of 四, so Dan'a'yo writes the name with 四.

四水 is a proper noun with three referents, all written 泗水 in Chinese. English Wiktionary lists them as:

1. The Si River, a river in Shandong, China. It is the river paired with the Zhu River (洙水) in the Confucian phrase 洙泗, since Confucius taught near their confluence (see the character [洙](../characters/洙.md)).
2. Sishui, a county of Jining in Shandong, named for the river.
3. Surabaya, the provincial capital of East Java in Indonesia. English Wiktionary marks the etymology of this sense as unknown, with a query whether it comes from 泗里末 (a phonetic spelling of the Malay name), and gives 四里貓 and 泗里末 as synonyms. It is therefore a Chinese name for the Indonesian city that has the same spelling as the Shandong river, not a translation of it.

Mandarin Sìshuǐ and Cantonese si3 seoi2 are as at English Wiktionary. Japanese スラバヤ and Korean 수라바야 are the ordinary transliterations of the Indonesian name, as with [[旧金山]], so `kwin` is false. The `vietnamese` field holds the compositional Hán-Việt reading tứ thủy (each character as its page gives it; not attested for this name).

Dan'a'yo keeps the shared CJKV sense: a place name, listed on Geography for Surabaya and on China for the river and county. One word homophone: [[私讐]] "personal grudge" (same 注音 ㄙㄧㄜㄙㄨ, checked 2026-10-13). The 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot.
