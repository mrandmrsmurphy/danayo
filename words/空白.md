## Notes
- [空 (char)](../characters/空%20(char).md) "empty" + [白 (char)](../characters/白%20(char).md) "white" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (both 空's and 白's own are themselves), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`.

**Fixed a real bug**: `aliases` had wrongly listed 余白/餘白 as orthographic variants — these are actually a different, distinct compound (余 "surplus" + 白, "blank space, margin," Japanese yohaku) that happens to share a near-identical meaning, not a spelling variant of 空白. Removed them; the same contamination had leaked into `korean`, which held a comma-joined "공백, 여백, 빈" mixing this word's own reading with 余白's own Korean reading (여백) and an unrelated native adjective (빈, "empty") — trimmed to just 공백.

空白 means blank or empty — an unfilled space, whether literal (a blank page, 空白ページ) or figurative (a gap in memory or knowledge, 記憶の空白). It overlaps in meaning with 余白 ("margin, blank space" specifically, i.e. unused space left over around content) without being the same word.

Mandarin kòngbái, Cantonese hung1 baak6, Japanese くうはく (kūhaku), and Korean 공백 (gongbaek) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese không bạch is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form; everyday Vietnamese more often says trống ("empty, blank") natively.
