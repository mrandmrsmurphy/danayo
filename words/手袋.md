>[!caution] The senses split — Japanese "glove", Chinese "handbag"
>手袋 is a glove or mitten in Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. Chinese 手袋 is a handbag, so a Chinese reader may hear a bag. Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).


## Notes
- [手 (char)](../characters/手%20(char).md) "hand" + [袋 (char)](../characters/袋%20(char).md) "sack"

手袋 is a glove, a mitten. English Wiktionary glosses Japanese 手袋 (read てぶくろ, a native reading) as "glove; mitten", but Chinese 手袋 (Mandarin shǒudài, Cantonese sau2 doi6) as "handbag". The Japanese field here gives the Sino-Japanese しゅたい. The Dan'a'yo sense is the Japanese one, and the word is listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md). The Korean 수대 and Vietnamese thủ đại fields are built from the readings of the two characters. A bag in general is the [袋](袋.md).
