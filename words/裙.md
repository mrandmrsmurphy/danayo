>[!caution] The senses split — Chinese and Japanese "skirt", Vietnamese "pants"
>裙 is a skirt in Chinese and Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. Vietnamese quần is glossed "pants", so a Vietnamese reader may hear trousers. Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md); for trousers use [袴](袴.md).


## Notes
- [裙 (char)](../characters/裙%20(char).md) "skirt"

裙 is a skirt. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 裙 (Mandarin qún, Cantonese kwan4) as "skirt; apron; dress; petticoat", and Japanese くん as "skirt hem" and a hip-wrapped pleated under-skirt worn as part of clerical attire. Vietnamese quần, the same character, is glossed "pants", so a Vietnamese reader may hear trousers. The Dan'a'yo sense is the skirt of Chinese and Japanese, and the word is listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md); for trousers use [袴](袴.md).

This word is the stand-in that legitimizes the character [裙](../characters/裙%20(char).md) as a free word. Its sound ㄍ⼜ㄫ is a new syllable, because the regular ㄍㄨㄋ already holds 君, 軍, 群, 郡 and 饉. The robe is the [袍](袍.md), and clothes in general are [衣服](衣服.md).
