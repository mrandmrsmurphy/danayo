## Notes
- [洞](../characters/洞.md) "cave" + [窟 (char)](../characters/窟%20(char).md) "cavern" — this word is the viable form of [洞](../characters/洞.md): the character's meaning (cave) is identical, but 洞 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. The compound pairs two near-synonyms for cave to make it usable.

Mandarin dòngkū, Cantonese dung6 fat1, Korean 동굴 (donggul), and Japanese どうくつ (dōkutsu) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday word for "cave" in its language. Vietnamese động quật is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 洞's động — a real reading previously entirely missing from 洞's own character page, replaced there by dọng/dộng, minor tonal variants rather than the primary form — and 窟's own quật, directly attested as a real term for "cave, cavern." `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 동콛 diverges from Sino-Korean 동굴 in the second syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄉㄛㄫㄎㄛㄊ unique among both words and characters).
