## Notes
- [情](../characters/情.md) "feelings, emotion" + [欲](../characters/欲.md) "desire, want" — desire that arises from feeling; sexual desire

情欲 is lust: sexual desire, the bodily wanting that stirs from feeling.  情 supplies the sense of passion or emotion and 欲 the wanting, so the compound is desire of the heart and flesh together, narrower than the general [[欲望]] ("desire, craving") and different in kind from the grasping greed of [[強欲]].  In the Buddhist and Confucian traditions it is one of the passions to be restrained, which is why it appears in the vault's Sin domain as well as under Emotions.

The traditional spelling 情慾, with 慾 for 欲, is listed as an alias.  Mandarin qíngyù, Cantonese cing4 juk6, Japanese じょうよく, Korean 정욕 and Vietnamese tình dục are the same compound.  Vietnamese tình dục usually means "sexuality" or "sex" in everyday use, a plainer and more clinical word than the moral lust of the Sinitic form.  `kwin` is false because the Dan'a'yo 칭욕 differs from the Korean 정욕.
