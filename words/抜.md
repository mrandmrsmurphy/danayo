## Notes
- [抜 (char)](../characters/抜%20(char).md) "to pull out; to extract" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character [抜 (char)](../characters/抜%20(char).md) as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

抜 is the basic verb "to pull out": to draw something up or out of where it is fixed, as in pulling a weed from the ground or a tooth from the jaw. In the classical language the same character carries two extended senses, both built on the image of drawing one thing out of a mass: "to select, to pick out" (the sense that survives in [選抜](選抜.md) "to select as the best") and "to surpass, to stand out among others," the person pulled up above the rest, as in [抜擢](抜擢.md) "to select for promotion." Chinese also uses it for "to capture, to seize" (taking a city or a position), the image of pulling it out of enemy hands.

The character is a 形声 compound with the hand radical 手 as its semantic component and the phonetic 犮, as the 抜 character page records. The form written here, 抜, is the Japanese shinjitai in which the right-hand component 犮 is replaced by 友 (the traditional form is 拔, kept above as an alias); Wiktionary treats 抜 as the shinjitai of 拔.

The readings are Mandarin bá, Cantonese bat6, Sino-Korean 발 (eumhun 뽑을 발, "to pluck, to pick out") and Sino-Vietnamese bạt. In Japanese the character is also the everyday native verb 抜く (nuku, "to pull out"; 抜ける nukeru, "to come out, to slip out"), and the `japanese` field records the Sino-Japanese reading ばつ (as in 選抜 senbatsu), following the vault's convention for these fields.

The word has the syllable ㄆㄚㄊ to itself, with no homophones, so it needs no compound to disambiguate it and can stand alone; this is why the character's own stand-in is the character itself and not 選抜. Dan'a'yo derives ㄆ here as the aspirated minority outcome for the voiced MC initial 並, a choice made so that the free verb "to pull out" holds a syllable of its own while 捌, the financial form of 八, shares 八's ㄅㄚㄊ. The Physical page lists the word among the basic manual actions performed on objects, and Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "to pull out, to extract," with "to select" carried by the compounds above.
