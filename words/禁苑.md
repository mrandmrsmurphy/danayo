## Notes
- [禁](../characters/禁.md) "prohibit, forbid" + [苑](../characters/苑.md) "garden, pasture" — 苑's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 禁's own is [[禁止]]; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 苑 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`.

禁苑 names an imperial or palace garden closed to the public — a forbidden park reserved for the ruler's private use, historically often adjacent to or part of a palace complex (comparable in spirit to the [[紫禁城]], "Forbidden City," though that names the palace itself rather than its grounds).

Mandarin jìnyuàn, Cantonese gam3 jyun2, Japanese きんえん (kin'en), and Korean 금원 (geumwon) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese cấm uyển is likewise compositional, combining cấm ("forbid") with uyển ("garden, park").
