## Notes
- [武 (char)](../characters/武%20(char).md) "military" + [士 (char)](../characters/士%20(char).md) "scholar, gentleman" — literally "military gentleman," the samurai/warrior class of pre-modern Japan.

Mandarin wǔshì, Cantonese mou5 si6, Japanese ぶし (bushi), Korean 무사 and Vietnamese võ sĩ are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 武's and 士's own stored citations. The word is old in Chinese, where it means a fighting man or warrior in general; in Japan it came to name the hereditary warrior class that rose to power from the Heian period and ruled until the Meiji abolition of the class system, and the English loan "samurai" (侍) names the same stratum from the retainer's side. Korean 무사 is likewise a general "warrior" and corresponds to the military side of the old officialdom, and Vietnamese võ sĩ carries the same sense, now often of martial artists.

The ethos attached to the Japanese sense is treated in [[武士道]] "bushido." Related vocabulary of soldiers and fighting is collected on the lexipedia War page.
