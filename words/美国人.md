## Notes
- [美 (char)](../characters/美%20(char).md) "beautiful, pretty" + [国](../characters/国.md) "country" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" — none of the three characters' own `stand_in` points here (美's is [[美]] itself, 国's is [[国家]], 人's is [[人]] itself), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 国 and 人 are each individually `kwin: false`.

美国人 names an American person.

Mandarin měiguórén, Cantonese mei5 gwok3 jan4, and Korean 미국인 (miguginn) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 美国人 is itself a common attested word for "American" in all three. Japanese uses アメリカ人 (Amerika-jin, loanword + 人) rather than a Sino-Japanese compound built on 美国. Vietnamese has two attested forms — người Mỹ (the common modern term) and người Hoa Kỳ (the older, more formal Sino-Vietnamese-derived form). No Dan'a'yo homophones.
