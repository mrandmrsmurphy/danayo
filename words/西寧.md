## Notes
- [西](../characters/西.md) "west" + [寧](../characters/寧.md) "peaceful, calm" — no stand-in relationship applies (西's own is [[西方]]; 寧's own is [[安寧]]). The simplified 西宁 is kept as an alias, because 宁 is an alias of the parent form 寧 on its character page.

This name is a collision between a Chinese city and a Vietnamese province, so the Chinese sense comes first.

1. Xining, the capital of Qinghai province in northwestern China, on the edge of the Tibetan plateau. English Wiktionary also records a historical county of the same name in Guangdong (from memory, not re-checked).
2. Tây Ninh, a province and city of southern Vietnam on the Cambodian border, home of the Cao Đài religion's Holy See and of Núi Bà Đen, "Black Lady Mountain" (from memory, not re-checked). English Wiktionary lists both the province and the city; the province was merged into another province in the 2025 reorganization (from memory, not re-checked).

The characters are "west" + "peaceful", so 西寧 reads "western peace". Readings in the Chinese sense: Mandarin Xīníng and Cantonese sai1 ning4; Japanese せいねい is attested for the Chinese city at English Wiktionary; Korean 서녕 is the compositional North Korean reading (寧 gives 녕 on its character page). Vietnamese Tây Ninh is the Hán-Việt reading and the real name of the Vietnamese sense.

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 西寧 as a Chinese proper noun (Xining; Tây Ninh province and city; the historical county) plus Japanese せいねい (Xining) and a Vietnamese Han-form entry for Tây Ninh; Chinese Wiktionary has a stub with Mandarin xīníng; Korean Wiktionary has a short entry labelled 중국어. Japanese and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no entry. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄝㄧㄋㄝㄫ unique across both words and characters).
