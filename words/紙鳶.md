## Notes
- [紙 (char)](../characters/紙%20(char).md) "paper" + [鳶 (char)](../characters/鳶%20(char).md) "kite (bird)" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (紙's is [[紙]] itself, 鳶's is [[鳶]] itself), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 紙 is individually `kwin: false`.

紙鳶 names the paper kite — literally "paper kite (bird)," the flying toy named after the bird of prey it was thought to resemble in flight. It is the classical/literary term, distinct from the modern colloquial 風箏 (fēngzhēng), which is stored here as an alias rather than the headword's own reading.

Mandarin zhǐyuān and Cantonese zi2 jyun1 are standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese たこ (tako) is a directly attested jukujikun reading of 紙鳶 itself (confirmed via web search) — the same word ordinarily written with the kokuji 凧. Korean 지연 (jiyeon) is the compositional Sino-Korean reading, though Korean more commonly uses the native word 연 alone for "kite." Vietnamese diều is the standard word. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
