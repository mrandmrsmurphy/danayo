## Notes
- [風 (char)](../characters/風%20(char).md) "wind" — the bare character used directly as a word, its own `stand_in`.

風 means wind: moving air (颱風, "typhoon"; 風雨, "wind and rain"; 微風, "breeze"), and by extension style, custom, or manner (風格, "style"; 風俗, "custom"; 作風, "conduct"), and rumor (風聲, "news, rumor"). The simplified form 风 is listed as an alias. Mandarin fēng, Cantonese fung1, Korean 풍, and Vietnamese phong are all standard, fully compositional readings (phong is the primary compositional reading among the character's stored candidates; phông is a rarer variant). Japanese carries two on'yomi in largely free variation: ふう (FUU, used here, as in 風潮 fūchō "tempest, trend") and ふ (FU, as in 風情 fūzei, "charm, atmosphere"), while the native 訓読み かぜ (kaze) remains the everyday word for "wind" itself; Korean has native 바람.

The character's own etymology is a 假借 (phonetic loan): 風 originally borrowed the graph for 鳳 ("phoenix"), whose feather-tail strands simplified over time into the modern bottom component that superficially resembles 虫 ("insect") but is not a true insect radical — 風 is instead its own Kangxi radical (182).
