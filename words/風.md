## Notes
- [風 (char)](../characters/風%20(char).md) "wind" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 風 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

風 means wind. Mandarin fēng, Cantonese fung1, Korean 풍, and Vietnamese phong are all standard, fully compositional readings (phong is the primary compositional reading among the character's stored candidates; phông is a rarer variant). Japanese carries two on'yomi in largely free variation: ふう (FUU, used here, as in 風潮 fūchō "tempest, trend") and ふ (FU, as in 風情 fūzei/風邪 kaze-adjacent compounds), while the native 訓読み かぜ (kaze) remains the everyday word for "wind" itself — the same Sino-Japanese/native split documented throughout this sweep.

The character's own etymology is a 假借 (phonetic loan): 風 originally borrowed the graph for 鳳 ("phoenix"), whose feather-tail strands simplified over time into the modern bottom component that superficially resembles 虫 ("insect") but is not a true insect radical — 風 is instead its own Kangxi radical (182). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 뿡 (fung, from Middle Chinese 非母 f + 東三韻 ɨuŋ) diverges from Sino-Korean 풍. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄈㄨㄫ unique across both words and characters).
