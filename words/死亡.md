## Notes
- [死](../characters/死.md) "die" + [亡](../characters/亡.md) "die" — stand-in for both [[死]] and [[亡]], which cannot appear independently

死亡 is a near-synonym doubling: both components already mean "die/death" on their own, combined into the standard formal/clinical word for death (死亡率 "mortality rate," 死亡人数 "death toll"). 死 is 会意 of [[歹]] ("death; remains of stripped bone") and a bowing mourner figure, depicting a funeral scene directly. 亡, by contrast, is 指事 in origin — a line marking the edge of a blade (the original sense of 芒), with "flee, lose, die" all later sound-loan extensions unrelated to the graphemic depiction itself.

Mandarin sǐwáng, Cantonese sei2 mong4, Japanese しぼう (shibō), and Korean 사망 (samang) are all the standard formal/clinical register word for death — each language's everyday speech instead prefers a plainer native or bare-character alternative (Japanese 死ぬ shinu; Korean 죽다 jukda). Sino-Vietnamese tử vong is likewise the formal/medical register term (tử vong suất "mortality rate"), while everyday Vietnamese uses the native chết.
