## Notes
- [浣](../characters/浣.md) "wash" + [衣](../characters/衣.md) "clothes"
- This word is the stand-in for [浣](../characters/浣.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

浣 is to wash by rinsing and rubbing, classically of clothes (the variant 澣, listed as the alias here, is the same word), and 衣 is "clothes," so 浣衣 is to wash clothing. It is a literary word of old Chinese poetry and prose, tied to the image of women washing clothes at a stream, as in 浣女 "a washerwoman," whose village scene appears in Tang landscape verse. The same 浣 gives the old expression for the three ten-day periods of a month (上浣, 中浣, 下浣), from the custom of officials' days off for washing.

It is the elevated counterpart of [洗濯](洗濯.md) "laundry, washing," the everyday term, and [洗](洗.md) "to wash" in general. The readings are Mandarin huànyī, Cantonese wun5 ji1, Japanese kan'i (かんい) and Korean 한의 (the Korean reading of 浣 recorded on its character page). The word is placed under [Clothing](../lexipedia/Clothing.md). Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "wash clothes, do laundry," as a literary register.
