## Notes
- [癌](../characters/癌.md) "cancer" + [症](../characters/症.md) "disease, symptom" — literally "cancer condition." 癌's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 症's own is [[病症]]; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 癌 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word.

癌症 is the standard Mandarin and Cantonese word for cancer, built from 癌 ("cancer, carcinoma") plus the highly productive medical suffix 症 ("condition, symptom") that also forms 炎症 ("inflammation") and 自閉症 ("autism"). Korean, by contrast, does not need the suffix: 암 alone already means "cancer" in ordinary Korean, so the compositional compound 암증 does not exist as a real Korean word — the `korean` field here is set to the bare 암, not a literal character-by-character rendering.

Japanese がんしょう (gan-shō) is a rarer, more clinical-register compositional form; everyday Japanese overwhelmingly prefers bare 癌 (がん, gan) on its own, the same simplification Korean makes.
