## Notes
- [豚 (char)](../characters/豚%20(char).md) "pig"

豚 is 会意, combining 月/肉 ("meat, flesh") with 豕 ("pig") — a pig raised for meat, specifically a young pig or piglet (as distinct from the more general 豬/猪). It survives in Japanese as the everyday word for pork as food: [[豚肉]] ("pork"), [[豚井]] (butadon, "pork rice bowl").

Mandarin tún, Cantonese tyun4, and Japanese とん (ton, on'yomi, fully productive: 豚肉 とんにく "pork") alongside native ぶた (buta, the everyday word for "pig" as a live animal) all preserve the sense. Korean 돈 (don) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 돼지 (dwaeji, the everyday word for "pig"). Vietnamese đồn (also độn) is the Hán Việt reading, specifically denoting a piglet or suckling pig (heo con, lợn con) rather than a pig in general.
