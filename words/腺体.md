## Notes
- [腺](../characters/腺.md) "gland" + [体](../characters/体.md) "body" — this word is 腺's own `stand_in`: the character's meaning (gland) is identical, but 腺 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word, so 体 ("body") grounds the gland in its anatomical context.

腺体 is a gland: an organ that makes and releases a substance such as sweat, milk or a hormone. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 腺體 (an alias; Mandarin xiàntǐ, Cantonese sin3 tai2) as "(anatomy) gland". It has no Japanese or Korean entry for the compound: Japanese and Korean use the bare 腺 (Japanese せん, Korean 선, "gland"; Vietnamese tuyến), so Japanese せんたい and Korean 선체 here are built from the characters. Korean 선체 is, in fact, a different word, 船體, "hull (of a ship)".

The gland as a bare character is bound, and this word is its stand-in; the body that holds it is the [[体]].
