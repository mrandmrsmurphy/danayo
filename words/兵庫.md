## Notes
- [兵](../characters/兵.md) "soldier" + [庫](../characters/庫.md) "storehouse"

兵庫 is Hyōgo, written "arms storehouse". The name comes from the old port of Hyōgo-no-tsu (now part of Kobe), which was associated with a storehouse for weapons. The reading ひょうご is the on-reading of both characters, with 庫 (こ) voiced to ご.

Hyōgo stretches across Honshu from the Sea of Japan to the Seto Inland Sea, with its capital Kobe. It is known for Himeji Castle, a UNESCO site, Awaji Island, the Takarazuka Revue, Kobe and Tajima beef, and the 1995 Great Hanshin earthquake.

In Dan'a'yo 兵庫 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 효고 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
