## Notes
- [煙](../characters/煙.md) "smoke" + [気 (char)](../characters/気%20(char).md) "feeling, air" — stand-in for [[煙]], which cannot appear independently

煙気 means smoke or flue gas: the visible mixture of gases and fine particles given off by burning (煙氣瀰漫, "smoke fills the air"; 工業煙氣, "industrial flue gas"). The variant 煙氣 is listed as an alias. The compound pairs 煙 "smoke" with 気 "air, vapor".

Mandarin yānqì, Cantonese jin1 hei3, and Japanese えんき (enki) are all real compositional readings for "smoke, flue gas" — though each language's everyday word for plain "smoke" is simply the bare first character (Mandarin 煙/烟 yān, Japanese native 訓読み けむり kemuri). Korean 연기 (yeongi) is a genuine, well-attested word for "smoke" in its own right, not merely a bound compositional form.
