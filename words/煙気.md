## Notes
- [煙](../characters/煙.md) "smoke" + [気 (char)](../characters/気%20(char).md) "feeling, air" — stand-in for [[煙]], which cannot appear independently

Mandarin yānqì, Cantonese jin1 hei3, and Japanese えんき (enki) are all real compositional readings for "smoke, flue gas" — though each language's everyday word for plain "smoke" is simply the bare first character (Mandarin 煙/烟 yān, Japanese native 訓読み けむり kemuri). Korean 연기 (yeongi) is a genuine, well-attested word for "smoke" in its own right, not merely a bound compositional form. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 언 (from Middle Chinese 影母 ʔ + 先韻 en) diverges from Sino-Korean 연 — the same 影母-initial divergence pattern seen on other characters in this vault with a null Middle Chinese initial.
