## Notes
- [糖](../characters/糖%20(char).md) "sugar" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 糖 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo. Its own 形声 phonetic component 唐 (OC \*ɡl'aːŋ) gives the syllable, semantic 米 ("rice") reflecting sugar's original derivation from grain/cane starches.

Mandarin táng, Cantonese tong4, and Korean 당 (dang) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese uses the native kun-reading あめ (ame, "sugar, candy" — an older sense of the character, as in 麦芽糖 "malt sugar/maltose") rather than the on-reading トウ (TOU) for the bare character alone. Vietnamese đường is the standard word for sugar. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
