## Notes
- [蛮](../characters/蛮.md) "barbarian" + [夷](../characters/夷.md) "barbarian" — a near-synonym doubling of two classical Chinese terms for peoples outside the Sinitic cultural sphere.

蛮夷 was the classical Chinese term for peoples regarded as outside Han civilization — 夷 originally denoted specific eastern peoples and 蛮 southern ones, before both generalized to "barbarian, non-civilized outsider" broadly, contrasted with 華夏 (huáxià, "the civilized [Chinese] world"). This Sino-centric civilizational framework was inherited and reapplied across the wider East Asian sphere: premodern Vietnamese rulers and historiographers, for instance, used the same 華夷 (Hoa-di) distinction to position Vietnam itself as civilized and its own neighbors as 夷, rather than treating the term as fixed to any one ethnicity — Emperor Gia Long is recorded using it in exactly this way to draw a border between Vietnam and its neighbors.

Japanese ばんい and Korean 만이 are both standard compositional Sino-readings. Vietnamese man di is likewise standard and directly attested in this historical/classical usage. No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄇㄚㄋ·ㄧㄜ unique across both words and characters).
