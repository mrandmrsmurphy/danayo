## Notes
- [龍 (char)](characters/龍%20(char).md) "dragon" + [断](characters/断.md) "cut off" — no `#cranberry`: 龍's own `stand_in` is 龍 itself, 断's is [[割断]]; neither points here. `kwin` is false (AND-rule: 龍 true, 断 false).

1. to trade from higher ground
2. to monopolize, to corner the market

From the book Mencius:

人亦孰不欲富貴？而獨於富貴之中，有私龍斷焉。……有賤丈夫焉，必求龍斷而登之，以左右望而罔市利。
Who indeed is there of men but wishes for riches and honour? But he only, among the seekers of these, tried to monopolize the conspicuous mound. […] It happened that there was a mean fellow, who made it a point to look out for a conspicuous mound, and get up upon it. Thence he looked right and left, to catch in his net the whole gain of the market.

**All real-language fields deliberately follow the actual attested word 壟斷 (lǒngduàn/농단/lũng đoạn) rather than a naive compositional reading of 龍+断**: the Mencius passage above is itself the origin of this substitution — 龍 was originally used as a phonetic loan for 壟 ("a mound, raised ground"), and the borrowed spelling 龍斷 stuck as an alternate written form even though every daughter language's real pronunciation still follows 壟, not 龍. Hence mandarin `lǒngduàn` (not `lóngduàn`), cantonese `lung5 dyun6` (not `lung4`), and korean `롱단` (North-Korean form of 농단, the word behind the famous 2016 "국정농단" scandal name — not `룡단`) all diverge from 龍's own stored tone/reading; vietnamese `lũng đoạn` similarly diverges from 龍's own stored "long." Japanese ろうだん uses 龍's own alternate on'yomi ROU (its stored list is RYUU/ROU) rather than the more common RYUU, so no divergence appears there. Dan'a'yo's own `룡돤`/`lyongdwan`/`ㄌ⼄ㄫㄉ⺢ㄋ` preserves the classical loan-spelling 龍 (compositional from the character's own stored reading), matching the aliased traditional/simplified forms 壟斷/隴斷/壟断/垄断/陇断 in meaning while keeping 龍 in Dan'a'yo's own headword.
