## Notes
- [門](../characters/門.md) "gate, category" + [階](../characters/階.md) "level, tier, storey" — literally "gate-level," the taxonomic rank of phylum/division.

Mandarin mén, Cantonese mun4, Japanese もん (mon), and Korean 문 are all the real, standard bare-character terms used in taxonomy, matching 門's own stored citations exactly. Vietnamese breaks the pattern: the real Vietnamese term is ngành, a native word (literally "branch," as of a river or family tree) rather than the expected Sino-Vietnamese reading of 門 itself (môn, 門's own stored citation) — stored here since ngành is what a Vietnamese biologist actually writes.

**Why append 階 at all, when none of the four source languages do**: Dan'a'yo appends 階 uniformly across all eight ranks of the taxonomic hierarchy ([[域階]], [[界階]], [[門階]], [[綱階]], [[目階]], [[科階]], [[属階]], [[種階]]) as a purely internal disambiguation device — natural languages rely on context alone to read 目 as "order" rather than "eye" inside a taxonomy chart, but a constructed language benefits from removing that ambiguity outright, especially since Dan'a'yo already independently uses bare [[目]] for "eye." In casual or space-constrained usage, the bare character (e.g. 目 alone) remains an understood abbreviation of the full 階-suffixed word, the same way scientific Latin abbreviates "Order" to "O." — but the full form is what a dictionary entry or first mention should use.

`kwin` is false: 門 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page, which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 階 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄇㄛㄋㄍ⼶ㄧ unique among both words and characters).
