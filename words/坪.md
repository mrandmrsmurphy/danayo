## Notes
- [坪 (char)](../characters/坪%20(char).md) "level ground; a unit of area"

坪 means "level ground, a lawn" (草坪, "lawn") and, as a unit, "tsubo/pyeong" — a traditional East Asian area unit equal to 36 square shaku, still in active real-estate use in Japan and Korea (roughly 3.3 square meters).

Mandarin píng and Cantonese ping4 preserve the "level ground" sense; the unit sense is not native to Mandarin/Cantonese usage. Japanese つぼ (tsubo, native kun'yomi) is the living, everyday term for the area unit, still used in Japanese real estate. Korean 평 (pyeong) parallels the Sino-xenic reading and is likewise the living, everyday unit term in Korean real estate. Vietnamese bình is dictionary-attested specifically for this Japanese-unit sense (Thiều Chửu's dictionary glosses 坪 as "phép đo nước Nhật-bản cho 36 thước vuông," "the Japanese measurement system where 36 square thước make one bình") — but it is a lexicographic reading rather than living Vietnamese vocabulary; Vietnam has no domestic tradition of this unit (its own traditional land units — mẫu, sào, công — are unrelated), and Vietnamese material discussing tsubo/pyeong today simply borrows those foreign terms directly rather than using "bình."

This word completes a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read byeng/병/ㄅ⼶ㄫ): [[丙]] ("third; third heavenly stem," already perfected, already cross-linking here) and [[柄]] ("handle; design," still unperfected).
