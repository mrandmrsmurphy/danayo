## Notes
- [半 (char)](../characters/半%20(char).md) "half" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

半 is "half": one of two equal parts, and by extension "partial" or "semi-". It stands alone as the numeral for one half — Dan'a'yo says 半 where Mandarin says 一半 (yībàn), with no 一 needed — and it is also the stem that builds compounds in which something is only partly one thing: [[半島]] "peninsula" (a half-island), [[半導体]] "semiconductor", [[上半期]] and [[下半期]] "first" and "second half of the period", [[夜半]] "midnight, the middle of the night", and, on [[文法 - 02音韻論]], 半母音, the half-vowel or glide.

The character is 会意: 八 "to divide" applied to 牛 "ox" — to cut an ox into two halves. Wiktionary reconstructs it from Proto-Sino-Tibetan *par and relates it to Middle Chinese *panH*. The character itself is a Japanese second-grade (kyōiku) kanji.

Mandarin bàn and Cantonese bun3 are the plain readings, and both also use 半 for the half hour (Hong Kong Cantonese 兩點半, "half past two"). Japanese はん (HAN) is the on'yomi, while the native word is なかば (nakaba), "middle, halfway"; 二時半 (nijihan) tells the half hour. Korean 반 (ban) is the same, 두 시 반 for "half past two". Vietnamese has two layers: the Sino-Vietnamese reading is bán, as in bán đảo "peninsula" and bán dẫn "semiconductor", while the everyday word for "half" is the native *nửa*, which is not a reading of the character. The `vietnamese` field keeps the Sino-Vietnamese bán; hvdic also records only Nôm variants (ban, bận, bớn, bướng) and no *nửa*. No HSK level was found for this word.

Three words share this syllable in Dan'a'yo (ban, ㄅㄚㄋ): [[畔]] "ridge, boundary", [[盤]] "tray" and [[般]] "category"; the homophone callout cross-references them.
