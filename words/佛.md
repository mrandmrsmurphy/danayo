## Notes
- [佛 (char)](../characters/佛%20(char).md) "Buddha"

佛 is 形声: semantic [[人]] ("person") + phonetic [[弗]] — a transcription character created to render Sanskrit *buddha* ("awakened one") into Chinese. The full transliteration is 佛陀 (fótuó); 佛 itself is the shortened form that came to be used as the standard word for "Buddha" throughout East Asia, and by extension "Buddhism, Buddhist" in compounds (佛教 "Buddhism," 佛法 "the Dharma," 佛寺 "Buddhist temple").

Mandarin fú, Cantonese fat6, and Japanese ぶつ/ふつ/ほつ (butsu/futsu/hotsu, on'yomi, as in 仏教 ぶっきょう "Buddhism") alongside native ほとけ (hotoke, the everyday word for "Buddha") all carry the sense throughout East Asian Buddhist vocabulary. Korean 불 (bul) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 부처 (bucheo, the everyday Korean word for "Buddha"). Vietnamese Phật is the standard reading, capitalized as a proper noun/title (Đức Phật, "The Buddha") — familiar throughout Vietnamese Buddhist vocabulary (Phật giáo, "Buddhism"); the character page's other stored candidate, phất, does not correspond to any sense of 佛 itself and looks like contamination from an unrelated character sharing the same phonetic component 弗 (e.g. 拂, "to flick, whisk," also read fú in Mandarin).

`japanese: ぶつ` uses 佛 (char)'s first-listed on'yomi (BUTSU before FUTSU/HOTSU), per the vault's established convention for stand-in words. Legitimizing note: 佛 (char)'s own `stand_in` is this exact word (itself), so 佛 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by itself. No homophones beyond the self-match (注音 ㄅㄨㄊ).
