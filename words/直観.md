## Notes
- [直 (char)](../characters/直%20(char).md) "straight, aligned" + [観](../characters/観.md) "observe" — literally "to observe directly," i.e. to grasp something immediately, without reasoning through it step by step.

直観 names intuition — direct, immediate understanding or perception that bypasses conscious, discursive reasoning. It is a significant term in both everyday and philosophical usage: in phenomenology and epistemology, 直観 (as a translation of German *Anschauung* or *Intuition*) names a mode of direct cognitive apprehension distinct from inferential judgment, a distinction central to Kantian and later continental philosophy as received into East Asian academic vocabulary.

Mandarin zhíguān, Cantonese zik6 gun1, Japanese ちょっかん (chokkan), Korean 직관 (jikgwan), and Vietnamese trực quan are all fully compositional and used consistently in both the everyday "gut feeling" sense and the more technical philosophical sense.
