## Notes
- [芽](../characters/芽.md) "bud, sprout" + [荘](../characters/荘.md) "villa, village" — no stand-in relationship applies (芽's own is [[新芽]]; 荘's own is [[別荘]]). The name is written 芽莊 in traditional characters (and 芽庄 in simplified), kept as aliases because 荘 is the parent form on its character page.

芽荘 (Nha Trang) is a coastal city on the south-central coast of Vietnam, in Khánh Hòa province. It is best known as a beach resort and seaside tourist town, with a sheltered bay and offshore islands, and as a diving and boating destination (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Wikipedia now describes Nha Trang officially as a ward (phường) of Khánh Hòa province after the 2025 administrative reorganization, so the administrative status has shifted; the city name as a whole is still in everyday use (checked 2026-10-11, the status line only). The Chinese characters are a phonetic/Hán-Việt rendering of the Vietnamese name rather than a translation: "bud village" is not what the name means, and the origin of Nha Trang is usually traced to a Cham-language name (from memory, not re-checked).

The `vietnamese` field holds the Hán-Việt reading nha trang (芽 nha, 荘/莊 trang; hvdic.thivien.net gives 芽 as nha and 莊 as trang), which is exactly the place name, so the reading is a perfect compositional match. Mandarin Yázhuāng and Cantonese ngaa4 zong1 are as in English Wiktionary. Japanese ニャチャン (from memory, not re-checked) is the actual name; the `japanese` field holds the compositional on'yomi がそう (芽 GA, 荘 SOU), and the Korean 아장 is likewise the compositional Sino-Korean reading.

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 芽莊 as a Chinese proper noun (a city in Vietnam, Mandarin Yázhuāng, Cantonese ngaa4 zong1; simplified 芽庄) and a Vietnamese Han-form entry for Nha Trang. Chinese Wiktionary has the same two entries. Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 芽莊 or 芽庄 page. No homophones (注音 ㄚㄐ⺢ㄫ unique across both words and characters).
