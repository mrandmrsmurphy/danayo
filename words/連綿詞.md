## Notes
- [連 (char)](../characters/連%20(char).md) "to link; successive" + [綿](../characters/綿.md) "continuous; cotton" + [詞](../characters/詞.md) "word" — "a continuous-link word."

連綿詞 is the traditional Chinese linguistic term for a bisyllabic morpheme whose two characters have no meaning apart from the pair: 徘徊 "to pace", 恍惚 "dim, hazy", 蜘蛛 "spider", 鴛鴦 "mandarin ducks", 葡萄 "grape". Wiktionary glosses it as a disyllabic morpheme, usually ideophonic and reduplicative in origin, and gives 聯綿詞 (simplified 联绵词) as the same word with 聯綿字 and 衍聲複詞 as synonyms. Japanese uses 連綿語 (れんめんご) for it, so the Japanese field here is the compositional reading of the three characters, not an attested word. The Korean field follows the North Korean readings of the characters.

In Dan'a'yo the term matters because of the `#cranberry` tag: the tag borrows the name from 連綿詞, the way "cran-" in "cranberry" exists only inside the compound. The vault applies the tag more broadly than the strict definition, to characters whose meaning is carried wholly by a shared compound; see [List of 連綿詞](../lookup/List%20of%20連綿詞.md) for the test and the examples. The strict sense, the native alliterative and rhyming pairs, is what the term names.
