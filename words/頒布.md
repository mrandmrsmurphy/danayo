## Notes
- [頒](../characters/頒.md) "confer" + [布](../characters/布.md) "spread"
- This word is the viable form of [頒](../characters/頒.md): the character's meaning (to promulgate, bestow) is identical, but 頒 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 頒布 is its `stand_in` compound.

頒 is to bestow or distribute from above (the original image is of handing out to many, with 頁 "head, person" in the character), and 布 here is "to spread out, make widely known." 頒布 is therefore to make something publicly known and in force by official announcement: to promulgate a law, decree or regulation. The word is characteristically official, and the object is typically a law (頒布法令), a calendar or an order, so the direction runs from authority down to the public.

The Government page pairs it with [諭示](諭示.md) as the act of issuing, and cites [勅令](勅令.md) "imperial edict" as what is promulgated. It contrasts with [散布](散布.md) "to scatter, disseminate," which is physical or informal spreading with no official force. In Japanese 頒布 (hanpu) has a second everyday use, to distribute copies of something (as in 頒布会, a members' distribution sale), a looser sense that Dan'a'yo does not adopt. The readings are Mandarin bānbù (alias 颁布, the simplified form), Cantonese baan1 bou3, Korean 반포 and Sino-Vietnamese ban bố. Dan'a'yo keeps the official sense, "promulgate, issue officially."
