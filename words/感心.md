>[!caution] The senses split — Japanese "admiration", Mandarin "touching"
>感心 is admiration in Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. Mandarin gǎnxīn and Cantonese gam2 sam1 are recorded only as a literary or Taiwanese word for "touching, moving", so a Chinese reader may hear "moved" rather than "impressed". Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md); for admiration prefer [感服](感服.md).

## Notes
- [感](../characters/感.md) "moved" + [心 (char)](../characters/心%20(char).md) "heart"

感心 is to be impressed: to be struck with admiration by someone's conduct or skill, in Dan'a'yo the Japanese sense of admiration. Wiktionary glosses Japanese かんしん as "admirable, commendable, praiseworthy; admiration; to admire, be deeply impressed". Mandarin gǎnxīn and Cantonese gam2 sam1 are recorded only as a literary and Taiwanese word, "touching; moving". Korean 감심 is the reading of the characters.

The word is listed on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md) as a Split. It is milder than [感服](感服.md), admiration that yields, and a sibling of [賛嘆](賛嘆.md), gasping in admiration. The respect it implies is [敬意](敬意.md).
