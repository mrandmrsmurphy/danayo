## Notes
- [過 (char)](../characters/過%20(char).md) "fault, transgression" (an extended sense beyond this vault's own primary "too, than" gloss for the character) + [失](../characters/失.md) "lapse, loss" — literally "a fault-lapse."

過失 means a fault, error, slip, mistake, or act of negligence — a faux pas in the broadest sense, and the standard legal/formal term across the sphere for negligence as a state of mind (as opposed to intentional wrongdoing).

Mandarin guòshī, Cantonese gwo3 sat1, Japanese かしつ, Korean 과실, and Vietnamese quá thất are all standard, fully compositional readings. **A genuine, well-attested homophone**: Korean 과실 is itself famously ambiguous between this word (過失, "fault, negligence") and [果実](果実.md) (果實, "fruit, berry") — the same real-world ambiguity carries straight through to this vault, since both compounds mechanically build to the identical Dan'a'yo citation 과싣/gwasid/ㄍ⺢ㄙㄧㄊ (過 and 果 are themselves Dan'a'yo homophones).

**Hebrew**: it best translates *shĕgāgāh* (שְׁגָגָה) "unintentional sin," the sin done in error for which Leviticus 4 and Numbers 15:22–29 provide a remedy. It is the opposite of a sin "with a high hand" (Numbers 15:30), which is [[反逆]].
