## Notes
- [家](../characters/家.md) "house; family" + [畜](../characters/畜.md) "livestock" — the family's kept, tended animals.

家畜 is the general term for any domesticated animal raised for use — draft animals, dairy and meat animals, and beasts of burden alike — as distinct from [[家禽]] (かきん-related 家禽 "domestic fowl, poultry"), which is reserved for birds specifically; the two terms partition the same conceptual space by animal class rather than overlapping. 畜 itself is a fairly restricted character outside this compound and its own stand-in [[畜生]] (originally "domestic animal," but which drifted in both Mandarin and Japanese into a strong pejorative — "brute," an insult directed at a person — a semantic path 家畜 itself never took).

Japanese かちく, Korean 가축, and Vietnamese gia súc are all plain, everyday, non-drifted terms for livestock, with no comparable narrowing or figurative extension in any of the three languages. No specific HSK level number is confirmed for the compound (Wiktionary marks it only as advanced-register vocabulary); 畜 itself is independently HSK 4.
