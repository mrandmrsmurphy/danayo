## Notes
- [伍 (char)](../characters/伍%20(char).md) "troops"

伍 (人 "person" + phonetic 五) originally meant a file of five soldiers — the basic unit of ancient Chinese military organization (五人為伍, "five men make a 伍"), and by extension "troops, ranks; to associate with" (as in 羞與為伍, "ashamed to associate with"). Because it shares its exact Mandarin pronunciation with the numeral [[五]] ("five"), 伍 also serves as that numeral's dedicated anti-forgery/financial variant — the same role [[壱]] plays for [[一]], [[貳]] for [[二]], [[漆]] for [[七]], and [[玖]] for [[九]] — since its extra strokes are much harder to alter on a financial document than 五's own simple form.

Mandarin wǔ, Cantonese ng5, Japanese ご (go, on'yomi, rare outside proper names and the financial-numeral role) alongside native いつつ (itsutsu, borrowed here from 五 itself, since 伍 has no distinct native Japanese reading of its own), Korean 오 (o), and Vietnamese ngũ all match [[五]]'s own readings exactly, reflecting their shared phonetic origin.

This word completes a four-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read 'o/오/ㄛ): [[五]] ("five," already perfected) and [[於]] ("in, at, during," already perfected) both already cross-link here; [[汚]] ("dirty") still awaits its own turn.
