## Notes
- [壁 (char)](../characters/壁%20(char).md) "wall"

壁 means "wall" — often specifically a solid partition or cliff-face, distinct from 牆 (a built fence-wall). It appears in 壁虎 ("gecko," literally "wall tiger") and 隔壁 ("next door," literally "partition wall").

**Content resolved**: `cantonese` was the malformed string `"bek3, bik1, bik3"`; `bik1` is the standard literary reading (壁虎, bik1 fu2; 壁畫, bik1 waa2) and was used as the field value. `bek3` is a legitimate but restricted vernacular reading, specifically tied to 隔壁 (gaak3 bek3, "next door"); `bik3` is a rare/marginal variant. `vietnamese` was the malformed string `"bích, bệch, bịch, vách"`; `bích` is the sole Hán Việt reading (bích họa 壁畫, "mural") and was used as the field value. `vách` is a legitimate, common Nôm doublet — the everyday native word for "wall, partition"; `bịch` and `bệch` are also genuine but archaic/marginal Nôm variants, weakly attested.

Mandarin bì preserves the sense directly. Japanese ヘキ (HEKI, on'yomi, formal register) alongside native かべ (kabe, "wall," the everyday noun) cover the sense. Korean 벽 (byeok) parallels the Sino-xenic reading and is likewise standard, everyday vocabulary.
