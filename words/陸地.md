## Notes
- [陸](../characters/陸.md) "continent" + [地 (char)](../characters/地%20(char).md) "land" — neither character's `stand_in` is this word (陸 is legitimized by [[大陸]], 地 by [[地]] itself), so 陸地 is an ordinary compound rather than a legitimizer.

陸地 means "land; dry land; landmass", i.e. land as opposed to water. Wiktionary lists it as a geography noun in Chinese, with "land" for Japanese and "land; shore" for Korean; the Vietnamese entry glosses lục địa as "continent".

1. **Land as opposed to sea.** The core sense, shared across all five languages: the solid part of the earth's surface, contrasted with 海洋 and 大洋. This is the sense Dan'a'yo keeps; no divergence from the shared CJKV meaning is proposed.
2. **Landmass.** Wiktionary lists 大陸 (dàlù) and 大片陸地 (dàpiàn lùdì) as Chinese synonyms for "landmass". In the vault, [[大陸]] is "continent" and is the specific term for one of the great landmasses; 陸地 is the general, unbounded "dry land".

Readings by language (checked against Wiktionary for 陸地 / 陆地): Mandarin lùdì, Cantonese luk6 dei6, Japanese りくち (rikuchi), Korean 육지 in the South Korean standard spelling (the vault's `korean` field holds the North Korean form 륙지, without 두음법칙), Vietnamese lục địa. In Vietnamese the word is the Sino-Vietnamese reading of 陸地 but means "continent" there, so a Vietnamese speaker will hear it as the sense of 大陸 rather than "dry land".

Within the vault, [[土地]] is the general word for ground, soil and territory, and [[地]] is the bound form that appears mostly in compounds; 陸地 narrows to dry land as against the sea (see the "land/ground" discussion on the Nature page).

The Dan'a'yo pronunciation is the plain concatenation of the two characters: 陸 lug / 룩 / ㄌㄨㄎ + 地 diǝ / 듸 / ㄉㄧㄜ. It differs from the Korean reading in both syllables (륙 vs 룩, 지 vs 듸). No syllable-boundary dot is written in the 注音: the only other way to cut ㄌㄨㄎㄉㄧㄜ would need a syllable beginning ㄎㄉ- (or a lone ㄉ, or ㄌㄨㄎㄉ / ㄌㄨㄎㄉㄧ), and none of these has a file in `syllables/`, so ㄌㄨㄎ + ㄉㄧㄜ is the only legal parse.
