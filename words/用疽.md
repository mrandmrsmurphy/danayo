## Notes
- [[用]] "use — here a phonetic substitute for 癰 (yōng, 'carbuncle, abscess'), already documented as one of 用's own stored `aliases`" + [[疽]] "ulcer, abscess" — stand-in for [[疽]], which cannot appear independently.

用疽 (properly 癰疽, of which 用疽 is this vault's phonetic-substitute spelling) names a carbuncle or abscess — a deep-seated, pus-filled swelling, historically one of the standard classical Chinese medical terms for such growths, alongside milder 癤 ("boil").

Mandarin yōngjū, Cantonese jung1 zeoi1, Japanese ようそ (yōso), and Korean 옹저 (ongjeo) are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 用's (as 癰) and 疽's own stored citations. **Vietnamese ung thư is a genuine, striking case of semantic narrowing, not an error**: 癰疽 is real Sino-Vietnamese for this exact classical medical sense (abscess/carbuncle/ulcer), but the same compound has since undergone a specific semantic shift in modern Vietnamese to mean specifically "cancer" — the everyday, standard Vietnamese word for the disease today. The literal, older "abscess" sense has been fully displaced by the newer, narrower "cancer" sense in living usage, the same way English "consumption" once meant tuberculosis generally before falling out of use for the more specific modern term. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 용조 diverges from Sino-Korean 옹저 in the second syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ⼄ㄫㄐㄜ unique among both words and characters).
