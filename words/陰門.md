## Notes
- [陰 (char)](../characters/陰%20(char).md) "yin, shaded, hidden" + [門](../characters/門.md) "gate, door" — literally "yin-gate," using 陰 in its anatomical euphemistic sense (compare English "private parts").

陰門 is a classical/medical term for the vulva, built the same "gate/door" way as its close near-synonym 陰戶 (using 戶 rather than 門 for "door"), both real, attested compounds for the same structure.

Mandarin yīnmén, Cantonese jam1 mun4, Korean 음문 (eummun), and Japanese いんもん (inmon) are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 陰's and 門's own stored citations. Vietnamese âm môn is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 陰's âm (one of two candidates on its own page) and 門's own môn, and is directly attested alongside its near-synonym âm hộ (陰戶). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 임몬 diverges from Sino-Korean 음문 in both syllables. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄧㄇㄇㄛㄋ unique among both words and characters).
