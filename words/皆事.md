## Notes
- [皆 (char)](../characters/皆%20(char).md) "all; every; universal" + [事 (char)](../characters/事%20(char).md) "act; matter; affair"

皆事 is the collective (universal) correlative in the 事 ("matter") column of the correlative table: "all matters, everything, all matters taken together". The character [皆 (char)](../characters/皆%20(char).md) (Dan'a'yo *gye*, ㄍ⼶) is the quantifier "all; every", which Wiktionary labels literary for Chinese 皆 ("all; every; everybody"); the free word is [皆](皆.md). [事 (char)](../characters/事%20(char).md) (Dan'a'yo *ji*, ㄐㄧ) supplies "act; matter; affair". Where the distributive [毎事](毎事.md) goes through the matters one at a time, 皆事 treats them as one totality: the predicate holds of the whole set of matters at once. Its part of speech is 代詞 (pronoun), the same as [皆人](皆人.md), [皆物](皆物.md), [皆処](皆処.md), [皆時](皆時.md) and the rest of the 皆 row, and as [此事](此事.md), [其事](其事.md), [彼事](彼事.md), [何事](何事.md) and [某事](某事.md) in its own column; only [毎事](毎事.md) is adverbial (副用名詞). Illustrative only (invented, not from `grammar/`): 皆事、順調 "all matters are going smoothly".

It contrasts with its column neighbours as follows. The distributive [毎事](毎事.md) "each matter" ranges over the matters one by one; the indefinite [某事](某事.md) names an unspecified matter and the interrogative [何事](何事.md) asks which one; the deictic cells [此事](此事.md) "this matter", [其事](其事.md) "that matter" (medial) and [彼事](彼事.md) "that matter yonder" (distal) each point at a single particular matter, while 皆事 covers all of them. The same collective/distributive split runs along the whole table, for example [皆人](皆人.md) against [毎人](毎人.md).

Cross-linguistic fields were checked against Wiktionary on 2026-10-11. Wiktionary has no entry for 皆事 itself, so no `mandarin`, `japanese`, `korean` or `vietnamese` field is entered: the word is a Dan'a'yo compositional construction, and none of the readings below is an attested reading of the compound. The nearest established equivalents are the following. Mandarin 一切 (yīqiè) is a pronoun glossed "all; every; everything; anything" and 凡事 (fánshì) is a literary adverb glossed "no matter what it is; in all things"; the vault has a word for [一切](一切.md) but none for 凡事. Wiktionary also lists 萬事 (wànshì, simplified 万事) as a noun "all; everything", with Japanese 万事 (ばんじ, banji), Korean 만사 (mansa) and Vietnamese *vạn sự* as descendants; 萬事如意 is listed there among its derived terms. This ten-thousand-matters idiom is the conventional Sinosphere way to say "everything", and 皆事 is the regular correlative form of the same notion, built from the table's own 皆 rather than 万. Related vault words for totality are [全部](全部.md), [全体](全体.md) and [所有](所有.md). The Japanese kun reading of 皆 is みな (mina), listed on Wiktionary; that it survives mainly in 皆さん "everyone" and is not a productive compound-forming prefix is from memory, not re-checked. A Vietnamese equivalent such as *mọi việc* "everything" is from memory, not re-checked.

On [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md)'s correlative table (line 126 is the 皆 ("all") row; the same chart is in `grammar/文法 - 97品詞.md`, line 144 in the 事 ("matter") row, with the column header cell 皆 on line 142), 皆事 is the first cell of the 皆 row, followed by [皆物](皆物.md) "all things", [皆人](皆人.md) "all people", [皆名](皆名.md) "all honoured persons", [皆処](皆処.md) "all places", [皆時](皆時.md) "all times", [皆様](皆様.md) "all manners", [皆多](皆多.md) "the whole amount" and [皆類](皆類.md) "all kinds". The table is 7 determiners (此, 其, 彼, 何, 毎, 某, 皆) × 9 noun classes (事, 物, 人, 名, 処, 時, 様, 多, 類), 63 forms, so 皆事 is parsed compositionally: 皆 = collective, 事 = matter. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared CJKV sense "all matters; everything"; no narrowing is needed.

The spellings are the plain concatenation of the two character pages: 羅馬字 *gye* + *ji* = gyeji, 諺文 겨 + 지 = 겨지, 注音 ㄍ⼶ + ㄐㄧ = ㄍ⼶ㄐㄧ, each checked letter by letter against 皆 (char) and 事 (char). The 注音 carries no syllable-boundary dot. `grammar/文法 - 02音韻論.md` allows only ㄇ ㄋ ㄫ, the glides and the unreleased stops ㄆ ㄊ ㄎ as syllable-final consonants, and ㄐ is none of them, so ㄐㄧ must be a syllable of its own (it exists in `syllables/`) and ㄍ⼶ is the preceding syllable (also in `syllables/`); there is no second complete partition. Contrast [皆物](皆物.md) and [皆人](皆人.md), whose second syllables begin with ㄇ and ㄋ, so that ㄍ⼶ㄇ and ㄍ⼶ㄋ would also be legal syllables, and which therefore need the dot.
