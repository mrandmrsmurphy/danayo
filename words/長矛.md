## Notes
- [長 (char)](../characters/長%20(char).md) "long" + [矛](../characters/矛.md) "halberd"
- This word is the viable form of [矛](../characters/矛.md): the character's meaning (spear, halberd) is identical, but 矛 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 長矛 is its `stand_in` compound.

矛 is one of the oldest Chinese weapon words, a socketed spear head on a long shaft, and it is the first half of 矛盾 "contradiction," from the *Han Feizi* story of the merchant who boasted that his spear (矛) could pierce anything and his shield (盾) nothing could pierce. 長 "long" in 長矛 makes the length of the shaft explicit, so the compound names a long spear, lance or pike, the thrusting weapon of infantry and cavalry in contrast to a short throwing spear.

It sits beside [槍](槍.md), the later and now more common word for a spear (and, in Chinese, for a modern firearm); 長矛 is the bookish, historical and military-writing term, and tends to appear in descriptions of ancient and medieval warfare. The readings are Mandarin chángmáo, Cantonese coeng4 maau4 and Korean 장모, and this page leaves the Japanese field blank. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense "spear, lance, pike," with no divergence. It is placed under both [Conflict](../lexipedia/Conflict.md) and [War](../lexipedia/War.md).
