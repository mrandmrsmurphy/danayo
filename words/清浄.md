## Notes
- [清](../characters/清.md) "clear, pure" + [浄](../characters/浄.md) "clean" — "pure and clean." Their own `stand_in` values: 清's is [[清潔]]; 浄's is [[乾浄]]; none points here, so 清浄 is an independent compound.

清浄 is pure and clean in the religious sense: free of defilement. It is a neologism for Dan'a'yo's purposes, taken from Japanese しょうじょう, which Wiktionary glosses "clean and pure." Mandarin qīngjìng and Cantonese cing1 zing6 exist but mean "peaceful, quiet," so those fields give the ordinary readings and not the Dan'a'yo sense; Korean 청정 is the reading of the two characters. It takes the term over from Buddhism, in line with the vault's practice of adopting other religions' words.

**Hebrew**: it best translates *ṭāhôr* (טָהוֹר) "clean," the ritual cleanness of Leviticus 11–15, whose opposite is [[不浄]] (*ṭāmēʾ*). For physical cleanliness use [[清潔]] and [[乾浄]].
