## Notes
- [脅](../characters/脅.md) "coerce, threaten" + [迫](../characters/迫.md) "force, compel" — no stand-in relationship applies (脅's own stand-in is [[脅威]]; 迫's own is [[強迫]]).

Mandarin xiépò, Cantonese hip3 baak1, Japanese きょうはく (kyōhaku), and Korean 협박 (hyeopbak) are all real, standard everyday words for "to force, coerce, intimidate" — especially common in legal/criminal contexts (脅迫罪/협박죄, "the crime of intimidation"). `vietnamese: hiếp bách` is the compositional reading; not independently verified as an everyday attested term. Closer in sense to physically/legally coercing someone into an action than to [[威脅]], which leans toward the act of threatening itself. `kwin` is false per the AND-rule: 脅 is individually `kwin: false` (despite 迫 being `true`). No homophones (注音 ㄏㄝㄆㄅㄚㄎ unique among both words and characters).
