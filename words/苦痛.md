## Notes
- [苦 (char)](../characters/苦%20(char).md) "suffering, bitter" is separately legitimized by [[苦]] itself. [痛](../characters/痛.md) "torment, pain" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 痛 cannot appear independently outside this compound. No cranberry: transitivity fails since 苦's own `stand_in` doesn't point here.

苦痛 combines 苦 ("suffering") with 痛 ("pain") — "agony, pain, suffering."

Mandarin kǔtòng, cantonese fu2tung3 (space removed), japanese くつう (kutsuu), and korean 고통 (gotong, an extremely common everyday word) were all already correct, fully compositional readings.
