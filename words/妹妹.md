## Notes
- [妹](../characters/妹.md) "younger sister," doubled.

The reduplication here is grammatical, not literal — 妹妹 doesn't mean "two younger sisters," it's the affectionate, everyday-register diminutive of plain 妹, the same way a name might be doubled into a pet name. Mandarin mèimei reduplicates the same way and is the standard everyday word for "little sister," used both as a term of address and third-person reference. Cantonese mui6-4 mui6-2 shows genuine changed-tone reduplication — the stored citation itself documents the tone shift away from the character's own plain tone 6 (妹's own citation) on both syllables, a well-attested Cantonese pattern for kinship diminutives and terms of endearment, not a transcription artifact.

Japanese いもうと (imōto) and Korean 여동생 (yeodongsaeng) are not reduplicated at all — both are real, distinct native/compound words for "younger sister" unrelated in form to the Chinese-style doubling, matching the established convention that reduplicated words hold each language's own genuine idiom rather than a fabricated doubled reading. `vietnamese` is left blank: the real Vietnamese word for "younger sister" is the native compound em gái, not a Sino-Vietnamese reading of 妹妹 itself.
