## Notes
- [無 (char)](../characters/無%20(char).md) "lack" + [聊 (char)](../characters/聊%20(char).md) "to chat" — literally "nothing to chat about," i.e. boring, tedious, dull.

無聊 is a genuinely pan-CJKV word, attested with consistent meaning across all five languages — rare for a compound this idiomatic. Mandarin wúliáo, Cantonese mou4 liu4, and Vietnamese vô liêu are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese ぶりょう (buryō) uses 無's secondary on'yomi BU rather than the primary MU — a legitimate branch selection, confirmed via Wiktionary as the real attested reading, meaning "ennui, tedium" (noun) or "bored" (adjectival). Korean 무료 (muryo) carries the same "bored, tedious" sense.

Note Japanese also has an unrelated homophone-adjacent word 無料 (muryō, "free of charge") written with a different second character (料, not 聊) — easy to confuse in transliteration but etymologically and semantically unconnected. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 므럇 diverges from Sino-Korean 무료 in both syllables. No homophones (注音 ㄇㄜㄌ⼘ㄨ unique among both words and characters).
