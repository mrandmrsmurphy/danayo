## Notes
- [脛](../characters/脛.md) "shinbone" + [骨 (char)](../characters/骨%20(char).md) "bone" — this word is the viable form of [脛](../characters/脛.md): the character's meaning (shinbone; tibia) is identical, but 脛 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 脛骨 is its `stand_in` compound.

脛骨 (tibia) is the larger, weight-bearing of the two lower-leg bones, running from the knee to the ankle — the smaller fibula (腓骨) runs alongside it but bears comparatively little weight. The classical idiom 傷筋動骨 ("to injure the sinew, disturb the bone") and everyday phrases about shin injuries reflect the tibia's exposed, easily-bruised position just under the skin.

Japanese けいこつ and Korean 경골 are both standard compositional Sino-readings. Vietnamese hĩnh cốt is attested, though modern Vietnamese medical terminology more commonly uses the native xương chày. No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄝㄫㄍㄛㄊ unique across both words and characters).
