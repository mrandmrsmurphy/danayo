## Notes
- [雍](../characters/雍.md) + [州 (char)](../characters/州%20(char).md) — Yong Province, the ninth of the [[九州]]

雍州 was the northwestern province, which the *Tribute of Yu* bounds between the Black River and the West River (黑水西河惟雍州) — roughly modern Shaanxi, Gansu, and parts of Ningxia — the Wei River valley and the loess plateau. It was the homeland of the state of Qin (秦), the state that ultimately conquered all other states to unify China in 221 BC. The Wei River valley around modern Xi'an (長安) became the imperial heartland of the Qin and Han dynasties; 雍州 thus transitions in Chinese history from a frontier zone at the edge of the civilized world to the seat of empire. The westernmost of the nine provinces, it also formed the gateway to Central Asia and the Silk Road.

雍's own `stand_in` is 雍雍, so this word is not its legitimizer.  The word names the province 雍州 as listed among the 九州.  Mandarin yōngzhōu, Cantonese jung1 zau1, Japanese ようしゅう, Korean 옹주 and Vietnamese Ung Châu are the readings of that name.  `kwin` is false because the Dan'a'yo 옹줏 differs from the Korean 옹주.
