## Notes
- [弾](../characters/弾.md) "bullet; to flick" + [指](../characters/指.md) "finger" — no stand-in relationship applies (弾's own stand-in is [[弾丸]]; 指's is [[手指]]).

弾指 is "to snap or flick the fingers", and from that, "in an instant, in the blink of an eye". It is an old word with a Buddhist background: the snap of the fingers, borrowed from an Indian custom, marks a warning, delight or assent, and 弾指 also names a very short unit of time. Dan'a'yo uses it as the stem of the phonetic term 弾指 + 子音, "flap consonant": the tongue-tip flicks once against the alveolar ridge in a single instantaneous contact, as against the repeated contact of a trill ([[振動音]]). The Dan'a'yo consonant ㄌ, /l ~ ɾ/, is the case in point.

The source-language phonetic terms already use 弾 for the same idea: Mandarin 弹音, Japanese 弾き音 and Korean 탄설음 all name the flap or tap, so 弾指 keeps that shared element and adds the image of a single snap.

Mandarin tánzhǐ and Japanese だんし (danshi) both have the finger-snap and instant senses; Japanese also uses 弾指 for "exclusion, rejection". Cantonese is taan4 zi2 (daan6 zi2 is also recorded), and Vietnamese đàn chỉ is the attested Sino-Vietnamese form, glossed as snapping the fingers and, figuratively, a very short time or something done with ease. Korean 탄지 is the compositional reading of 弾's 탄 and 指's 지 and was not separately verified in a Korean dictionary. Dan'a'yo's own stored reading of 弾 is dan (단) for all of its senses, including the "to flick" sense that Mandarin reads tán, so the Dan'a'yo reading of the word is danjiǝ. No homophones (注音 ㄉㄚㄋㄐㄧㄜ unique across both words and characters).
