## Notes
- [朝 (char)](../characters/朝%20(char).md) "morning; imperial" + [廷](../characters/廷.md) "imperial court"
- This word is the viable form of [廷](../characters/廷.md): the character's meaning (imperial court) is identical, but 廷 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 朝廷 is its `stand_in` compound.

朝廷 is the court as the seat of government: the sovereign and the ministers and officials who attend and act for him. 朝 here is the "court" or "audience" sense (read cháo, ちょう, 조), from holding court in the morning, not the "morning" reading zhāo; 廷 is the court or hall in which affairs are heard. Mandarin cháotíng, Cantonese ciu4 ting4, Japanese ちょうてい (chōtei), Korean 조정 (jeongjeong, a homophone of 調整, "adjustment") and Vietnamese triều đình are the same word, used for imperial and royal courts alike (朝廷命官, "an official appointed by the court").

Dan'a'yo-specific sense: the shared one. The court as government is the sense kept here, contrasted with [[宮廷]] (the palace as place and household) and [[王朝]] (the dynasty or reign as a span of time).
