## Notes
- [間](../characters/間.md) "between" + [諜](../characters/諜.md) "spy"
- This word is the viable form of [諜](../characters/諜.md): the character's meaning (spy) is identical, but 諜 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 間諜 is its `stand_in` compound.

諜 is to spy, gather secret intelligence, and 間 is "gap, interval," here in its old sense of slipping into the gaps of the enemy's affairs: the spy is the one who gets in between. In Sunzi's chapter on the use of spies (用間) the word 間 itself is the term for a spy, and the compound 間諜 joins the two. It is a general word for a person doing espionage, mainly for a foreign or hostile state, and it carries the sense of treachery (as in the charge of 間諜罪, spying), where the more neutral modern term for an intelligence officer is a different word.

The Events page places it as the nearest match for Rosenfelder's "agent," which it can fill only by narrowing: 間諜 is a spy or intelligence operative, not "one who acts on another's behalf." Its natural home is the Espionage section of [Conflict](../lexipedia/Conflict.md), where it now stands beside the verbs 偵 and 窺, so the word is listed on both pages. The readings are Mandarin jiàndié, Cantonese gaan3 dip6, Japanese kanchō (かんちょう), Korean 간첩 and Sino-Vietnamese gián điệp (the usual word for a spy in Vietnamese). Dan'a'yo takes the sense "spy, secret agent."
