## Notes
- [犬 (char)](../characters/犬%20(char).md) "dog" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes 犬(char) as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

犬 is "dog" — a canonical 象形 pictograph depicting the side view of a dog, one of the earliest and most basic pictographic characters in the sinographic system, and one of the twelve zodiac animals (戌). As a Swadesh basic-vocabulary word, 犬 names one of the most culturally universal domesticated animals across the CJKV sphere; in compounds it forms 犬吠 (barking) and serves as the semantic radical (部首 094) in a large family of characters denoting animals, hunting, and animal-related concepts.

Mandarin 犬 (quǎn) is a literary/formal register word, confined mostly to compounds (忠犬, "loyal dog") and set phrases; everyday spoken Mandarin uses 狗 (gǒu) instead. Cantonese patterns the same way, with 狗 (gau2) as the everyday word. Japanese has a native/Sino-Japanese split typical of basic-vocabulary animal terms: native いぬ (inu) is the everyday word, while the on'yomi けん (ken) is confined to formal or compound usage (番犬, banken, "guard dog"). Korean 견 (gyeon) is likewise a formal, literary reading, with native 개 (gae) used in everyday speech. Vietnamese khuyển (Hán Việt) is a literary/formal reading found in compounds like khuyển mã ("dogs and horses," a self-deprecating term for loyal service), with the everyday native word being chó.

kwin is false: Dan'a'yo 퀀 does not match Korean 견. 犬 reads 퀀 (kwen/ㄎ⼔ㄋ) in Dan'a'yo — MC 溪母 (kʰ, aspirated velar stop) + 先合韻 (wen, with a labial glide). Both Dan'a'yo and Korean preserve the aspirated velar initial (ㅋ/ㅋ), but Dan'a'yo additionally preserves the MC labial glide of 先合韻 as -w- (퀀), which Sino-Korean drops (견, without the glide).
