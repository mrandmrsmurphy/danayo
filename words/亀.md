## Notes
- [亀 (char)](../characters/亀%20(char).md) "turtle"

亀 is 象形, depicting a turtle viewed from above — shell, head, tail, and four legs all visible. Dan'a'yo uses the Japanese shinjitai form 亀 rather than either the traditional 龜 or Simplified Chinese 龟, since both of those are visually distinctive enough to read as unfamiliar to audiences outside their own writing tradition — one of the rare cases where Dan'a'yo departs from Simplified Chinese specifically in favor of shinjitai.

Mandarin guī, Cantonese gwai1, and Japanese き/きゅう/きん (ki/kyū/kin, on'yomi) alongside native かめ (kame, the everyday word for "turtle") all cover the core sense. Korean 구 (gu) is the Sino-Korean reading here; 龜 also carries a separate, rarer Sino-Korean reading 균 (gyun), but only in the unrelated sense "crack, fissure" (龜裂, gyunyeol) — not used for this word, which is specifically about the animal. Vietnamese quy (also spelled qui in older orthography) is the standard reading, familiar from linh quy ("sacred turtle") and the legend of Kim Quy, the golden turtle who gave Emperor Lê Lợi his sword.
