## Notes
- [武 (char)](../characters/武%20(char).md) "military" + [士 (char)](../characters/士%20(char).md) "scholar" + [道 (char)](../characters/道%20(char).md) "way"

武士道 is bushido, the way of the warrior: the ethical and moral code of the samurai, a code of attitudes, behaviour and lifestyle. English Wikipedia dates its origins to the Kamakura period and its formalization to the Edo period (1603–1868), and compares it loosely with European chivalry, with major differences. English Wiktionary lists the word in Mandarin (wǔshìdào), Japanese (ぶしどう) and Korean (무사도), all glossed as the ethical code of the samurai, and Vietnamese võ sĩ đạo, which also colloquially means a samurai or bushi. Cantonese mou5 si6 dou6 matches. Its Japanese synonyms there are 士道 and 武士の道.

It is built from [[武士]], warrior, and the [[道]] of a discipline, the same ending as the "ways" of [[武道]], martial arts. 士道 is a Japanese compound but is not a Dan'a'yo word; the three characters are listed individually.
