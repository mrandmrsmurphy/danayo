## Notes
- [軽 (char)](../characters/軽%20(char).md) "light" + [歌](../characters/歌.md) "song" + [劇 (char)](../characters/劇%20(char).md) "drama" — light opera; a musical-dramatic form distinguished from grand opera by its comic or romantic tone, spoken dialogue, and accessible subject matter.

The term is a calque of German *Operette* and French *opérette*, built from 軽 "light" and the word for opera; when and where it was first coined was not verified here. The form reached East Asia via two routes: the European operetta tradition (Johann Strauss II, Franz Lehár, Offenbach) and an indigenous Japanese light theatrical tradition that absorbed Western musical elements. The distinction from opera proper (歌劇) turns on register and intent — 軽歌劇 entertains rather than elevates, favoring romantic comedy and social satire over tragic grandeur.

The 宝塚歌劇団 (Takarazuka Revue), founded in 1914, shaped Japanese popular musical theatre in ways that diverged sharply from Western operetta conventions: an all-female cast, pastel aesthetics, and an audience culture unlike anything in Vienna or Paris. Korean 경가극 follows the same structural logic. Vietnamese *ca kịch* is a looser equivalent covering various musical-dramatic fusions, though it lacks the precise contrast with grand opera that 軽歌劇 carries.

None of the three characters is legitimized here: 軽's `stand_in` is itself, 歌's is 歌曲 and 劇's is itself.  Mandarin qīnggējù, Cantonese hing1 go1 kek6, Japanese けいかげき and Korean 경가극 are the same compound, and the alias 輕歌劇 is the traditional form.  `kwin` is false because the Dan'a'yo 킹그걱 differs from the Korean 경가극.  The 注音 needs no syllable dot, and no other word shares it.
