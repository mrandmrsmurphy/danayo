## Notes
- [攀](../characters/攀.md) "climb" + [縁](../characters/縁.md) "fate; edge"
- This word is the viable form of [攀](../characters/攀.md): the character's meaning (to climb) is identical, but 攀 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 攀縁 is its `stand_in` compound.

攀 is to climb by grasping, pulling oneself up hand over hand, and 縁 here keeps its older sense of "edge, something to hold onto" rather than the later "fate, affinity." So 攀縁 is literally climbing by catching hold of what is there: the way a vine climbs a trellis, or a person scrambles up a rock face. In this sense it appears in botany (climbing plants, 攀縁植物) and in the literary figure of "clinging to a patron to rise," which carries a faintly pejorative sense of social climbing (攀縁附貴 "cling to the powerful").

It also has a Buddhist use for the mind clinging to an object of perception or desire (攀縁心), the term for restless grasping that meditation aims to quiet. The readings are Mandarin pānyuán, Cantonese paan1 jyun4 and Korean 반연; the Japanese form (はんえん) is not recorded in this page's frontmatter. Dan'a'yo keeps the core sense "climb, scramble up, cling to for support," and treats the social-climbing and Buddhist senses as figurative extensions of it. It is placed under [Directions](../lexipedia/Directions.md).
