## Notes
- [太 (char)](../characters/太%20(char).md) "great" + [陰 (char)](../characters/陰%20(char).md) "moon, yin" + [太 (char)](../characters/太%20(char).md) "great" + [陽 (char)](../characters/陽%20(char).md) "sun, yang" + [暦 (char)](../characters/暦%20(char).md) "calendar"

太陰太陽暦 names a calendar system that tracks months by the moon's phases (like [[太陰暦]] alone) while periodically inserting leap months to keep the year synchronized with the solar cycle and the seasons (like [[太陽暦]] alone) — the two systems combined rather than either one in isolation. Months average roughly 29.5 days (one synodic lunar month), but a purely lunar 12-month year runs about 11 days short of the solar year, so a leap month is inserted roughly every third year (traditionally 7 times per 19-year cycle, the Metonic cycle) to prevent the calendar from drifting away from the agricultural seasons over time.

This is the structural basis of the traditional Chinese calendar (農暦/农历), the traditional Korean calendar, and the pre-1873 Japanese calendar, as well as the Hebrew and several other historical calendar systems worldwide — distinguished from a purely lunar calendar like the Islamic Hijri calendar (which lets months drift freely through the seasons) and a purely solar calendar like the Gregorian calendar (which ignores the moon's phase entirely).

Japanese たいいんたいようれき and Korean 태음태양력 are both standard compositional Sino-readings. Vietnamese âm dương lịch ("yin-yang calendar") is the real, standard term — dropping both instances of 太, the same simplification already seen in [[太陰暦]]'s own real-world Vietnamese âm lịch. No specific HSK level applies to a term this specialized. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄚㄧㄧㄇㄊㄚㄧ⼘ㄫㄌㄝㄎ unique across both words and characters).
