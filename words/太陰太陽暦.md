## Notes
- [太 (char)](../characters/太%20(char).md) "great" + [陰 (char)](../characters/陰%20(char).md) "moon, yin" + [太 (char)](../characters/太%20(char).md) "great" + [陽 (char)](../characters/陽%20(char).md) "sun, yang" + [暦 (char)](../characters/暦%20(char).md) "calendar"

太陰太陽暦 is a lunisolar calendar: one that follows the moon's monthly cycle and is also kept in step with the solar year by adding leap months. English Wiktionary glosses Japanese たいいんたいようれき as "lunisolar calendar", and lists the Chinese equivalent as 陰陽曆, "lunisolar calendar" (Mandarin yīnyánglì). It has no Korean or Vietnamese entry for these forms, so Korean 태음태양력 here is built from the characters.

English Wikipedia explains the reconciliation: a synodic month is about 29.5306 days and a tropical year about 365.2422 days, so a year is about 12.37 months, and a twelve-month lunar year falls about 11 days short of the solar year. A leap month is therefore needed every two to three years, and several lunisolar calendars use 7 leap months in every 19-year Metonic cycle. It sets lunisolar calendars against purely lunar calendars such as the Islamic calendar, which have no such rule.

The two halves are the [[太陰暦]], the lunar calendar, and the [[太陽暦]], the solar calendar.
