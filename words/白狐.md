## Notes
- [白 (char)](../characters/白%20(char).md) "white" + [狐](../characters/狐.md) "fox" — literally "white fox."

白狐 covers both the zoological sense (arctic fox, or a white-coated regional fox) and, especially in Japanese, a mythological one: びゃっこ (byakko) is the white fox associated with Inari, the Shinto deity of rice, fertility, and prosperity — depicted as the deity's messenger and often carved as a shrine guardian statue. Mandarin báihú and Cantonese baak6 wu4 are straightforward, primarily zoological. Korean 백호 is worth a note: it is also the Korean reading of the unrelated 白虎 ("white tiger," one of the Four Symbols of Chinese constellations) — the two are indistinguishable in Korean speech, disambiguated only by context or hanja. Vietnamese bạch hồ is compositional and used in similar fashion to the Mandarin/Cantonese sense.
