## Notes
- [衷](../characters/衷.md) "inmost" + [情](../characters/情.md) "feelings"
- This word is the viable form of [衷](../characters/衷.md): the character's meaning (innermost, sincere) is identical, but 衷 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 衷情 is its `stand_in` compound.

衷 is built from 衣 "clothing" around 中 "middle," and was first the undergarment worn next to the skin, hence what is innermost; from there it came to mean one's inner heart. 情 is "feeling," so 衷情 is the feeling in one's inner heart, the sincere sentiment one does not usually show. It is a literary, warm-register word used in set phrases such as 一訴衷情 "to pour out one's heart" and 剖白衷情 "to lay one's feelings bare," and in love poetry and letters, where it names the feeling confided to someone.

This sets it apart from the plainer [情況](情況.md) and [情態](情態.md), which describe circumstances and states of affairs, and from [情欲](情欲.md) "desire," since 衷情 has no sensual tone. The readings are Mandarin zhōngqíng, Cantonese zung1 cing4 and Korean 충정. The Japanese form (ちゅうじょう, a rare literary word) is not recorded here, so that field is left blank. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense "heartfelt emotion, inner feelings" with no divergence, placed under [Emotions](../lexipedia/Emotions.md).
