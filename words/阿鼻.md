## Notes
- [阿 (char)](../characters/阿%20(char).md) "ah" (a Buddhist transliteration syllable) + [鼻 (char)](../characters/鼻%20(char).md) "nose" (likewise transliterative here, unrelated to its literal meaning)

阿鼻 is a Chinese Buddhist transliteration of Sanskrit Avīci ("without interval/interruption"), the lowest and most tormented of the eight hot hells, where suffering is unceasing. Mandarin 阿鼻 (ābí), Cantonese 阿鼻 (aa3bei6), Japanese 阿鼻 (あび), and Sino-Vietnamese A Tỳ are all attested standalone forms, though the fuller compound 阿鼻地獄/아비지옥/địa ngục A Tỳ ("Avīci hell") is more common in everyday religious usage across all four languages. 阿鼻 also appears bound in the chengyu [[阿鼻叫喚]] ("agonized cries in the midst of tragedy," from the wailing said to fill this hell).

Korean 아비 is a coincidental homograph with the unrelated native word 아비 ("father," a lowered/informal register term) — a real-Korean-only collision, not a Dan'a'yo homophone, since the two concepts are written with entirely different characters/no characters at all.
