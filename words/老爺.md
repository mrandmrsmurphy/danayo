## Notes
- [老 (char)](../characters/老%20(char).md) "old person, age" + [爺](../characters/爺.md) "father, grandfather" — 爺's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, but 老's own is [[老]] itself, so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply; 老爺 legitimizes 爺 alone. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 老 is individually `kwin: false`.

老爺 means grandfather, or a respectful term for a master or old gentleman.

Mandarin lǎoye, Cantonese lou5 je4, Japanese ろうや (rōya), and Korean 노야 (noya) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 老爺 is itself a common attested word for "master, elder" in all four languages. Vietnamese lão gia is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading and itself the standard Vietnamese term. **Fixed a real bug**: `羅馬字`/`諺文` had lyau'ya/럇야 (the same 老-syllable mismatch found on [[老人学]] and [[老子]]) — `注音` (ㄌㄚㄨ⼘) had stayed correct throughout. No homophones.
