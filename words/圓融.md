## Notes
- [圓 (char)](../characters/圓%20(char).md) "round, circle" + [融 (char)](../characters/融%20(char).md) "melt, blend, harmonize" — literally "round and blended." No stand-in relationship applies (both characters' own `stand_in` point to themselves).

圓融 traces to a genuine Buddhist philosophical term (Tendai and Huayan schools): each thing keeping its own distinct position while interfusing with everything else without obstruction — a state of complete, unobstructed harmony (六相圓融, "the six aspects in perfect interfusion"). By extension, in ordinary usage it describes a person who moves through social situations with that same quality: tactful, diplomatically smooth, able to harmonize competing interests without friction — this vault's closest word for Rosenfelder's "tact." Distinct from the more negative [[圓滑]] below, which shares the same "round" image but reads as glib or self-servingly slick rather than genuinely tactful.

Mandarin yuánróng, Cantonese jyun4 jung4, Japanese えんゆう, Korean 원융, and Vietnamese viên dung are all standard, fully compositional readings. `kwin` is true: both 圓 and 融 are individually `kwin: true`. No homophones (注音 ⼔ㄋ⼜ㄫ unique across both words and characters).
