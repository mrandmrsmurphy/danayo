## Notes
- [[召喚]] "to summon" + [状](../characters/状.md) "document, certificate, form" (the bureaucratic-document register, distinct from 状's more common "shape, form" sense already seen in [[形状]]) — literally "a summoning document." `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: `characters/召.md` is `kwin: false`, `characters/喚 (char).md` is `kwin: true`, `characters/状.md` is `kwin: false` → compound `false`.

召喚状 means "summons, subpoena" — the legal document ordering someone to appear. Japanese しょうかんじょう (shōkanjō) and Korean 소환장 (sohwanjang) are both real, standard legal terms for exactly this document.

Mandarin zhàohuànzhuàng and Cantonese ziu6 wun6 zong6 are compositional; modern Mandarin/Taiwan legal practice more often uses 傳票 (chuánpiào) or 拘票 for this specific document instead — but Dan'a'yo reserves 伝票/傳票 for its own distinct Japanese-derived business sense ("voucher, expense slip," not yet its own word page), so 召喚状 is used here instead of following Mandarin's own preferred term, to avoid a collision between two genuinely different real-world senses of a similar-looking compound.

Vietnamese triệu hoán trạng is the compositional Hán Việt reading, not independently confirmed as a standard term; living Vietnamese instead uses the native phrase trát đòi hầu tòa ("a warrant demanding a court appearance") for this specific document.
