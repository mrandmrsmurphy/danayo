## Notes
- [指](../characters/指.md) "to point at; to denote" + [小 (char)](../characters/小%20(char).md) "small" + [詞](../characters/詞.md) "word; the basic unit of speech"

指小詞 is the grammatical term for the diminutive: a form that denotes something small, young or dear. Chinese linguistics uses 指小詞 itself (Wiktionary lists 小稱詞 as a synonym), and Japanese has the same term as 指小辞 (shishōji). The three characters spell the definition: a word that *points to* smallness. In Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese the fields hold compositional readings of the three characters; Japanese itself writes the final element 辞, so ししょうし is the reading of this spelling, not an attested one.

In Dan'a'yo the diminutive is made by reduplication: a reduplicated noun denotes the small or dear version of the thing, as in 女児女児 "little daughter" from [[女児]], and the pattern is common in kinship and intimate terms (see 文法 - 05形態). The term names the resulting form, not the process: it is the thing a speaker produces, in the way that [[迂回語]] names the expression produced by circumlocution. The related category of degree is [[最高級]], the superlative, which reduplicated adjectives express.
