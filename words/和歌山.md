## Notes
- [和](../characters/和.md) "peaceful" + [歌](../characters/歌.md) "song" + [山](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain"

和歌山 is Wakayama, written "peaceful song mountain". The castle town, originally written 若山, was rewritten in the Edo period with 和歌 ("waka poem"), after the nearby bay of Waka-no-ura (from memory, not re-checked). The reading わかやま is native: わか is the 和歌 of the bay and やま the "mountain".

Wakayama occupies the south-west of the Kii Peninsula, with its capital Wakayama, the seat of the Kii Tokugawa branch. It holds the Shingon centre Kōyasan and the pilgrimage routes of Kumano Kodō, and grows mandarin oranges and ume plums.

In Dan'a'yo 和歌山 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 와카야마 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
