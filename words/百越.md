## Notes
- [百 (char)](../characters/百%20(char).md) "hundred" + [越 (char)](../characters/越%20(char).md) "exceed"
- A historical term for the diverse Yue peoples of southern China in the first millennium BC through first millennium AD.

百越 (Baiyue, "the Hundred Yue") is the old collective name for the many peoples who lived south of the Yangzi in the south and southeast of what is now China and in northern Vietnam, each with its own name and polity (Wu, Yue, Minyue, Nanyue, Ouyue, Luoyue and others). The name occurs in the 呂氏春秋 (卷二十, 恃君覽), which places 百越之際 ("the borders of the Hundred Yue") south of the Yang and Han rivers; later it was used in Han and Tang sources for the whole southern region. Mandarin Bǎi Yuè, Cantonese baak3 jyut6, Japanese ひゃくえつ (hyakuetsu), Korean 백월 (baegwol) and Vietnamese Bách Việt are the same name; in Vietnam it is part of the background of the name "Việt" and of the national origin story.

In Dan'a'yo it is a historical term and place-region name, not a modern ethnic category, and is housed in Geography beside the southern provinces. It is plural by nature ("hundred" means "many"), as 百姓 ("the hundred surnames") is for the people at large.
