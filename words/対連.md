## Notes
- [対](../characters/対.md) "face; pair with" + [連 (char)](../characters/連%20(char).md) "connect; link" — no stand-in relationship applies (対's own `stand_in` is [[反対]], 連's is itself).

対連 (duilian) is the antithetical couplet — a pair of lines matching in structure, tone pattern, and often meaning, central to Chinese poetry and calligraphy (famously displayed as 春聯 spring-festival couplets on doorframes). The real, most common written form of this word is actually 對聯/对联, using [[連]]'s close relative 聯 (a true distinct character, not a mere script variant, despite being registered as an alias here) — 聯 and 連 diverge in Cantonese specifically (聯 is lyun4, 連 is lin4, confirmed via CantoDict/Wiktionary), even though they share the same Mandarin reading (lián) and are near-identical in Korean/Japanese. Per this vault's standing convention (as with [[審査]]/査-vs-查), the `cantonese` field here follows 連's own canonical Dan'a'yo-derivation reading (lin4) rather than the real everyday 聯-based form (lyun4) that most Cantonese speakers would actually use for 對聯.

Vietnamese đối liên (Wiktionary-confirmed, matching 連's own "liên" exactly) and Japanese たいれん are both standard readings for this word. No specific HSK level number is confirmed (Wiktionary gives no HSK level for 對聯/对联). No homophones (注音 ㄉㄛㄧㄌ⼶ㄋ unique across both words and characters).
