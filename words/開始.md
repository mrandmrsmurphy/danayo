## Notes
- [開](../characters/開.md) "open" + [始](../characters/始.md) "begin"

開始 means to begin or start, combining "open" and "begin" into the general-purpose verb for commencing an action or process. Mandarin 開始 (kāishǐ) and Cantonese 開始 (hoi1ci2) are both everyday, high-frequency verbs used for starting essentially anything — a meeting, a story, a machine. Sino-Vietnamese khai thuỷ preserves the same compositional reading but is a comparatively formal/literary register; everyday Vietnamese more often uses the native-feeling khởi sự ("to start an undertaking") or bắt đầu for this meaning.

Japanese 開始 (かいし) and Korean 개시 (gaesi) both skew more formal/technical than their Chinese counterparts — used for the formal commencement of an event, service, or proceeding (試合開始 "start of the match"; 개시 시간 "opening time" for a business) — rather than as the default everyday verb for "start," a role native/verbal forms (始める hajimeru; 시작하다 sijakhada, itself built on 始's own character) fill instead.
