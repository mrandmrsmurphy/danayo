## Notes
- [玄 (char)](../characters/玄%20(char).md) "deep; profound; mysterious" + [米 (char)](../characters/米%20(char).md) "rice (uncooked)"

玄米 names brown rice — unpolished rice with the bran layer still intact — literally "dark/mysterious rice," a reference to the darker, tan-brown color the bran gives the grain compared to white rice (白米), from which it is otherwise distinguished only by milling. It contrasts nutritionally and gastronomically with polished white rice throughout the region: retaining the bran and germ gives it more fiber and nutrients at the cost of a chewier texture and shorter shelf life.

The compound is most at home in Japanese, where げんまい (genmai) is the everyday word for brown rice, familiar from products like 玄米茶 (genmaicha, roasted brown rice green tea). Sino-Korean 현미 is used in the same sense. Standard Mandarin, however, does not use 玄米 as its everyday term — it instead prefers native compounds like 糙米 (cāomǐ) or 次米, both meaning the same "brown rice" but built from unrelated characters ("rough/coarse rice") rather than from 玄; 玄米's own literal Mandarin reading (xuánmǐ) is understood but not the term in common use. No Sino-Vietnamese reading is attested for the compound.
