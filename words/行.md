## Notes
- [行 (char)](../characters/行%20(char).md) "attend; go to" — stand-in for [[行 (char)|行]], which cannot appear independently

行 is 指事: a crossroads, two paths crossing — "to walk, to travel." One of the most productive characters in the corpus (40th most-used in Classical Chinese), it anchors both plain motion vocabulary (旅行 "travel," 飛行 "fly, flight," 通行 "pass through") and a wide abstract-action layer built on "to carry out, to conduct" (行動 "act, action," 執行 "execute, perform," 施行 "carry out [a law/policy]"). The astronomical extension 行星 ("planet," literally "a wandering/traveling star," paralleling the etymology of the English word "planet" itself from Greek "wanderer") shows the same motion-image generalizing independently into two unrelated language families' scientific vocabulary. 銀行 ("bank," literally "silver house/firm") and 五行 (Wu Xing, the Five Phases) both draw on a separate historical sense of 行 as "a row, a business firm, a category" — the same character carrying the etymologically distinct "row/business" reading that motivates several of its own multi-way homophone/Vietnamese-reading splits below.

**Content note**: the character's own `vietnamese` field stores an unusually wide 6-item set (hàng, hành, hãng, hăng, hạnh, ngành) — genuinely reflecting real polysemy rather than pure contamination, since 行 legitimately covers several distinct senses across its own tonal readings in Chinese itself (xíng "to go/act" vs. háng "a row, a firm"). hành was picked here as the reading matching this word's own "attend, go to" gloss (hành động "action," hành trình "journey"); hàng/ngành belong to the row/business/field senses instead. Japanese shows the same split pattern: こう (KOU, used here — 旅行 ryokō, 銀行 ginkō) covers the motion/institution senses, while ぎょう (GYOU) is reserved for a more religious/ceremonial register (修行 shugyō "ascetic training," 行事 gyōji "a ceremony, an event") and あん (AN) survives only in a handful of fixed terms (行灯 andon, "a paper lantern") — no native 訓読み exists at all (`japanese_native: ø`), an unusual gap for a character this fundamental and productive. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 항 (hang, from Middle Chinese 匣母 ɣ + 唐開韻 ɑŋ) diverges from Sino-Korean 행 in the vowel.

**Real homophone group found**: `注音 ㄏㄚㄫ`/`항` is also carried by two other existing standalone words, [[桁]] ("beam," already listed as one of 行's own Derived Characters — a genuine phonetic descendant) and [[項]] ("nape, item"). Added the full mutual callout across all three pages.
