## Notes
- [痩](../characters/痩%20(char).md) "thin" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 痩 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

痩 means thin or emaciated — 形声, combining semantic [[Radical 104|疒]] ("sickness") with phonetic 叟, tying leanness conceptually to wasting illness rather than to a neutral body type. As a stative in Dan'a'yo, 痩 functions as a predicate or modifier (人痩 "the person is thin"), contrasting with 肥/胖-type "fat" vocabulary.

Mandarin 瘦 (shòu) and Cantonese 瘦 (sau3) both use the character as a general stative adjective for thinness, in compounds like 瘦肉 (shòuròu/sau3 juk6, "lean meat") and 瘦身 (shòushēn/sau3 san1, "to slim down"). Korean 수 (瘦) appears mainly in Sino-Korean compounds rather than everyday speech, where native 여위다/마르다 ("to become thin/gaunt") are preferred. Japanese treats the concept primarily verbally rather than adjectivally: kun'yomi 痩せる (yaseru) means "to become thin, to lose weight," and the on'yomi そう (sou) surfaces mainly in compounds such as 痩身 (soushin, "slimming"), rather than as a standalone descriptive word — a divergence from the stative usage Dan'a'yo inherits from Chinese.

No homophones beyond [[手]] "hand" and [[銹]] "rust" — all three share 注音 ㄙ⼜ / 羅馬字 syu exactly.
