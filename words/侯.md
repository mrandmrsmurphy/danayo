## Notes
- [侯 (char)](../characters/侯%20(char).md) "marquis"

侯 is 象形, originally depicting a target for archery (an arrow-riddled cloth or hide target); the marquis rank is thought to derive from a nobleman's role presiding over ceremonial archery contests. It names the second-highest of the five traditional Chinese noble ranks (公侯伯子男, "duke, marquis, count, viscount, baron"), and survives in the modern sense "aristocrat, nobleman" more generally, as well as in family names.

Mandarin hóu, Cantonese hau4, and Japanese こう (kō, on'yomi, largely confined to historical/literary contexts and Chinese-derived titles) all preserve the sense. Korean 후 (hu) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 제후 (jehu, "feudal lord, vassal ruler"). Vietnamese hầu is the Hán Việt reading, attested in hầu tước (侯爵, "marquis") — the character page's other stored candidate, hậu, belongs to unrelated characters (后, "empress," or 後, "after") and was not used here.

This word is part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read hou/홋/ㄏㄛㄨ): [[厚]] ("thick") and [[吼]] ("roar"), both still awaiting their own turn in this sweep — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed on each when it comes up.
