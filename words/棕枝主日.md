## Notes
- [[棕枝]] "palm tree" + [[主日]] "Lord's Day" — each half is itself a word in the vault.
- Individual characters: [棕](../characters/棕.md) + [[枝]] + [主](../characters/主.md) + [日 (char)](../characters/日%20(char).md)
- The Mandarin and Cantonese readings reflect the established Chinese form 棕枝主日 (*zōngzhī zhǔrì*) rather than the literal 棕櫚主日 reading (*zōnglǚ zhǔrì*).

Palm Sunday is the Sunday before Easter, commemorating Jesus's entry into Jerusalem when crowds spread palm branches on the road before him (John 12:13). The event is recorded in all four Gospels, each noting the branches slightly differently — palms (φοῖνιξ) in John, cut branches (κλάδοι) in Matthew, leafy branches (στοιβάδες) in Mark, and just clothing in Luke — giving rise to minor variation in the holiday's name across traditions.

The Chinese term 棕枝主日 / 棕櫚主日 ("palm branch / palm tree Lord's Day") is the standard across Chinese-speaking Christian communities. Korean 종려주일 (棕櫚主日) uses 棕櫚 (종려) for the palm and 주일 for the Lord's Day. Japanese churches differ by tradition: the Catholic name is 枝の主日 (*eda no shujitsu*, "Branch Sunday"), while Protestant usage has 棕櫚の主日 (しゅろのしゅじつ), the form matching the `japanese` field here.

In Dan'a'yo 棕枝主日 is the Christian feast of Palm Sunday, the Sunday before Easter ([[復活節]]); it is a proper name of the feast, not a general word for a Sunday ([[主日]]).
