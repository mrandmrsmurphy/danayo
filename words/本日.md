## Notes
- [本 (char)](../characters/本%20(char).md) "base, origin" + [日 (char)](../characters/日%20(char).md) "day" — literally "base day," today.
- This is a very poetic way to say "today". For the normal way, use <ruby>[今日](../words/今日.md)<rt>ㄍㄧㄇㄋㄧㄊ</rt></ruby>

Mandarin běnrì, Cantonese bun2 jat6, and Japanese ほんじつ (honjitsu) are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 本's and 日's own stored citations exactly — all are similarly formal/literary registers, matching this word's own "poetic" sense. **Found and fixed two real bugs**: `cantonese` was missing a space ("bun2jat6"), and `korean` was stored as 오늘, a native Korean word for "today" — inconsistent with this vault's standing convention that `japanese`/`korean` fields hold the Sino-Xenic bound reading, not the everyday native word. Corrected to 본일, the real, formal Sino-Korean compound matching 本's own 본 + 日's own 일. Vietnamese bữa nay is the real native word.

`kwin` is false: 日 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page, which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 本 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄛㄋㄋㄧㄊ unique among both words and characters).
