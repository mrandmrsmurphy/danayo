## Notes
- [五 (char)](../characters/五%20(char).md) "five"

五 is 象形, though the modern X-like form no longer transparently depicts anything specific — early forms are thought to represent two crossed sticks used for counting. Like the other low numerals, 五 has a dedicated anti-forgery/financial variant, [[伍]] (also literally "a group of five," historically a unit of five soldiers), used on financial documents for the same reason as [[一]]/[[壱]], [[二]]/[[貳]], [[七]]/[[漆]], and [[九]]/[[玖]]. 五 heads an enormous family of canonical "Five X" compounds across Chinese culture — [[五行]] (the Five Elements), [[五常]] (the Five Virtues), [[五倫]] (the Five Relationships), [[五穀]] (the Five Grains), among many others catalogued on its own character page.

Mandarin wǔ, Cantonese ng5, Japanese ご (go, on'yomi) alongside native いつ(つ) (itsu(tsu), casual object-counting), Korean 오 (o) alongside native 다섯 (daseot), and Vietnamese ngũ (the Hán Việt reading, as in ngũ hành for [[五行]]) all preserve the numeral sense directly.

This word is part of a four-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read 'o/오/ㄛ): [[伍]] ("troops," still awaiting its own turn), [[於]] ("in, at, during," already perfected), and [[汚]] ("dirty," still awaiting its own turn). 於's own callout, previously malformed and incomplete, has been corrected alongside this iteration to cross-link all four properly.
