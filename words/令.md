## Notes
- [令 (char)](../characters/令%20(char).md) "cause"

令 is 会意: a kneeling figure (人) positioned beneath a covering sign — one summoned under authority. From this image derive both the causative sense "to cause, to make (someone do something)" (令人..., "to make a person [feel/do]...") and the administrative sense "order, decree, command" — in the Tang document hierarchy, 令 ranked below 詔 (imperial edict). As a modern grammatical prefix, 令 functions causatively: 令人擔心 ("cause people to worry, worrying"), 令人滿意 ("satisfying," literally "cause people to be satisfied").

Mandarin lìng, Cantonese ling6, and Vietnamese lệnh (the Hán Việt reading, as in mệnh lệnh "order, command" and ra lệnh "to give an order") all preserve both the causative and administrative senses. Japanese has two on'yomi, れい/りょう (rei/ryō, fully productive in compounds like 命令 めいれい "order, command" and 法令 ほうれい "laws and regulations"). Korean 령 (ryeong) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 하여금 (hayeogeum, the everyday causative particle "to cause/make [someone do something]").

This word is part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read leng/렁/ㄌㄝㄫ): [[鈴]] ("small bell," still awaiting its own turn) and [[零]] ("zero," already perfected — its own callout has been added alongside this iteration).
