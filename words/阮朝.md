## Notes
- [阮](../characters/阮.md) "moon lute" + [朝 (char)](../characters/朝%20(char).md) "morning" — no stand-in relationship applies (阮 is a 名専字, a character used only in names, and this is a name; 朝's own is the single-character word [[朝]]). Here 阮 is the surname Ruǎn (Vietnamese Nguyễn) and 朝 is "dynasty", as in [[王朝]] and [[宋朝]].

The Nguyễn dynasty, the last dynasty of Vietnam, 1802–1945 (English Wiktionary and Chinese Wiktionary agree on the dates). It was founded by Nguyễn Phúc Ánh, who took the reign name Gia Long after defeating the Tây Sơn (from memory, not re-checked); its predecessor was the realm of the Nguyễn lords in the south, the Guangnan state of 1558–1777 (see [[広南]], and Chinese Wikipedia). The imperial capital was Huế (see [[順化]]). Chinese Wikipedia records that the state name changed in turn: 大越 (1802–1804), 越南 (1804–1839), then 大南 from 1839 (see [[越南]] for the modern name).

The dynasty falls into an independent period and a colonial one: before the 1880s the Nguyễn emperors ruled Vietnam outright, then the French made the country a protectorate, divided it into Tonkin, Annam and Cochinchina (Chinese Wikipedia; see [[安南]] and [[法国]]), and left the court at Huế with limited powers until the last emperor, Bảo Đại, abdicated in 1945 (the last point is from memory, not re-checked). The `vietnamese` field holds Hán-Việt Nguyễn triều (nguyễn 阮, triều 朝), the form English Wiktionary and Chinese Wiktionary give for the Vietnamese entry. Nguyễn is also by far the most common Vietnamese surname (from memory, not re-checked).
