## Notes
- [矩](../characters/矩%20(char).md) "carpenter's square" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 矩 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

矩 originally names the carpenter's square, a tool for drawing right angles — paired with 規 (compass) in the classical idiom 規矩 ("compass and square," i.e. rules, standards, or proper conduct). By extension it took on a moral sense, "standard, rule," as in Confucius's famous self-description of following his heart's desire "without overstepping the bounds" (從心所欲，不踰矩). In modern physics and mathematics, 矩 also names "moment" (as in 力矩, torque/moment of force) and "quadrature."

Mandarin jǔ, Cantonese geoi2, Japanese く (ku), Korean 구 (gu), and Vietnamese củ are all standard, fully compositional readings.
