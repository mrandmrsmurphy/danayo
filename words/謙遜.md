## Notes
- [謙](../characters/謙.md) "self-effacing, humble" + [遜](../characters/遜.md) "inferior"
- This word is the stand-in for [[謙]], which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

謙遜 is humility or modesty: not putting oneself forward, deferring to others, and playing down one's own merits. 謙 is "modest, self-effacing" (the 易經 has a hexagram named 謙) and 遜 is "to yield, to give way", so the compound is self-lowering deference. Mandarin qiānxùn (谦逊 in simplified script), Cantonese him1 seon3, Japanese けんそん (kenson) and Korean 겸손 (gyeomson) are the same compound, ranked as a virtue throughout the sphere, and in everyday Japanese and Korean speech it also names the conventional habit of deflecting a compliment.

In Dan'a'yo it is the virtue of humility, set against [[傲慢]] ("proud, arrogant"), which is housed on the Emotions and Sin pages.
