>[!caution] The senses split — Japanese "training, practice", Chinese "study the ancients"
>稽古 is training or practice in Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. Chinese records it only as a literary word for learning from the ancients or studying ancient texts, so a Chinese reader may hear "classical study" rather than practice. Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).


## Notes
- [稽](../characters/稽.md) "respect, study classics" + [古](../characters/古.md) "ancient, old"

稽古 means training and practice. English Wiktionary glosses Japanese けいこ as "training; to practice". For Chinese 稽古 (Mandarin jīgǔ, Cantonese kai1 gu2) it gives only the literary "to learn from the ancients; to study ancient texts". The Dan'a'yo sense is the Japanese one, and the word is listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md). Korean 계고 and Vietnamese kê cổ are built from the readings of the two characters.

This word is the stand-in that legitimizes the character [稽](../characters/稽.md), which is too bounded to stand alone.
