## Notes
- [稽](../characters/稽.md) "respect, study classics" + [古](../characters/古.md) "ancient, old" — this word is the viable form of [稽](../characters/稽.md): the character's meaning (to study the classics; to examine antiquity) is identical, but 稽 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 稽古 is its `stand_in` compound.

稽古 splits into two related senses across the source languages. In classical Chinese, it means literally "to examine antiquity" — studying ancient texts, precedents, and historical records (the sense still primary in the Vietnamese term). In Japanese, けいこ has narrowed and shifted to name **practice or training in a traditional art or discipline** — martial arts, tea ceremony, traditional dance, calligraphy — regardless of whether the content is itself "ancient": 稽古着 (keikogi, "practice uniform"), 稽古場 (keikoba, "training hall/dojo") are everyday vocabulary in these contexts. The link between the two senses is the idea of disciplined study of an established tradition, whether textual or physical.

Korean 계고 is a standard compositional Sino-Korean reading. Vietnamese kê cổ retains the classical "examine antiquity" sense. No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄝㄧㄍㄛ unique across both words and characters).
