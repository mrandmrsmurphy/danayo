## Notes
- [親](../characters/親.md) "parent" is separately legitimized by [[親戚]]. [族](../characters/族.md) "tribe, clan" is separately legitimized by [[家族]]. No cranberry.

Mandarin qīnzú, japanese しんぞく, korean 친족, and vietnamese thân tộc (all also the real common words for "relatives, kin") confirmed standard and compositional.

Dan'a'yo distinguishes 親族 from [[親戚]]: 親族 is the formal register, the kin group as a unit (the legal or clan sense, as in Japanese civil-law 親族 and Chinese "members of the same clan"); 親戚 is the everyday word for the relatives one actually knows. Both are real in Mandarin, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese, and Wiktionary draws no distinction between them, so this split is a Dan'a'yo design choice made for predictability, not a claim about all source usage. Compare [[氏族]] and [[同族]].
