## Notes
- [音](../characters/音.md) "sound" + [韻](../characters/韻.md) "rhyme" — no `#cranberry`: 音's own `stand_in` is [[音楽]], 韻's is [[押韻]]; neither points here.

音韻 (yīnyùn) traditionally denotes the sound-system of a language as a whole (historical Chinese phonology, 音韻學, is the traditional field studying rhyme categories) — a systemic, holistic view of sound, as opposed to [[音素]]'s more individual/theoretical "phoneme" unit; the two terms both translate "phoneme" in some contexts but are used with this distinct emphasis in East Asian linguistics. Japanese おんいん (on'in) and Korean 음운 (eumun) both preserve this same systemic sense, as in 音韻論/음운론 ("phonology" as a field). Sino-Vietnamese âm vận is likewise attested with this sense.
