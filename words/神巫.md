## Notes
- [神 (char)](../characters/神%20(char).md) "god" + [巫](../characters/巫.md) "shaman" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (神's is [[神]] itself, 巫's is [[巫女]]), so `#cranberry` does not apply and neither is legitimized by this word specifically.

神巫 names a shaman or spirit-medium who serves and channels the gods, distinct from the narrower [[巫女]] ("shrine maiden," the Shinto ritual attendant role). Korean 강신무 (gangsinmu) is a real, specific ethnographic term for a Korean shaman who receives their calling through spirit-possession (as opposed to a 세습무, one who inherits the role hereditarily) — a genuine technical distinction in Korean shamanism, not a loose compositional gloss. Japanese かんなぎ (kannagi) is the same reading used for bare 巫; 神巫 is one of several classical alternate spellings for this word (alongside 巫 and 神子, the latter also listed as an alias here since 神子 in this sense is read みこ, the same word underlying 巫女).

The character-compound literally reads "god" + "shaman," and cross-linguistically the concept clusters closely with witchcraft and sorcery more broadly — Mandarin and Cantonese readings are fully compositional (shénwū, san4 mou4), while Vietnamese thần vu is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form (colloquial Vietnamese more often uses phù thủy for "sorcerer/witch").
