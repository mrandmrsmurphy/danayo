## Notes
- [橙](../characters/橙.md) "orange" + [果](../characters/果.md) "fruit"
- This word is the viable form of [橙](../characters/橙.md): the character's meaning (orange fruit) is identical, but 橙 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 橙果 is its `stand_in` compound.

橙 is the orange tree and its fruit, and 果 "fruit" makes the fruit reading explicit and bounds the character into a free word. This also keeps it apart from the other main use of 橙, the color orange (橙色), which borrows its name from the fruit. In ordinary Chinese the fruit is usually 橙子 or 橙, so 橙果 is a Dan'a'yo-style compound that spells out "orange fruit" rather than a set phrase of the other languages.

The sphere's usage varies by language. In Japanese 橙 is read daidai (だいだい) and traditionally names the bitter or Seville orange, a different fruit from the sweet orange now usually called オレンジ or 蜜柑 in the broad sense, so this page leaves the Japanese field blank. Readings: Mandarin chéngguǒ, Cantonese caang2 gwo2, Korean 등과. Dan'a'yo takes the general sense "orange (the fruit)," and it is placed under [Food](../lexipedia/Food.md).
