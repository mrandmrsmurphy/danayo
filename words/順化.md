## Notes
- [順](../characters/順.md) "in order, smooth" + [化](../characters/化.md) "-ification" — the written form of the Vietnamese name Thuận Hóa; no stand-in relationship applies to 化 as a bare component (its stand-in is [[変化]]), and the stand-in of 順 is [[順次]].

順化 is the Chinese-character name of Huế, a city of central Vietnam on the Perfume River (Sông Hương), best known as the imperial capital of the Nguyễn dynasty from 1802 to 1945 and for the Imperial City, palaces, tombs and temples that survive there, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993. The word is typed 固有名詞 and has this one sense only. English Wiktionary lists it as a Chinese proper noun (Mandarin Shùnhuà, Cantonese seon6 faa3) in two senses, the city and the former city of the old Thừa Thiên Huế province, and as the Han-form of Vietnamese Thuận Hóa, glossed "Hue (city, province of Vietnam)". Chinese Wikipedia's article is 順化市 (Vietnamese Thành phố Huế).

Where the name comes from. As English Wikipedia reads it, Thuận Hóa was a territory ceded by Champa to Đại Việt in 1306, and the town itself, then known as Phú Xuân (富春), became the provincial capital in 1687 and the capital of the southern Nguyễn lords' state from 1738. The spelling of the modern name Huế is, from my memory and not checked against a source, a Vietnamese phonetic shift of the second syllable 化 (Hán-Việt hóa, with the variant reading huế, which the vault's own [化](../characters/化.md) page lists among its Vietnamese readings); the former province Thừa Thiên-Huế is written 承天順化 in Korean Wikipedia and 承天化 in Japanese Wikipedia, so the name appears both in full and shortened to its last syllable. Vietnamese Wikipedia and English Wikipedia both date the city's period as capital to 1802–1945 under the Nguyễn, and Vietnamese Wikipedia adds the Tây Sơn period (1788–1801). Under Vietnam's 2024–2025 reform (English, Chinese and Vietnamese Wikipedia, as read on 2026-10-11) the province became a directly governed city, Huế, in January 2025, and no county-level units remain beneath it. I did not verify the detail of the present administrative divisions beyond that.

Not a compound common noun. Taken as two ordinary words, 順 and the productive suffix [[－化]] ("-ification") would form "to adapt, to become docile or acclimatized", and under the vault's affix policy such a stem + －化 expression is segmented as two words, [[順次]] being the stand-in for 順 and [[－化]] the suffix, and is not coined as a compound of its own. That ordinary sense is therefore not a second sense of this entry. Notably, however, 順化 is an attested ordinary word in Japanese: English and Japanese Wiktionary list じゅんか as a noun and suru-verb meaning "acclimatization; to acclimate" (an alternative form of 馴化), citing Kanjipedia, so the common-noun reading is real in Japanese and not a mere hypothetical one; in Chinese the entry on Chinese Wiktionary has only the Japanese section and a translation list with no Chinese common-noun sense given. The place name, by contrast, is not compositional at all: it is a proper noun whose syllables were chosen long ago and are not read for meaning.

The `mandarin` Shùnhuà and `cantonese` seon6 faa3 are as at English Wiktionary. The `vietnamese` field holds the Hán-Việt reading thuận hóa (順 thuận, 化 hóa, both at hvdic.thivien.net, which has no entry for the compound) and is also the historical name; the modern name is Huế. `japanese` じゅんか is the attested reading of the ordinary word (above) and not of the city, which Japanese calls フエ (Japanese Wikipedia also uses 順化 in 順化皇城, the Imperial City of Huế). `korean` 순화 is the compositional North Korean reading (順 순, 化 화); the real Korean name of the city is 후에 (Korean Wikipedia, which writes the Vietnamese name with the hanja 城舖化).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 順化 (Chinese proper noun, Japanese noun and verb, Vietnamese proper noun, as above); Japanese Wiktionary has じゅんか "to become used to an environment and adapt"; Chinese Wiktionary has 順化 only as a Japanese entry and 顺化 as a Chinese entry with no definition filled in, and a translation list giving Huế and Thuận Hóa for Vietnamese. Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 順化 page. Chinese Wikipedia has 順化市; Japanese Wikipedia (フエ), Korean Wikipedia (후에) and Vietnamese and English Wikipedia (Huế) have the city under its modern name, not under 順化. No homophones (注音 ㄙ⼜ㄋㄏ⺢ unique across words, chengyu and characters); the 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot because ㄋㄏ cannot begin a syllable.
