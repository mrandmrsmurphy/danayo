## Notes
- [瑠](../characters/瑠.md) "lapis lazuli" + [璃 (char)](../characters/璃%20(char).md) "glass" — 瑠's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 璃's own is itself; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 瑠 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word.

瑠璃 has two linked senses: lapis lazuli, the deep-blue gemstone, and glaze or coloured glass. Both come from one old word. Chinese 琉璃 (liúlí) is a Buddhist-era borrowing from Sanskrit *vaiḍūrya*, the blue-green gemstone of the Pure Land texts, and the same word was applied to the coloured glaze on roof tiles and to glass itself. 瑠 is the Japanese variant of 琉 and is carried here as its alias; 璃 turns up in [[玻璃]], plain glass. Dan'a'yo keeps both senses, "lapis lazuli" and "glaze".

The languages split the senses differently. Japanese るり is the stone and its colour (瑠璃色, a deep blue), and also names the ruri-bitaki, a blue flycatcher. Korean 유리 narrowed to plain glass, and ordinary glass there is that word. Vietnamese lưu ly is the Sino-Vietnamese reading and is also the name of the forget-me-not (hoa lưu ly), another blue thing. Mandarin liúlí and Cantonese lau4 lei4 keep the glaze and coloured-glass sense, as in glazed roof tiles.
