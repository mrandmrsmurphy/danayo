## Notes
- [完](../characters/完.md) "complete" + [璧](../characters/璧.md) "flawless jade"
- This word is the viable form of [璧](../characters/璧.md): the character's meaning (perfect, flawless) is identical, but 璧 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 完璧 is its `stand_in` compound.

The word comes from the idiom 完璧歸趙 "return the jade intact to Zhao," told in the *Shiji* (史記, Biography of Lian Po and Lin Xiangru): the envoy Lin Xiangru carried the famous jade disc (璧) of Zhao to the state of Qin and brought it back unharmed when Qin proved unwilling to pay for it. 完 "complete, intact" + 璧 "jade disc" thus began as a literal description of an unflawed jade, and was extended to anything flawless. Note that 璧 is a different character from 壁 "wall," and in Japanese the word is often miswritten 完壁.

The reading is shared across the sphere: Mandarin wánbì, Cantonese jyun4 bik1, Japanese kanpeki (かんぺき), Korean 완벽, Sino-Vietnamese hoàn bích. In every language it is used mainly of abstract results (a performance, plan, or answer) rather than of jade. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense "perfect, flawless" with no divergence.
