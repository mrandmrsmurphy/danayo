## Notes
- [将 (char)](../characters/将%20(char).md) "general" + [棋](../characters/棋.md) "chess; board game" — stand-in for [[棋]], which cannot appear independently.

将棋 is a broad term covering chess-like strategy board games in general (shogi, xiangqi, and related games), built from 将 ("general") + 棋 ("chess, board-game piece") — a "general's board game." Note that this term is broad, covering all versions; to specify Japanese shogi in particular, one must say 日本将棋 ("Japan's shogi").

Japanese しょうぎ (shōgi) is the specific, narrow, everyday word for the Japanese game itself (not the broad umbrella sense this Dan'a'yo word covers). Mandarin jiàngqí and Cantonese zoeng3 kei4-2 are used specifically to mean shogi (Chinese "chess" in the broad native sense is normally 象棋, xiàngqí, "elephant chess," i.e. xiangqi — a different compound entirely, not built on 将). Korean 장기 (janggi) is itself the name of Korean chess (a xiangqi-descended game), a further sense-narrowing of the same characters within the Sinosphere.

Vietnamese tướng kỳ is attested as a literal Sino-Vietnamese gloss/calque for 将棋 (used in Vietnamese descriptions of shogi), built from tướng (將, the Hán Việt reading for the "general/military commander" sense — a different reading from tương, which covers 將's separate "will, shall, about to" grammatical senses) + kỳ (棋, the Sino-Vietnamese literary reading for "chess"; cờ is a native-layer doublet of the same root, not an unrelated word, and is what everyday Vietnamese actually uses in cờ tướng, "Chinese chess/xiangqi," reversing the word order). Vietnamese lacks a common broad umbrella term matching this word's scope — "tướng kỳ" is learned/dictionary-style rather than a natural headword, while everyday speech uses the native-order "cờ tướng" for xiangqi specifically and just borrows "shogi" for the Japanese game. The character page's other candidates for 棋 (cơi, cời, kè) are unrelated Nôm phonetic-loan readings for different native words (to poke/stir a fire, a betel-nut tray, to pair up closely) and were excluded.

kwin is false: [[棋]] is kwin, but [[将]] (char) is not, and the AND-rule requires every constituent to be kwin for the compound to qualify.
