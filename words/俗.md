## Notes
- [俗 (char)](../characters/俗%20(char).md) "vulgar"

俗 is 形声: semantic [[人]] ("person") + phonetic [[谷]]. Its core sense is "what people collectively do" — the customs and common practice of ordinary people ([[習俗]] "custom, mores"; 民俗 "folk customs"; 風俗 "social customs"). "Vulgar" is a secondary, often pejorative extension: what is common among the masses, as opposed to refined or sacred, comes to mean "unrefined, worldly, secular" — contrasted historically with 僧 ("monastic, clergy").

Mandarin sú, Cantonese zuk6, and Japanese ぞく (zoku, on'yomi) all preserve both senses. Korean 속 (sok) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 풍속 (pungsok, itself a Sino-Korean compound meaning "custom, folkways"). Vietnamese tục is the Hán Việt reading, attested in phong tục ("custom") and thô tục ("vulgar," directly matching this word's own sense); the character page's other stored candidate, thói, is a native Vietnamese word meaning "habit" (thói quen, "habit") — a related but distinct native gloss rather than a genuine alternate reading of 俗 itself, and was not used here.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (sog/속/ㄙㄛㄎ) with another word still awaiting its own turn in this sweep, [[速]] ("quick") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed when it comes up.
