## Notes
- [堪 (char)](../characters/堪%20(char).md) "withstand; endure"

堪 means "to withstand, endure, be able to bear" (不堪, "unbearable, incorrigible"; 堪忍, "to endure" — also a Buddhist term, 堪忍 kham nhẫn translating Sanskrit Sahā, "the world of endurance/suffering").

Mandarin kān and Cantonese ham1 preserve the sense directly. Japanese カン/タン (KAN/TAN, on'yomi, formal register) alongside native こた(える) (kota(eru), "to withstand, hold up against") cover the sense. Korean 감 (gam) parallels the Sino-xenic reading. Vietnamese kham is the Hán Việt reading, phonologically regular from Middle Chinese kʰ-...-am (level tone), attested in kham khổ ("to endure hardship") and bất kham ("unmanageable, incorrigible," directly paralleling 不堪). The character page's other stored candidates, khom and khăm, are legitimate but separate Nôm phonetic-loan readings — khom for the unrelated native verb "to stoop, bend" (khom lưng), khăm for the unrelated native word "a mean trick" (chơi khăm) — not Sino-Vietnamese doublets, and were excluded.

This word completes a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read kam/캄/ㄎㄚㄇ): [[勘]] ("investigate; proofread," already perfected, already cross-linking here) and [[龕]] ("shrine," still unperfected).
