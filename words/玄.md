## Notes
- [玄 (char)](../characters/玄%20(char).md) "deep; profound; mysterious" — stand-in for [[玄 (char)|玄]], which cannot appear independently

玄 depicts twisted silk thread dyed a deep blackish-red, per its own listing under [[Radical 095]] (which the character page's own Notes still only cite bare, without a full graphemic write-up — that page remains largely unperfected itself, flagged here for the separate character-perfection sweep rather than fixed in this word-creation pass). From that dark, hard-to-name color the character generalized to "deep, profound, obscure, mysterious" — the shade so deep it borders on the unknowable, the same intuition behind the opening line of the Dao De Jing's 玄之又玄 ("mystery upon mystery, the gateway to all wonders"), still the character's most famous classical citation. The color sense survives concretely in 玄武 ("the Black Tortoise," one of the Four Symbols/directional guardian beasts, associated with the north and the color black) and in the everyday compound 玄米 ("brown rice," unpolished rice retaining its dark bran layer) — both already listed, thinly, on the character's own unfinished Words section.

Japanese carries two on'yomi with a real functional split: げん (GEN) dominates ordinary vocabulary (玄関 genkan "entrance/foyer," 玄米 genmai), while けん (KEN) survives only in a smaller set of specialized/classical terms; the native 訓読み くろ (kuro, "black") ties directly back to the character's original dye-color sense rather than the abstract "profound" meaning that dominates in Chinese-derived compounds. Vietnamese huyền is the standard Sino-Vietnamese reading (huyền bí "mysterious, occult," huyền thoại "legend, myth") — a register that, like Japanese げん, leans toward the abstract/mystical sense over the literal color, which Vietnamese instead names with the native đen. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 훤 (hwen, from Middle Chinese 匣母 ɣ + wen) diverges from Sino-Korean 현 in onset, Dan'a'yo preserving a rounded glide the Korean form has lost.

**Real homophone group found**: `注音 ㄏ⼔ㄋ`/`훤` is also carried by two other existing standalone words, [[懸]] ("hang, suspend, hoist") and [[県]] ("county, district, prefecture") — a three-way coincidental convergence with no shared etymology among any of the three senses. Added the full mutual callout across all three pages.
