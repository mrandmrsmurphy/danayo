## Notes
- [爾 (char)](../characters/爾%20(char).md) "you" (classical second person) + [等 (char)](../characters/等%20(char).md) "and others; plural"

爾等 is Dan'a'yo "ye" or "y'all," the plural second-person pronoun in old-fashioned speech. It is a #neologism of an anachronism, a new old word: a way to say "y'all" in "olden speech", built like [我等](我等.md) "we" and [君等](君等.md) "you all" from a pronoun plus the plural 等. According to Wiktionary, Chinese 爾等 (ěrděng, Cantonese ji5 dang2, simplified 尔等) is a literary pronoun, "you (plural); you all". Wiktionary has no Japanese, Korean or Vietnamese section; `japanese` じとう is the compositional reading, and `korean` 너희 and `vietnamese` bay are the native plural second-person pronouns, as on 君等, not compositional readings.

In Dan'a'yo the word [爾](爾.md) means "yes". 爾等 is not "yes" plus 等: the character 爾 here carries its classical Chinese sense "you", and 尔 is among its aliases on its character page. Used in the 1945 surrender broadcast ([玉音放送](../translation/玉音放送.md)).
