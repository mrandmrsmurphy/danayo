## Notes
- [爾 (char)](../characters/爾%20(char).md) "you" (classical second person) + [等 (char)](../characters/等%20(char).md) "and others; plural"

爾等 is Dan'a'yo "ye," a plural second-person pronoun in the antiquarian register, built like [我等](我等.md) "we" and [君等](君等.md) "you all" from a pronoun plus the plural 等. It is a #neologism in Dan'a'yo: 爾等 supplies the archaic "ye" that the Japanese 爾 and Chinese 爾等 carry in old proclamations. According to Wiktionary, Chinese 爾等 (ěrděng, Cantonese ji5 dang2, simplified 尔等) is a literary pronoun, "you (plural); you all." Wiktionary has no Japanese, Korean or Vietnamese section; `japanese` じとう is the compositional reading, and `korean` 너희 and `vietnamese` bay are the native plural second-person pronouns, as on 君等, not compositional readings.

爾 is the classical "you" and has 尔 among its aliases on its character page; 爾等 is the plural "ye" built on it. (The vault's [爾](爾.md) word page currently glosses "yes"; a possible 爾爾 for "yes" is left for later.) Used in the 1945 surrender broadcast on [Japan](../lookup/Japanese/Japan.md).
