## Notes
- [彼 (char)](../characters/彼%20(char).md) "yon; that (far)" — stand-in for [[彼 (char)|彼]], which is too bounded to appear independently (the character's `stand_in` is this word)

彼 is "yon" — the distal demonstrative in Dan'a'yo's three-way system: [[此]] (near) vs. [[其]] (middle) vs. 彼 (far). 彼 marks entities remote from both speaker and addressee, corresponding to Japanese あの/あれ (ano/are, that over there) and Korean 저 (jeo-, that yonder). The grammar documents list it as a 修飾語 (see [文法 - 97品詞](../grammar/文法%20-%2097品詞.md), where it is "far" under demonstratives, and [文法 - 04句法](../grammar/文法%20-%2004句法.md), "distal deixis"). Like the other 修飾語 it stands before the word it modifies, and it is the distal row of the correlative table on [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md).

In classical Chinese, 彼 is a demonstrative and third-person pronoun meaning "that; he/she/it (over there)": 彼此 (this and that; one another; each other). Wiktionary glosses it as literary "that; those" and literary "he; she; it", and cites Mengzi 彼惡敢當我哉 ("How dare he withstand me?"), where 彼 is the pronoun. Dan'a'yo keeps only the distal-demonstrative value and uses it productively across the correlative chart.

The correlative paradigm has nine cells in the 彼 row, and every one is an existing word: [彼事](彼事.md) (that far matter), [彼物](彼物.md) (yon thing), [彼人](彼人.md) (that person yonder), [彼名](彼名.md) (that person, polite), [彼処](彼処.md) (yonder; over there), [彼時](彼時.md) (that other time), [彼様](彼様.md) (in that other way), [彼多](彼多.md) (that much) and [彼類](彼類.md) (that other kind). The same nine-column row structure is shared with [此](此.md) (this), [其](其.md) (middle), 何 (what), 毎 (every), 某 (a certain) and 皆 (all), which gives 7 determiners by 9 noun classes, 63 words. The 彼 row on the Grammar page and in the 97品詞 chart match these nine words and their 注音. The sibling words 此 and 其 are the proximal and medial determiners; the 某事 page already contrasts 彼事 (known but deictically distant) with 某事 (deliberately vague). A short example, taken from the 是 page's illustrations (illustrative, not a quotation): 彼是誰 "who is that?"

Mandarin 彼 (bǐ) is literary: 彼此 (each other; one another), 彼岸 (the far bank; the other shore; in Buddhism, the realm of nirvana beyond suffering). Colloquial Mandarin uses 那 (nà) for the distal demonstrative. Cantonese 彼 (bei2): literary; colloquial uses 嗰 (go2, "that over there"). Japanese 彼 (かれ, kare) has shifted in modern usage to mean "he; him; boyfriend"; 彼女 (かのじょ, kanojo) "she; girlfriend"; the original distal demonstrative sense survives in literary contexts and in 彼岸 (ひがん, the opposite shore; the equinoctial week). The `japanese` field holds the Sino-Japanese on-reading ひ (hi), not the native かれ. Korean 피 (彼) is the Sino-Korean reading; native 저/그 serve as distal demonstratives. Vietnamese bỉ (彼) is a Sino-Vietnamese literary reading (hvdic.thivien.net lists it). In Southern Min (Zhangzhou and Taiwanese Hokkien, and Puxian Min) Wiktionary records 彼 as a living "that" demonstrative, as in 彼本冊 "that book" (from Wiktionary, not re-checked elsewhere).

For the vault's forbidden-character list, 另 ("another"), which is forbidden, is replaced by 他 or 彼 per [文法 - 98違法字](../grammar/文法%20-%2098違法字.md), so 彼 also carries an "other" nuance in that role (as recorded there; from memory, not re-checked against other documents).

彼 is written with the semantic radical 彳 and the phonetic 皮, and reads 브 (bǝ/ㄅㄜ) in Dan'a'yo, taken from the character page.
