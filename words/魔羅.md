>[!caution] The senses split — Mara the demon, and Japanese slang
>魔羅 is Mara, the Buddhist demon, in Chinese and Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. Japanese まら also has a slang sense, "penis", dated from the early 800s, so a Japanese reader may hear the slang. Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).


## Notes
- [魔](../characters/魔.md) "demon" + [羅](../characters/羅.md) "Rome; net; spread"

魔羅 is Mara, the Buddhist demon. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 魔羅 (aliases 摩羅 and 魔罗; Mandarin móluó) as the Buddhism term "mara (demon)", and Japanese まら, from the early 800s, as "Mara: a Buddhist demon that personifies the 'death' of spiritual life" and as "penis". It lists the "penis" sense also for several Ryukyuan languages. The word is listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md). The Korean 마라 and Vietnamese ma la fields are built from the readings of the two characters. Compare [魔女](魔女.md).
