## Notes
- [王 (char)](../characters/王%20(char).md) "king" + [位](../characters/位.md) "position, rank, throne"

王位 is the throne of a king: the seat and authority of a king (王), as opposed to [[帝位]] (specifically an emperor's) or [[爵位]] (a noble's title below the crown). 王 is a 象形 pictograph of a ceremonial axe, a symbol of martial authority kept near the ruler's seat and used in state rituals — an origin shared with [[全 (char)]], listed as one of 王's derived characters.

王位 appears throughout East Asian historiography and folklore wherever succession, usurpation, or abdication is at stake — "to ascend to 王位" ([[即位]]), "to vacate 王位," "to contest 王位." Together with [[王冠]] ("crown") and [[王妃]] ("queen consort"), already cross-linked from [[王 (char)]]'s Chengyu/Words sections, 王位 completes the basic vocabulary cluster of monarchy: the person (王), the regalia (王冠), the consort (王妃), and the office itself (王位).

The Government page lists it as "the throne" and also says Dan'a'yo has no general word yet for a throne in the abstract: 王位 is the specific throne of a king, and the one-tier-higher imperial throne is [帝位](帝位.md). Related words for what holds or takes the throne are [即位](即位.md) "to ascend the throne" and [在位](在位.md) "to reign." The readings are Mandarin wángwèi, Cantonese wong4 wai6, Japanese ōi (おうい), Korean 왕위 and Sino-Vietnamese vương vị. Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "a king's throne, royal rank."
