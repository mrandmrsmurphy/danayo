## Notes
- [天 (char)](../characters/天%20(char).md) "heaven" + [尊](../characters/尊.md) "honoured; venerable" — "the one honoured in heaven."

天尊 is an honorific for a high deity, literally "the Venerable of Heaven." Wiktionary records three uses. In Buddhism it was an early Chinese translation of Sanskrit *Bhagavān* ("he who is honoured by the gods") and so a title of the Buddha, later displaced by 世尊. In Daoism it is the title of the highest immortals, above all the Three Pure Ones, 元始天尊, 靈寶天尊 and 道德天尊, and today it is used mostly in that sense. And in the Tang-dynasty Church of the East (景教) it was used for God the Father. The Mandarin and Cantonese fields come from Wiktionary; the Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese fields are compositional readings of the two characters.

The word is older than all three traditions. Zhang Hua (232–300) used it in a sacrificial poem as a respectful name for Heaven itself, 穆穆天尊, and the later religions each took it over. Dan'a'yo's own word for God is [天主](天主.md), the Catholic term; 天尊 is the title of honour for a deity of any of these traditions, and sits beside [神](神.md), "a god", and [佛](佛.md), "Buddha".
