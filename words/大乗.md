## Notes
- [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) "great" + [乗 (char)](../characters/乗%20(char).md) "vehicle, conveyance"

大乗 (Sanskrit **महायान**, mahāyāna, "great vehicle") names the branch of Buddhism dominant across East Asia — China, Japan, Korea, and Vietnam — as opposed to **上座部** (Theravada, "Teaching of the Elders"), the branch dominant in Sri Lanka and mainland Southeast Asia. Its defining distinctive is the bodhisattva ideal: rather than seeking individual liberation (nirvana) alone, the Mahayana practitioner vows to postpone final release until all sentient beings can be liberated together — hence "great," able to carry many, versus a smaller vehicle for oneself alone. The term is self-descriptive and somewhat polemical in origin: early Mahayana texts contrasted themselves against **小乗** ("small vehicle"), a term now considered a pejorative exonym and generally avoided in favor of the neutral "Theravada" in modern scholarly and interfaith usage.

Mahayana Buddhism encompasses a wide range of traditions unified by this bodhisattva orientation: Pure Land (浄土), Chan/Zen (禅), Tiantai/Tendai (天台), and Vajrayana/Esoteric Buddhism (密教) are all typically classified as Mahayana schools, despite their considerable doctrinal and ritual differences from one another.
