## Notes
- [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) "great" + [乗 (char)](../characters/乗%20(char).md) "vehicle, conveyance"

大乗 is the Mahayana, the Great Vehicle: Sanskrit महायान (mahāyāna), a major branch of Buddhism. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 大乘 (an alias; Mandarin Dàchéng, Cantonese daai6 sing6) as, in Buddhism, "Mahayana", Japanese だいじょう as "Mahayana", Korean 대승 as "Mahayana; the great vehicle", and Vietnamese Đại thừa the same. The Cantonese reading was recorded here as sing4 and is now sing6, the reading Wiktionary gives for 乘 as "vehicle".

English Wikipedia describes the Mahāyāna as the largest branch of Buddhism, followed by the older branch Theravada, a broad group of traditions, texts and philosophies that developed in ancient India from about the 1st century BCE. Its scriptures, such as the Lotus Sutra, emphasize the ideal of the bodhisattva, who vows to attain buddhahood for the sake of saving all living beings, and it is for that reason also called the Bodhisattva Vehicle. Wikipedia calls Hīnayāna, "Small Vehicle", an umbrella term used by the Mahāyāna for the pre-Mahāyāna paths to liberation.

The related words are [[佛教]], Buddhism, and [[菩薩]], bodhisattva.
