## Notes
- [険 (char)](../characters/険%20(char).md) "precipitous, dangerous" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

険/險/险 means precipitous or dangerous — originally describing rugged, steep terrain (its semantic component 阜, "mound, hill"), extended to danger and risk generally. 険 itself is the Japanese shinjitai simplification of traditional 險, with its phonetic component 僉 further simplified to 㑒. It's highly productive in compounds spanning both the literal danger sense ([危険](危険.md) "dangerous," [冒険](冒険.md) "adventure," literally "to brave danger") and the modern financial sense ([保険](保険.md) "insurance," literally "to guard against risk").

Mandarin xiǎn, Cantonese him2, and Korean 험 (heom) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese けん (ken, on'yomi) is productive in compounds; the native kun'yomi けわ(しい) (kewa(shii), "steep, perilous," already 険's own stored `japanese_native` value) is the everyday adjective. Vietnamese hiểm is the standard Hán Việt reading, well attested in nguy hiểm (危険, "dangerous") and hiểm họa ("hazard, peril").
