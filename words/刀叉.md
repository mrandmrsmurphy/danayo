## Etymology

## Notes
"Knife and fork" — the everyday Chinese term (composed of one character each from 刀子 "knife" and 叉子 "fork"), used specifically of Western-style dining. Japanese and Korean instead directly borrow the English loanword phrase (confirmed via web search) rather than using a native/Sino compound — ナイフとフォーク, 나이프와 포크. **Real content bug found and fixed**: `japanese` had a garbled, non-existent string ("ナイホク") — corrected. `vietnamese: dao nĩa` is likewise the everyday native Vietnamese term (native words, not a Sino-Vietnamese calque), well known from Western-dining etiquette. Filled the blank `pos: 名詞`. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 탓채 diverges entirely from the loanword-phrase-based Korean field. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄚㄨㄑㄚㄧ unique among both words and characters).
