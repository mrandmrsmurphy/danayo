## Notes
- [痴](../characters/痴.md) "stupid, foolish" + [漢](../characters/漢.md) "man" — literally "foolish man." 痴's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 漢's own is [[漢族]]; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 痴 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word.

痴漢 originally meant simply a foolish or disreputable man, but across the modern Sinosphere the word has narrowed sharply to one specific kind of disreputable behavior: sexual harassment or molestation, especially of the groping-on-crowded-transit variety. Japanese ちかん (chikan) is the dominant register — internationally the best-known form of the word, closely associated with train molestation and the subject of dedicated public-awareness campaigns and legal statutes in Japan. Korean 치한 (chihan) carries the same narrowed "molester, sex offender" sense, borrowed directly from the Japanese usage.

Mandarin chīhàn and Cantonese ci1 hon3 retain more of the word's older, broader "vile/disreputable man" sense alongside the narrower "molester" reading, so context does more disambiguating work than in Japanese or Korean, where the sexual-offense sense is now essentially the only one in ordinary use. kwin is true here — the Dan'a'yo compound cihan (치한) happens to coincide exactly with the Korean pronunciation 치한, mirroring the `kwin: true` already set on both constituent characters.
