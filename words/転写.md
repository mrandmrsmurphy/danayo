## Notes
- [転](../characters/転.md) "to turn; to transfer" + [写](../characters/写.md) "to copy; to depict" — "to copy over from one place or form into another," hence "transcription, transliteration." Neither character's `stand_in` points here (転's is [[転化]], 写's is [[複写]]), so no legitimizer relationship applies. `kwin` is false because Dan'a'yo 줜샤 differs from Sino-Korean 전사. The traditional form is 轉寫, and 转写 is the mainland simplified form.

In this sense 転写 means rendering a form written in one script or sound system in another, such as writing Japanese in Latin letters or a foreign name in the local script, while keeping the original form recoverable. The Dan'a'yo grammar uses it for 外来語転写, the transcription of foreign words into the Dan'a'yo sound system. It is related to but different from [[翻訳]] (translation of meaning) and [[表記]] (the way something is written in a given orthography). 転写 concerns the mapping of one writing or sound system onto another, not what the words mean.

Across the languages, the same two characters have a wider range than the English pair "transcribe/transliterate." Japanese てんしゃ covers copying text or diagrams from one medium to another, transfer printing, and, in biology, the transcription of DNA into RNA (遺伝子の転写). Korean 전사 shares the biological sense. In Mandarin, however, the DNA process is called 转录, and 转写 is reserved for the script-to-script and copying senses (zhuǎnxiě). In linguistics it is used for the conversion of a text into another notation, as in 罗马字转写. Cantonese zyun2 se2 is the corresponding reading. Wiktionary has no Vietnamese reading for the compound, and the Hán-Việt hvdic.thivien.net has no entry for it either, so no `vietnamese` field is given here. Vietnamese ordinarily says phiên âm or chuyển tự for this concept.

Dan'a'yo uses 転写 for the script-and-sound sense that matters to the project, with no further narrowing.
