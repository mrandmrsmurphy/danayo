## Notes
- [関 (char)](../characters/関%20(char).md) "close" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

関/關/关 depicts a door barred shut with wooden sticks (the phonetic component 丱, itself pageless in this vault), giving the core sense "to close, to shut." It's highly productive in compounds extending from the physical "barrier, checkpoint" sense — [[関係]] ("relationship," things that "connect through a barrier point") and [[関心]] ("concern, interest") both derive from this same figurative extension, as does [[関数]] ("function," a mathematical borrowing of the same "gateway/relation" logic). [[関島]] ("Guam") is a purely phonetic use, coincidentally mirrored by this word's own Dan'a'yo romanization gwam, which happens to look just like "Guam" itself — pure coincidence, not a source for the transliteration.

Mandarin guān, Cantonese gwaan1, and Korean 관 (gwan) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese has two attested on'yomi, かん (kan, the everyday reading, used in nearly all modern compounds like 関係 かんけい) and けん (ken, an older/rarer alternate reading) — かん is correctly the one stored here. Vietnamese quan is the standard Hán Việt reading, well attested in quan hệ (関係, "relationship") and quan tâm (関心, "to care about, be concerned with"). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 괌 diverges from Sino-Korean 관 (adding a glide). No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄍ⺢ㄇ unique among both words and characters).
