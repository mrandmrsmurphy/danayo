## Notes
- [同](../characters/同.md) "same" + [化](../characters/化.md) "-ify, to become" — no stand-in relationship applies (同's own stand-in is [[同一]]; 化's is [[変化]]).

同化 is "to assimilate": to make or become the same, and as a noun, assimilation. In phonetics it is the process by which one sound takes on features of a neighbour, so that two unlike sounds become alike. It has the same shape in social and cultural use (a group taking on the customs of the society around it) and in biology (the body turning food into its own substance).

It is a derivative of the fully productive suffix [[－化]] ("-ification, to become or render X"), on the bound stem 同 "same". Because 同 is bound and is no word of its own, the compound could not be read off two words as other －化 derivatives can, and so it has its own page. The meaning is wholly compositional: 同 + 化, "to become the same".

On the phonology page 同化 is something Dan'a'yo rules out between syllables: no sandhi ([[連声]]) and no assimilation is permitted, and each syllable remains independent, so that speakers who find syllable-final consonants hard are not tempted to blend them into their neighbours.

Mandarin tónghuà (Cantonese tung4 faa3) is both a verb and the phonological term, and its antonyms there include 異化 and 分化; Japanese どうか (dōka) is "assimilation" as a noun and "to assimilate" as a verb; Korean 동화 is the same word, and Dan'a'yo's own reading 동화 matches it exactly, so `kwin` is true. Vietnamese *đồng hóa* is recorded with all three senses (general, cultural and linguistic). No homophones (注音 ㄉㄛㄫㄏ⺢ unique across both words and characters).
