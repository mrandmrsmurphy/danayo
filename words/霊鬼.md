## Notes
- [霊](../characters/霊.md) "spirit, ghost" + [鬼](../characters/鬼.md) "devil, demon" — no `#cranberry`: neither constituent's own `stand_in` points here (霊's is [[霊魂]], 鬼's is [[鬼神]]).

霊鬼/灵鬼 (línggui) is a real, attested Classical/Daoist Chinese term for a cultivated immortal soul dwelling between the mortal and underworld realms ("ghost immortal," 鬼仙), distinct from the more ordinary meaning of 鬼 alone. Japanese れいき (reiki) is a real, if archaic, term for the spirit of the dead turned malevolent — found in classical texts like the *Nihon Ryōiki*. Korean 영귀 (yeonggwi) is attested with the broader sense "an eerie or numinous ghost," also used of a mythical thousand-year tortoise spirit.

Sino-Vietnamese linh quỷ is compositionally straightforward (linh 靈 + quỷ 鬼), but does not appear to be an independently attested fixed dictionary compound the way the other four languages' forms are.
