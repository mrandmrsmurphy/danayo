## Notes
- [新](../characters/新%20(char).md) "new" + [潟](../characters/潟.md) "lagoon"

新潟 is Niigata, written "new lagoon". The word 潟 (かた) means a tidal flat or lagoon, and the name is that of the port town that grew up at the mouth of the Shinano River. The reading にいがた is native, with 新 (にい, a variant of あたらしい) and 潟 (がた) voiced.

Niigata is a long snowy prefecture along the Sea of Japan, with its capital Niigata. It is known for Koshihikari rice, sake, the island of Sado, and the opening of Kawabata Yasunari's novel Snow Country, which is set in its mountains.

In Dan'a'yo 新潟 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 니가타 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
