## Notes
- [春 (char)](characters/春%20(char).md) "spring" + [節 (char)](characters/節%20(char).md) "festival" — technically just two independent words juxtaposed: <ruby>[春](/words/春.md)<rt>ㄑㄨㄋ</rt></ruby> + <ruby>[節](/words/節.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄊ</rt></ruby>, since both [[春]] and [[節]] stand independently ([[春]]'s own `stand_in` is 春 itself; [[節]]'s is likewise 節 itself).

春節 has two related senses: (1) Lunar New Year's Day, the Spring Festival, the most important traditional CJK holiday; and (2) by metonymy, the spring season generally.

Mandarin chūnjié and Cantonese ceon1 zit3 preserve the sense directly — 春節 is the standard Mandarin term for Chinese New Year specifically. Japanese しゅんせつ (shunsetsu) and Korean 춘절 (chunjeol) parallel the Sino-xenic reading, though both languages more often use their own native New Year terms for the holiday itself (お正月/正月, oshōgatsu, in Japan, which now celebrates the solar new year; 설날, seollal, in Korea) — leaving 春節/しゅんせつ/춘절 to carry the more literal "spring festival/season" sense in those languages, or to specifically denote the Chinese holiday as observed elsewhere. Vietnamese xuân tiết is the direct Hán Việt compositional reading; Tết (春節's own second character 節 contributes the doublet Tết, the Lunar New Year holiday itself, discussed at [[季節]]) is the actual everyday Vietnamese name for the holiday, with xuân tiết reserved for the more literal/literary compositional sense.

kwin is true: both [[春]] (char) and [[節]] (char) are individually kwin, satisfying the AND-rule for the compound.
