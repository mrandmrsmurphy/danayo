## Notes
- [十](../characters/十%20(char).md) "ten" + [二](../characters/二%20(char).md) "two" + [月 (char)](../characters/月%20(char).md) "month" — literally "ten-two month" (compare [[十二]] "twelve," the same two characters).

十二月 is the standard Gregorian-calendar name for December, formed the same transparent "number + 月" way as every other month in this vault's calendar vocabulary (compare [[十一月]] "November," [[一月]] "January").

Mandarin shí'èryuè, Cantonese sap6ji6jyu6, and Korean 십이월 (sibiwol) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese じゅうにがつ (jūnigatsu) uses the Sino-Japanese on'yomi じゅう and に for both "ten" and "two," unlike the mixed native/Sino readings seen in some other Japanese month names (compare 十一月's じゅういちがつ, which mixes Sino-Japanese じゅう with native いち). Vietnamese tháng mười hai is a native construction ("month ten-two"), not a Sino-Vietnamese compound — the everyday way all Vietnamese month names are formed. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 십늬웓 diverges from Sino-Korean 십이월 in the middle syllable (늬 vs. 이 — the same divergence already documented on [[十二]] itself). No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄆㄋㄧㄜ·⼔ㄊ unique among both words and characters).
