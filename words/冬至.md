## Notes
- [冬 (char)](../characters/冬%20(char).md) "winter" + [至 (char)](../characters/至%20(char).md) "to arrive, extreme" — literally "winter's extreme," the point winter reaches its peak (shortest day, longest night in the Northern Hemisphere).

冬至 is one of the 二十四節氣 (twenty-four solar terms), the traditional East Asian solar calendar system dividing the year into 24 roughly-equal periods — falling around December 22nd, it's the counterpart to [[夏至]] (summer solstice) and shares its "至" structure with the two equinoxes, [[春分]] and [[秋分]] (which use 分, "division," instead, since an equinox splits day and night evenly rather than reaching an extreme).

Mandarin dōngzhì, Cantonese dung1 zi3, Korean 동지 (dongji), and Japanese とうじ (tōji) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday term for the winter solstice in its language — 冬至 remains a significant traditional festival day across the Sinosphere (associated with eating tangyuan in China, red bean porridge in Korea). Vietnamese đông chí is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 冬's đông and 至's own chí. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 통즤 diverges from Sino-Korean 동지 in the first syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄊㄛㄫㄐㄧㄜ unique among both words and characters).
