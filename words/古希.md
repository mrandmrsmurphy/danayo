## Notes
- [古](../characters/古.md) "ancient, old" + [希](../characters/希.md) "rare, hope"

From the classical proverb 人生七十古來稀 — "It has been rare since ancient times for people to live to seventy." The phrase alludes to the exceptional longevity of reaching age 70, drawn from Du Fu's poem 曲江.

古希 is the standard congratulatory name for the age of seventy.  The original spelling is 古稀, with 稀 "rare"; the form with 希 is the common one in Japanese, where it is read こき and belongs to the series of longevity celebrations (長寿祝い) that mark 60 (還暦), 70 (古希), 77 (喜寿), 80 (傘寿) and 88 (米寿).  Korean 고희 works the same way, and a 고희연 is the banquet held for a seventieth birthday.

Mandarin gǔxī, Cantonese gu2 hei1 and Japanese こき are the ordinary readings.  Vietnamese cổ hy is the Sino-Vietnamese reading of the 古稀 spelling, the form familiar from the line 人生七十古來稀 (nhân sinh thất thập cổ lai hy); the character 希's own `vietnamese` field lists he and hi, so this reading follows the word's classical spelling rather than a compositional reading of 希.
