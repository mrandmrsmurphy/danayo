## Notes
- [毛 (char)](../characters/毛%20(char).md) "fur" — stand-in for [[毛 (char)|毛]], which cannot appear independently

毛 is a 象形 pictograph of a feather or a tuft of hair/fur, generalizing across the whole animal kingdom's body-covering: 毛皮 ("fur, pelt," an animal's whole coat), 羽毛 ("feathers, plumage," extending it to birds specifically), and 羊毛 ("wool," the specific fiber product of sheep's 毛). The character's phonetic descendants show the same core shape put to unrelated semantic work once combined with other components — 表 ("show, indicate," originally a fur garment worn outward, hence "outer/surface"), 耗 ("deplete, waste"), and 芼 ("to cook [vegetables]") — none of which retain any trace of the "hair/fur" meaning itself.

Japanese carries two on'yomi with a real functional split: もう (MOU) is the default in nearly all ordinary compounds (毛皮 kegawa actually favors the native reading instead, but 羊毛 yōmō and 脱毛 datsumō both use もう), while ぼう (BOU) survives only in a small set of technical/classical terms. The native 訓読み け (ke) is the everyday standalone word for "hair, fur" in casual Japanese speech — 髪の毛 (kaminoke, "the hair of one's head," redundantly doubling 髪 and 毛) shows both the Sino-Japanese and native layers coexisting side by side in a single compound. Vietnamese mao appears mostly in learned/technical Sino-Vietnamese compounds rather than everyday speech, which instead uses native lông ("body hair, fur, feather") for the same referents 毛皮/羽毛 cover here. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 맛 (mau, from Middle Chinese 明母 m + 豪韻 ɑu) diverges from Sino-Korean 모 in the coda, Dan'a'yo retaining a final consonant Korean has dropped entirely — the same MC-coda-preservation pattern already documented on [[寸]] and [[月]] earlier in this sweep.
