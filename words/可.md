## Notes
- [可 (char)](../characters/可%20(char).md) "can; may; able; permissible" — the character's `stand_in` is 可 itself, so this word is the character's own usable word form.

- See [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md).

可 is the potential prefix of Dan'a'yo, one of the 修飾語 (prefixing particles) listed in [文法 - 04句法](../grammar/文法%20-%2004句法.md) beside [[不]] (negation), [[已]] (perfective), [[未]] (negative perfective) and the deictics 此/其/彼. Unlike the 関詞 (adpositions), which are suffixed, 修飾語 stay prefixed to the verb. The grammar's own example is 可行 "can go", and [文法 - 05形態](../grammar/文法%20-%2005形態.md) defines 可– as marking ability or capacity (能力・可能), giving the Latin-glossed paradigm 我 可罪 "posse peccare", 我 不可罪 "posse non peccare", 我 可不罪 "non posse peccare", 我 不可不罪 "non posse non peccare". Permission is handled separately: the grammar assigns it to [[可以]] (許可詞), so the bare prefix is about what is possible or within one's capacity, not about what is allowed. The lexipedia [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md) page lists it under Modals as "can".

Combinations with 不 are fixed by order: [[不可]] "cannot" (negation outside the potential), [[可不]] "may refrain, able not to" (potential outside the negation), and [[不可不]] "cannot not, must". The vault's translation of a Chinese essay (lookup/HSK/China.md, the 躺平 draft) renders 人大可不必如此 "there is no need at all to be so" with 大可不必, a Mandarin idiom built on the same pieces. The compound 可 plus 以 gives [[可以]], and 可 plus 能 gives [[可能]] "possible; might". Bound compounds such as [[可読]] "readable" and [[許可]] "permission" use the character in the same sense.

Etymology: 可 is a 形声 character (OC \*kʰaːlʔ) whose phonetic 丂 is an old variant of 考 and whose semantic is 口 "mouth", an utterance of assent. It was the main possibilitative and permissive modal of Classical Chinese, a function Dan'a'yo keeps in the potential prefix (character details on its own page). Cross-linguistic equivalents are listed here only as native comparisons and are not Dan'a'yo readings (from memory, not re-checked against Wiktionary): Mandarin 能 néng and 会 huì for ability, 可以 kěyǐ for permission, with bare 可 kě also meaning "but" in some contexts; Cantonese 可 ho2 mostly in compounds, with 識 sik1 and 得 dak1 as native modals; Japanese できる and the suffix ～られる, with 可 read カ in compounds such as 可能 and コク in 可汗; Korean 수 있다 and 할 수 있다, with Sino-Korean 가 in 가능; Vietnamese có thể and được, with Sino-Vietnamese khả in khả năng. The `japanese`, `korean` and `vietnamese` fields hold the Sino-Xenic bound readings of the character (カ, 가, khả), not these native equivalents. The Dan'a'yo sense is the shared CJKV potential, with no divergence.
