## Notes
- [分 (char)](../characters/分%20(char).md) "divide" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

分 combines 八 ("to separate") with 刀 ("knife") — to separate something with a blade, "to divide." It's one of the most productive roots in the vault's number/math vocabulary, covering fractions ([[分数]], [[分之]]), percentages ([[百分率]]), calculus operations ([[積分]] "integrate," [[微分]] "differentiate"), and the everyday minute-of-time sense (as a counter suffix, e.g. in [[十分]], "ten minutes").

Mandarin fēn, Cantonese fan1, and Korean 분 (bun) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese has three attested on'yomi — ふん (fun, used specifically for the time-counter "minutes" sense, as stored here), ぶん (bun, the general "divide, portion" sense), and ぶ (bu, a rarer reading in specific fixed compounds). Vietnamese phân is the standard Hán Việt reading, attested in phân số (分数, "fraction") and phân tích (分析, "analyze"); 分's own page also stores phận (as in số phận, "fate, destiny"), an unrelated figurative extension not relevant to this word's core "divide" sense. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 분 matches Sino-Korean 분 exactly. No Dan'a'yo homophones at the word level (注音 ㄅㄨㄋ unique among words; several characters — 墳, 憤, 焚, 賁 — happen to share this reading, but no other *word* does).
