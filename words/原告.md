## Notes
- [原](../characters/原.md) "plain, original" + [告](../characters/告.md) "inform" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (原's own is [[原始]]; 告's own is [[告訴]]), so 原告 is an independent compound. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: `characters/原.md` is `kwin: true`, `characters/告.md` is `kwin: false` → compound `false`.

原告 means "plaintiff" — the party who initiates a lawsuit, contrasting with the not-yet-coined "defendant." Mandarin yuángào, Japanese げんこく (genkoku), and Korean 원고 (wongo) are all standard, fully compositional legal terms. Cantonese jyun4 gou3 is compositional.

Vietnamese nguyên cáo is real and directly attested, but is secondary in modern Vietnamese legal practice to nguyên đơn (原 + 単/單 "single party," a differently-structured compound), which is the more commonly used present-day term for "plaintiff."
