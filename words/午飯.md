## Notes
- [午](../characters/午.md) "noon" + [飯](../characters/飯.md) "cooked rice; food" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (午's is 正午, 飯's is 米飯).

Mandarin wǔfàn and Cantonese ng5 faan6 are the ordinary words for "lunch." Korean 오반 (obun) is the genuine Sino-Korean reading of 午飯, a real but now old-fashioned term for a substantial midday meal; the modern everyday Korean word for "lunch" is 점심, which is 點心 (lit. "dot the heart"), a different compound originally meaning a light snack. Vietnamese bữa trưa is the native word for the midday meal (bữa ăn trưa is a longer synonym).

Japanese ごはん is a genuine reading of 午飯 ("lunch"), but it is a homophone of the unrelated everyday word 御飯 ("cooked rice; a meal," written with 御, not 午), an instance of the recurring Sino-Japanese homophone-across-different-hanja pattern (cf. [[羽翼]]/右翼, [[思考]]).
