## Notes
- [先](../characters/先.md) "first; before" + [生](../characters/生.md) "life; born" — an independent compound; neither character's own `stand_in` points here (先's is `優先`, 生's is `生活`); literally "born before [you]," an honorific for someone senior in age or experience

先生's primary living sense differs sharply by language. In modern Mandarin, xiānsheng chiefly means "Mr., sir" — a general polite title for a man (regardless of profession), with the "teacher" sense archaic/historical (superseded today by 老师, lǎoshī); the English gloss leads with this Mandarin-primary sense. Cantonese sin1 saang1 follows the same Mandarin-like pattern (also "husband," in some dialectal use). Japanese せんせい (sensei) and Korean 선생 (seonsaeng), by contrast, genuinely and overwhelmingly mean "teacher" as the living, primary sense in both languages (선생님 being the everyday respectful address for teachers).

Vietnamese tiên sinh is a real, richly documented Sino-Vietnamese honorific (not the generic word for "teacher," giáo viên, which is built from unrelated roots) carrying the exact same range of senses found across the sphere — teacher, respected elder/expert, and even (in the form tiên sanh) a wife's term of address for her husband.
