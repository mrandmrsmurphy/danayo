## Notes
- [盧](../characters/盧.md) "cottage, hut" (used here purely phonetically, as a Mandarin-based transliteration character) + [森](../characters/森.md) "forest" + [堡](../characters/堡.md) "fort" — a phono-semantic transliteration of "Luxembourg," not a compositional description of the country.

盧森堡/卢森堡 is the standard Mandarin/Cantonese transliteration of Luxembourg, the small European grand duchy — the characters were chosen primarily to approximate the foreign sound (Lú-sēn-bǎo ≈ "Luxem-bourg"), though 森 "forest" and 堡 "fort/castle" happen to evoke fitting imagery for a country whose name derives from a Roman-era fortress ("Lucilinburhuc," little castle).

Japanese ルクセンブルク (Rukusenburuku) and Korean 룩셈부르크 (Ruksembeureukeu) both transliterate the name directly from a European source (German/French Luxemburg) using katakana/hangul rather than borrowing the Chinese-character transliteration, and Vietnamese Lúc-xăm-bua likewise transliterates directly from French rather than using a Sino-Vietnamese reading of 盧森堡's characters — all three follow the more general pattern, common across the sphere, of transliterating modern country names independently rather than through the Chinese-character intermediary.
