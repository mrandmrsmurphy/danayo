## Notes
- [禁](../characters/禁.md) "prohibit, forbid" + [断](../characters/断.md) "sever, cut off, stop" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (禁's is [[禁止]], 断's is [[割断]]), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`.

禁断 intensifies plain prohibition into an absolute, uncrossable ban — something forbidden so severely that even approaching it is taboo, as in 禁断の果実 ("forbidden fruit") or 禁断症状 ("withdrawal symptoms," the medical sense of being cut off from a substance one is dependent on). This dual usage — moral/religious absolute prohibition and the physiological experience of enforced abstinence — makes it broader than either [[禁忌]] (cultural taboo) or [[禁制]] (formal/administrative ban) alone.

Mandarin jìnduàn, Cantonese gam3 dyun6, Japanese きんだん (kindan), and Korean 금단 (geumdan, well known from 금단현상, "withdrawal symptoms") are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese cấm đoán is likewise compositional, combining cấm ("forbid") with đoán here in its "cut off, sever" sense rather than its more common homographic sense "to guess."
