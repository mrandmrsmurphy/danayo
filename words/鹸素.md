## Notes
- [鹸 (char)](characters/鹸%20(char).md) "alkali; base" + [素](characters/素.md) "element" — a semantic (not phonetic) coinage: sodium is named for its role in forming alkalis (sodium hydroxide, NaOH, is a classic strong base), avoiding the standard Chinese element character 鈉/钠 (nà).

**Form.** `mandarin`/`cantonese` above (nà/naap6) give the readings of the avoided standard element character 鈉/钠, not a compositional reading of 鹸素 — per the same convention established for [[巨金]]/[[惰素]]/[[燐素]]/[[高素]] this sweep; the Cantonese value in particular is reasoned from the shared phonetic component 内 (as in 納, naap6) rather than independently confirmed this session, since search tooling was unavailable. `korean`/`japanese`/`vietnamese` (나트륨/ナトリウム/natri) are all loanwords from "Natrium," the German/Latin-derived name for the element, rather than readings of 鹸素 as a compound.

**Comparative note.** Like [[巨金]] (titanium, calquing Titan/"giant") and [[惰素]] (argon, calquing Greek "lazy/inert"), 鹸素 is a semantically-motivated coinage rather than a purely phonetic one — 鹸 ("alkali, base") reflects sodium's defining chemical behavior rather than any part of its international name.
