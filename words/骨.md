## Notes
- [骨 (char)](../characters/骨%20(char).md) "bone; skeleton" — stand-in for [[骨 (char)|骨]], which cannot appear independently

骨 is 形声: semantic [[Radical 130|肉]] ("flesh") + phonetic 冎 (the original pictograph for "bone" itself, with no character page of its own in this vault though it also backs [[咼]]/[[拐]]) — 肉 was added later purely to disambiguate the borrowed pictograph. The character anchors an unusually large and systematic anatomical compound family, naming individual bones directly (脛骨 "tibia," 蹠骨 "metatarsal," 肋骨 "rib," 恥骨 "pubis," 頭骨 "cranium," 背骨 "spine") alongside structural/figurative extensions: 骨格 ("framework," the skeleton generalized to any supporting structure), 拳骨 ("fist," a bone-knuckle metonym), and 骨肉/骨肉相連 ("flesh and bone," "bone of my bone and flesh of my flesh," directly paralleling the Biblical idiom already noted on [[肉]] earlier in this sweep). 粉骨砕身 ("to have one's body smashed to pieces") uses the anatomical sense at its most literal and severe.

Japanese carries a single on'yomi こつ (KOTSU, 骨格 kokkaku), alongside the fully productive native 訓読み ほね (hone), the everyday word for a bone. Vietnamese cốt is the reading used here (hài cốt "remains, bones"), picked over the character's noisier remaining stored variants (cút, cọt, gút), which show no comparable independent life for this sense. **Real `kwin` bug found and fixed on both pages**: this word and `characters/骨 (char).md` were both stamped `kwin: true`, but Dan'a'yo `諺文: 곧` (GOD) and Sino-Korean `골` (GOL) are different Unicode syllables — corrected both to `kwin: false`, the same MC-checked-coda-vs-Korean-liquid pattern already documented on [[月]]/[[越]]/[[舌]] earlier in this sweep: Dan'a'yo 곧 (god, from Middle Chinese 見母 k + 沒韻 uət) preserves the checked coda as -d, where Sino-Korean 골 has regularized it to -l. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄛㄊ unique among both words and characters).
