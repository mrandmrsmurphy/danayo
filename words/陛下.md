## Notes
- [陛](../characters/陛.md) "steps of the throne" + [下 (char)](../characters/下%20(char).md) "below" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes 陛 as a bound character (下's own stand-in is [[下]] itself; transitivity fails, so no `#cranberry`).

陛下 literally means "below the steps [of the throne]" — an indirect, deferential form of address for a monarch, on the same logic as addressing a courtier rather than the sovereign directly out of respect. Mandarin 陛下 (bìxià), Cantonese 陛下 (bai6haa6), Japanese 陛下 (へいか), Korean 폐하 (pyeha), and Sino-Vietnamese bệ hạ are all the standard honorific address for an emperor, king, or queen across the CJKV monarchies — still in living use today in Japan (今上陛下, referring to the reigning emperor) and other constitutional monarchies, alongside the related but lower-ranking 殿下 (denka/전하/điện hạ, "Your Highness," used for princes and other royalty below the sovereign).
