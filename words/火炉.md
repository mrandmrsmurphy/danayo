## Notes
- [火 (char)](../characters/火%20(char).md) "fire" + [炉](../characters/炉.md) "stove, hearth" — this word is the stand-in compound that legitimizes the bound character 炉 (per its own stored `stand_in`).

火炉/火爐 names a furnace, stove, or boiler — a fire-container broadly, covering both traditional hearths and modern heating/industrial equipment. 炉 itself is a Japanese shinjitai/Chinese simplified form (already documented on its own page) that replaces the traditional phonetic 盧 with 戸; 爐 is the traditional form, kept here as this word's own alias.

Mandarin huǒlú, Cantonese fo2 lou4, and Korean 화로 (hwaro) are all standard, fully compositional readings — Korean 화로 is additionally 炉's own stored `korean_native` value, i.e. the compositional Sino-Korean reading and the real everyday Korean word for "brazier, hearth" happen to coincide. Japanese かろ (karo) is likewise compositional from 火's on'yomi カ + 炉's on'yomi ロ; everyday Japanese instead uses native いろり (irori, "sunken hearth," already 炉's own stored `japanese_native` value) for the traditional form, or borrows loanwords like ストーブ (sutōbu) for a modern stove. Vietnamese hỏa lô is likewise compositional, from 炉's two attested Hán Việt readings (lô and lư — lô is the one that also gives fully Vietnamized lò, the everyday native word for "stove, kiln," as in lò sưởi "heater"). `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 화로 matches Sino-Korean 화로 exactly. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄏ⺢ㄌㄛ unique among both words and characters).
