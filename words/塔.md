## Notes
- [塔 (char)](../characters/塔%20(char).md) "pagoda"

**Content corrected**: `mandarin` was `"tǎ, da"`. `da` (neutral tone) is genuinely attested, but only as a variant-character substitution tied to one specific unrelated colloquial compound, 疙瘩/圪塔 (gēda, "a lump, bump, pimple") — where 塔 stands in phonetically for that compound's second syllable — not a general secondary reading of 塔 itself, and unrelated to this word's own "pagoda" sense. Dropped rather than kept as a spurious second value. `vietnamese` was the malformed string `"tháp, thạp, thóp"`; `thạp` (a large jar/vessel, as in the well-known Đông Sơn bronze thạp đồng) and `thóp` (the fontanelle on an infant's skull) are both real, well-attested Vietnamese words, but are Nôm loans of the glyph for unrelated native vocabulary, not Sino-Vietnamese readings of "tower" — narrowed the field to `tháp` alone.

塔 means "tower, pagoda, stupa" — a Buddhist religious structure, and by extension any tower-shaped building (塔 originally entered Chinese as a transliteration element for Sanskrit stūpa/Pali thūpa).

Mandarin tǎ and Cantonese taap3 preserve the sense directly. Japanese トウ (TOU, on'yomi, fully productive: 塔 alone functions as an everyday noun, 五重塔, gojūnotō, "five-story pagoda") covers the sense, with no dedicated native kun'yomi. Korean 탑 (tap) parallels the Sino-xenic reading and is likewise everyday vocabulary (불탑, "Buddhist pagoda"). Vietnamese tháp is the standard Hán Việt reading, equally common in everyday speech (tháp Eiffel, "Eiffel Tower"; ngọn tháp, "a spire, tower"; bảo tháp, "a stupa, reliquary tower").
