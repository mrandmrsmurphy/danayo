## Notes
- [北](../characters/北.md) "north" + [海](../characters/海.md) "sea" — the Northern Sea; both the northernmost of the classical [[四海]] and the modern North Sea of Europe

北海 is the Northern Sea: in classical Chinese, the most remote region of the north and the northernmost of the four seas, and in modern usage the North Sea of Europe. English Wiktionary lists it for Chinese (Běihǎi, Cantonese bak1 hoi2) as "most remote area in the north" (literary), the obsolete "Bohai Sea", "North Sea", "Beihai" in Beijing (Beihai Park), the prefecture-level city of Beihai in Guangxi, and a historical Kingdom of Beihai in Shandong. For Japanese ほっかい it gives "northern sea", with 南海 as its antonym, and "North Sea". It lists Korean 북해 with no gloss, and has no Vietnamese entry, so Vietnamese Bắc Hải here is built from the characters.

The classical sense has two famous settings. The *Zhuangzi*, in its first chapter, opens in the 北冥, the northern darkness: "北冥有魚，其名爲鯤", a fish named Kūn that turns into the bird Péng, whose wings rise on the whirlwind ninety thousand li, and which then flies to the 南冥, the southern darkness. And in the *Hanshu*, the Xiongnu moved the captive envoy Su Wu to "北海上無人處", an uninhabited place on the Northern Sea, to tend rams, and told him he could return when the rams gave milk; English Wikipedia says the chanyu exiled him to Lake Baikal and that he was detained nineteen years. Compare [[西海]] and [[南海]], the other seas of the [[四海]].
