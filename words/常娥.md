## Notes
- [常](../characters/常.md) "constant" + [娥](../characters/娥.md) "beautiful (lady)" — Chang'e, the lady who lives in the moon.

常娥 is Chang'e, the goddess of the moon in Chinese myth. In the best-known story she is the wife of the archer [羿](../characters/羿.md), who shot down nine of the ten suns. He received the elixir of immortality from the [Queen Mother of the West](西王母.md); she took it, rose to the moon and stayed there, where she is pictured with a jade hare. She is honoured at the Mid-Autumn Festival, and China's lunar exploration programme is named after her.

Her name was first written 姮娥. The first character was replaced by 常 under the Han dynasty to avoid the personal name of Emperor Wen (劉恆), whose given name 恆 is the phonetic of 姮, and later versions add the woman radical to give the usual modern 嫦娥. The vault has no page for 嫦 or 姮, so Dan'a'yo writes the name 常娥, keeps 嫦娥 and 姮娥 as aliases, and counts 娥 as a 名専字 that this name uses. The Mandarin form is the standard one; the Cantonese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese fields are compositional readings of the two characters (Korean follows the North Korean reading), whereas the names in actual use in Korea and Vietnam, 항아 and Hằng Nga, come from the older 姮娥.
