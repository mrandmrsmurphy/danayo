## Notes
- [鼓 (char)](../characters/鼓%20(char).md) "drum" — stand-in for [[鼓 (char)|鼓]], which cannot appear independently

鼓 is 会意, combining 壴 ("drum," pictograph, no vault page) with 攴 ("hand holding a drumstick") — a hand striking a drum — which explains why the character does double duty as both the noun "drum" and the verb "to drum, to play [an instrument]," already reflected in this word's own frontmatter gloss. `characters/鼓 (char).md` is itself already fully perfected (stamped 2026-08-05, proper `## Words` citation list, complete `mc_id`/`danayo_id`/`stand_in`) — evidently perfected separately after this word's own frontmatter was last touched, since this page's `date-last-perfect` had lagged behind at 2026-08-03; no character-side bug remains here.

The verbal sense generalizes figuratively to "to rouse, to encourage" — 鼓舞 ("to encourage, to cheer," literally "to drum and dance") — the same drum-to-motivation metaphor found in English "to drum up support." 鞀鼓 ("pellet drum," a small hand-shaken rattle-drum) names a specific traditional instrument type within the broader category.

Japanese carries a single on'yomi こ (KO, 太鼓 taiko "a large drum" — though written with a different first character), alongside the native 訓読み つづみ (tsuzumi), which names a specific traditional hand-drum rather than "drum" generically — a narrower native term than the Sino-Japanese reading's broader coverage, the reverse of the usual pattern in this sweep where the native reading is the more general everyday word. Vietnamese cổ is the character's sole stored reading. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 고 (go, from Middle Chinese 見母 k + 模韻 uo) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 고.
