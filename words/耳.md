## Notes
- [耳 (char)](../characters/耳%20(char).md) "ear" — stand-in for [[耳 (char)|耳]], which cannot appear independently

耳 names the ear, both the anatomical organ and its extension to "eyes and ears" as instruments of perception and surveillance (耳目 "spies, informants"), and figuratively to anything ear-shaped or ear-adjacent (耳朶 "earlobe," 巻耳 "field chickweed," named for its ear-shaped leaves).

Mandarin ěr, Cantonese ji5, Japanese に (ni, on'yomi — the everyday word is instead the native 耳 みみ, mimi), Korean 이 (i), and Sino-Vietnamese nhĩ are all standard readings. **A genuine Dan'a'yo homophone**: 耳 and [[而]] ("and; but") share an identical reading (ni/니/ㄋㄧ) despite being semantically and etymologically unrelated — added reciprocal homophone callouts to both pages. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 니 (ni, from Middle Chinese 日母 ȵ + 之韻 ɨ) diverges from Sino-Korean 이, which has dropped the nasal initial entirely — the same MC 日母-loss pattern already documented on [[肉]] and [[而]] earlier in this sweep.
