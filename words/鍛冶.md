## Notes
- [鍛](../characters/鍛.md) "forge" + [冶](../characters/冶.md) "smelt, cast" — literally "to forge and cast," the craft and trade of metalworking: to work as a blacksmith, or the blacksmith's trade itself.

鍛冶 names the craft of metalworking as a profession — shaping metal (chiefly iron and steel) into tools, weapons, and implements — and by extension the smith who practices it. It is distinct from [[鍛錬]] (built on the same leading character 鍛): 鍛錬 names the specific repeated *act* of forging by striking, and has drifted heavily in modern usage toward a figurative sense of rigorous training or self-discipline ("tempering" one's body or character), while 鍛冶 stays anchored to the literal trade and craft of metalworking, without that figurative extension.

Japanese かじ (kaji) is the standard, everyday word for blacksmithing and blacksmiths, productive in compounds like 鍛冶屋 (kajiya, "blacksmith's forge; blacksmith"). Korean 단야 is one of several attested Sino-Korean terms for the trade (alongside 야장/철장/야공), though the everyday native word for the person is 대장장이. Vietnamese đoán dã is compositional, built from 鍛's own đoán and 冶's own dã; the everyday living Vietnamese term for a blacksmith is the native thợ rèn.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 돤야 diverges from Sino-Korean 단야 in the first syllable, since 鍛 independently diverges from Korean 단 on its own character page.
