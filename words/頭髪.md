## Notes
- [頭 (char)](../characters/頭%20(char).md) "head" + [髪](../characters/髪.md) "hair" — stand-in for [[髪]], which is never used on its own

The `mandarin` field carries two readings from a regional split: Mainland Putonghua tóufà (fourth tone, matching 髪's own `fà`) and Taiwan Guoyu tóufǎ (third tone). Wiktionary documents the citation tone of 髮/发 splitting along the same Mainland/Taiwan line (from an earlier check, not re-fetched today).

Japanese とうはつ (tōhatsu) and Korean 두발 (dubal) are both the standard formal/institutional word for "hair" (dress codes, grooming regulations), alongside more everyday native alternatives (Japanese 髪の毛, kami no ke; Korean 머리카락, meorikarak). Vietnamese keeps the field value tóc, the ordinary everyday native word for hair — the fuller native compound đầu tóc ("head-hair") is more common than the literal Sino-Vietnamese calque đầu phát, which is attested but rare next to it.

The Korean and Vietnamese remarks above are from memory, not re-checked. No other word shares the 注音 ㄊㄛㄨㄈㄚㄊ (checked 2026-10-13).
