## Notes
- [飛 (char)](../characters/飛%20(char).md) "fly" + [鼠](../characters/鼠.md) "rat" — literally "flying rodent." No stand-in relationship applies (飛's own stand-in is itself; 鼠's is [[熊鼠]]).

飛鼠 is the flying squirrel: a squirrel with a membrane between its fore and hind legs, which it spreads to glide from tree to tree. It is a rodent, listed with the squirrel [[栗鼠]] and not with the bat. In Chinese 飛鼠 is also a dialect and older word for the bat, in Cantonese, Hakka and other southern varieties, but Dan'a'yo does not use it that way: the bat is [[蝙蝠]] (and [[天鼠]]), and 飛鼠 is the flying squirrel only, so the two animals never share a name.

Wiktionary records the flying-squirrel sense for Mandarin in Taiwan, for Taiwanese Hakka and for Cantonese in Guangdong and Guangxi, as a dialect synonym of the formal 鼯鼠. Mandarin fēishǔ and Cantonese fei1 syu2 are the standard readings. For the other languages the `japanese`, `korean` and `vietnamese` fields hold the compositional Sino-readings (ひそ, 비서, phi thử), which were not checked against a dictionary, and everyday speech uses native words instead: from memory, Japanese ムササビ and モモンガ, Korean 하늘다람쥐 and Vietnamese sóc bay.
