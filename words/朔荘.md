## Notes
- [朔](../characters/朔.md) "first day of the lunar month, north" + [荘](../characters/荘.md) "villa, village" — no stand-in relationship applies (朔's own is [[朔日]]; 荘's own is [[別荘]]). The name is written 朔莊 in traditional characters (and 朔庄 in simplified), kept as aliases because 荘 is the parent form on its character page.

朔荘 (Sóc Trăng) is a city in the Mekong Delta of southern Vietnam, the seat of the province of the same name, which English Wiktionary notes was abolished in 2025. The area has a large ethnic Khmer population, and its pagodas and Khmer festivals are what it is chiefly known for (from memory, not re-checked). The name itself is Khmer in origin, usually given as from <i>Srok Khleang</i> (from memory, not re-checked), so the characters 朔荘 are a phonetic/Hán-Việt rendering of the Vietnamese form and carry no meaning of their own; "first day of the month village" is not what the name means.

The `vietnamese` field holds the Hán-Việt reading sóc trang (朔 sóc, 荘/莊 trang; hvdic.thivien.net gives 朔 as sóc and 莊 as trang). Vietnamese spells the place name Sóc Trăng with ă, so the Hán-Việt reading differs from the real name in the vowel of the second syllable. Mandarin Shuòzhuāng and Cantonese sok3 zong1 are as in English Wiktionary. Japanese ソクチャン (from memory, not re-checked) is the actual name; the `japanese` field holds the compositional on'yomi さくそう (朔 SAKU, 荘 SOU), and the Korean 삭장 is the compositional Sino-Korean reading.

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 朔莊 as a Chinese proper noun (the former province and the city of Sóc Trăng, Mandarin Shuòzhuāng, Cantonese sok3 zong1; simplified 朔庄), with no Vietnamese section. Chinese Wiktionary has a stub with Mandarin shuòzhuāng and no definition, only a Russian translation. Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 朔莊 or 朔庄 page. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄚㄎㄐ⺢ㄫ unique across both words and characters).
