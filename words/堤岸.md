## Notes
- [堤](../characters/堤.md) "dike, levee" + [岸](../characters/岸%20(char).md) "bank, shore" — no stand-in relationship applies (堤's own is [[堤防]]; 岸's own is the single-character word [[岸]]).

This is a Chinese word with an ordinary sense and a famous place-name sense, so the ordinary one comes first.

1. An embankment, a levee, the raised bank of a river. English Wiktionary lists this as the noun sense, placing it under the synonym set of 水壩 "dam"; the Dan'a'yo word for the thing is [[堤防]], and a related compound is [[堰堤]]. Chinese Wiktionary has a 堤岸 page with no definition filled in, only a Russian translation list (dam, dike, riverside embankment). I could not confirm 堤岸 as an ordinary, current word in Japanese, Korean or Vietnamese (see attestation below), so treat this sense as a transparent Chinese compound ("dike" + "bank") rather than a CJKV-wide shared word.
2. Cholon (Chợ Lớn), the Chinatown of Saigon, now part of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. This is how the quarter is written and known in Chinese, and it is the sense the word is actually used for in practice.

Chợ Lớn is the native Vietnamese name; it means "big market" (chợ "market" + lớn "big"; from memory, not re-checked). The quarter grew up beside the Saigon area, settled by Chinese (Cantonese, Teochew and other) merchants; it was a separate city for a long time and was merged with Saigon in the 1930s, around 1931 (from memory, not re-checked; the Vietnamese Wikipedia lead says the two merged during the 1930s to 1950s). The Chinese name 堤岸 is not a translation of Chợ Lớn. English Wiktionary reports that An Chi (2016) rejects the folk etymology "embankment" and, pointing to the variant 提岸, proposes that 堤岸 is itself a borrowing from the Vietnamese name Sài Gòn, the same source as Cantonese 西貢 (compare [[柴棍]], the Dan'a'yo name of Saigon, and [[胡志明市]], the city as a whole). So the "embankment" reading is probably a later re-interpretation by Chinese speakers rather than the origin.

The `vietnamese` field holds the Hán-Việt reading, đê ngạn (堤 đê, 岸 ngạn; hvdic.thivien.net lists 堤 as đê and đề, and Vietnamese Wikipedia gives Đê Ngạn as the Hán-Việt name of Chợ Lớn; the variant Đề Ngạn is used in An Chi's title). It is not the everyday name: Vietnamese speakers say Chợ Lớn. Mandarin Dī'àn (written 堤'àn at English Wiktionary) and Cantonese tai4 ngon6 are the established readings; English Wiktionary also gives Hokkien thê-hōaⁿ and two Wu readings. Japanese ていがん and Korean 제안 are compositional Sino-Xenic readings built from 堤 TEI/제 and 岸 GAN/안; the quarter is normally called チョロン (Cholon) in Japanese and 쩌런 or 촐론 in Korean (from memory, not re-checked).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has a Chinese entry 堤岸 with both senses (noun "embankment"; proper noun "Cholon"), with Mandarin, Cantonese, Hokkien and Wu pronunciations. Chinese Wiktionary has a page with a pronunciation (tí'àn, dī'àn), no definition, and a Russian translation list. Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 堤岸 page. hvdic.thivien.net has no word entry for 堤岸 and lists the characters only. Vietnamese Wikipedia names 堤岸 as the chữ Hán form of Chợ Lớn. 注音 ㄙㄝㄚㄋ is shared with one word, 提案 (see the callout above); no character has it.

Dan'a'yo keeps the Chinese sense order: the ordinary embankment sense first, the Saigon quarter second. The embankment sense is fully covered by [[堤防]], so in practice 堤岸 is the name of the quarter.
