## Notes
- [会](../characters/会%20(char).md) "meet, gather" + [安](../characters/安.md) "peaceful" — no stand-in relationship applies (会's own is the single-character word [[会]]; 安's own is [[平安]]). The name is written 會安 in traditional characters, kept as an alias because 会 is the parent form on its character page.

会安 (Hội An, "Hoi An") is a small city on the Thu Bồn river estuary in the former Quảng Nam province (see [[広南]]), central Vietnam, and one of Southeast Asia's best-preserved old trading ports. From the sixteenth to the seventeenth century it was a major entrepôt where Chinese and Japanese merchants had their own quarters, and the covered "Japanese bridge" is its emblem; its old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (listed 1999). Europeans knew it as Faifo (all from memory, not re-checked). The name is read as "meeting peace" or "gathering place of peace", an auspicious port name (from memory, not re-checked).

The `japanese` field holds the compositional on'yomi かいあん; Japanese actually writes the place ホイアン, and the Japanese merchants of the Edo period knew it as 会安 (from memory, not re-checked). Korean 회안 is likewise the compositional reading. Mandarin Huì'ān and Cantonese wui6 on1 are as in English Wiktionary; Vietnamese Hội An is the real place name and a perfect compositional match (hội 會, an 安, per hvdic.thivien.net).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 會安 as a Chinese proper noun (a city in Vietnam, Mandarin Huì'ān, Cantonese wui6 on1) and a Vietnamese Han-form entry for Hội An; Chinese Wiktionary has a stub with Mandarin Huì'ān. Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no entry. No homophones (注音 ㄏ⼔ㄚㄋ unique across both words and characters).
