## Notes
- [嘆 (char)](../characters/嘆%20(char).md) "moan; sigh"

嘆 means "to sigh, moan, exclaim" (感嘆, "to exclaim, sigh with feeling"; 嘆息, "to sigh").

Mandarin tàn and Cantonese taan3 preserve the sense directly. Japanese タン (TAN, on'yomi, formal register: 嘆願, tangan, "petition, entreaty") alongside native なげ(く) (nage(ku), "to lament, grieve") cover the sense. Korean 탄 (tan) parallels the Sino-xenic reading. Vietnamese thán is the standard Hán Việt reading (cảm thán, "exclamation"; thán từ, "interjection"; thán tức, "to sigh"). The character page's candidate `than` is a genuine doublet — the vernacular, un-tonal-shifted layer of the same character, meaning "to lament, complain" (than thở, than vãn) — though it happens to be a homograph of the unrelated native word than "coal/charcoal" (from 炭, this word's own homophone [[炭]]). The remaining candidates (han, hen, thăn, thơn) are attested only as historical Nôm phonetic-loan readings of the 嘆 glyph, with no living semantic connection to "sigh" — han's living senses trace to 頇/馯 ("to greet," "rusty"), hen to an unrelated word for "asthma," thăn to 胂 ("loin meat"), and thơn has no independent living lexeme at all.

This word completes a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read tan/탄/ㄊㄚㄋ): [[灘]] ("beach; shoal") and [[炭]] ("charcoal"), both still unperfected and awaiting their own turn.
