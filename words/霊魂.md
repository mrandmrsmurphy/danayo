## Notes
- [霊](../characters/霊.md) "spirit, ghost" + [魂 (char)](../characters/魂%20(char).md) "soul" — 霊魂 is the `stand_in` that legitimizes 霊 as a standalone character.

霊魂 names the soul or spirit as the animating, surviving essence of a person — the part believed to persist beyond bodily death or to be present in supernatural beings. 霊 (líng) alone tends toward the numinous or ghostly — an ancestral spirit, a haunting presence, a sacred or uncanny force — while 魂 (hún) is the more classical philosophical term for the soul as the vital, thinking element of a person, historically paired in Chinese cosmology with 魄 (the more physical, bodily soul-component). Compounded together, 霊魂 covers the general Western-style concept of "soul/spirit" broadly enough to translate Christian, Buddhist, and secular usages alike.

Cross-linguistically the concept carries different weights of religious commitment. In Chinese contexts 靈魂 appears in Buddhist, Daoist, folk-religious, and modern secular psychological usage without any single doctrinal home — a Buddhist text may discuss it in the context of rebirth, a folk tradition in the context of ancestor worship, and a modern secular speaker simply to mean "psyche" or "spirit" in an everyday, non-committal sense. In Japanese 霊魂 (reikon) leans toward the animistic and Shinto-inflected sense of a spirit that can linger, haunt, or be enshrined, distinct from the more purely philosophical 魂 (tamashii) used in native vocabulary. Korean 영혼 and Vietnamese linh hồn both function as the standard, largely secularized translation equivalent for "soul" in Christian contexts (following extensive missionary-era Bible translation into both languages), alongside their older folk-religious and Buddhist senses.
