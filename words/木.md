## Notes
- [木 (char)](../characters/木%20(char).md) "tree; wood" (木 is its own stand-in: the word 木 legitimizes the character 木, and the character is not too bounded to stand alone)

木 in Dan'a'yo is the noun for "tree; wood" and serves as the determiner (限定詞) for trees and woody plants — appearing in naming compounds to classify arboreal species: 楠木 (camphor wood), 檀木 (sandalwood), 柊木 (holly osmanthus), 桧木 (hinoki cypress), 梓木 (catalpa wood), 樺木 (birch). The character is a canonical 象形 pictograph of a tree: vertical trunk, lateral branches above, roots below — among the most visually transparent pictographic characters, its tree-shape visible from Oracle Bone Script onward. 木 appears on the Swadesh list (entry 51: "tree") and serves as Kangxi radical 75, underlying more than 400 sinographic characters related to wood, trees, and wooden objects. As a determiner, 木 contrasts with [[草]] (herbaceous plants/grass) in marking the woody-plant category.

Mandarin 木 (mù): wood; tree — 木头 (mùtou, wood; blockhead — colloquial), 木材 (mùcái, lumber; timber), 树木 (shùmù, trees and shrubs), 木工 (mùgōng, carpentry); the everyday Mandarin word for "tree" is 树 (shù), while 木 leans toward the material "wood" in compound contexts. Cantonese 木 (muk6): 木材 (muk6 coi4, timber), 木工 (muk6 gung1, carpenter); colloquially 樹 (syu6) for a tree. Japanese 木 (き/ki, native; もく/MOKU or ぼく/BOKU, Sino-Japanese): 木 (ki, a tree), 木曜日 (mokuyōbi, Thursday — lit. "wood-day"), 木材 (mokuzai, lumber), 樹木 (jumoku, trees). Korean 나무 (namu) is the native word for tree and wood; Sino-Korean 목 (木) in compounds: 목재 (木材, lumber), 목요일 (木曜日, Thursday), 수목 (樹木, trees). Vietnamese gỗ: gỗ cứng (hardwood), cây gỗ (a tree); Sino-Vietnamese mộc (木) in compounds: mộc lan (magnolia — lit. "wood orchid"), mộc nhĩ (木耳, wood ear mushroom).

Senses (per Wiktionary's Chinese entry for 木, checked 2026-10-11): (1) tree, woody plant — the sense that places 木 on [Plants](../lexipedia/Plants.md); (2) wood, timber; (3) wooden (木屋 "wooden house"); (4) Wood, one of the five Wu Xing phases, which places 木 on [Elements](../lexipedia/Elements.md) beside 火, 土, 金, 水 (see [[五行]]); in classical usage short for 木曜日, Thursday (see [[木曜日]]); (5) literary or in compounds, coffin (就木 "to enter the coffin", i.e. to die); (6) plain, simple, slow, emotionless (木訥); (7) numb (麻木, "numb, insensitive"; 她手指凍木了 "her fingers were numb with cold"). Senses 5-7 are Chinese extensions; Dan'a'yo 木 as a free word is chiefly "tree; wood", and the rest are best expressed through compounds. The Dan'a'yo sense is the same as the shared CJKV one, with no deliberate narrowing or shift.

Compounds in the vault include [[木板]] (board), [[木版]] (printing block), [[木偶]] (puppet), [[木星]] (Jupiter), [[木曜日]] (Thursday), [[樹木]] (tree; plant), [[材木]] (lumber) and [[草木]] (vegetation); the full list is on the character page [木 (char)](../characters/木%20(char).md).

Native equivalents, for comparison only: Japanese き (ki), Korean 나무 (namu), Vietnamese cây (tree) and gỗ (wood). The Sino-Xenic bound readings are what the `japanese`, `korean` and `vietnamese` fields hold (もく/モク, 목, mộc).

Homophone note: the character [[沐]] "bathe, cleanse" shares the exact reading ㄇㄛㄎ, but has no standalone word file, so no callout is placed (沐 appears in 沐浴).
