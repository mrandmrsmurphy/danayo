## Notes
- [梯](../characters/梯.md) "ladder" + [子](../characters/子.md) "generic noun suffix" — this word is the stand-in for [梯](../characters/梯.md), which is never used on its own.

梯子 is transparently 梯 "ladder" plus 子 in its most common modern role: a light, semantically-empty noun suffix (the same 子 seen throughout [[子]]'s own huge "thing" word-list — 椅子 "chair," 帽子 "hat," 扇子 "fan" — rather than its literal "child" sense). The word denotes the ordinary portable ladder, and by extension in colloquial Japanese, the practice of visiting several venues of the same kind in one outing (梯子する, "to bar-hop" or "pub crawl").

The ordinary Japanese word is the native はしご (hashigo), which English Wiktionary glosses as "ladder" and, in a second sense, visiting several venues of the same kind in one session (pub crawl, arcade-hopping); its etymology there is marked as unresolved. The `japanese` field holds the compositional Sino-Japanese reading ていし (teishi, 梯 TEI + 子 SHI), which is not checked against a dictionary and is not an attested everyday word.

Korean has no real Sino-Korean life for this compound: everyday Korean uses the purely native word 사다리 (sadari) for "ladder," not a Hanja compound. 제자 (jeja) is used here only as the mechanical concatenation of 梯's 제 and 子's 자, as the mechanical concatenation, not an attested real word — and it happens to coincide exactly with the common Korean reading of an entirely unrelated word, 弟子 ("disciple, apprentice," also 제자), a pure homophone collision with no semantic connection. `vietnamese` is omitted because everyday Vietnamese uses the native word *thang* for "ladder," with no attested Sino-Vietnamese compound reading for 梯子 itself.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄊㄝㄐㄜ (checked 2026-10-13). The Korean and Vietnamese remarks are from memory, not re-checked.
