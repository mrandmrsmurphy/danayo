## Notes
- [礼](../characters/礼%20(char).md) "manners" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 礼 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

礼/禮 names ritual propriety, etiquette, and rites — one of the [[六芸]] (Six Arts) of the classical Chinese gentleman's education, alongside music, archery, charioteering, calligraphy, and mathematics. Classical ritual theory organized rites into the Five Rites (五禮):

1. **吉禮 (jílǐ)** — Auspicious Rites: sacrifices to gods and ancestors.
2. **凶禮 (xiōnglǐ)** — Ominous Rites: funerals, mourning, and rituals for disasters like famine.
3. **賓禮 (bīnlǐ)** — Guest Rites: etiquette for hosting guests, diplomats, or feudal lords.
4. **軍禮 (jūnlǐ)** — Army Rites: military protocols, including troop movements and victory celebrations.
5. **嘉禮 (jiālǐ)** — Festive Rites: weddings, births, and coming-of-age ceremonies.

Mandarin lǐ, Cantonese lai5, Japanese れい (rei), Korean 례 (rye), and Vietnamese lễ are all standard, fully compositional readings, retaining productive use in modern compounds such as 礼儀/禮儀 ("etiquette"), 礼物/禮物 ("a gift"), and 洗礼/洗禮 ("baptism").
