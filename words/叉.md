## Notes
- [叉 (char)](../characters/叉%20(char).md) "fork; prong; tine"

叉 is 会意: [[又]] ("right hand") + 丶 ("a dot") — fingers spread apart, holding or forming a fork shape. It names both the utensil and the general shape of anything forked or branching: [[三叉]] ("three-forked, tripartite; trident"), [[音叉]] ("tuning fork"), [[魚叉]] ("harpoon"), and [[交叉]] ("intersection," literally "a fork in the road").

Mandarin chā, Cantonese caa1, and Japanese しゃ/さい/さ (sha/sai/sa, on'yomi, rare outside proper names) alongside native また (mata, "a fork, branch," the everyday word) all preserve the sense. Korean 차 (cha) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 갈래 (gallae, "a branch, fork"). Vietnamese xoa is the Hán Việt reading.
