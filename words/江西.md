## Notes
- [江](../characters/江.md) "river" + [西](../characters/西.md) "west" — shortened from the Tang Jiangnan West Circuit. No stand-in relationship applies (江's own is [[大江]]; 西's own is [[西方]]).

This name has older and non-Chinese senses, listed first. English Wiktionary gives a historical sense, the region to the north of the lower Yangtze (sometimes including the Central Plains, in contrast with 江東), named because the river runs north-east in this stretch and the north bank is therefore west of it, and the Kangsŏ ward (江西區域) of the North Korean city of Namp'o. English Wiktionary's Korean entry for 강서 also lists a place named Gangseo, which I take to be the Gangseo district of Seoul (from memory, not re-checked).

Jiangxi, the province, is in south-eastern China, and its capital is [[南昌]] (from memory, not re-checked). English Wiktionary says the name is shortened from the Tang Jiangnan West Circuit (江南西道), and gives the one-character abbreviation 贛. The Japanese field holds こうせい (English Wiktionary Japanese); the Korean field holds the Sino-Korean 강서, which is also the name of the Seoul district and the North Korean ward, and differs from the Dan'a'yo 諺文 강세 only in the vowel of 西; the usual South Korean name for the province today is 장시 (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Giang Tây is the Sino-Vietnamese name. Cantonese gong1 sai1 is the Jyutping on English Wiktionary. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄚㄫㄙㄝㄧ unique across both words and characters).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English and Chinese Wiktionary (Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese sections) list the senses above.

1. The historical region to the north of the lower Yangtze.
2. Kangsŏ, a ward of Namp'o, North Korea.
3. Gangseo, a district of Seoul, South Korea (강서).
4. Jiangxi, the province of China.
