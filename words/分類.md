## Notes
- [分 (char)](../characters/分%20(char).md) "divide" + [類](../characters/類.md) "kind, category" — a verb-object compound, literally "to divide into kinds." Neither character's `stand_in` points here (分's is [[分]] itself, 類's is [[種類]]), so this is not a legitimizer word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 類 is individually `kwin: false` (뤼 vs. 류).

分類 is the general word for sorting things into categories, and for the result of having done so: "to classify, to categorize" and "classification." Mandarin fēnlèi (simplified 分类) and Vietnamese phân loại are used both as verbs and as nouns; Japanese ぶんるい is a noun that takes する to become a verb (分類する), and Korean 분류 likewise takes 하다. Wiktionary attests the compound in all five languages, and the Sino-Vietnamese dictionary hvdic glosses it as dividing things into classes or categories according to their nature. In Dan'a'yo it is listed as a verb, parallel to [[分析]] ("to analyze; analysis"), but the nominal reading ("a classification") is equally ordinary, and context decides.

Its relationship to its neighbors is a division of labor. [[種類]] ("type, kind") names the *kind* itself, the group that something belongs to; 分類 is the *act* of sorting things into such groups, or the scheme that results; and [[分類学]] ("taxonomy") is the *discipline* that studies how to classify. A speaker therefore says that things "are sorted into three 種類" by 分類, and a 分類 is what a 分類学 produces. It also sits near [[区分]] (a division or partition, with no commitment to the groups being natural kinds) and [[各種]] ("every kind"). The Dan'a'yo prefix and affix patterns that sort characters, as in 未分類字 ("unclassified characters"), 不可分類 ("cannot be classified") and 転分類 ("to reclassify"), are built on this verb; 転 here is the productive "change, shift" element rather than anything lexicalized in the source languages, so 転分類 is a Dan'a'yo formation and is not separately attested.

Classification has an old and prestigious history in the Sinosphere, which is why the word matters for the writing-system chapter: the traditional [[六書]] ("six categories of characters") is itself a 分類 of characters by how they are built, and dictionaries since the Shuowen Jiezi have arranged characters by [[部首]] ("radical"), a second classification principle. Modern uses (図書分類 "library classification," 分類学 "taxonomy," 分類問題 "classification problem" in machine learning) all descend from the same neutral sense. Across the five languages the meaning is stable; there is no known clash with a different established word of the same spelling.

Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese readings are all standard (Wiktionary, hvdic). Korean 분류 is also the reading of other Sino-Korean words (for example 分流, "branch stream"), a purely Korean-side accident that does not affect Dan'a'yo's own spelling.
