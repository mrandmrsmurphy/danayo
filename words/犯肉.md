## Notes
- [犯](../characters/犯.md) "violate, offend" + [肉](../characters/肉%20(char).md) "meat, flesh" — "violate flesh," the crude verb "to fuck." Their own `stand_in` values: 犯's is [[犯罪]]; 肉's is itself; none points here, so 犯肉 is an independent compound.

犯肉 is the crude verb "to fuck." It is a **vulgar neologism** and a Dan'a'yo-original coinage, in the same spirit as [[臭膣]] ("bitch"): curse words do not travel across languages, and without a coinage Dan'a'yo would have no crude register at all, only the clinical [[性交]], the literary [[交溝]] and the euphemistic [[交接]]. The compound is 犯 "to violate, offend" plus 肉 "flesh," "to violate flesh." The vulgar sense of 犯 has a real basis in the other languages: Japanese 犯す (おかす) means to violate or rape, and the slang 犯る (やる) is the crude "do, fuck"; Korean 범하다 (犯하다) also means to violate; and older Chinese used 犯 of violating a woman. The word carries a slightly attacking vibe, and the overlap with [[強姦]] (rape) is accepted for so vulgar a word. Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and Korean readings in the fields are mechanical, built from the two characters, and are not claims of use.

**Grammar.** 犯肉 is a verb-object compound, like [[犯罪]] "commit a crime," so it cannot take a further object directly. The partner is added with an adposition, the comitative [[共]] ("with"), as in 共彼犯肉 "fuck with him/her" (nothing is done *to* the partner as an object), or the subject is plural, as in 彼等犯肉 "they fuck." Compare [[侵犯]] (to violate) and [[犯人]] (a criminal).
