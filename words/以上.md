## Notes
- [以 (char)](../characters/以%20(char).md) "by means of; (as a boundary marker) from X onward" + [上 (char)](../characters/上%20(char).md) "above" — "from this point upward," hence "above, more than, and up." Neither character's own `stand_in` points here (以's is itself, 上's is itself), so no legitimizer relationship applies.

以上 is the upward member of the 以 family that includes [[以前]] "before" and [[以外]] "other than": 以 marks a reference point, and 上 says the region meant lies above it. Its main use is after a number or quantity, where it means "that amount or more": 二年以上 "two years or more," 百人以上 "a hundred people or more." The stated number is included, so 二年以上 covers exactly two years as well as longer, unlike English "over two years," which excludes it; the best English rendering is "two years and up." It can equally follow a rank, degree, or position (課長以上 "section chief and above"), and then it names everything at or higher than that point on the scale.

Like the other postposition-like nouns of this family, 以上 follows the noun or quantity it anchors and does not precede it. A second use is textual: 以上 points back at everything already said or listed ("the above"), and in Japanese it is also written alone at the end of a list, letter, or speech to mean "that is all." That "aforementioned" sense is the neighbour of [[上述]], which is a modifier ("the aforementioned X") rather than a pointer to the whole preceding stretch.

It contrasts with 以前, which is about time before a point (and does not include the point), and with 以外, which picks out what lies outside a named set and excludes it. 以上 is also the opposite of 以下 "below, or less," which has no page here yet. Japanese 以上 unambiguously includes the number it is anchored to. Mandarin 以上 normally does as well, and Chinese law states that 以上, 以下 and 以内 include the stated number, but the English Wiktionary notes that in everyday Chinese the interval can be read as inclusive or exclusive depending on context. In Dan'a'yo it is inclusive, as in Japanese.

Checked 2026-10-11 through the Wiktionary parse API. The Chinese Wiktionary has 以上 as a noun (Mandarin yǐshàng, Cantonese ji5 soeng6) glossed as above a point in order, degree, number or position, as "greater than or equal to," and as a summing-up of the preceding text, with antonym 以下. The English Wiktionary agrees and adds the sentence-final internet-slang use "that's all." The Japanese Wiktionary gives いじょう, with the number sense and the sense of "all that has been said above," plus the closing formula at the end of lists and letters. Korean 이상 is attested as the Sino-Korean reading of the hanja form 以上 on the Korean and Chinese Wiktionaries (the Korean Wiktionary glosses it only as 이상, without a separate definition). Vietnamese has no 以上 page on the Vietnamese Wiktionary; `vietnamese: dĩ thượng` is attested instead by hvdic.thivien.net ("more than, above a level," "going upward") and is given as the Vietnamese reading in the Korean Wiktionary's 以上 entry. From memory, not re-checked: everyday Vietnamese tends to use the native trở lên instead.

Dan'a'yo keeps the shared CJKV sense and does not narrow, shift, or broaden it. It sits with [[以内]] "within" and [[以外]] "other than" among the grammatical words that mark a limit.
