## Notes
- [今 (char)](../characters/今%20(char).md) "now"

今 is 指事: an indicator beneath 亼 (a covered gathering) marking the present moment; Shuowen reads it as an inverted 曰 ("to speak"), suggesting the present is specifically the moment of speech — what is being said right now. It is one of the most frequent characters in the entire Classical Chinese corpus and heads a large family of temporal compounds: [[今年]] ("this year"), [[今夜]] ("tonight"), and the vault's own greeting compounds [[今朝安]]/[[今昼安]]/[[今夜安]] ("good morning/afternoon/evening").

Mandarin jīn, Cantonese gam1, and Vietnamese kim (the Hán Việt reading, as in kim nhật 今日 "today," and hiện kim 現今 "at present") all preserve the sense directly. Japanese has two on'yomi, こん/きん (kon/kin, fully productive in compounds like 今日 きょう "today" and 今年 ことし "this year," both irregular kun-readings built on this character) alongside native いま (ima), the everyday word for "now." Korean 금 (geum) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 이제 (ije, "now").

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (gim/김/ㄍㄧㄇ) with [[金]] ("metal") — both sharing the same OC phonetic ancestor, 亼, per 金's own character-page etymology, so the homophone here is a genuine phonetic-family relationship rather than pure coincidence.
