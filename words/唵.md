## Notes
- [唵 (char)](characters/唵%20(char).md) "Om" — stand-in for [[唵 (char)|唵]], which cannot appear independently

唵 is a learned Sanskrit transliteration character, rendering ॐ (oṃ), the sacred syllable that opens most Buddhist and Hindu mantras (唵嘛呢叭咪吽, "Om mani padme hum," the six-syllable mantra of Avalokiteśvara).

**Content corrected**: this entry's English gloss previously read "Ohm" — the SI unit of electrical resistance (Ω, named for Georg Ohm), an unrelated word. Corrected to "Om," matching the Sanskrit sacred syllable this character actually transliterates (the second listed gloss, "Aum," the fuller Sanskrit spelling, was already correct).

Mandarin ǎn and Cantonese am2 render the transliteration directly. Japanese アン/オン (AN/ON, on'yomi, confined to Buddhist mantra contexts) cover the reading; the character's only kun'yomi, ふく (fuku, "to hold in the mouth"), belongs strictly to the unrelated native Chinese sense the graph (口+奄) was originally built for, not to this borrowed sense — left blank on the character page rather than reused here. Korean actually splits the character's readings by sense rather than reusing one across both: 옴 (om) is the reading proper to this Sanskrit borrowing, distinct from 암 (am), which belongs to the native "hold in mouth" sense — corrected from an earlier version of this page that used 암. Vietnamese úm is the vernacular Buddhist-register reading, attested directly in the mantra transliteration "Úm ma ni bát di hồng" (Om mani padme hum) and confirmed via hvdic.thivien.net as the specific reading for this sense. The character page's other stored candidate, ướm, is a genuine but wholly unrelated Nôm phonetic loan — 唵 reused for its sound to write the unconnected native Vietnamese word "to try, try on" (ướm giày, "to try on shoes") — excluded here as it has nothing to do with the Sanskrit sense.
