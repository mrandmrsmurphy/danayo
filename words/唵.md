## Notes
- [唵 (char)](../characters/唵%20(char).md) "Om"

唵 is a learned Sanskrit transliteration character, rendering ॐ (oṃ), the sacred syllable that opens most Buddhist and Hindu mantras (唵嘛呢叭咪吽, "Om mani padme hum," the six-syllable mantra of Avalokiteśvara).

**Content corrected**: this entry's English gloss previously read "Ohm" — the SI unit of electrical resistance (Ω, named for Georg Ohm), an unrelated word. Corrected to "Om," matching the Sanskrit sacred syllable this character actually transliterates (the second listed gloss, "Aum," the fuller Sanskrit spelling, was already correct).

Mandarin ǎn and Cantonese am2 render the transliteration directly. Japanese アン/オン (AN/ON, on'yomi, confined to Buddhist mantra contexts) alongside native ふく (fuku, "to hold in the mouth," reflecting the character's own 口+奄 composition) cover the reading. Korean 암 (am) parallels the Sino-xenic reading. Vietnamese úm is the vernacular Buddhist-register reading, attested directly in the mantra transliteration "Úm ma ni bát di hồng" (Om mani padme hum); a more formal/literary doublet, án, is also attested for the same sense. The character page's other stored candidate, ướm, is a genuine but wholly unrelated Nôm phonetic loan — 唵 reused for its sound to write the unconnected native Vietnamese word "to try, try on" (ướm giày, "to try on shoes") — excluded here as it has nothing to do with the Sanskrit sense.
