## Notes
- [支](../characters/支.md) "branch" + [離](../characters/離.md) "to depart, separate" — branching apart, i.e. fragmented, disjointed, incoherent (of writing, an argument, or a situation falling to pieces).

Mandarin zhī lí and Cantonese zi1 lei4 are standard, fully compositional readings, matching 支's and 離's own stored citations exactly, and confirmed via Wiktionary with the sense "to disintegrate, disunite" (verb) / "incoherent, disjointed, fragmented, broken" (adjective) — notably, Wiktionary does *not* list "boring" among the attested Mandarin/Cantonese senses. Japanese しり and Vietnamese chi le are compositional only, not independently confirmed.

**A genuine Korean semantic drift, worth noting explicitly**: Korean 지리 (jiri), the direct Sino-Korean reading of this same compound, nativized to 지루 and is the root of 지루하다 (jiruhada), the everyday Korean word for "boring, tedious, dull." This is a real, documented sound/sense shift (支離 zhīlí "fragmented" → 지리 → 지루하다 "boring") — plausibly because something disjointed and incoherent also tends to feel tedious. Dan'a'yo keeps this word's primary sense as "fragmented, disjointed" (the pan-Sinitic meaning) rather than "boring" (the Korean-specific development); see [[無聊]] for the pan-CJKV word actually meaning "boring." `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 저레 diverges from Sino-Korean 지리 in both syllables. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄝㄌㄝㄧ unique among both words and characters).
