## Etymology
- [蹴 (char)](../characters/蹴%20(char).md) "kick" + [鞠](../characters/鞠.md) "raise, bow" — literally "kick-ball."

## Notes
This word covers a cluster of cognate pre-modern East Asian football-like games rather than a single translated concept: Chinese cùjū (蹴鞠), Japanese kemari (蹴鞠, けまり), and Korean chukguk (蹴鞠, 축국) are each that culture's own real, independently-attested name for its own historical game, not compositional coinages — comparable to the proper-noun/named-referent exception used elsewhere in this vault (cf. [[冥王星]], [[出谷記]]). Mandarin cùjū/cùjú, cantonese cuk1 guk1, japanese けまり, and korean 축국 are all directly attested. Vietnamese xúc cúc is the honest compositional reading (蹴's own xúc + 鞠's own cúc); no independently attested Vietnamese name for a cognate game was found. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 축국 matches Sino-Korean 축국 exactly. No homophones (注音 ㄑㄨㄎㄍㄨㄎ unique among both words and characters).
