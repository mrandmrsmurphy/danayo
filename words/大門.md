## Notes
- [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) "big" + [門](../characters/門.md) "gate, door" — literally "big gate," though in living usage 大 no longer carries an active sense of "big" here; it simply distinguishes the main/front gate of a building from any smaller side or interior doors, without implying the gate itself is unusually large. Perhaps the ancient precursor this contrasted against — a smaller, everyday household door — is what made the "big" sense meaningful in the first place, before drifting into a fixed, no-longer-literal label.

大門/大门 names the main gate or entrance of a building or compound — the front door in its most formal, architectural sense, as opposed to a side door or an interior doorway.

Mandarin dàmén, Cantonese daai6 mun4, Korean 대문 (daemun), and Japanese だいもん (daimon) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday term for "main gate" in its language. Vietnamese đại môn is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 大's đại and 門's own môn, and is attested in real architectural contexts (e.g. 大成門, Đại Thành Môn, a Confucian-temple gate name). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 대몬 diverges from Sino-Korean 대문 in the second syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄉㄚㄧㄇㄛㄋ unique among both words and characters).
