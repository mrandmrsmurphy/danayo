## Notes
- [複](../characters/複.md) "compound, composite" + [素](../characters/素.md) "element" + [数](../characters/数.md) "number" — literally "compound-element number," a complex number *a*+*bi*, where *a* and *b* are real numbers and *i* is the imaginary unit.

Japanese ふくそすう and Korean 복소수 both independently spell this concept with the extra middle character 素 ("element"), deliberately avoiding the genuine ambiguity of Mandarin and Cantonese: 複數/复数 (fùshù/fuk1 sou3) does double duty in Chinese for both "plural" (grammatical) *and* "complex number" (mathematical), disambiguated only by context. Dan'a'yo follows the Japanese/Korean pattern rather than the Mandarin one, for a concrete reason: this vault's own [[複数]] already names the grammatical sense ("plural"), so reusing that same compound for the mathematical sense would create a genuine collision within Dan'a'yo itself — the same collision Japanese and Korean resolved by inserting 素, independently of each other. `mandarin`/`cantonese` here hold the real Chinese term fùshù/fuk1 sou3 (identical to [[複数]]'s own, reflecting the real ambiguity in those languages), not a fabricated compositional three-character reading. Vietnamese số phức is the standard, fully compositional, living term.
