## Notes
- [不 (char)](../characters/不%20(char).md) "not" + [許 (char)](../characters/許%20(char).md) "to permit" — the direct negation of 許, "to forbid, to not allow."

不許 functions as both a plain verb ("to forbid") and, especially in Mandarin, an imperative prohibition when placed directly before another verb (不許動! "Don't move!", literally "not permitted to move") — a construction with real rhetorical force, common in commands and official notices. Japanese ふきょ (不許可, fukyoka, "disapproval, non-permission") and Korean 불허 both stay closer to the formal/bureaucratic register (denying an official application or request) than the sharper imperative-command use Mandarin makes of it. Vietnamese bất hứa (confirmed via web search) carries the same plain "not permitted" meaning. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 볻효 diverges from Sino-Korean 불허 in both syllables. No homophones (注音 ㄅㄛㄊㄏ⼄ unique among both words and characters).
