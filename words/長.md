## Notes
- [長 (char)](../characters/長%20(char).md) "long" — stand-in for [[長 (char)|長]], which cannot appear independently

長 is 象形 of an old man with long hair, the character page's own Notes cross-referencing the same top component shared with [[老]] and its variant [[考]] — an ancient alternate form 镸 survives today only in a couple of compounds. **Note**: `characters/長 (char).md` carries `date-last-perfect: 2026-03-01`, the oldest surviving stamp found in this sweep — its own `## Notes` uses informal prose in place of the standard SKIP/Stroke/MC/Levels bullet structure, has no `## Words`/`## Derived Characters` headings at all (just bare bullets), and stores `vietnamese` as a single malformed comma-separated string (`trường, trườn, tràng, trành, trưởng`) rather than a proper YAML list — flagged here for the character-perfection sweep rather than rebuilt inside this word-creation pass.

Beyond the literal hair-length imagery, 長 splits into two related but distinct senses already visible in its own compound family: "long" in extent (長矛 "spear, lance," 長靴 "boot," 長短 "length, long and short," 延長 "to extend, prolong") and "elder, chief, senior" (長上 "elder, superior") — the same dual sense recurring in this character's own Vietnamese and Japanese reading sets. `trường` was picked from the character's noisy stored Vietnamese string as the reading matching this word's own "long" gloss (trường kỳ "long-term," trường thọ "longevity") — the string's other candidates split across the elder/chief sense (trưởng, as in trưởng lão "elder") and unrelated verb forms (trườn, "to crawl"). Japanese carries a single on'yomi ちょう (CHOU, 延長 enchō), but the character's own two native readings track the same sense-split directly: おさ (osa, "a chief, an elder") and なが-い (nagai, "long," the ordinary everyday adjective). `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 장 (jang, from Middle Chinese 澄母 ɖ + 陽開韻 ɨɐŋ) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 장.

**Real homophone group found**: `注音 ㄐㄚㄫ`/`장` is also carried by two other existing standalone words, [[杖]] ("staff, stick") and [[章]] ("chapter"). Added the full mutual callout across all three pages.
