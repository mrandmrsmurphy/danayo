## Notes
- [女 (char)](../characters/女%20(char).md) "woman" — stand-in for [[女 (char)|女]], which cannot appear independently

女 is a 象形 pictograph of a kneeling woman with folded arms — one of the vault's most fundamental characters (`danayo_id: 25`) and itself Radical 038 — yet across the whole CJKV sphere the bare morpheme rarely stands alone as an ordinary word for "woman." Each language instead reaches for a native or compounded substitute in everyday speech, keeping 女/nǚ/녀/nữ largely confined to compounds, classifiers, and formal registers: Mandarin uses 女的 or 女生 colloquially and reserves bare 女 for classifier-like slots (男女 "male and female," forms marked 男/女) or literary compounds (女人, 女性); Cantonese similarly favors 女仔 in speech; Vietnamese nữ survives almost exclusively in Sino-Vietnamese compounds (phụ nữ "women," nữ sinh "female student"), with the everyday word being the native đàn bà/con gái; and Korean 여자 (yeoja, "woman") is itself already a compound (女子) rather than the bare morpheme standing alone. This near-universal reluctance to use the bare character independently is exactly what its high `boundedness` score (80) captures.

Japanese is the outlier with the richest life for this morpheme: alongside the Sino-Japanese on'yomi じょ (JO, as in 女性 josei, 女優 joyū), it keeps a fully native訓読み reading おんな (onna) that functions as an ordinary standalone word for "woman," and a third on'yomi にょ (NYO, historically borrowed via Buddhist Chinese) survives in fixed Buddhist/classical vocabulary such as 天女 (tennyo, "heavenly maiden/celestial nymph") — a three-way reading split (じょ/にょ/おんな) unusually wide even by the standards of common Jōyō characters.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 느 (nǝ, from Middle Chinese 娘母 ɳ + 魚韻 ɨʌ) diverges from Sino-Korean 녀 in both onset and vowel — Dan'a'yo flattens the MC 魚韻 final to a plain schwa-like -ǝ, while Sino-Korean regularizes the same final to -yeo. Per the vault's standing convention, the `korean` field records the North Korean/문화어 form 녀 rather than the South Korean 두음법칙-shifted 여 (which drops the word-initial ㄴ before a following i/y-glide) — 女 is in fact the textbook example of that very sound law, since 女子 is pronounced 녀자 in the North and 여자 in the South.
