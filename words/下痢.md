## Notes
- [下 (char)](../characters/下%20(char).md) "down, below" + [痢](../characters/痢.md) "dysentery-like illness" — literally "downward [flow of] illness," a transparent medical description of the condition itself.

**Content check, not a gap**: `korean` is stored as 설사, the ordinary everyday Korean word for diarrhea — not the compositional reading 하리 that 下's own 하 + 痢's own 리 would give. This is a deliberate real-word choice rather than an oversight: 하리 (下痢) does exist as a Sino-Korean medical/classical term, but 설사 (itself ultimately from a different Sino-Korean pair, 泄瀉) is overwhelmingly the word Korean speakers actually use, the same "prefer the real common word over a compositional derivation" logic already applied elsewhere in this vault. Japanese げり (geri) likewise uses its own native/lexicalized reading rather than a plain on'yomi concatenation (下痢 read on'yomi would give げり anyway here, a coincidence — げ is 下's rarer on'yomi specifically preserved in medical vocabulary, distinct from the far more common そう/if). Vietnamese hạ lị (confirmed via web search) is the classical/formal Hán Việt reading, with modern everyday Vietnamese instead using the native tiêu chảy or ỉa chảy. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 하릐 diverges from Sino-Korean 설사 entirely, a case where the two aren't even built from related readings, similar in kind to [[三稜鏡]]'s loanword-vs-compositional divergence earlier this stretch. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄚㄌㄧㄜ unique among both words and characters).
