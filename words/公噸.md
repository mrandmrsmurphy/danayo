## Notes
- [公 (char)](../characters/公%20(char).md) "public, metric" + [噸 (char)](../characters/噸%20(char).md) "ton" — "tonne, metric ton." 噸 is itself a purely phonetic loan-character for English "ton" (per its own page, with no genuine native gloss in any daughter language), so 公噸's readings largely follow that same pattern. Mandarin gōngdūn and Cantonese gung1 deon1 are both directly attested standard terms for "metric ton" in Chinese-speaking regions.

**Real content bugs found and fixed**: `korean` had been filled with 메트릭톤 (a different, English-loanword-based Korean phrase, "metric ton"), not this word's own compositional reading — corrected to 공톤 (公's own 공 + 噸's own 톤), which conveniently also matches this word's own 諺文 exactly, flipping `kwin` from false to true. `japanese` had crammed two candidates into one comma-joined string, one of which (グラムトン, "gram-ton") doesn't correspond to any real unit and looks like corpus noise — trimmed to the one genuinely attested term, メトリックトン; Japanese has no kanji-compositional reading in real use here, matching 噸's own lack of a native kun'yomi. `vietnamese: công đốn` is the honest compositional reading (公's own công + 噸's own đốn); the real everyday Vietnamese word for this unit is the native tấn. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄛㄫㄊㄛㄋ unique among both words and characters).
