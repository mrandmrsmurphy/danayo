## Notes
- [気 (char)](../characters/気%20(char).md) "feeling" + [炎](../characters/炎.md) "flame, inflammation" — 炎 here stands for 焰 "flame" in the original 氣焰

Carries both senses of the Chinese 氣焰: "arrogance, bluster" (derogatory) and "imposing force, fighting spirit" (neutral or positive). The Japanese きえん tends toward the latter sense.

The original spelling is 氣焰 (also written 気焔 or 気焰), with 焰 "flame": the "flames of one's spirit", an energetic show of vitality.  気炎 is the Japanese-style spelling that substitutes the common 炎 for the rarer 焰, and the older forms are listed as aliases.  The Mandarin and Cantonese fields give the readings of the real word 氣焰 (qìyàn, hei3 jim6), where 焰 is yàn and jim6, not the readings of 炎 itself (yán, jim4).  A typical use is 気炎を上げる, "to hold forth with spirit", often said of a lively, boastful talker, and 気炎万丈, "towering spirits".

Korean 기염 follows the same compound.  `kwin` is false because the Dan'a'yo 킈엄 differs from the Korean 기염.  No Vietnamese form was found attested, so none is given.
