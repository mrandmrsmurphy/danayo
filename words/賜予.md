## Notes
- [賜](../characters/賜.md) "grant, bestow" + [予](../characters/予.md) "beforehand"

賜予 "to bestow, to grant" pairs near-synonymous verbs of giving — 賜 (formal, often used of a superior granting to an inferior, as in imperial gifts) and 予 (here used in its older sense "to give," distinct from its more common modern sense "beforehand"). The Japanese reading しよ corresponds to 賜与, a formal/administrative term for an official grant or allowance; 사여 is the parallel Sino-Korean term. 賜予 also serves as the **stand-in** for [賜](../characters/賜.md), which is too bounded to appear alone in Dan'a'yo discourse.

The Existence page lists 賜予 on one line with [贈与](贈与.md), as "to donate, bestow, grant," and the two differ mainly in direction and register: 贈与 is a plain gift or donation between parties, while 賜予 is a bestowal from above (a ruler, a superior, an authority), as the Notes above describe. A neighbor is [授与](授与.md) "to confer, award," which is the conferring of an honor or qualification. The alias 賜與 is the traditional spelling, and the readings are Mandarin cìyǔ, Cantonese ci3 jyu5, Japanese shiyo (しよ) and Korean 사여. Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "bestowal, grant, gift," with the formal, from-above register as its characteristic.
