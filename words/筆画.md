## Notes
- [筆 (char)](characters/筆%20(char).md) "pen; stylus" + [画](characters/画.md) "draw" — a stroke of a CJK character, the minimal unit of its construction.

**Content corrected**: `korean` was `획수` (hoeksu), which actually means "stroke count, number of strokes" — a related but distinct concept from this word's own sense. Corrected to `필획` (pilhoek), the Standard Korean Dictionary's term for "the dots and strokes that make up a character," an explicit synonym of 字劃 (jahoek) and the correct match for "a stroke" itself.

Mandarin bǐhuà and Cantonese bat1 waak6 preserve the sense directly (筆画順序, "stroke order"). Japanese ひっかく (hikkaku) is a genuine dictionary-attested term (字画, jikaku, is a common synonym), glossed as the line or dot produced from when the brush touches the paper to when it lifts. Korean 필획 (pilhoek) parallels the Sino-xenic reading. Vietnamese bút hoạ is directly attested as the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading (bút 筆 + hoạ 畫), glossed "nét trong chữ Hán" ("a stroke in a Han character"); it functions as a dictionary calque rather than everyday vocabulary, however — ordinary Vietnamese simply says nét ("a stroke, line") on its own (chữ này có mấy nét, "how many strokes does this character have"). 画's other stored Hán Việt reading, hoạch (as in kế hoạch, "plan"), covers 画's separate "to demarcate, plan" sense rather than "draw/stroke"; the remaining candidates (dạch, vạch, vệch) are Nôm (vernacular) readings for unrelated native words in the "line, mark, streak" family, not Sino-Vietnamese doublets.
