## Notes
- [挺](../characters/挺.md) "stand upright" + [身](../characters/身.md) "body"
- This word is the viable form of [挺](../characters/挺.md): the character's meaning (to stand upright) is identical, but 挺 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 挺身 is its `stand_in` compound.

挺身 literally "to straighten the body, throw out the chest", and so "to step forward boldly, to put oneself forward". Mandarin tǐngshēn is best known in the set phrase 挺身而出 ("to step forward courageously" when others hold back or a crisis arises); Japanese ていしん (teishin; 挺身する, "to volunteer oneself, throw oneself into") and Korean 정신 (jeongsin; in the historical term 정신대, "volunteer corps") use the same compound. The Korean reading is a homophone of 精神 (정신, "spirit, mind"), which the context normally separates.

Dan'a'yo-specific sense: the same as the shared one. In the Conflict domain it names the act, stepping forward to face danger or take responsibility, and complements [[勇敢]], which describes the courageous trait or person.
