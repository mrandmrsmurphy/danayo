## Notes
- [圓 (char)](../characters/圓%20(char).md) "round, circle" + [滑 (char)](../characters/滑%20(char).md) "slippery, smooth" — literally "round and slippery." No stand-in relationship applies (both characters' own `stand_in` point to themselves).

A genuine register divergence, not a shared meaning: in Mandarin, 圓滑 describes a **person** and carries a mildly negative shade — slick, glib, evasively smooth in dealing with people, avoiding real friction or commitment to protect oneself rather than out of genuine tact (compare [[圓融]] above, the same "round" image without that self-serving undertone). Japanese えんかつ and Korean 원활, by contrast, are used mainly of a **process** — smooth, without a hitch (円滑な進行, "smooth progress"; 원활한 소통, "smooth communication") — usually neutral-to-positive, not a character judgment at all.

Mandarin yuánhuá, Cantonese jyun4 waat6, Japanese えんかつ, Korean 원활, and Vietnamese viên hoạt are all standard, fully compositional readings. `kwin` is true: both 圓 and 滑 are individually `kwin: true`. No homophones (注音 ⼔ㄋㄏ⺢ㄊ unique across both words and characters).
