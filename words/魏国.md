## Notes
- [魏](../characters/魏.md) "Wei" + [国](../characters/国.md) "country" — legitimizing note: 魏's own `stand_in` is [[魏国]] itself, already annotated in its own citation; 国's own `stand_in` is [[国家]], so transitivity fails — no `#cranberry`.

Unlike modern nation-states (which nearly always take the "-国" suffix, e.g. 韓国, 中国), Chinese historical states from the Three Kingdoms period are conventionally referred to by their bare name alone: Japanese ぎ, Korean 위, and Sino-Vietnamese Ngụy all give just 魏's own individual reading rather than a compositional reading of the full 魏国 compound. Mandarin Wèiguó and Cantonese ngai6gwok3 do keep the full compound here for disambiguation, since 魏 alone functions as a common surname.
