## Notes
- [痴](../characters/痴.md) "stupid, foolish" + [情](../characters/情.md) "feelings, emotion" — literally "foolish passion." 痴's own stand-in is [[痴漢]] and 情's is [[感情]], so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply; neither constituent is legitimized by this word.

痴情 names an infatuation so strong it overrides judgment — passion that has tipped into foolishness. The sense rides entirely on 痴's negative charge: unlike a neutral word for "love" or "affection," 痴情 frames the emotion as excessive, blind, or unreasoning from the outset.

Usage splits along a fault line across the sphere. In Mandarin and Cantonese the word skews toward an almost admiring register: 痴情 (chīqíng) and 痴情種 (chīqíngzhǒng, "a hopeless romantic; one who loves faithfully and without reservation") describe unwavering, self-sacrificing devotion — foolish in the eyes of the world, perhaps, but sincere. Japanese ちじょう (chijō) and Korean 치정 (chijeong) instead lean toward the word's darker edge, appearing overwhelmingly in a legal/crime register: 痴情のもつれ (chijō no motsure) and 치정 사건 (chijeong sageon) both mean "crime of passion," where the infatuation curdles into obsession, jealousy, or violence. Vietnamese si tình sits closer to the Chinese pole, used of someone hopelessly, helplessly smitten (yêu si tình, "to love to the point of infatuation").
