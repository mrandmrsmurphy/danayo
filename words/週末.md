## Notes
- [週](characters/週.md) "week" + [末](characters/末.md) "end"

週末 means "weekend."

Mandarin zhōumò and Cantonese zau1 mut6 preserve the sense directly. Japanese しゅうまつ (shūmatsu) and Korean 주말 (jumal) are both the standard, everyday words for "weekend" in their respective languages.

Vietnamese cuối tuần ("weekend," literally "end of week") is the everyday native term, but it is not a compositional reading of 週末's own two characters, and its second element has a notable etymological wrinkle: tuần ("week") does not derive from 週/周 at all — it descends from the unrelated character 旬 (xún, "a 10-day period," one of the three ten-day divisions of a lunar month), whose meaning stretched to cover the imported 7-day week when Vietnamese never calqued 週/周 for that concept the way Mandarin/Japanese/Korean did. 週's own actual Hán Việt reading is chu (chu niên 週年, "anniversary"; chu kỳ, "cycle, period") — never used to mean "week" in Vietnamese; the character page's other candidate, châu, is a secondary Nôm-associated reading, not a distinct word for "week" either. cuối itself is native Vietnamese for "end/final," unrelated to 末.
