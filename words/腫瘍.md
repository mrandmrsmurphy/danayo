>[!caution] The senses split — Japanese and Korean "tumor", Chinese a traditional-medicine term
>腫瘍 is a tumor in Japanese and Korean, and the Dan'a'yo sense. Chinese uses 腫瘤 (zhǒngliú) for a tumor and records 腫瘍 only as a traditional-medicine term for the early stage of a pus-forming infection. Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).

## Notes
- [腫](../characters/腫.md) "to swell; tumor" + [瘍](../characters/瘍.md) "ulcer; sore"

腫瘍 is a tumor or neoplasm: an abnormal growth of tissue. English Wiktionary glosses Japanese しゅよう as "(oncology, pathology) tumor" and Korean 종양 as "tumor". For Chinese 腫瘍 (Mandarin zhǒngyáng, Cantonese zung2 joeng4) it gives only the traditional-medicine sense "early, nonulcerated stage of pyogenic infection"; the ordinary Chinese word for a tumor is 腫瘤 (zhǒngliú), which Dan'a'yo does not use. The Dan'a'yo sense is the Japanese and Korean one, and the word is listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).

This word is the viable form of [腫](../characters/腫.md): the character means a swelling or tumor, but is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo, so 腫瘍 is its `stand_in` compound. The related swelling is the [腫脹](腫脹.md), and a malignant tumor is [癌症](癌症.md), cancer.
