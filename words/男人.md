## Notes
- [男](../characters/男.md) "man" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" — stand-in for [[男]], which cannot appear independently

男人 is "man, male" — the same construction pattern as [[女人]] ("woman"), reinforcing 男 with the general noun 人. 男 is never used on its own, so 男人 is its stand-in word.

Mandarin 男人 (nánrén) is everyday and neutral, the direct counterpart of 女人, alongside the more formal/clinical 男性 (nánxìng, "male"). Cantonese 男人 (naam4jan4) patterns the same way. Unlike its counterpart, though, this word doesn't have the same well-attested archaic/ritual-register life in Japanese, Korean, or Vietnamese: [[女人]] found a real, independently attested literary reading in all three (にょにん, 녀인/여인, nữ nhân), tied specifically to women being singled out as a ritually marked category (女人禁制, "forbidden to women," a real historical Buddhist practice) — men, as the unmarked default in that context, plausibly never needed a parallel term (an interpretation, not a sourced claim). The readings above (だんじん, 남인, nam nhân) are therefore given as compositional cross-references rather than claims of natural everyday or literary usage. **Korean 남인 deserves a specific caution**: it is homophonous with 南人 ("southerner"), the name of a real and historically significant Joseon-dynasty political faction (Namin, one of the major factional parties) — a genuine ambiguity risk if this reading were ever used in running Korean text.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄋㄚㄇㄋㄧㄋ (checked 2026-10-13). The Namin faction remark and the register claims above are from memory, not re-checked.
