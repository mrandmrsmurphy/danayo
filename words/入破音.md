## Notes
- [入 (char)](../characters/入%20(char).md) "enter" + [破 (char)](../characters/破%20(char).md) "burst" + [音](../characters/音.md) "sound" — no stand-in relationship applies (入 and 破 are their own stand-ins; 音's is [[音楽]]).

入破音 is the "inward burst": the implosive, a stop in which the larynx is lowered and the airstream is drawn *into* the mouth, as the speaker closes and releases the airway while the vocal folds vibrate (a voiced glottalic ingressive stop). It is the second member of Dan'a'yo's three-way system of bursts, between the plain outward [[破裂音]] and the throat-driven [[喉破音]]. Vietnamese is the one source language in which this matters for learners: its b and đ are implosive for many speakers, and the phonology page notes that a stop produced as an implosive is a non-standard but intelligible realization.

The word is **Japanese** 入破音 (にゅうはおん), and Dan'a'yo takes the term from there rather than from the other two languages that name this sound. Chinese says 內爆音 and Korean 내파음, which use 内 "inward" for the implosive; but in Japanese phonetics 内破音 means the *unreleased* stop, a closure held with no audible release (a final -p, -t or -k), and Japanese keeps that term clearly separate from 入破音. Dan'a'yo already has its own word for the unreleased coda stop, 無放出無声塞音, so it keeps 入破音 for the implosive and leaves 内破音 unused to avoid the collision. 入破 also shares 破 "burst" with [[破裂音]], so the system reads as a set, and Dan'a'yo does not take Chinese 爆.

The `mandarin`, `cantonese` and `korean` fields give the literal reading of the characters, as for other coined forms, and are not the native terms (Chinese 內爆音, Korean 내파음). Wiktionary and hvdic have no Vietnamese entry, so that field is omitted. No homophones (注音 ㄋㄧㄆㄆㄜㄨㄇ unique across both words and characters).
