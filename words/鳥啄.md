## Notes
- [鳥 (char)](../characters/鳥%20(char).md) "bird" + [啄 (char)](../characters/啄%20(char).md) "peck"

Wiktionary's entry for 鳥啄 (simplified 鸟啄) is a Kinmen Hokkien word, "pickaxe"; 啄 is glossed "to peck (to strike with the beak or bill)". Wikipedia (Eight Principles of Yong) records 鳥啄 (niǎozhuó, "bird's pecking") as Li Puguang's metaphorical name, from his Yuan-era 永字八法解, for the seventh principle, the left-falling stroke (啄, modern 撇). See [[撇]], [[怪石]] and the [[Stroke]] page. In Dan'a'yo the calligraphy sense is the one used. Its readings are compositional.
