## Notes
- [皆 (char)](../characters/皆%20(char).md) "all; every; universal" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person; human being"

皆人 is the collective (universal) correlative in the 人 ("person") column of the correlative table: "everyone, all people, everybody taken together". The character [皆 (char)](../characters/皆%20(char).md) (Dan'a'yo *gye*, ㄍ⼶) is the quantifier "all; every", the free word being [皆](皆.md); [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) (Dan'a'yo *nin*, ㄋㄧㄋ) is the plain word for a person. Where the distributive [毎人](毎人.md) goes through the people one at a time, 皆人 treats them as one totality: the predicate holds of the whole group at once. Its part of speech is 代詞 (pronoun), like the rest of the 皆 row ([皆事](皆事.md), [皆物](皆物.md), [皆名](皆名.md), [皆処](皆処.md) and so on) and like [此人](此人.md), [其人](其人.md), [彼人](彼人.md) and [某人](某人.md); only [毎人](毎人.md) in this column is adverbial (副用名詞). Illustrative only (invented, not from `grammar/`): 皆人、知 "everyone knows".

It contrasts with its column neighbours as follows. The distributive [毎人](毎人.md) "each person" ranges over the people one by one, as [毎事](毎事.md) does against [皆事](皆事.md). [某人](某人.md) "someone; a certain person" is the indefinite, naming an unspecified individual (not a universal), and the interrogative slot asks which person: 誰 is the normal form, [何人](何人.md) is rude and [何名](何名.md) very polite, per the note in `grammar/文法 - 97品詞.md` (line 154). The deictic cells [此人](此人.md) "this person", [其人](其人.md) (also the gender-neutral third-person pronoun) and [彼人](彼人.md) "that person yonder" each point at one particular person, while 皆人 covers all of them. The polite counterpart in the 名 column is [皆名](皆名.md) "everyone (polite); all distinguished persons". The neighbouring vault words [大家](大家.md) and [人人](人人.md) are separate, independent words, not aliases of this one: the former is the Mandarin/Cantonese colloquial pronoun "everyone" (Wiktionary glosses 大家 as a pronoun "everyone; everybody; all of us"; its page here documents the unrelated Japanese and Korean senses), and 人人 is glossed "everyone" on its own page. Dan'a'yo prefers 皆人 as the systematically compositional form within the correlative table, while 大家 is the more idiomatic Mandarin counterpart. Do not confuse 皆人 with [皆様](皆様.md), which in this vault is the 様 ("manner") cell "in every way; in all respects": the Japanese word 皆様 (and the less formal 皆さん) means "everyone" in the honorific register used in public address, and that sense belongs to 皆人 (neutral) and [皆名](皆名.md) (polite) here, not to 皆様.

Cross-linguistic fields were checked against Wiktionary on 2026-10-11. Wiktionary has no entry for 皆人 itself (the same spelling is used in traditional and simplified Chinese), so no `mandarin`, `cantonese`, `japanese`, `korean` or `vietnamese` field is entered: the word is a Dan'a'yo compositional construction, and none of the readings below is an attested reading of the compound. The earlier fields (Mandarin dàjiā, Cantonese daai6 gaa1, Japanese 皆さん, Korean 모두, Vietnamese mọi người) were real equivalents of the meaning, not readings of 皆人, and are removed for that reason. Established equivalents: Mandarin 大家 (dàjiā, Cantonese daai6 gaa1) is a pronoun "everyone; everybody"; Mandarin 眾人 (zhòngrén, Cantonese zung3 jan4) is glossed "everybody; everyone" and also, classically, "ordinary people"; 萬人 (wànrén) is "ten thousand people; a large number of people; all people", and Japanese 万人 is "everybody, all people". Japanese 皆さん is a pronoun "everybody, everyone" on Wiktionary. Korean 모두 is an adverb "all, wholly" and noun "entirety", and 여러분 is a noun used to address a group, "ladies and gentlemen; everyone (present)". That 皆 survives in Japanese mainly as the kun reading みな (mina) in 皆さん, that Korean 모두들 adds a plural nuance, and that Vietnamese *mọi người* is the standard "everyone" are from memory, not re-checked (the Wiktionary lookups for 皆様 and 皆 itself were rate-limited on 2026-10-11).

On [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md)'s correlative table (line 126 is the 皆 ("all") row; the same chart is in `grammar/文法 - 97品詞.md`, line 146 in the 人 ("person") row, with the column header cell 皆 on line 142), 皆人 is the third cell of the 皆 row, after [皆事](皆事.md) "all matters" and [皆物](皆物.md) "all things" and before [皆名](皆名.md) "all honoured persons", [皆処](皆処.md) "all places", [皆時](皆時.md) "all times", [皆様](皆様.md) "all manners", [皆多](皆多.md) "the whole amount" and [皆類](皆類.md) "all kinds". The table is 7 determiners (此, 其, 彼, 何, 毎, 某, 皆) × 9 noun classes (事, 物, 人, 名, 処, 時, 様, 多, 類), 63 forms, so 皆人 is parsed compositionally: 皆 = collective, 人 = person. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared CJKV sense "everyone; all people"; no narrowing is needed.

The spellings are the plain concatenation of the two character pages: 羅馬字 *gye* + *nin* = gyenin, 諺文 겨 + 닌 = 겨닌, 注音 ㄍ⼶ + ㄋㄧㄋ = ㄍ⼶ㄋㄧㄋ, each checked letter by letter against 皆 (char) and 人 (char). The 注音 carries a syllable-boundary dot, ㄍ⼶·ㄋㄧㄋ, because the string has a second complete partition: `grammar/文法 - 02音韻論.md` allows ㄋ as a syllable-final consonant, and both ㄍ⼶ㄋ (*gyen*, 見) and ㄧㄋ exist in `syllables/`, so ㄍ⼶ㄋ + ㄧㄋ would also be a legal reading. The dot marks the intended ㄍ⼶ + ㄋㄧㄋ. Contrast [皆事](皆事.md), whose second syllable begins with ㄐ, which cannot close a syllable, and so needs no dot.
