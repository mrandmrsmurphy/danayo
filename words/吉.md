## Notes
- [吉 (char)](../characters/吉%20(char).md) "lucky"

吉 is 会意: 士 ("a scholar, virtuous person") + [[口]] ("mouth") — good words and blessings originating from a person of virtue and learning. It means "lucky, auspicious, favorable" — the direct opposite of [[凶]] — most familiar in [[吉祥]] ("auspicious, propitious"), a term traditionally displayed in calligraphy at the New Year to invite good fortune.

Mandarin jí, Cantonese gat1, and Japanese きち/きつ (kichi/kitsu, on'yomi, fully productive: 吉日 きちじつ "an auspicious day") alongside native よし (yoshi, "good, fine," an archaic native reading still used in given names) all preserve the sense. Korean 길 (gil) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 길하다 (gilhada, built on the same Sino-Korean root, "to be auspicious"). Vietnamese has two attested readings, cát (the more standard, as in cát tường 吉祥) and kiết (a recognized regional/dialectal variant) — both genuinely in use, not a contamination case.
