## Notes
- [莫 (char)](../characters/莫%20(char).md) "not exist" + [斯](../characters/斯.md) "this" + [素](../characters/素.md) "element" — a transliteration of *Moscow*, after the Joint Institute for Nuclear Research in Moscow Oblast, where the element was synthesized. 莫斯 is the first two syllables of 莫斯科, the standard Mandarin transliteration of "Moscow," truncated here rather than spelled out in full. Japanese and Korean instead borrow the IUPAC name as a loanword (モスコビウム, 모스코븀).

Moscovium is element 115, a superheavy synthetic post-transition metal first reported in 2003 by the joint Dubna–Lawrence Livermore team and named by IUPAC in November 2016 after Moscow Oblast (the region, not the city proper), home of the Dubna laboratory where the discovery experiments ran. Only a few dozen atoms have ever been produced; its most stable known isotope, ²⁹⁰Mc, has a half-life well under a second.

The real Chinese element name is 镆/鏌 (mò), adopted in 2017 — and unusually for the four 2017 names, it is a *repurposed* pre-existing character rather than a fresh coinage: 鏌 is the second character of 鏌鋣 (Mòyé), the legendary sword of the Spring-and-Autumn swordsmith couple 干將 and 莫邪. The character is phono-semantic (金 metal + 莫 phonetic), so its readings match [[莫 (char)|莫]]'s own exactly: `mò`/`mok6`, which is what the `mandarin`/`cantonese` fields here store per this series' convention (per [[丹金]], [[加州金]], [[奥加素]], [[田納素]]) — the same phonetic-component coincidence as 鿫/奥 and 鿬/田. Vietnamese uses *Moscovi*, continuing this series' "-i" pattern (Californi, Livermori, Meitneri, Tennessi).
