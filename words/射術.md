## Notes
- [射](../characters/射.md) "shooting" + [術 (char)](../characters/術%20(char).md) "skill" — no stand-in relationship applies (射's own `stand_in` is [[射出]], 術's is itself).

射術 is a Dan'a'yo neologism for archery in general (one of the [[六芸]], the Six Arts) — coined precisely because no single real CJKV term covers the concept neutrally. Mandarin/Cantonese instead use 箭術/箭术 or 射箭 (both action-of-shooting-arrows compounds); Japanese archery is specifically 弓道 (kyūdō, "the way of the bow"), with Western-style archery distinguished as 洋弓; Korean and Japanese alike tend toward 弓術 (bow-skill) rather than a shooting-centered term. No `mandarin`/`cantonese`/`japanese`/`korean`/`vietnamese` fields are given for this reason — there is no single attested real-world word to cite, only the several near-synonyms described above, and `kwin: false` follows since no real `korean` value exists to match `諺文` against.

### Ancient kinds
The Six Arts curriculum names five specific archery techniques:
- **白矢 (_Báishǐ_):** The arrow goes through the target so the white head shows on the other side.
- **參連 (_Cānlián_):** Rapid fire; the second arrow is released before the first hits.
- **剡注 (_Yǎnzhù_):** A high-speed, flat trajectory.
- **襄尺 (_Xiāngchǐ_):** The lord and the archer stand a specific distance apart to show respect.
- **井儀 (_Jǐngyí_):** Four arrows hitting the target in a square pattern.
