## Notes
- [亜](../characters/亜.md) "second, sub-" + [鉛 (char)](../characters/鉛%20(char).md) "lead" — literally "sub-lead," zinc, so named for its physical/metallurgical resemblance to lead.

亜鉛 (zinc) is the standard Japanese-derived term for the element, used compositionally across Mandarin yàqiān, Cantonese aa3 jyun4, Korean 아연, and Vietnamese á duyên (the standard Sino-Vietnamese chemistry term, paralleling the same "sub-lead" naming logic). Modern standard Chinese instead uses the dedicated character 鋅 (xīn) for zinc, unrelated in form to 亜鉛 and not documented as a Dan'a'yo word in this vault — the two terms name the same element via different etymological strategies, not a genuine homophone pair. 亞鉛 is simply the traditional-character spelling of this same word, kept here as an alias rather than a separate entry.

`kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 아연 coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 아연, since both 亜 and 鉛 independently carry `kwin: true` on their own character pages.
