## Notes
- [字](../characters/字%20(char).md) "glyph, character" + [南](../characters/南.md) "mutter, chatter" (via its alias 喃) — no stand-in relationship applies (字's own stand-in is itself).

字南 (chữ Nôm) is the historical Vietnamese writing system that adapted Chinese characters to write the Vietnamese vernacular — as opposed to 漢字/chữ Hán, which wrote Sino-Vietnamese/Literary Chinese directly. It was displaced by the Latin-based Quốc Ngữ alphabet in the 20th century.

Japanese チュノム and Korean 쯔놈 are phonetic transliterations of the Vietnamese name "chữ Nôm" itself, rather than compositional on'yomi readings — since Chu Nom is a specifically Vietnamese phenomenon with no independent East Asian equivalent term. Mandarin zìnán and Cantonese zi6 naam4 are compositional.
