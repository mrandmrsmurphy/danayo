## Notes
- [匹 (char)](../characters/匹%20(char).md) "animals (classifier)" — this word is also the `stand_in` of its own character page (and 疋 is listed there as an alias, a variant spelling that is never used independently)

匹 is the Dan'a'yo classifier (量詞) for animals, following the rule NP + Numeral + Classifier from the [grammar](../grammar/文法%20-%2004句法.md): 有 犬三匹 "there are three dogs" (the grammar's own example). A noun phrase with a classifier is always definite. In the grammar's twelve-classifier table 匹 is glossed "animals" and exemplified by cat, elephants, mice, and platypuses; it sits beside [条](条.md) (long thin things), [冊](冊.md) (books), [台](台.md) (machines), and [杯](杯.md) (cupfuls), and the general classifier [個](個.md) can stand in for it informally. Examples other than the grammar's are illustrative constructions, not attested sentences.

**A Dan'a'yo-specific generalization.** The Dan'a'yo gloss "animals" is broader than any single source-language usage. The character's original senses are "a bolt of cloth; a matched pair" (see the character page: a cloth measure of four zhang, with the horse-classifier use as a later sense), and the classifier use for animals is anchored in horses. Mandarin 匹 (pǐ) is the classifier for horses (and mules and similar draft animals) as well as bolts of cloth, not for animals in general; other animals take 只, 條, 頭, and so on. Japanese 匹 (ひき/hiki, also written 疋) is the everyday counter for small animals, fish, and insects (一匹 いっぴき, 二匹 にひき, 三匹 さんびき), while large animals take 頭 (とう) and birds 羽 (わ). Korean's everyday animal counter is the native 마리, and Sino-Korean 필 is mainly used for horses and cloth (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese uses con. Dan'a'yo takes the Japanese breadth and extends it into a single, uniform animal classifier, so that the elephant and the mouse in the grammar's table both take 匹; this is a deliberate simplification rather than a retention of Mandarin or Japanese usage. The vault's [Animals](../lexipedia/Animals.md) page describes it simply as the counter word for animals, which is consistent.

The cross-linguistic fields hold the Sino-Xenic readings of the character: Mandarin pǐ, Cantonese pat1, Japanese ヒツ (ひつ), Korean 필, Vietnamese thất. They are readings of 匹, not the native animal counters (ひき, 마리, con) discussed above.
