## Notes
- [禍](../characters/禍.md) "calamity, disaster" + [害](../characters/害.md) "damage, harm" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (禍's is [[禍事]], 害's is [[残害]]), so `#cranberry` does not apply. **Corrected here**: the old body text wrongly claimed this word was the stand-in for 禍; 禍's own character page has always pointed to [[禍事]] instead. `kwin: true` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: true`.

禍害 pairs two near-synonymous disaster/harm morphemes into a general term for calamity or ruinous harm — often used of a person or thing that brings misfortune (a "menace" or "scourge"), rather than only an impersonal event.

Mandarin huòhài, Cantonese wo6 hoi6, Japanese かがい (kagai), and Korean 화해 (hwahae) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese hoạ hại is likewise compositional.
