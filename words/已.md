## Notes
- [已 (char)](../characters/已%20(char).md) "-ed; perfective" — stand-in for [已 (char)](../characters/已%20(char).md) itself, whose `stand_in` field points here (the bare character is bound to this word form).

已 is the perfective aspect marker in Dan'a'yo — "-ed; (has) done; completed." It is a 修飾語 that **suffixes** to the verb, like the other tense-aspect suffixes [[将]] (prospective) and [[－中]] (continuous): the grammar book gives 行已 "went, has gone" (`grammar/文法 - 05形態.md`, §接尾辞, where it is labelled [[完了]]相), 捕已魚之男人 "the man who caught the fish" and 男人捕已之魚 "the fish which the man had caught" (same file, relative-clause examples), and 肋骨六条 与 歯五個 被砕已 "six ribs and five teeth were broken" (`grammar/文法 - 04句法.md`). The learner booklets give 我 食已 "I have eaten" (e.g. `grammar/文法 - 92普通話小冊子.md`; the Korean booklet writes it with the 注音 ㄙㄧㄎㄜ). It marks completion regardless of when: the event is realized and done, not necessarily past. The ordering rule says that 已 may co-occur with 中, in the fixed order 中已 (first in progress, then completed), and that 将, if present, always sits closest to the verb, so the full chain would run verb + 将 + 中 + 已. Among the aspect markers, 已 (realized) contrasts with [[未]] ("not yet"; unrealized) and [[将]] ("will"; prospective).

The Dan'a'yo-specific sense is purely grammatical: a completion suffix on verbs, not the independent Chinese adverb "already". Per Wiktionary (已, checked 2026-10-11), Classical Chinese 已 originally meant "to stop; to finish" and the "already; have done" sense developed from it, gradually displacing the older 既 in the mid-to-late first millennium BCE (Pulleyblank). Wiktionary also lists a separate classical sentence-final particle 已 (possibly a fusion of 也 and 矣). [[矣]] is a distinct particle, used only after verbs and often conflated with 已 in secondary sources; in Dan'a'yo 矣 is a separate emphatic sentence-final word, not an aspect suffix. The character is traditionally treated as a variant of 巳 in origin (its glyph origin is uncertain; see the character page). The character page lists 㠯 as an alias of the character; it is not used here.

The comparison chart (from memory, not re-checked): Mandarin 了 (le, perfective suffix after verb: 吃了 ate; has eaten) and 已经 (yǐjīng, already; has done). Cantonese 咗 (zo2, perfective suffix: 食咗 ate; has eaten). Japanese た/てしまった (ta / te shimatta, past / done and finished: 食べた ate; 食べてしまった finished eating). Korean -았/었다 (-at/-eot-da, past/perfective ending: 먹었다 ate; has eaten) and 이미 (imi, already). Vietnamese đã (pre-verbal perfective: đã ăn has eaten / already ate). The `japanese` (い), `korean` (이) and `vietnamese` (dĩ) fields hold the Sino-Xenic bound readings; the native equivalents above belong to Notes only. Vietnamese dĩ was re-checked on hvdic.thivien.net. Note the word-order contrast: Mandarin 了 and Cantonese 咗 follow the verb, but Vietnamese đã precedes it, whereas Dan'a'yo always postposes 已 directly after the verb.

Inconsistencies left unedited in the grammar documents: `grammar/文法 - 04句法.md` (and `.en.md`) §修飾語 states "Most 修飾語 are prefixes" and gives 已行 "has gone" (prefix order), and its 矣 example reads 已行矣, which contradicts the suffix order in `文法 - 05形態.md`, `Linguistics Features.md`, and all five booklets; `grammar/文法 - 97品詞.md` writes the 注音 of 已 as ㄧ rather than ㄜ (the character page and `Bopomofo.md` give ㄜ).
