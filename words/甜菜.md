## Notes
- [甜](../characters/甜.md) "sweet" + [菜](../characters/菜.md) "vegetable" — 甜 is a [名専字](../lookup/List%20of%20名専字.md), so it appears only inside compounds such as this one; 菜's own stand-in is [[野菜]].

甜菜 means sugar beet, the root crop (*Beta vulgaris*) grown for sugar. It is also written 菾菜, an alias here; that older spelling originally named chard, the leaf form of the same species, and 甜菜 later became the standard name for the sugar-bearing root. Sugar beet was developed as a European crop in the early nineteenth century, and the name 甜菜 came into East Asia with it; 甜菜糖 is beet sugar.

Mandarin tiáncài, Cantonese tim4 coi3 and Japanese てんさい (TEN+SAI) are the Sino-form. Korean 사탕무 ("sugar radish") and Vietnamese củ cải đường ("sugar radish") are native descriptive names, and the `korean` and `vietnamese` fields hold those real terms.
