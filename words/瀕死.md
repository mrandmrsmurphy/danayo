## Notes
- [瀕](../characters/瀕.md) "near death" + [死](../characters/死.md) "die"
- This word is the viable form of [瀕](../characters/瀕.md): the character's meaning (near death) is identical, but 瀕 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 瀕死 is its `stand_in` compound.

瀕死 means "close to death, moribund": 瀕 is "to border on, to be at the verge of" (originally the waterside, the edge of a shore) and 死 is "to die". Mandarin bīnsǐ, Cantonese pan4 sei2, Japanese ひんし (hinshi; 瀕死の重傷, "fatally severe injuries") and Korean 빈사 (binsa) are the same compound, usually describing a person, animal or even an industry or species that is on the brink.

In Dan'a'yo it is a stative word for the condition of being about to die, placed with the decay-and-death vocabulary: [[死亡]] names the event of death, while 瀕死 is the stretch just before it.
