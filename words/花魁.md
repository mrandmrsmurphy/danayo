## Notes
- [花](../characters/花.md) "flower" + [魁](../characters/魁.md) "oiran"
- This word is the stand-in for [魁](../characters/魁.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.
- The Japanese reading おいらん (oiran) is a jukujikun (special compound reading) not derived from standard on/kun readings.

魁 means the head, the first, the leader (as in 魁首 and 奪魁 "to take first place"), so 花魁 is literally "the head of the flowers": the foremost among the flowers, and by figure the foremost among beautiful women. That figure has gone in different directions in the sphere, and the shared form hides three different referents. In Chinese 花魁 is the "champion" of a flower (traditionally the plum blossom, first to bloom) or the most celebrated beauty or courtesan of a place. In Japanese 花魁 is the specific historical role of the oiran, the highest-ranking courtesan of the Edo-period pleasure quarters, an accomplished artist-entertainer whose elaborate dress and ceremony made her a cultural icon, and in that sense it is a narrow historical term. In Vietnamese hoa khôi is the modern "beauty queen," the winner of a beauty contest, with no connection to courtesans at all.

Dan'a'yo keeps the Japanese historical sense named in the glosses, "oiran, leading courtesan," a deliberate narrowing of the broader "foremost beauty" figure. It is placed under [Society](../lexipedia/Society.md) as a social rank, and sits near [芸妓](芸妓.md) "geisha." The readings are Mandarin huākuí, Cantonese faa1 fui1, Korean 화괴 and Sino-Vietnamese hoa khôi.
