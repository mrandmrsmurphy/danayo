## Notes
- [動 (char)](../characters/動%20(char).md) "move" + [物 (char)](../characters/物%20(char).md) "thing" — literally "moving thing" (English Wiktionary lists this as a literal second sense), the natural criterion distinguishing animal life from plant life.

動物 is "animal" — the general biological category, as opposed to 植物 "plant" (built the same way, from 植 "plant, grow" + 物). It is the plain, everyday, unmarked term in every language here, with no competing native word crowding it out. English Wiktionary gives 植物 as a coordinate term and 靜物 ("still life; inanimate object") as the antonym of the literal sense.

Mandarin 動物/动物 (dòngwù). Cantonese 動物 (dung6mat6). Japanese 動物 (どうぶつ, dōbutsu) is equally everyday — 動物園 (dōbutsuen, "zoo"), 動物病院 (dōbutsu byōin, "veterinary clinic"). Korean 동물 (dongmul) is likewise the standard word — 동물원 (dongmurwon, "zoo") mirrors the Japanese compound exactly. Vietnamese động vật is the everyday Sino-Vietnamese term, alongside the semantically narrower native con vật ("creature, beast," often with a slightly more informal or diminutive flavor).

No other word shares the 注音 ㄉㄛㄫㄇㄨㄊ (checked 2026-10-13).
