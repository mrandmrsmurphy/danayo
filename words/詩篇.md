## Notes
- [詩](../characters/詩.md) "poem" + [篇 (char)](../characters/篇%20(char).md) "chapter, section (of writing)" — literally "poem-chapters"; this word is the stand-in compound that legitimizes the bound character 篇 (per its own stored `stand_in`).

詩篇 names both "poems" generally and, capitalized as a proper noun, the Book of Psalms specifically — the standard CJK Bible-translation name for that book, since the Psalms are themselves a collection of sung poems/hymns.

Mandarin shīpiān, Cantonese si1 pin1, and Korean 시편 (sipyeon) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 시편 is itself the standard Korean Bible name for Psalms. Japanese しへん (shihen) is likewise compositional. **Corrected 2026-09-22**: `vietnamese` previously stored Thánh Thi ("holy hymn/canticle"), a real Vietnamese Catholic liturgical term but not built from either of this word's own constituent characters (聖/thánh doesn't correspond to 詩 or 篇 at all) — corrected to Thi Thiên, the standard Vietnamese Protestant Bible name for the Book of Psalms, directly compositional from 詩's thi + 篇's thiên. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 시편 matches Sino-Korean 시편 exactly. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄆ⼶ㄋ unique among both words and characters).
