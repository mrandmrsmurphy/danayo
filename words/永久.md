## Notes
- [永](../characters/永.md) "eternal; perpetual; forever" + [久 (char)](../characters/久%20(char).md) "long; for a long time"

永久 is a semantic dyad of temporal endlessness: 永 "eternal" (its character pictures a long, meandering watercourse, hence "unending") plus 久 "long-lasting." Together they say that something lasts, or is meant to last, without foreseeable end. Unlike [[永遠]], this word is not the `stand_in` of 永 (that role belongs to 永遠); 永久 is simply a second compound built on the same bound root.

**永久 versus 永遠.** Wiktionary lists 永久 in Mandarin as an adjective ("permanent; long-lasting; eternal") and 永遠 as an adverb ("forever; always; permanently") and a noun ("eternity"); in Japanese it lists えいきゅう as both an adjective (na-type) and a noun, and gives えいえん and えいきゅう as synonyms of each other. So the two overlap heavily, and the main difference attested there is grammatical habit: 永久 modifies ("permanent", as in Wiktionary's derived terms 永久居民 "permanent resident", 永久居留權 "permanent residency", 永久凍土 "permafrost", 永久性 "permanence", 永久產權 "freehold"), while 永遠 is the usual word for "forever" as an adverb or for "eternity" as a noun. Dan'a'yo adopts that same tilt and no sharper one: 永久 is the 性詞 "permanent; lasting without end" and [[永遠]] is the 実詞 "eternal; forever". Where 永遠 leans toward timeless eternity, 永久 leans toward lasting permanence of a state or arrangement; this is a tendency of usage, not a hard line. See also [[恒久]] and [[悠久]], the other 久 compounds in the duration family. The antonym Wiktionary lists for Mandarin 永久 includes [[臨時]] "temporary".

In Japanese, 永久 is also the name of an era (年号), Eikyū, 1113-1118 per Wiktionary. From memory, not re-checked here: 永久歯 (えいきゅうし) "permanent teeth", 永久磁石 (えいきゅうじしゃく) "permanent magnet", 永久欠番 (えいきゅうけつばん) "retired number", and 永久保存 "permanent preservation" are common in Japanese.

Across the CJKV sphere: Mandarin yǒngjiǔ, Cantonese wing5 gau2, Japanese えいきゅう (historical kana えいきう), Korean 영구, and Vietnamese vĩnh cửu are all attested on Wiktionary and all share the same sense. The Korean form is used in the 1919 Korean Declaration of Independence (永久完全한 慶福 "perfect and lasting blessing"; 東洋의 永久한 平和 "lasting peace of the East"), rendered in [宣言書](../translation/宣言書.md).

Pronunciation: 注音 ㄨㄧㄫㄍ⼜ is the plain concatenation of 永 ㄨㄧㄫ and 久 ㄍ⼜ (羅馬字 wing + gyu, 諺文 윙 + 규). No syllable-boundary dot is written: the only other way to split the string would be ㄨㄧ + ㄫㄍ⼜, but ㄫ never occurs as an onset (文法 - 02音韻論), and a split after ㄍ would leave ㄍ as a coda, where only nasals, glides and ㄆㄊㄎ are allowed. The same reasoning applies to [[恒久]] ㄏㄨㄫㄍ⼜, which is also written without a dot.
