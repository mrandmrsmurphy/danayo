## Notes
- [山 (char)](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain" — stand-in for [[山 (char)|山]], which cannot appear independently

山 is a 象形 pictograph of three mountain peaks — in the Oracle Bone Script rendered as triangular points rather than the vertical strokes of the modern form, closely paralleling the related pictograph 丘 ("hillock/mound," itself a lower, gentler landform). Unlike many of this vault's other "high-boundedness, cannot stand alone" single characters, 山 is prolific rather than narrow: it anchors an enormous compound family spanning both literal geography (山地 "mountainous terrain," 山脈 "mountain range," 火山 "volcano," 山崩 "landslide") and figurative or toponymic use (南山 "South Mountain" as a longevity symbol in poetry, countless place names like 旧金山 "San Francisco" — literally "old gold mountain," from the Gold Rush era — and 山本/桃山 as surname/era elements in Japanese).

Japanese carries a genuine on'yomi split worth flagging: さん (SAN, the everyday reading, used in 富士山 Fujisan and most compounds) versus せん (SEN, a rarer alternate reading confined to a handful of fixed terms and some place names), while the native 訓読み やま (yama) remains one of the most productive words in the whole language, appearing bare as "mountain" and forming its own extensive compound family independent of the Sino-Japanese layer entirely — a clean four-way split (さん/せん/やま as three readings, plus the character's role as a bound radical) unusually rich for a 3-stroke character. Vietnamese similarly preserves two attested Sino-Vietnamese layers, sơn (the dominant modern form, as in Sơn Tây "Western Mountains") and the rarer san, both retained here matching the character's own stored readings rather than collapsing to one. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 산 (san, from Middle Chinese 生母 ʃ + 山開韻 ɣɛn) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 산, a match shared by no fewer than five other characters in this vault's corpus (刪, 傘, 散, 産, 珊) that happen to converge on the identical Dan'a'yo/Korean syllable ㄙㄚㄋ/산 — none of which have their own standalone word page, so 山 remains the sole word-level occupant of this syllable.
