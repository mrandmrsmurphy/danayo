## Notes
- [唄 (char)](../characters/唄%20(char).md) "Buddhist chant; hymn"

唄 is a bound transliteration element, from Sanskrit bhāṇaka ("chanter, reciter," via the fuller transliteration 唄匿); it survives only as the second half of 梵唄 (fànbài, "Buddhist chant, hymn" — literally "voice of Brahmā"), never as an independent word. This is the character's one genuinely shared Sino-xenic sense across the Sinosphere.

**Content corrected**: this entry previously stored `mandarin: bei` and glossed the word as "ugh," reflecting a completely different, unrelated sense — modern colloquial Mandarin 呗 (bei, neutral tone) is a common sentence-final modal particle marking resignation or obviousness ("well, ...; of course; I guess," as in 就這樣吧唄, "let's just do it that way then"). That sense is a purely Mandarin-internal innovation with no Sino-Vietnamese/Sino-Korean/Sino-Japanese/Cantonese counterpart, and its pronunciation doesn't descend from the same Middle Chinese source as this word's Dan'a'yo derivation (bai/ㄅㄚㄧ), which instead matches Cantonese baai6, Korean 패, Vietnamese bái, and Japanese on'yomi バイ — all reflexes of the Buddhist-chant reading. The "ugh"/"bei" entry conflated the two unrelated senses; corrected to represent the Buddhist-chant sense the Dan'a'yo reading actually derives from.

Cantonese baai6, Korean 패 (pae, as in 범패 beompae, a UNESCO-recognized traditional Korean Buddhist chant genre), and Vietnamese bái (phạm bái) all parallel this sense narrowly, confined to the same loan compound in each language. Japanese has an additional, unrelated, far more productive native kun'yomi reading うた (uta, "song, ballad" — 子守唄 komoriuta "lullaby," 小唄 kouta, 長唄 nagauta), which is the character's dominant living identity in Japanese but belongs to a separate word (not this entry, which tracks the shared on'yomi バイ Buddhist-chant sense instead).

This word completes a three-way homophone group (all read bai/배/ㄅㄚㄧ) with [[倍]] ("double; times," already perfected) and [[牌]] ("playing card," still unperfected).
