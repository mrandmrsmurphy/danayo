## Notes
- [胴](../characters/胴.md) "torso" + [体](../characters/体.md) "body" — this word is 胴's own `stand_in`: the character's meaning (trunk/torso) is identical, but 胴 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word, so the compound pairs it with the near-synonym 体 to make it usable.

胴体 means the torso or trunk of a body, and extends to the body of a vehicle: the fuselage of an aircraft. English Wiktionary glosses Japanese どうたい as "trunk, torso, body" and "fuselage", and Korean 동체 the same. For Chinese 胴體 (an alias; Mandarin dòngtǐ) it gives only the narrower "trunk (of a slaughtered animal); carcass", so a Chinese reader may hear the carcass sense. It has no Cantonese entry, so dung6 tai2 here is built from the characters.

The whole body is the [[体]], and the torso carries the limbs and the head.
