## Notes
- [唾](../characters/唾.md) "saliva; spit; to spit" + [液](../characters/液.md) "liquid; fluid" — stand-in for [唾](../characters/唾.md), which cannot appear independently

唾液 is "saliva" — the formal and technical term for the oral fluid produced by the salivary glands, used in digestion, speech articulation, and oral health. The compound is anatomical: 唾 (saliva, spit — the substance) + 液 (liquid, fluid — the category). It is used in medical and scientific contexts across CJKV, contrasting with more colloquial expressions: Mandarin 口水 (kǒushuǐ, "mouth water"), Japanese 唾/つば (tsuba), Korean 침 (chim, spit), all of which are the everyday register equivalents.

Mandarin 唾液 (tuòyè) is the medical and formal standard — 唾液腺 (salivary gland), 唾液分泌 (saliva secretion). Japanese 唾液 (だえき, daeki) is the anatomical term; everyday speech uses つば (tsuba) for spit. Korean 타액 (taek) is the formal medical term; 침 (chim) is the colloquial word. Vietnamese thóa dịch is the Sino-Vietnamese compound (thóa = 唾, dịch = 液); natural Vietnamese uses nước bọt (literally "mouth/foam water").

The Dan'a'yo 諺文 transcription 타역 differs from Sino-Korean 타액: 液 in Dan'a'yo reads 역 (yek, ⼶ㄎ) rather than the Sino-Korean 액 (aek). 唾液 belongs to the pan-CJKV medical vocabulary stratum where all five language forms share the same written compound even as phonological details diverge, making the Sinographic characters the reliable cross-linguistic anchor.
The Body page lists 唾液 as "spit," and Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "saliva," the formal, medical register, with the colloquial words described above for ordinary speech. It is related to the existing words [液体](液体.md) "liquid," the stand-in for 液, and [血液](血液.md) "blood," which also pairs a substance with 液. The readings are Mandarin tuòyè, Cantonese to3 jik6, Japanese daeki (だえき) and Sino-Vietnamese thóa dịch. The medical compounds 唾液腺 and 唾液分泌 and the colloquial equivalents are the page's existing account and have not been re-checked here.
