## Notes
- [塚 (char)](../characters/塚%20(char).md) "burial mound" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the character 塚 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

塚/총/trủng name a raised burial mound, but only Vietnamese trủng has visibly slipped into archaic/poetic register in ordinary usage — it survives mainly in classical verse (Nguyễn Du's 青塚, "green grave-mound," referring to Wang Zhaojun's legendary tomb), with modern Vietnamese reaching for mộ or the native gò mả instead. Mandarin, Cantonese, and Korean all keep 塚/총 in active, if somewhat literary, use — most visibly in 古塚 ("ancient burial mound") and 貝塚 (kaizuka in Japanese, "shell mound," a standard archaeological term for a midden of discarded shells).

English Wiktionary glosses the Chinese character as "tomb; burial mound" (a 形声 of 土 "earth" and the phonetic 冢, with 墳 as the dialect synonym), and lists the compounds 貝塚, 義塚, 荒塚, 衣冠塚 and 墳塚. It does not list 古塚, which the paragraph above uses as an example, so that example is unverified. For Japanese it gives "mound, hillock, tumulus (implies a manmade origin)" and the noun つか, "a mound or hillock, generally manmade; a grave, a tumulus or burial mound", with 墓 for a small grave and 古墳 for a large one, and the kun reading つか (cognate with 築く, "to build up"). The on readings are ちゅう (go'on) and ちょう (kan'on).


The homophones above are the other two words that share the 注音 ㄑㄛㄫ.
