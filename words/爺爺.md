## Notes
- [爺](../characters/爺.md) "grandpa, master" + [爺](../characters/爺.md) "grandpa, master" — a reduplication; 爺's own stand-in is [[老爺]], not this word.

爺爺 is the intimate word for a grandfather: "grandpappy", "grandpapa". The doubling is the affectionate, child's-eye register, the same pattern as [[媽媽]]; the neutral, formal term is [[祖父]]. In Dan'a'yo, as with other reduplications, the doubling marks the diminutive-intimate register and is not a literal "two grandfathers".

爺 began as a word for "father" (爺娘 meant "parents" in Tang and Song texts) and later shifted down a generation and up in rank: "grandfather" in northern Chinese, and "master, lord" in 老爺. Mandarin yéye (neutral-tone second syllable) means the paternal grandfather, with the maternal side using a separate word (外公, 姥爺). Cantonese je4 je2 likewise names the paternal grandfather, with a tone change on the second syllable. Japanese uses じいじ, the child's word for grandpa, and Korean 할아버지 and Vietnamese ông are native words, so neither reduplicates a Sino-reading; the `japanese`, `korean` and `vietnamese` fields hold those real words.
