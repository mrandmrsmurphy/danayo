## Notes
- [公 (char)](../characters/公%20(char).md) "sir; public" + [邸](../characters/邸.md) "residence" — stand-in for [[邸]], which cannot appear independently

**公邸** (*gōngdǐ*) is the official residence provided to a holder of public office, as against the private home of the same person. It is used chiefly for the residence of a head of government or state, a minister, a governor, or a diplomat: 首相公邸 (prime minister's residence), 大使公邸 (ambassador's residence). The 公 marks it as belonging to the office rather than to its occupant, and the occupant leaves it when the term ends.

In Japanese the word has a precise institutional contrast. The 首相官邸 (*Shushō Kantei*) is the prime minister's working office and seat of the Cabinet, while the 首相公邸 (*Shushō Kōtei*) is the separate building where the prime minister may live; the two stand side by side in Nagatachō, Tokyo. In Chinese 公邸 appears above all for ambassadors' and consuls' residences, which are kept distinct from the embassy chancery ([[大使館]]), the office building. The Korean reading 공저 is a Sino-Korean form kept for this sense; everyday Korean more often uses 공관 (公館) for an official residence.

In Dan'a'yo 公邸 is the residence only, never the office. The near-synonym [[公館]] is also glossed "official residence" and is listed with it on the Government page; 公邸 is the home of the officeholder, and 公館 the larger official building (Korean 공관 is its reading). It keeps the shared CJKV sense.
