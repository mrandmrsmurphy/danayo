## Notes
- [程](../characters/程.md) "extent, degree, amount" + [度](../characters/度.md) "degree, manner" — `#cranberry`: both characters' own `stand_in` point to this exact compound (程's and 度's alike), so transitivity holds (A=B=AB) and both are legitimized as independent Dan'a'yo entries specifically by this word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 程 is individually `kwin: false`.

程度 means extent or degree — how far something goes, how much of a quality is present (ある程度, "to a certain extent"; 高度な, borrowing 度 again for "high degree/altitude"). Since both constituent characters independently mean roughly "degree/measure," the compound is close to a doubled reinforcement rather than two distinct semantic contributions.

Mandarin chéngdù, Cantonese cing4 dou6, Japanese ていど (teido), and Korean 정도 (jeongdo) are all standard, fully compositional readings, each an extremely common everyday word for "degree, extent" in its language. Vietnamese trình độ is likewise compositional and equally common, especially in the sense of "level" (of skill, education, or proficiency): trình độ học vấn ("educational level").
