## Notes
- [遷](../characters/遷.md) "to move, transfer" + [怒 (char)](../characters/怒%20(char).md) "anger"

遷怒 means to vent anger on someone or something unrelated to its cause — to "transfer" one's anger onto an innocent target rather than addressing its true source. It draws directly on the Confucian ideal named in the *Analects* (論語·雍也), where Confucius praises his disciple Yan Hui as the only one who truly loved learning, "不遷怒，不貳過" ("he did not transfer his anger, and did not repeat a mistake") — self-mastery over displaced emotion and repeated error as twin marks of moral cultivation.

Unlike in Chinese, the Dan'a'yo word does not require a following 於 + object to specify the target of displaced anger; the verb stands complete on its own. Japanese せんど (sendo) preserves the classical on'yomi reading tied to this same Analects allusion, though everyday Japanese more often expresses the idea with the native phrase 怒りを遷す/移す (ikari o utsusu, "to shift one's anger"). Korean 천노 and Vietnamese thiên nộ likewise track the classical Sino-Xenic compound directly.
