## Etymology
- [安](../characters/安.md) "peaceful" + [慰](../characters/慰.md) "comfort, console"

## Notes
- A verb, "to comfort, console" — an emotional-support sense, distinct from 安's own more general "peaceful" sense. Filled blank `pos: 動詞` and corrected `hsk_level` from a wrong `2` to the real official level, `"5"`. `vietnamese: an ủi` is directly attested and extremely common, the standard everyday Vietnamese verb for comforting someone.
- **Genuine cross-linguistic finding, not a bug**: Korean 안위 is a real homophone collision — the far more common word spelled the same way in Hangul is 安危 ("safety vs. danger," as in 국가의 안위, "the nation's safety/security"), an entirely different compound built from 危 ("danger"), not 慰. Since Hangul carries no tone or character information, the two are indistinguishable in speech and only disambiguated by context or Hanja; this page's `korean: 안위` is correctly the reading for 安慰 itself, just worth flagging for anyone encountering 안위 elsewhere and assuming safety rather than comfort.
- Japanese あんい (安慰) is a real, dictionary-attested term but a literary/formal one; everyday Japanese instead reaches for the native verb 慰める (なぐさめる) / noun 慰め (なぐさめ) for "to comfort."
- Compare the reversed compound [[慰安]] (慰 + 安, いあん/위안), near-synonymous but built the opposite way around — the same two morphemes swapped, paralleling other reversal pairs like [[紹介]]/介紹 noted elsewhere in this vault. `kwin` is false: 安's own `kwin` is `true` but 慰's is `false`, and the AND-rule (one `false` constituent makes the whole compound `false`) applies. No homophones (注音 ㄚㄋ·ㄛㄧ unique among both words and characters).
