## Notes
- [様 (char)](../characters/様%20(char).md) "form, style" + [式](../characters/式.md) "style"
- This word is the viable form of [式](../characters/式.md): the character's meaning (style; form) is identical, but 式 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 様式 is its `stand_in` compound.

様 is a 形声 character meaning shape, appearance, manner (the 様 of 様子 "appearance, state"), and 式 is a form, formula or ceremony, so 様式 is the established form something takes: a style of art or architecture (ゴシック様式 "the Gothic style"), a set pattern of behavior, or a prescribed form to fill in (a form, an application format, as in 書類の様式). It is the abstract "style, type" word, used for periods and schools of art and for ways of life (生活様式 "lifestyle").

The Mind page distinguishes it from its neighbors: [格式](格式.md) is the prescribed arrangement something must follow (a document's format, the form of a letter), and [柄](柄.md) is a visual or surface pattern such as a fabric design, where 様式 names the structural style. The readings are Mandarin yàngshì (alias 樣式), Cantonese joeng6 sik1, Japanese yōshiki (ようしき) and Korean 양식. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "type, style, pattern." Note that it is a Dan'a'yo homophone of [養殖](養殖.md) (see the callout above), so context decides.
