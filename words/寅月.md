## Notes
- [寅](../characters/寅.md) "revere; third Earthly Branch, tiger" + [月 (char)](../characters/月%20(char).md) "month" — this word is the stand-in for [寅](../characters/寅.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

First month of the ancient Chinese lunar calendar, named for the third Earthly Branch 寅 (tiger).

The traditional year begins not with 子 (rat) but with 寅 (tiger), following the convention established in the Zhou dynasty. The winter solstice falls in 子月 (11th month), making 寅月 the first month of spring — the season of new beginnings.

Japanese とらつき reads both halves natively (寅's kun'yomi とら "tiger" + 月's kun'yomi つき "month") rather than the Sino-Japanese on'yomi concatenation いんげつ, matching how zodiac-month names are conventionally read in Japanese. Vietnamese tháng Dần likewise uses the native word *tháng* ("month") plus the zodiac name Dần, rather than a fully Sino-Vietnamese compound — the same native-plus-zodiac-name pattern as Japanese, not a coincidence given both traditions calque the same Chinese calendrical system. No homophones (注音 ㄧㄋ·⼔ㄊ unique across both words and characters).
