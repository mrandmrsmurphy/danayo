## Notes
- [四 (char)](../characters/四%20(char).md) "four" + [字 (char)](../characters/字%20(char).md) "character; glyph" + [成 (char)](../characters/成%20(char).md) "to become; fixed; established" + [語](../characters/語.md) "expression; language" — no stand-in relationship applies (四's own stand-in is itself, 字's is itself, 成's is itself, and 語's is [[言語]]).

四字成語 is "four-character fixed expression" — a metalinguistic term for the culturally marked category of set phrases four characters in length across the CJKV literary sphere. Literally: "four-character (四字) formed/fixed (成) expression (語)." It is the umbrella category for the vault's [[chengyu/Dan'a'yo Chengyu|Dan'a'yo Chengyu]], [[chengyu/Misc. Chengyu|Misc. Chengyu]] and [[chengyu/Biblical Chengyu|Biblical Chengyu]] collections and the 240+ pages under `chengyu/`: each page there is an article on one four-character fixed expression, whether inherited from the classical canon, coined in modern Chinese, drawn from scripture, or a vault-original coinage (`origin: 単亜語`). The term is descriptive rather than prescriptive: many four-character sequences are ordinary compounds, and 四字成語 names the expectation that a four-character form carries rhythm, memorability and aphoristic or intertextual weight beyond its surface meaning.

It is the four-character-specific counterpart of [[成語]] "chengyu" (the bare term, which in everyday Mandarin usually suffices and which the vault likewise glosses as "four-character sayings"), and sits beside [[熟語]] "idiom" (a general set expression of any length). Neither of those is an alias of this word, so 四字熟語 is not listed in `aliases`; only the simplified-script spelling 四字成语 is.

Cross-linguistically, the Mandarin and Cantonese readings (sì zì chéngyǔ, sei3 zi6 sing4 jyu5), Korean 사자성어 (sa-ja-seong-eo, the standard school term for classical four-character idioms, with dedicated textbooks), and Vietnamese tứ tự thành ngữ are all straight Sino-Xenic readings of these four characters. Japanese is different: the standard term is 四字熟語 (よじじゅくご, yojijukugo), with 熟 "ripened" in place of 成 "formed", so the real Japanese word is a distinct compound and its reading is not what the `japanese` field holds. The field instead carries the character-by-character on'yomi しじせいご (SHI + JI + SEI + GO). From memory, not re-checked (Wiktionary was unreachable when this page was last reviewed): 四字成語 itself may occur in Japanese as a rarer variant of 四字熟語, but yojijukugo is the established term.

Dan'a'yo 싀지싱요 (siǝjising'yo) follows each constituent character's own page, and 四 deliberately reads 싀 (ㄙㄧㄜ, from Middle Chinese s + iɪ) rather than the Sino-Korean 사. There is no pronunciation-boundary dot in 注音: the join 四|字 puts ㄜ before ㄐ, and ㄐ is not a coda in the syllable structure (C)(G)V(C); 字|成 puts ㄧ before ㄙ, likewise not a coda; and 成|語 puts ㄫ before ⼄, but ㄫ never occurs in syllable-initial position (文法 - 02音韻論), so ㄙㄧ + ㄫ⼄ is not a legal alternative. Each boundary has exactly one legal syllabification, in contrast to 言語 ㄝㄋ·⼄ where ㄋ could start the next syllable.

Illustrative, invented usage: 「大器晩成」は有名な四字成語です。 ("大器晩成 is a famous four-character idiom.")
