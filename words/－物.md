## Notes
- [物 (char)](../characters/物%20(char).md) "thing; result" — the same character as the free word [物](物.md); －物 is its bound, fully productive suffix form.

－物 is one of Dan'a'yo's two **nominalizing suffixes**, the one that marks the **object or product** of an action ([[文法 - 05形態]], §名詞化). Attached to a verb phrase, it returns a noun for *the thing that is X-ed*: [[食物]] "that which is eaten, food", 創造物 "that which is created, a creation", 絵画物 "a drawing, the result of 絵画". It pairs with [[－事]], which names the *act* instead, and the pair is how Dan'a'yo keeps apart what Mandarin and Japanese often leave to context: 食物 (the food) against 食事 (the act of eating, a meal). An 事詞 may take either; a 性詞 does not govern an object and so takes only －事, never －物.

－物 is a **派生辞**: its output is a new noun, and it can sit inside a longer chain. It is productive in the plain sense, so any transparent *verb phrase + 物* is valid Dan'a'yo without its own page, and a derivative earns a page only when its meaning is lexicalized. Words already enumerated stay valid. Many are the older "kind of thing" pattern, where 物 means a category of being or matter: [[動物]] "animal", [[植物]] "plant", [[生物]], [[鉱物]], [[穀物]], [[貨物]], [[怪物]], [[汚物]]. Compounds in which 物 is the *head* of an ordinary noun phrase, such as [[事物]] and [[万物]], are separate words and not derivations.

Full productivity rests on two things: the grammar's own nominalization system requires it, and it is a living suffix in the written source languages. Mandarin 建筑物 (jiànzhùwù), Japanese 建築物 (kenchikubutsu) and Korean 건축물 (geonchungmul) all mean "a structure", a built thing, and the same pattern gives Mandarin 产物 "product", Japanese 生産物 and Korean 생산물. Japanese reads it ぶつ or もつ (食物 shokumotsu but 動物 dōbutsu), and has the native free word もの as well. Vietnamese vật is bound in Sino-Vietnamese compounds such as động vật "animal" but is a weaker living suffix than in the other three.

The same character is also the free word [[物]] "thing; result", which is not an affix and can stand alone and take case markers. Like 化 and the character 化, the two are different words with different pages, and the filename tells them apart: －物 is the suffix, 物 is the noun. Two further words share this syllable in Dan'a'yo (mud, ㄇㄨㄊ), the free word 物 and [[勿]] "do not"; the homophone callout cross-references them, and a bound suffix is never confused with either in practice.

The page filename uses the fullwidth hyphen `－` (U+FF0D) to mark a word that cannot stand alone but is fully productive; the ASCII form `-物` is kept as an alias.
