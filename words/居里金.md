## Notes
- [居](../characters/居.md) "reside" + [里 (char)](../characters/里%20(char).md) + [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "metal" — a transliteration of *Curie*, after Marie and Pierre Curie, continuing this vault's avoid-the-real-element-character convention (the actual Chinese chemistry character for curium is 锔, jú, a dedicated element-only glyph unrelated to any of 居/里/金) — the same pattern as [[丹金]]/[[奥加素]]/[[田納素]]/[[莫斯素]]/[[日本素]] elsewhere in this series.

居里 is the standard Mandarin transliteration of the surname "Curie" (as in 居里夫人, "Madame Curie" — Marie Curie), already exactly two syllables and so used here in full rather than reduced further, unlike some other scientist-named elements in this series that compress a longer transliterated name. 金 ("metal") closes the compound as the generic element-category suffix, the same role it plays in [[丹金]] (hafnium) and [[邁金]] (meitnerium). Mandarin/Cantonese here are the plain compositional concatenation of the three constituent characters' own readings (jūlǐjīn/geoi1 lei5 gam1), not the real element name.

Japanese and Korean both borrow the IUPAC scientific name wholesale as a loanword (キュリウム kyuriumu, 퀴륨 kwiryum) rather than deriving it compositionally from the transliterated-character reading, the standard pattern for actinide names in both languages regardless of which naming convention Mandarin/Cantonese follow. Vietnamese similarly nativizes the international name directly as Curi, continuing this series' established "-i" ending pattern for scientist-named elements (already seen on [[日本素]]'s Nihoni) rather than compositionally deriving a reading from 居/里/金's own individual Vietnamese fields. `kwin` is false, following the same AND-rule exception already documented on [[丹金]]: the authoritative test is a plain string comparison of 諺文 (기리김) against `korean` (퀴륨), which plainly differ, even though the constituent characters' own `kwin` values would suggest otherwise by the heuristic AND-rule.
