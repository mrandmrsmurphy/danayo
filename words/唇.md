## Notes
- [唇 (char)](../characters/唇%20(char).md) "lips"

唇 (also written 脣) is 形声, with phonetic [[辰]]. It means "lips" (嘴唇, "lips"), and figuratively appears in the idiom 唇亡齒寒 ("when the lips are gone, the teeth feel cold" — mutual dependence, ruin for one spells ruin for the other).

Mandarin chún and Cantonese seon4 preserve the sense directly. Japanese シン (SHIN, on'yomi, rare, largely confined to formal/anatomical compounds) alongside native くちびる (kuchibiru, "lips," the everyday word) cover the sense. Korean 진 (jin) parallels the Sino-xenic reading (a member of the same phonetic-series cluster as 辰/神/晨/震, all sharing 辰 as phonetic component), alongside native 입술 (ipsul, "lips"). Vietnamese thần is likewise a genuine Hán Việt reading via the same 辰 phonetic series (shared with 神 "deity," 辰 itself, 晨 "morning," 震 "thunder"), attested in the classical idiom rendering thần vong xỉ hàn (唇亡齒寒); but it is marginal and literary rather than an everyday word — modern Vietnamese uses the native môi for "lips" (môi hở răng lạnh is how the idiom is actually rendered in living speech), and thần in isolation is otherwise understood as "deity" (神).
