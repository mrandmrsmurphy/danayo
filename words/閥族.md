## Notes
- [閥](../characters/閥.md) "clan" + [族](../characters/族.md) "tribe"
- This word is the viable form of [閥](../characters/閥.md): the character's meaning (clan, clique) is identical, but 閥 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 閥族 is its `stand_in` compound.

閥 originally meant the doorposts at the gate of a distinguished house and recorded a family's merit and rank (the sense of 門閥 "a great house"), from which it came to mean a powerful clique or house, as in 財閥 "financial conglomerate" and 軍閥 "warlord faction." 族 "clan, kin group" fixes the sense on a family: 閥族 is a clan whose standing rests on inherited rank, wealth or political influence, as opposed to a clan in the plain genealogical sense.

On the Kinship page the clan cluster divides the territory cleanly: [氏族](氏族.md) is the lineage-based clan, [部族](部族.md) the ethnographic tribe, [同族](同族.md) the relation of being of the same clan, and 閥族 specifically the powerful, influential clan. In usage it is a literary and often critical word, as it can imply that a family holds power by privilege. It is best known in Korean (벌족, a "powerful family"), with Mandarin fázú, Cantonese fat6 zuk6 and Japanese batsuzoku (ばつぞく) less common. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "powerful clan, influential family group," with no divergence. It is placed under [Kinship](../lexipedia/Kinship.md).
