## Notes
- [老 (char)](../characters/老%20(char).md) "old person; age" — stand-in for [[老 (char)|老]], which cannot appear independently

老 is 会意: [[人 (char)|人]] ("man") + [[毛 (char)|毛]] ("hair") + [[匕]] ("cane") together depicting a man with long hair leaning on a cane — "old." The character page's own Notes flag it as the textbook example of 転注 ("mutual annotation"), cognate with [[考]] (Old Chinese *kʰluːʔ) — the two characters were historically interchangeable in certain senses, one of classical philology's standard illustrations of that category of the traditional Six Writings. Beyond the plain "old" sense, the compound family spans respectful/institutional titles (老師 here glossed narrowly as "sage, elder monk," 老爺 "grandfather, master") and the philosophical proper name 老子 (Laozi, "Old Master," attested directly in this vault's own Words list).

Korean 로 and Vietnamese lão are the standard Sino-xenic readings (lão nhân "an old person," lão hóa "aging," Lão Tử "Laozi," directly paralleling [[老子]]).

Japanese carries a single on'yomi ろう (ROU, 老人 rōjin "elderly person," 老化 rōka "aging"), alongside a native root お- (bound, as in 老いる oiru "to age, to grow old") rather than a free-standing native word — a narrower native presence than most characters, closer to [[矢]]'s classical-register profile than to a fully productive native/Sino-Japanese split.
