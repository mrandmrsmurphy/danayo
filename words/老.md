## Notes
- [老 (char)](../characters/老%20(char).md) "old person; age" — stand-in for [[老 (char)|老]], which cannot appear independently

老 is 会意: [[人 (char)|人]] ("man") + [[毛 (char)|毛]] ("hair") + [[匕]] ("cane") together depicting a man with long hair leaning on a cane — "old." The character page's own Notes flag it as the textbook example of 転注 ("mutual annotation"), cognate with [[考]] (Old Chinese *kʰluːʔ) — the two characters were historically interchangeable in certain senses, one of classical philology's standard illustrations of that category of the traditional Six Writings. Beyond the plain "old" sense, the compound family spans respectful/institutional titles (老師 here glossed narrowly as "sage, elder monk," 老爺 "grandfather, master") and the philosophical proper name 老子 (Laozi, "Old Master," attested directly in this vault's own Words list).

**Content bug found and fixed**: the word's own `korean` field was stored as 노, the South Korean 두음법칙-shifted form (word-initial ㄹ regularized to ㄴ) — but per this vault's standing rule, the `korean` field always records the North Korean/문화어 pronunciation, which keeps the original ㄹ onset: 로. Corrected to match the character's own already-correct field. `vietnamese: lão` was chosen from an unusually noisy 8-item list stored on the character's own page (láu, lão, lảo, lảu, lẩu, lẽo, lếu, rảu) — lão is the one independently well-attested for this exact sense (lão nhân "an old person," lão hóa "aging," and Lão Tử "Laozi," directly paralleling this vault's own 老子), the rest almost certainly stored-field contamination from unrelated homophone characters, the same pattern already documented multiple times in this sweep.

Japanese carries a single on'yomi ろう (ROU, 老人 rōjin "elderly person," 老化 rōka "aging"), alongside a native root お- (bound, as in 老いる oiru "to age, to grow old") rather than a free-standing native word — a narrower native presence than most characters in this sweep, closer to [[矢]]'s classical-register profile than to a fully productive native/Sino-Japanese split. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 랏 (lau, from Middle Chinese 來母 l + 豪韻 ɑu) diverges from Sino-Korean 로 in the coda, Dan'a'yo retaining a final consonant Korean lacks. No homophones (注音 ㄌㄚㄨ shared only by characters — 𢭐, 労, 牢 — none with a self-pointing `stand_in`).
