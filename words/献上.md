## Notes
- [献](../characters/献.md) "offer, present" + [上](../characters/上%20(char).md) "up, above"
- This word is the stand-in for [献](../characters/献.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

To present something upward — to a superior, ruler, or deity. In Japanese, けんじょう (kenjō) is a formal humble term for gifting to someone of higher status; 献上品 (kenjōhin) is a "tribute item" or offering gift. Chinese 献上 (xiànshàng) carries the same directional meaning of presenting upward, distinguishing this act from equal-to-equal giving. Korean 헌상 (獻上) appears in historical contexts for tributary gifts presented to the king or court. Vietnamese hiến thượng maintains the Sino-Vietnamese reading; the everyday term for an offering is cúng dường (from Sanskrit).

献 originally named a ritual vessel and the offering made in it, and so "to present with ceremony"; 上 supplies the direction, upward. The word covers a secular act in Dan'a'yo: presenting something to a human superior or sovereign. It is not the word for a religious offering to a deity, a sense the Religion page records as still uncoined, and it differs from [贈与](贈与.md) "to give, donate," which carries no upward direction or ceremony. The Time page cites the pair among the six separate senses of English "present" (gifting, in this case), and the Existence page notes the formal, archaic register. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "offer as tribute, present, dedicate."
