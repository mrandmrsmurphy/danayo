## Notes
- [九 (char)](../characters/九%20(char).md) "nine" doubled — this word reduplicates 九 itself rather than pairing it with a second distinct character; both frontmatter fields double the single constituent's own reading accordingly.

九九 names the multiplication table — the reduplication comes from the traditional East Asian method of memorizing the times tables by rote chant, which in Chinese and Japanese begins from the highest values (九九八十一, "nine nines are eighty-one") rather than the lowest, giving the whole mnemonic chant its "nine-nine" name. Japanese 九九 (kuku) is the standard, everyday word every schoolchild learns (九九を覚える, "to memorize the times tables"); Mandarin jiǔjiǔ (九九表, "the 9×9 table") and Cantonese gau2 gau2 parallel this. Korean 구구 (구구단, "the multiplication table," the far more common derived form) carries the same sense. **Vietnamese content note**: filled with bảng cửu chương ("the nine-chapters table"), the real, universally known Vietnamese term for the multiplication table taught in schools — not a literal reduplication of cửu, since Vietnamese names the concept via a different (though historically related) fixed idiom rather than doubling the numeral.

`kwin` is false: 九's own `kwin` is already false on its own character page (Dan'a'yo 큐 vs Sino-Korean 구), so the reduplicated compound inherits the same mismatch in both syllables.
