## Notes
- [穀](../characters/穀.md) "cereal, grain" + [倉](../characters/倉.md) "granary" — 倉's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 穀's own is [[穀物]]; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 倉 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 倉 is individually `kwin: false`.

**Fixed a real bug**: `羅馬字` had gogcang, missing 倉's own glide (cwang, confirmed against `syllables/ㄑ⺢ㄫ.md`) — `諺文`/`注音` had already correctly used the glide-inclusive forms. Corrected to gogcwang.

穀倉 names a granary or barn — the storehouse where harvested grain is kept — and extends figuratively to any region known as a major grain-producing area (a country's "breadbasket," 穀倉地帯).

Mandarin gǔcāng, Cantonese guk1 cong1, Japanese こくそう (kokusō), and Korean 곡창 (gokchang, as in 곡창지대, "granary region/breadbasket") are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese cốc thương is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form; everyday Vietnamese more often uses the native vựa lúa ("rice granary/breadbasket").
