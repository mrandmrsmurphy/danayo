## Notes
- [老 (char)](../characters/老%20(char).md) "old" + [街](../characters/街.md) "street" — "old street"; no stand-in relationship applies to 老 (its own is the single-character word [[老]]); 街's is [[街道]], which is the ordinary Dan'a'yo word for "street" and is not made redundant by this one.

This spelling serves two unrelated uses, an ordinary Chinese noun and the Chinese-character name of a Vietnamese city and province, so the ordinary sense comes first. The word is typed as a noun (名詞), the part of speech of its primary ordinary sense; the Lào Cai sense is a second sense of the same noun, as with [[大力]] and [[広南]], not a second part of speech.

1. An old street; the historic street or quarter of a town, kept for its old buildings and shops and often a tourist attraction. English Wiktionary glosses it "old town, historic district" and Chinese Wiktionary "各城市或聚落的古老街區" (the old street quarter of a city or settlement), both marking it as the plain literal sense. The usage is strongest in Taiwan and mainland China, where English Wikipedia's page "Old Street" lists over a dozen places so named (Tamsui Old Street, Daxi Old Street, Qibao Old Street in Shanghai, Gaochun Old Street in Nanjing and others) and says the term may be "a single street, or a cluster of buildings in a neighborhood". The Dan'a'yo word [[街道]] is the neutral word for a street, and 老街 the specific one for an old, historic one.
2. Lào Cai, the city and province of north-west Vietnam on the border with China, where the Red River (Sông Hồng) meets a tributary, facing Hekou (河口) in Yunnan across the river; it is the crossing for the railway between Hanoi and Kunming. In Chinese the place is written 老街 (English Wiktionary lists three Vietnamese senses of the Chinese entry: the province, and a ward, formerly the city, of Lào Cai). Under the 2025 reorganization of Vietnam's administrative units, as Japanese and Chinese Wikipedia read it on 2026-10-11, the city of Lào Cai was dissolved into wards and the province was merged with Yên Bái province to its south, with its administrative centre at Yên Bái; the name stays on the province and on Lào Cai ward. The same Chinese spelling is also the name of other places, which English Wiktionary lists and this vault does not give separate senses: Laukkai (老街), the capital of the Kokang self-administered zone in Shan State, Myanmar, and several townships called Laojie (老街鄉 or 老街鎮) in China.

The origin of the Vietnamese name. A familiar claim, ascribed in Vietnamese Wikipedia to the scholar Đào Duy Anh, is that Lào Cai is the French spelling "Lao-kai" of the Hán-Việt Lão Nhai; Japanese Wikipedia likewise gives the Hán-Việt reading as Lão Nhai but says the place name derives from a local-language reading of 老街, possibly Hmong <i>Log Kab</i>. Vietnamese Wikipedia argues the reverse, that Lào Cai comes from the Hmong name of the old market (Hmong <i>Log Kab</i>, "Lao Cai"), which Jean Dupuis recorded as "Lao-kai" on his 1879 map of Tonkin, and that the French then fixed the spelling; on that account Đào Duy Anh overlooked the local language. So the Chinese spelling 老街 is best read as a Chinese rendering that matches the sound and meaning of the local name, and Lào Cai is not a Hán-Việt word; which account is right I cannot judge.

The `vietnamese` field holds the compositional Hán-Việt reading lão nhai (老 lão, 街 nhai, both at hvdic.thivien.net, which has no entry for the compound); it is the Han-form reading, not the place name Lào Cai. Mandarin lǎojiē and Cantonese lou5 gaai1 are as at English Wiktionary. Japanese ろうがい (老 ROU, 街 GAI) and Korean 로가 (老 로, 街 가, the North Korean form) are the compositional Sino-Xenic readings; I found no attestation of 老街 as a Japanese or Korean word. The Japanese and Korean names of the city are ラオカイ and 라오까이, and Korean Wikipedia writes the province 老街省 as "노가성" (the South Korean form of the same Sino-Korean reading), also giving 라오까이성 as the usual form.

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 老街 as a Chinese noun (Mandarin lǎojiē, Cantonese lou5 gaai1) with the sense "old town, historic district" and as a Chinese proper noun with the four senses above; Chinese Wiktionary and Vietnamese Wiktionary have the same entry (Vietnamese Wiktionary glosses the noun "phố cổ, khu phố cổ"). Japanese and Korean Wiktionaries have no 老街 page. Chinese Wikipedia has a disambiguation page 老街 and an article on 老街省; English Wikipedia's "Old Street" page has the Chinese-speaking usage; Japanese and Korean Wikipedias have no 老街 page and use ラオカイ and 라오까이. No homophones (注音 ㄌㄚㄨㄍ⼘ㄧ unique across words, chengyu and characters). The 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot because ㄌㄚㄨㄍ⼘ㄧ has only one legal division.
