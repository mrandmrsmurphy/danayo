## Notes
- [百 (char)](../characters/百%20(char).md) "hundred"

百 is the bare numeral "hundred," the base for every multiple of one hundred: the digit-plus-百 compounds such as [二百](二百.md), [七百](七百.md) and [八百](八百.md) are fully transparent, and the explicit [一百](一百.md) "one hundred" is the form used when the numeral must be stated as a whole figure rather than as a bare unit. The character is a phonetic-semantic compound of 一 "one" and 白, which supplies the sound; the two are near-identical in Middle Chinese (p/b + ɣæk, a regular voicing alternation on the same rime).

Beyond exact counting, 百 is the classic East Asian "many" or "all": [百年](百年.md) "a hundred years" (a lifetime or a century), [百事](百事.md) "myriad issues," [百越](百越.md) "the Hundred Yue peoples," and [百済](百済.md) "Baekje" all use it for an indefinite large totality. Its reading is stable across the sphere (Mandarin bǎi, Cantonese baak3, Japanese hyaku, Korean 백, Sino-Vietnamese bách), though each language also keeps a native word beside it: Japanese もも, Vietnamese trăm.

Because 百 is exactly [白](白.md) in Dan'a'yo (both 注音 ㄅㄚㄎ), the two are homophones, and the context of a numeral position normally disambiguates them. In Dan'a'yo 百 keeps the shared CJKV sense with no divergence.
