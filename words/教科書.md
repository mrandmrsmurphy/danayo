## Notes
- [教](../characters/教.md) "teach" + [科](../characters/科.md) "branch, section" + [書](../characters/書.md) "book" — "book for a teaching subject." None of the three is a stand-in here: 教 is legitimized by [[教授]], 科 by [[学科]] and 書 by [[書本]], and this compound simply uses all three as bound morphemes.

教科書 is the standard word for a school textbook: the book assigned for a particular 教科 ("teaching subject, curriculum subject"), as opposed to a general reference or reader. It is built on the same 教 as [[教材]] ("teaching materials", the broader category that includes workbooks, charts and audiovisual aids) and on 科 as in [[学科]] and [[科学]], with 書 supplying the "book" sense (compare [[書籍]] and the stand-in [[書本]]).

The word is a Japanese coinage of the modern school system (wasei kango): English Wiktionary describes it as orthographically borrowed from Japanese and then spread through the sphere. Japanese きょうかしょ (kyōkasho) is the term of the national textbook-authorization (検定) system, with テキスト as a looser synonym. Mandarin 教科书 jiàokēshū (traditional 教科書, Cantonese gaau3 fo1 syu1) and Korean 교과서 (gyogwaseo, with hanja 敎科書) are the standard, everyday word in each language. Mandarin also has the near-synonym 課本 and the more formal 教材, while Japanese and Korean both distinguish 教科書 (a state-approved classroom book) from 教材/교재 (any teaching material).

Vietnamese is the outlier: it uses the native-syntax calque sách giáo khoa ("book of teaching and subject"), whose word order is Vietnamese (head noun first) rather than a Sino-Vietnamese compound; English Wiktionary labels it a calque. No Hán-Việt reading of the string 教科書 as one word is listed on hvdic.thivien.net, so no `vietnamese` field is given, following the convention that native words belong in prose instead of the field.

Here `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 굣콰쇼 differs from Sino-Korean 교과서 in every syllable (教 gyou with final glide vs 교, 科 kwa vs 과 with aspiration, 書 syo vs 서). This is a clean compound with no homophone in the vault.
