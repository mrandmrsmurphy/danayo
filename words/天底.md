## Notes
- [天 (char)](../characters/天%20(char).md) "sky" + [底 (char)](../characters/底%20(char).md) "bottom" — literally "sky's bottom." The direct opposite of [[天頂]] ("zenith," literally "sky's crown/top"): the nadir is the point on the celestial sphere directly beneath the observer, always 180° from the zenith.

Mandarin tiāndǐ, Cantonese tin1 dai2, Korean 천저 (cheonjeo), and Japanese てんてい (tentei) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real astronomical term for "nadir" in its language. Vietnamese thiên để is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese — **a real reading gap found and fixed 2026-09-22**: 底's own character page was missing để (this specific compound's own attested Vietnamese reading, distinct from đáy or the previously-stored near-miss đé) entirely; added, sourced directly against Vietnamese Wikipedia's own explicit correction of common mistranslations of this term (thiên để, not thiên đề/thiên đế). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 턴데 diverges from Sino-Korean 천저 in both syllables. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄊㄝㄋㄉㄝㄧ unique among both words and characters).
