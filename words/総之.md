## Notes
- [総](../characters/総.md) "whole; total" + [之](../characters/之%20(char).md) "of; it" — literally "to sum it up."

総之 (traditional 總之, simplified 总之) is a sentence adverb and discourse linker: it closes off a run of examples, objections, or qualifications with the one conclusion that holds regardless, "in short; in sum; in a word; all in all." The 之 is the old third-person pronoun, so the sense is "gathering it (all together)," the same image as 総 "to bind together with a rope." It is related to [畢竟](畢竟.md) "after all," which also lands on the final state of things but looks at how matters end up, whereas 総之 summarizes what has just been said. It is not the noun [大旨](大旨.md) "gist," which names the content of a summary rather than introducing one, and it sits beside [要約](要約.md) "summary" and [結果](結果.md) "result" in the same family of wrap-up vocabulary.

According to Wiktionary (en and zh, checked 2026-10-11), the word is attested in Chinese: Mandarin zǒngzhī, Cantonese zung2 zi1, glossed "in short; in a word" and "anyhow," with 總而言之 and 總的來說 as relatives; the Japanese Wiktionary lists 總之 only as a Chinese entry ("要するに"). It is not a Japanese dictionary entry in its own right, but it is used in Japanese readings of Classical Chinese (漢文訓読), where 総之 is read これを総ずるに (kore o sōzuru ni), "to sum this up"; a Japanese character-history reference gives this expression explicitly (per the user, not re-checked here). Ordinary Japanese says 総じて (そうじて) "generally, as a rule," or 要するに, so the field `japanese: そうし` is the Sino-Xenic bound reading of the two characters. No Korean or Vietnamese Wiktionary entry for 총지 or tổng chi could be found, so those two fields are compositional (総 Sino-Korean 총 + 之 지; Sino-Vietnamese tổng, confirmed for 總 at hvdic, + chi). Their independent attestation was not verified.

Dan'a'yo-specific sense: the shared one, a concluding adverb at the head of a sentence. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄛㄫㄊㄧ unique across words and characters).
