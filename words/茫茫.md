## Notes
- [茫](../characters/茫.md) "vast, boundless" + [茫](../characters/茫.md) "vast, boundless" — reduplicated for vividness; an ideophone (擬詞) rather than a plain stative, since the doubling itself is what carries the intensified, atmospheric sense, the same pattern as [[懍懍]] and [[業業]].

Reduplicated 茫茫 covers both a purely visual sense — a landscape so vast it blurs into haze, as in 茫茫大海 "the boundless ocean" or 茫茫沙漠 "the endless desert" — and a cognitive one, a mind or memory gone hazy and indistinct (茫茫たる記憶 in Japanese, "a hazy, indistinct memory"). The two senses share one image: something too vast to bring into focus, whether that vastness is physical space or one's own recollection. The Vietnamese reading *mang mang* used here is the direct Sino-Vietnamese one (the page's earlier claim that a line "江水茫茫江岸平" from Nguyễn Du's *Truyện Kiều* is its classical citation is unverified, since *Truyện Kiều* is written in chữ Nôm); modern colloquial Vietnamese has since drifted this into *mênh mang*, blending in a native element, so the two forms coexist with the older one preserved mainly in poetry.

English Wiktionary gives 茫茫 one Chinese sense, an adjective, "boundless and indistinct; vast", and cites the folk song *Chilege* (敕勒歌), "天蒼蒼，野茫茫，風吹草低見牛羊" ("Blue, blue appear the skies; vast, vast the grassland lies"), attributed to 斛律金 in 546 but more likely an older Xianbei folk song recorded in the *Yuefu shiji*. Its derived terms include 白茫茫, 霧茫茫, 茫茫然, 茫茫苦海 and 茫茫一片. The Dimensions page lists both senses given above.

Mandarin mángmáng and Cantonese mong4 mong4 are as at English Wiktionary.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄇㄚㄫㄇㄚㄫ (checked 2026-10-12).
