## Notes
- [総](../characters/総.md) "whole; total" + [統](../characters/統.md) "command; govern; unite"

**総統** (*zǒngtǒng*, "supreme governor") is the standard Dan'a'yo word for the president or head of state of a republic. The compound literally means "overall command" — 総 "total; comprehensive" + 統 "to govern; to unify; to command" — carrying a stronger sense of unified executive authority than its Japanese and Korean counterpart 大統領.

The term is most closely associated with the Republic of China ([[中華民国]]), where 總統 has been the title of the head of state under the 1947 constitution (in force 1948) and remains so in Taiwan today; the head of state before that was styled 臨時大總統 (Provisional President) in 1912. In Chinese-speaking usage it is also the ordinary word for the president of any republic, including the United States. Vietnamese *tổng thống* is likewise the standard term for a president in Vietnamese political discourse.

Japanese and Korean usage differ. Both ordinarily use 大統領 (*daitōryō* / *daetongnyeong*) for a president, and Japanese 総統 (*sōtō*) is now chiefly encountered as the title of Taiwan's president and, historically, as the rendering of the German *Führer* (as in Hitler's title). Korean 총통 (*chongtong*) is likewise used for Taiwan's president. In Dan'a'yo 総統 is the general word for the head of state of a [[共和国]]; it keeps the shared Chinese and Vietnamese sense, not the narrower Japanese/Korean one.

For the specifically Korean usage, see [[国務領]], the title of the head of the [[大韓民国]] Provisional Government (1925–1927).
