## Notes
- [棚 (char)](../characters/棚%20(char).md) "shelf" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the character 棚 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

Dan'a'yo takes 棚 in the Japanese sense, "shelf", which is the Buildings page's "shelf". The Chinese senses differ (see below).

Japanese has essentially abandoned the Sino-Japanese reading of this character in ordinary use: 棚 stands almost always as native たな (tana), not on'yomi ほう, and that holds even inside compounds — 本棚 (hondana, "bookshelf"), 食器棚 (shokkidana, "cupboard"), and even the technical term 大陸棚 (tairikudana, "continental shelf") all keep the native reading rather than switching to Sino-Japanese. Korean does the opposite in that exact same technical term: 대륙붕 ("continental shelf") keeps the Sino-Korean reading 붕, even though the everyday standalone word for an ordinary household shelf is 선반 (native), not bare 붕 — so Korean and Japanese land on opposite readings (Sino vs. native) for the same geological term while both abandon the Sino form for everyday furniture. Vietnamese shows a similar drift: kệ, the ordinary word for "shelf" (kệ sách, "bookshelf"), doesn't derive transparently from a Sino-Vietnamese reading of 棚 either.

English Wiktionary gives the Chinese character (a 形声 of 木 "tree" and the phonetic 朋) none of the "shelf" sense. Its Chinese senses are "tent; awning; canopy (of reed mats)", "shed; shack", "ceiling" (頂棚), the Hong Kong Cantonese and Hokkien "scaffolding; trellis", and Cantonese and Hokkien classifiers for groups of people, sets of teeth or bones, and theatrical plays. Its compounds include 大陸棚 ("continental shelf"), 棚屋, 草棚 and 攝影棚. So "shelf" is a Japanese-led sense, and Chinese speakers who read the word on this page will meet a different meaning for 棚 elsewhere.

Mandarin péng and Cantonese paang4 are as at English Wiktionary. The Japanese field holds the Sino-Japanese reading ほう, while the everyday word is たな.

The homophone listed above, 房, is the only other word sharing the 注音 ㄅㄚㄫ (checked 2026-10-12).
