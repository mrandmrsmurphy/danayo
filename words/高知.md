## Notes
- [高](../characters/高%20(char).md) "high" + [知](../characters/知%20(char).md) "know"

高知 is Kōchi, written "high knowledge". The castle town was first called Kōchiyama and later renamed with the present characters by Yamauchi Kazutoyo in the early Edo period, partly to avoid a name associated with floods (from memory, not re-checked). The reading こうち is the on-reading of 高 (こう) plus 知 (ち).

Kōchi occupies the Pacific side of Shikoku, with its capital Kōchi, whose castle is one of the few with its original keep. It was the old Tosa domain of Sakamoto Ryōma, and is known for katsuo no tataki, the Shimanto River and the yosakoi festival.

In Dan'a'yo 高知 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 고치 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
