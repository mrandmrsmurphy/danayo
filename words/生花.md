## Notes
- [生](../characters/生.md) "to grow, to be born" + [花](../characters/花.md) "flower" — "grow flowers."

生花 is, as a verb, to put forth blossoms, to bloom, in a literary register (Wiktionary marks it as a literary verb). It is also a noun in a second, unrelated formation, 生 "fresh, live" + 花 "flower": fresh-cut or live flowers, as opposed to paper or silk ones; [鮮花](鮮花.md) is the plainer word for that. Figuratively, of writing, 生花 means brilliant and skilful, as in 生花之筆 and 妙筆生花, "a pen that brings forth flowers." It is the second half of [枯木生花](../chengyu/枯木生花.md). Japanese 生花 (せいか) is fresh flowers, and 生け花 (いけばな) is the art of flower arranging, which shares the character 生 "alive" but is a different word. The Chinese, Korean and Vietnamese readings all agree.

The character 花 is bound; its stand-in is [草花](草花.md), "flowering plants, ornamental flowers", so 生花 is the compound in which 花 is used for the act of flowering.
