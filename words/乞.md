## Notes
- [乞 (char)](../characters/乞%20(char).md) "beg"

乞 means "to beg, to request, to ask for" — most familiar today in [[乞丐]] ("beggar") and 乞食 (begging for food), including the Buddhist term describing monks' alms-begging practice. It also appears in classical constructions as a humble request marker ("please grant me...").

Japanese has two on'yomi, こつ/き (kotsu/ki), alongside native こ(う) (ko(u), the everyday verb "to beg, request, ask for"). Korean 걸 (geol) is the Sino-Korean reading, alongside native 빌 (bil), the everyday native verb root for "to beg." Vietnamese khất is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in hành khất ("to beg; a beggar") and the Buddhist term khất thực ("to beg for alms") — the character page's other two candidates (gật, "to nod"; khắt, part of khắt khe "strict, harsh") belong to unrelated words and were not used here.
