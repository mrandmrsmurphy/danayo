## Notes
- [十 (char)](../characters/十%20(char).md) "ten(th)" + [月 (char)](../characters/月%20(char).md) "month" — literally "tenth month."

十月 is the standard Gregorian-calendar name for October, formed the same transparent "number + 月" way as every other month in this vault's calendar vocabulary (compare [[九月]] "September," [[十一月]] "November"). It's distinct from [[戌月]] ("dog month"), the corresponding month of the older lunar/zodiacal calendar, which does not align neatly with October on the solar calendar.

Mandarin shíyuè, Cantonese sap6jyu6, and Korean 시월 (siwol) are all standard, fully compositional readings — Korean 시월 is a known irregular contraction of 십월 (a rare 두음법칙-unrelated elision, the same pattern seen in 유월 for 六月), kept here since it's the actually-attested standard form. Japanese じゅうがつ (jūgatsu) uses the Sino-Japanese on'yomi じゅう for "ten" (unlike the native-kun'yomi-based はちがつ, 九月's くがつ, etc. — Japanese month names mix native and Sino-Japanese numeral readings unpredictably by month). Vietnamese tháng mười is a native construction ("month ten"), not a Sino-Vietnamese compound — the everyday way all Vietnamese month names are formed. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 십웓 diverges from Sino-Korean 시월 (which contracts away the ㅂ). No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄆ·⼔ㄊ unique among both words and characters).
