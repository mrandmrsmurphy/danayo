## Notes
- [狼 (char)](../characters/狼%20(char).md) "wolf" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

狼 names the wolf, formed 形声 from semantic 犬 ("dog") + phonetic 良. Beyond the literal animal, it's productive in figurative compounds across the CJKV sphere for cunning, ferocity, or predatory danger — 狼狽 ("flustered, in disarray," from a folk-etymological pair of animals that could only move together), 天狼星 ("Dog Star," i.e. Sirius), and the chengyu 羊衣餓狼 ("a wolf in sheep's clothing").

Mandarin láng, Cantonese long4, and Korean 랑 (rang) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese ろう (rō, on'yomi) is productive in Sino-Japanese compounds, while the native kun'yomi おおかみ (ōkami) is the everyday word for the animal itself. Vietnamese lang is the standard Hán Việt reading, though everyday Vietnamese instead uses the native chó sói for "wolf." `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 랑 matches Sino-Korean 랑 exactly.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (lang/랑/ㄌㄚㄫ) with [[冷]] ("cool, cold").
