## Notes
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) "one; whole" + [貫](../characters/貫.md) "pierce; string through" — "one thread running through": the same principle or manner from beginning to end.

一貫 is the quality of being the same all the way through: a policy, argument, attitude, or procedure that does not change from one part to the next. It is the usual word for "consistent" and "consistency", and it also works adverbially for "throughout, from start to finish" (Japanese 一貫して, Chinese 一贯, "consistently, all along"). Its negation 不一貫 "inconsistent" is the natural way to say that a system fails to behave uniformly, as in the writing-system chapter's remark that shinjitai in practice is 不一貫 (see [[文法 - 03文字法]]).

The image is of a single cord strung through many coins or beads, which is exactly what 貫 depicts (a string of cash coins was itself called a 貫, and the character is the basis of the Japanese weight unit as well). The compound is old: it descends from the Analects, where Confucius says 吾道一以貫之, "my way is threaded by a single thing" (Analects 4.15 and 15.3). Everyday Chinese 一贯 and Japanese 一貫 (いっかん) both carry that abstract sense of unbroken consistency, and Wiktionary lists Chinese as adjective and adverb, Japanese as noun and verb ("integration, consistency with regard to policy, manner, approach"). In Japanese the word has a second, more concrete life in the sense of an integrated process, as in 一貫教育 (a school system that runs continuously from one stage to the next) and 一貫生産 (end-to-end production). Korean 일관 is used the same way (일관성 "consistency", 일관되게 "consistently"), and Vietnamese *nhất quán* is the ordinary word for "consistent" (hvdic.thivien.net confirms the reading and the Analects origin). The compound is shared across all five languages with the same core meaning, so no Dan'a'yo-specific narrowing is needed.

Within the vault it is distinct from [[一定]] "fixed, regular" (which says a value does not vary, not that a principle runs through a whole), from [[一致]] "agreement, coincidence" (two things matching, not one thing staying the same), and from [[統一]] "unification" (making disparate things one). It shares 貫 with [[貫通]] "to pierce through", the stand-in that legitimizes bound 貫; 一貫 is a separate word and not a stand-in for either of its characters.

Readings: Mandarin, Cantonese (jat1 gun3), Japanese and the Korean 일관 are from Wiktionary and standard usage; Vietnamese from hvdic.thivien.net. Dan'a'yo 諺文 읻관 differs from the Korean reading 일관 (一 is stored as 읻), so `kwin` is false. No homophones (注音 ㄧㄊㄍ⺢ㄋ is unique among words and chengyu).
