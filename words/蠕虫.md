## Notes
- [蠕](../characters/蠕.md) "wriggle, squirm" + [虫](../characters/虫.md) "bug, insect" — the Swadesh list's basic-vocabulary term for "worm" (entry 50).

蠕虫 is the general biological term for "worm" — any elongated, legless, soft-bodied invertebrate — built from 蠕 ("to wriggle, to squirm," the motion characteristic of worms) plus the general insect/bug classifier 虫. Its own alias 蠕蟲 is the traditional-character spelling (蟲→虫, the standard simplification).

Mandarin 蠕虫 (rúchóng) is the formal/biological term, with everyday speech instead reaching for 虫子 (chóngzi, "bug, worm, creepy-crawly," a much broader and more colloquial category) or specific names like 蚯蚓 (qiūyǐn, "earthworm," the Swadesh table's own Mandarin/Cantonese gloss for this entry). Cantonese patterns the same way (蠕虫 formal vs. 蟲 or specific names colloquial). Japanese 蠕虫 (ぜんちゅう, zenchū) is a real, standard biological/medical term (used for parasitic worms, e.g. 蠕虫症, helminthiasis), distinct from the everyday native word 虫 (mushi, "bug" generally, not specific to worms). Korean has no direct compositional or native equivalent commonly used — 연충 (yeonchung) exists as the Sino-Korean compositional cross-reference, but everyday Korean instead uses 벌레 (beolle, "bug, worm" generally) or specific terms like 지렁이 (jireongi, "earthworm"). Vietnamese similarly lacks an everyday compositional form — nhu trùng is given here as the compositional Sino-Vietnamese cross-reference, while everyday speech uses giun ("worm," specifically) or sâu ("caterpillar, grub, worm" more broadly).

kwin is false: neither 蠕 nor 虫 individually match their own Sino-Korean readings against Dan'a'yo (蠕's Dan'a'yo 누 differs from Korean 연; 虫's Dan'a'yo 중 differs from Korean 충), so the compound comes out false under the AND-rule established earlier in this sweep.
