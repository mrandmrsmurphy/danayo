## Notes
- [鼻 (char)](../characters/鼻%20(char).md) "nose" — stand-in for [[鼻 (char)|鼻]], which cannot appear independently

鼻 is 形声: semantic [[Radical 132|自]] ("nose" — its own original meaning before being repurposed to "self," the same semantic shift already documented on [[自]]'s own page) + phonetic 畀 (no character page of its own in this vault). The character effectively preserves the "nose" meaning that its own semantic component lost, a genuine case of a compound outliving the plain sense of one of its parts. Beyond the plain anatomical sense (鼻水 "snot"), 阿鼻 transliterates Sanskrit Avīci, the lowest hell in Buddhist cosmology — a purely phonetic loan riding on this syllable, continued in 阿鼻叫喚 ("agonized cries amid tragedy, as if from Avīci itself").

Japanese carries two on'yomi, び (BI, used here — the character's dominant reading) and rarer ひ, alongside the fully productive native 訓読み はな (hana), the everyday word for a nose. Vietnamese tị is the character's sole stored reading, confined to a learned/technical register (as in compounds referencing the nose medically) rather than everyday speech, which uses the native mũi. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 비 (bi, from Middle Chinese 並母 b + 脂A三開韻 iɪ) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 비. No homophones (注音 ㄅㄧ shared only by characters — 碑, 痺, 脾, 琵, 婢, 皮 — none with a self-pointing `stand_in`).
