## Notes
- [及 (char)](../characters/及%20(char).md) "reach out to" + [第 (char)](../characters/第%20(char).md) "-th; ordinal"

及第 means to pass: originally to pass the imperial civil service examination (科挙), and now, in Japanese, to pass a grade. The literal sense is "to reach the ranking", to take a place on the list of the successful. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 及第 (Mandarin jídì, Cantonese kap6 dai6) as the historical, intransitive "to pass an imperial examination", and Japanese きゅうだい as "passing a grade; making the year", being able to advance to the next academic year, and "to pass a grade". It has no Korean or Vietnamese entry, so Korean 급제 and Vietnamese cập đệ here are built from the characters. Its antonym is [[落第]], which Wiktionary glosses as failing an imperial examination, by extension failing a test, and in Japanese failing to advance a grade.

English Wikipedia says the imperial examination system lasted 1,300 years, with the first earnest use of written examinations for recruitment under the Sui dynasty, and that the Korean gwageo were the civil service examinations of the Goryeo and Joseon periods. The poet Meng Jiao (孟郊, 751–814) passed the *jinshi* at 46, in 796, and wrote the poem 登科後, "After Passing the Examination", with the lines 春風得意馬蹄疾，一日看盡長安花, "in the spring wind, proud, the horse's hooves fly; in a single day I have seen all the flowers of Chang'an". See [[科挙]] for the system.
