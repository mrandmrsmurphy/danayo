## Notes
- [江](../characters/江.md) "river" + [戸](../characters/戸.md) "door" + [川 (char)](../characters/川%20(char).md) "river"

江戸川 is the Edo River, in the Kantō region of Japan. English Wikipedia says it splits from the Tone River at the northernmost tip of Noda, passes Nagareyama and Matsudo, and empties into Tokyo Bay at Ichikawa in Chiba Prefecture, forms the borders between Tokyo, Chiba and Saitama prefectures, and is 59.5 kilometres long; it was originally the lower course of the Tone, which the Tokugawa shogunate diverted in 1654.

English Wiktionary lists Japanese 江戸川 (えどがわ) as the Edo River, as one of the 23 special wards of Tokyo (Edogawa), and as a surname, as in the novelist Rampo Edogawa. It lists Chinese 江戶川 (an alias; Mandarin Jiānghùchuān, Cantonese gong1 wu6 cyun1) with the same river and ward, and as a surname rendering the Japanese. The Japanese reading is a native one: 戸 here is the 戸 of Edo, present-day Tokyo, and 川 is read がわ. The Dan'a'yo reading ㄍㄚㄫㄏㄛㄑ⺢ㄋ comes instead from the Middle Chinese values of the characters, and Korean 강호천 and Vietnamese giang hộ xuyên are built from the characters in the same way.
