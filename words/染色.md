## Notes
- [染](../characters/染.md) "dye" + [色](../characters/色.md) "color"
- This word is the viable form of [染](../characters/染.md): the character's meaning (to dye) is identical, but 染 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 染色 is its `stand_in` compound.

染 is to soak something in a coloring agent so the color takes, and 色 "color" names what is imparted, so 染色 is "to color by dyeing." The word covers dyeing cloth, yarn and hair, and it is also the standard technical term for staining specimens in biology and medicine (细胞染色 / 染色体 "chromosome," literally "stainable body"), which explains the English gloss "stain."

The reading is shared across the sphere: Mandarin rǎnsè, Cantonese jim5 sik1, Japanese senshoku (せんしょく), Korean 염색 (Korean also uses 염색 for hair-dyeing, a very common modern use). Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "to dye; to stain," with no divergence, and it is placed under [Clothing](../lexipedia/Clothing.md) for the textile sense.
