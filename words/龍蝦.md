## Notes
- [龍 (char)](../characters/龍%20(char).md) "dragon" + [蝦](../characters/蝦.md) "shrimp" — literally "dragon shrimp"; no `#cranberry`: 龍's own `stand_in` is 龍 itself, 蝦's is [[毛蝦]]; neither points here. `kwin` is true (AND-rule: both constituents individually true) — corrected from a stale `false`.

Japanese イセエビ (ise-ebi, "Ise shrimp/prawn," a real species name after Ise Bay) is the standard, everyday Japanese word for lobster, matching the established real-attested-usage convention. Vietnamese tôm hùm ("tiger shrimp," a real native compound) likewise is the everyday word rather than a Sino-Vietnamese compositional form. Mandarin lóngxiā and Cantonese lung4 haa1 are the plain compositional forms. **Fixed a real bug**: `korean` had been `가재`, the word for freshwater crayfish/crawfish — a related but distinct crustacean, not lobster — corrected to `바닷가재` ("sea crayfish"), the real, standard Korean word specifically for lobster.
