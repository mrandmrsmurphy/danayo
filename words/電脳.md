## Notes
- [電](../characters/電.md) "electricity" + [脳 (char)](../characters/脳%20(char).md) "brain" — no `#cranberry`: neither constituent's own `stand_in` points here (電's is [[電気]], 脳's is 脳 itself). Literally "electric brain."

電腦/电脑 (diànnǎo) is the standard, everyday word for "computer" throughout the Mandarin-speaking world (Mainland, Taiwan, Hong Kong), and Cantonese 電腦 (din6nou5) likewise. Japanese でんのう (電脳) is a real, attested word for computer with the same "electric brain" etymology, but it is not Japan's ordinary everyday term — that role belongs to the loanword コンピュータ; 電脳 instead survives in more formal/technical or hobbyist registers, and (via its use in works like *Ghost in the Shell*) has also acquired a niche pop-culture sense of "cyberbrain."

Korean does not use 전뇌 (電腦) as a standard word for computer at all — 컴퓨터 (a direct English loanword) is the only ordinary term, with the older native-formation 전자계산기 ("electronic calculating device") as a dated technical alternative. 전뇌 exists only in the same sci-fi/cyberpunk "cyberbrain" register that Japanese 電脳 acquired, borrowed directly from that Japanese pop-culture usage rather than functioning as genuine Sino-Korean vocabulary for "computer."

Sino-Vietnamese điện não is understood as the etymological equivalent of 電腦, but the standard modern Vietnamese word for "computer" is the native calque máy tính (literally "counting machine") — điện não itself is not in everyday use, appearing mainly as a gloss on the Chinese term rather than as live Vietnamese vocabulary.
