## Notes
- [甜](../characters/甜.md) "sweet" + [瓜](../characters/瓜.md) "cucumber, melon" — 甜 is a [名専字](../lookup/List%20of%20名専字.md), so it appears only inside compounds such as this one; 瓜's own stand-in is [[胡瓜]].

甜瓜 means melon, the sweet muskmelon (*Cucumis melo*). In Chinese it covers the species broadly, including the Hami melon. Japanese まくわうり (真桑瓜) names the specific Oriental variety *makuwa*, the small, sweet, yellow-skinned melon, and the generic word for a melon in Japanese today is the loanword メロン. Korean 참외 is the matching everyday word for this same yellow melon. The Dan'a'yo sense follows the shared "sweet melon"; the large watery [[西瓜]] (watermelon) and the savory [[胡瓜]] (cucumber) are the other two 瓜 words it contrasts with.

Vietnamese điềm qua is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading; everyday Vietnamese uses native words such as dưa for melons. The `japanese` and `korean` fields hold the real native names.
