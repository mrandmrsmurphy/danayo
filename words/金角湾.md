## Notes
- [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "gold" + [角 (char)](../characters/角%20(char).md) "horn" + [湾](../characters/湾.md) "bay" — "gold horn bay"; no stand-in relationship applies (金 and 角 are their own stand-ins; 湾's is [[海湾]]). The alias 金角灣 is the traditional spelling, since 灣 is an alias of 湾.

金角湾 is a proper noun with three referents, all of them bays. The first two are named for the same image; the third is a Dan'a'yo choice.

1. The Golden Horn (Turkish Haliç or Altın Boynuz, Greek Χρυσόν Κέρας), the long narrow inlet of the Bosphorus that divides old and new Istanbul in Turkey, and the natural harbour of [[君士坦丁堡]] (Constantinople). This is the sense given at English Wiktionary ("Golden Horn"), at Japanese and Korean Wikipedia (きんかくわん, 금각만) and at Chinese Wikipedia (金角湾 (伊斯坦布尔)). English Wiktionary describes the English name as a calque of the Greek Χρυσόκερας. The reasons usually given for the name, the horn-like curve of the inlet and the gold of its sunset light or of the wealth that passed through its port, are from memory, not re-checked. Vietnamese Wikipedia has the place as Sừng Vàng "gold horn", a native translation, not a Hán-Việt word.
2. Golden Horn Bay (Russian Бухта Золотой Рог, Zolotoy Rog), the horn-shaped inlet at Vladivostok (海參崴 in Chinese) in Russia, on which the city's main harbour stands. Chinese Wikipedia has it as a separate article, 金角湾 (符拉迪沃斯托克), and says it was named by Nikolay Muravyov-Amursky in 1859 after the similarly shaped harbour of Constantinople. Chinese Wiktionary's stub for 金角灣 carries only this sense (Zolotoy Rog or Golden Horn Bay in Vladivostok). Chinese Wikipedia's page 金角湾 is a disambiguation between these two.
3. Cam Ranh Bay (Vietnamese Vịnh Cam Ranh), the deep-water bay on the south-central coast of Vietnam in Khánh Hòa province, between Nha Trang and Phan Rang, known as a naval base under the French, Americans and Soviets. This is a Dan'a'yo-specific usage, a deliberate choice of the vault's author and not found in any dictionary: the city on its shore is [[金角]]. Chinese Wikipedia writes the bay 金蘭灣 (simplified 金兰湾) and English Wiktionary lists 金蘭灣 under the derived terms of 灣, and the city is 金蘭 at English Wiktionary (Mandarin jīnlán, Cantonese gam1 laan4). Japanese Wikipedia has the bay only as カムラン湾. No Chinese, Japanese, Korean or Vietnamese source or Wiktionary was found that calls Cam Ranh 金角灣 or 金角湾. The two spellings differ in one character, 角 "horn" against 蘭 "orchid"; Dan'a'yo uses 角, does not treat 金蘭灣 as an alias, and has no page for 金蘭. The Vietnamese name is not Hán-Việt; Vietnamese Wikipedia explains Cam Ranh as a transcription of Cham <i>Kăm M'ran</i>, "a place where ships gather, a port".

Mandarin Jīnjiǎowān and Cantonese gam1 gok3 waan1 are as at English Wiktionary; Japanese きんかくわん and Korean 금각만 are the readings at English Wiktionary and the Japanese and Korean Wikipedias. The `vietnamese` field holds the compositional Hán-Việt reading kim giác loan (金 kim, 角 giác, 灣 loan, each as at hvdic.thivien.net); hvdic has no entry for the compound itself, and the real Vietnamese names are Sừng Vàng for the Golden Horn (sense 1) and Vịnh Cam Ranh for sense 3. In Dan'a'yo the word keeps the shared CJKV sense: [[海湾]] is the common noun for a bay or gulf, [[海峡]] for the strait, and 金角湾 only the proper name.

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 金角灣 as a Chinese proper noun ("Golden Horn") with a Japanese and Korean descendants line, and 金角湾 as a simplified-form redirect. Chinese Wiktionary has a 金角灣 stub (Vladivostok sense only). Japanese Wiktionary, Korean Wiktionary and Vietnamese Wiktionary have no 金角灣 or 金角湾 page (the parse API returned no page for each). Chinese, Japanese and Korean Wikipedias have the articles named above for the Istanbul sense; Vietnamese Wikipedia has Sừng Vàng. Sense 3 is not in any dictionary; the Chinese name of Cam Ranh Bay, 金蘭灣, is on Chinese Wikipedia (金兰湾) and at English Wiktionary under 灣, and the city of Cam Ranh is 金蘭 at English and Chinese Wiktionary. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄧㄇㄍㄛㄎ·⺢ㄇ unique across both words and characters). The dot is needed because ㄍㄧㄇㄍㄛㄎ⺢ㄇ could also be cut as ㄍㄧㄇ·ㄍㄛ·ㄎ⺢ㄇ ("go" and "kwam" are both syllables), and the dot after ㄎ keeps 角's final.
