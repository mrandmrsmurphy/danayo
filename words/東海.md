## Notes
- [東](../characters/東.md) "east" + [海](../characters/海.md) "sea"
- In Classical Chinese cosmology, the Eastern Sea is one of the [四海](四海.md) — the four seas encircling the known world, alongside [西海](西海.md), [南海](南海.md), and [北海](北海.md). This is the primary Dan'a'yo sense.
- Modern geopolitical usages are language-dependent and secondary: what Koreans call 東海 (동해) is what Japanese call 日本海; what Chinese call 東海 (东海) is the East China Sea. Disambiguation is needed in any modern geographic context.

Mandarin Dōnghǎi, Cantonese dung1 hoi2, Korean 동해 (donghae), and Japanese とうかい (tōkai) are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 東's and 海's own stored citations. Vietnamese đông hải is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 東's đông and 海's own hải.
