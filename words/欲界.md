## Notes
- [欲](../characters/欲.md) "desire" + [界](../characters/界.md) "realm" — a translation of Sanskrit *kāma-dhātu*, the realm ruled by desire.

欲界 is the lowest of the [三界](三界.md), the three realms of Buddhist cosmology: the world of beings still bound by the desires for food and for sex. In the account of the *Abhidharmakośa* (倶舎論) it runs from the hells up to the six heavens of desire (六欲天) and takes in the hungry ghosts, animals and humans on the way; schools that add the asuras speak of the six paths (六道). Every being in it has a body of matter and the appetites that go with one, the gods among them, though the higher the heaven the weaker the craving. The six heavens are the Four Heavenly Kings, Trāyastriṃśa (the thirty-three gods, ruled by Śakra), Yāma, Tuṣita, the heaven of delighting in creation, and the heaven that borrows others' creations. Next above it lies the [色界](色界.md). The Mandarin, Cantonese and Japanese readings are the attested ones; the Korean follows the North Korean readings of the characters.

Compare [欲望](欲望.md), "desire", the quality that names the realm.
