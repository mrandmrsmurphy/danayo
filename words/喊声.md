## Notes
- [喊](../characters/喊.md) "war cry, shout" + [声](../characters/声.md) "sound, voice"
- This word is the stand-in for [喊](../characters/喊.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

The sound of shouting — a raised voice in anger, encouragement, or battle. In Chinese, 喊声 (hǎnshēng) covers any loud cry, from a vendor's call to a battle shout; the more literary 呐喊 (nàhǎn) emphasizes the rallying or protest dimension. In Korean, 함성 (喊聲) is the standard word for "battle cry" or "roar of a crowd," common in sports broadcasts (관중의 함성 "the roar of the crowd") and historical narratives of war. Japanese かんせい (kansei) is largely confined to classical or military contexts.

喊 is a 形声 character with the 口 "mouth" radical, naming a loud shout meant to carry over a distance (to call out, to yell), and 声 "voice, sound" makes it the thing heard. The word therefore sits close to the general [叫](叫.md) "to cry out" and [叫喚](叫喚.md) "to shout, cry out," but its core is the massed or ringing cry, the one raised in battle or by a crowd.

Here Dan'a'yo's use is narrower than Chinese. The Speech page treats 喊声 as the specialized military register, "a shout, battle cry, war cry," with the sense of a noun for the cry itself, in contrast to the verbal [呼喚](呼喚.md) "to call out to," while Chinese uses it for any loud cry. The Sino-Vietnamese reading is hảm thanh. Readings: Mandarin hǎnshēng (alias 喊聲), Cantonese haam3 sing1, Japanese kansei, Korean 함성.
