## Notes
- [白 (char)](../characters/白%20(char).md) "white" + [頭 (char)](../characters/頭%20(char).md) "head" + [翁 (char)](../characters/翁%20(char).md) "venerable old man" — literally "white-headed old man," an image of white hair on an elder.

The compound carries three related senses across the Sinosphere. Literally, it names a white-haired elder. By visual extension it names the light-vented bulbul (Pycnonotus sinensis), a common East Asian songbird with a distinctive white patch on its head resembling an old man's white hair. It is also, separately, the standard traditional-medicine name for the dried root of Pulsatilla cernua (Chinese pulsatilla/anemone), a fibrous, whitish-tufted root used to treat dysentery and inflammation — the plant's shaggy white seed-heads likewise evoke an old man's hair.

Mandarin báitóuwēng, Cantonese baak6 tau4 jung1, Japanese はくとうおう (hakutouou), Korean 백두옹 (baekduong), and Vietnamese bạch đầu ông are all fully compositional and used consistently for the herbal-medicine sense in particular, which is the compound's most technically stable and widespread modern usage.
