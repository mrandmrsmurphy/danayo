## Notes
- [飛 (char)](../characters/飛%20(char).md) "fly" — stand-in for [[飛 (char)|飛]], which cannot appear independently

飛 is 象形 of a bird flying upwards, one of the vault's `hapax`-tagged characters (appearing once in a fixed, non-productive graphemic role rather than anchoring a wide phonetic family the way most characters do). The character page's own Notes already document a real aspectual distinction directly relevant to this word: bare 飛 denotes a short, immediate jaunt of flight, while [[飛行]] names prolonged or intentional flying — "the means of travel known as flying" as a whole, an inheritance from the same aspectual split found in Mandarin. The compound family plays out this distinction consistently: 飛鳥 ("a flying bird," also the proper name Asuka) and 飛翔 ("to soar") both read as momentary/vivid images, while 飛行/飛行机 ("flight/airplane") name the sustained activity and its vehicle. 飛語/流言飛語 ("baseless rumor(s) flying about") extends the flight image figuratively to unverified information spreading fast.

Japanese carries a single on'yomi ひ (HI), alongside the native verb 飛ぶ (とぶ, tobu), the everyday word for "to fly"; Korean has native 날다. Vietnamese phi is the Sino-Vietnamese reading used here (phi công "pilot," phi trường "airport"), distinct from the character's other stored reading bay, the native Vietnamese verb "to fly" itself.
