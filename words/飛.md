## Notes
- [飛 (char)](../characters/飛%20(char).md) "fly" — stand-in for [[飛 (char)|飛]], which cannot appear independently

飛 is 象形 of a bird flying upwards, one of the vault's `hapax`-tagged characters (appearing once in a fixed, non-productive graphemic role rather than anchoring a wide phonetic family the way most characters in this sweep do). The character page's own Notes already document a real aspectual distinction directly relevant to this word: bare 飛 denotes a short, immediate jaunt of flight, while [[飛行]] names prolonged or intentional flying — "the means of travel known as flying" as a whole, an inheritance from the same aspectual split found in Mandarin. The compound family plays out this distinction consistently: 飛鳥 ("a flying bird," also the proper name Asuka) and 飛翔 ("to soar") both read as momentary/vivid images, while 飛行/飛行机 ("flight/airplane") name the sustained activity and its vehicle. 飛語/流言飛語 ("baseless rumor(s) flying about") extends the flight image figuratively to unverified information spreading fast.

**Content fixed**: `pos` was `動詞` on this word's own page but `事詞` on `characters/飛 (char).md` — per this sweep's established convention that a stand-in word always mirrors its own character's `pos` exactly (already confirmed on [[走]]/[[行]] earlier), corrected to `事詞` to match. **Incidental fix**: added the missing `>[!tip]` disambiguation banner to both this word's page and `characters/飛 (char).md` itself, neither of which had one.

Japanese carries a single on'yomi ひ (HI), alongside no separate native 訓読み (`japanese_native: ø` on the character page — と(ぶ) tobu is the actual everyday native verb "to fly," so this is a likely real gap, the same `ø`-despite-clear-native-life pattern already flagged on [[手]]/[[玉]] earlier in this sweep). Vietnamese phi is the Sino-Vietnamese reading used here (phi công "pilot," phi trường "airport"), distinct from the character's other stored reading bay, the native Vietnamese verb "to fly" itself. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 뻬 (fei, from Middle Chinese 非母 f + 微合韻 ʉi) diverges from Sino-Korean 비. No homophones (注音 ㄈㄝㄧ unique among both words and characters).
