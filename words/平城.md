## Notes
- [平](../characters/平.md) "level" + [城](../characters/城.md) "castle, city wall" — no stand-in relationship applies (平's own is [[水平]]; 城's own is [[城郭]]). Literally "level castle" or "castle on the plain".

The string 平城 names three places and also has one rare ordinary sense.

1. (Japanese, rare) a castle built on level ground or flatland.
2. Pingcheng, the capital of the Northern Wei dynasty (北魏) from 398 until the move to Luoyang in 494, in what is now [[大同]] in Shanxi ([[山西]]); earlier a county of the Han dynasty (the Wei dates are from memory, not re-checked). English Wiktionary glosses it "Pingcheng County, during the Han and Northern Wei dynasties, located around modern-day Datong, Shanxi".
3. Heijō, short for 平城京 (Heijō-kyō), the capital of ancient Japan during most of the Nara period (710–784, then briefly again to 794, from memory, not re-checked), at the site of present-day [[奈良]]; also short for 平城宮 (Heijō-kyū), the imperial palace at the north end of the city. English Wiktionary notes that the short form is the on'yomi of an alternative spelling of 奈良.
4. Pyongsong, a city in South Pyongan Province, North Korea (Hangul 평성시, 平城市), the provincial capital, about 20 km north-east of [[平壌]] (the province name is [[平安]]; provincial-capital status and the distance are from memory, not re-checked). It is a science and education centre and a satellite of Pyongyang (from memory, not re-checked).

English Wiktionary lists the Pyongsong sense first and the Pingcheng sense second under Chinese; here the older Chinese and Japanese senses come before the Korean one. The castle sense is first because it is the one ordinary sense, listed by English Wiktionary (a rare noun under へいじょう) and Japanese Wiktionary.

Readings. Mandarin Píngchéng and Cantonese ping4 sing4 are given by English Wiktionary for the Chinese proper noun; Chinese Wiktionary has Píng chéng for the string, glossed as Pyongsong. Japanese へいじょう is given by English Wiktionary for senses 2 and 3 (and, rare, for the castle). Korean 평성 is the reading from the character pages, which is the North Korean form; the Korean Wiktionary has 平城 as a hanja form of 평성. Vietnamese Bình Thành is the Sino-Vietnamese reading built from the character pages (not attested for the string: Vietnamese Wiktionary has no entry, and the Vietnamese Wikipedia titles the Korean city Pyongsong).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11. English Wiktionary: Chinese proper noun, both the Pyongsong city and the Pingcheng historical county senses; Japanese section as above. Chinese Wiktionary: Pyongsong only. Japanese Wiktionary: the castle noun only, under へいじょう (with ひらじょう and ひらじろ as other readings). Korean Wiktionary: hanja form of 평성. Vietnamese Wiktionary: no entry. Cantonese ping4 sing4 is from English Wiktionary.
