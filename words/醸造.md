## Notes
- [醸](../characters/醸.md) "brew" + [造](../characters/造.md) "create"
- This word is the viable form of [醸](../characters/醸.md): the character's meaning (to brew, ferment) is identical, but 醸 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 醸造 is its `stand_in` compound.

醸 (traditional 釀) is a 形声 character with the 酉 "wine vessel" radical and means to brew, to let ferment, and by extension to bring about gradually (as in 醸成 "to foster, give rise to"), and 造 is "to make." So 醸造 is "to make by fermenting": the industrial and craft production of sake, beer, soy sauce, vinegar and miso, in which a microbial fermentation does the transformation. The extended sense of brewing up an atmosphere or a situation is carried by 醸 more than by the compound.

It is the broad production term, which is why the Buildings page reserves a place for 醸造所, the brewery (Japanese 醸造所 じょうぞうじょ, Korean 양조장, with Mandarin using an unrelated word, 釀酒廠). The aliases 釀造 and 酿造 are the traditional and simplified Chinese spellings. The readings are Mandarin niàngzào, Cantonese yeung6 zou6, Japanese jōzō (じょうぞう) and Korean 양조. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "brew, ferment," with no divergence, placed under [Food](../lexipedia/Food.md).
