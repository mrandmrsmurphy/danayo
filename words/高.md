## Notes
- [高 (char)](../characters/高%20(char).md) "high" — stand-in for [[高 (char)|高]], which cannot appear independently. Also serves as the vault's periodic-table abbreviation prefix for gallium via <ruby>[高素](/words/高素.md)<rt>ㄍㄚㄨㄙㄛ</rt></ruby>

高 is 象形 of a tall building, the character page's own Notes comparing it to [[京]], a similarly tower-shaped pictograph that never became a Kangxi radical in its own right the way 高 did. Beyond the plain "high, tall" sense, the character generalizes to social/moral elevation (高人 "a person of noble character," 崇高 "lofty, sublime") and educational advancement (高等 "higher level, advanced," 高校 "secondary school," 高考 "the higher [college entrance] exam"). Distinctively, 高 also serves this vault's transliteration needs for several place/ethnonym names sharing the Dan'a'yo syllable coincidentally or semi-coincidentally: 高盧 (Gaul), 高句麗/高麗 (Goguryeo/Goryeo, genuine historical dynastic names that literally begin with this character), and 高綿 (Cambodia).

Japanese carries a single on'yomi こう (KOU, 高等 kōtō), with no separate native 訓読み listed on the character's own page (`japanese_native: ø`) despite たかい (takai, "high, tall") being an extremely common, fully productive everyday adjective — the same `ø`-despite-clear-native-life gap already flagged on [[手]]/[[玉]]/[[飛]] earlier in this sweep. Vietnamese cao is the character's primary reading (over the character's other stored variant sào, an unrelated traditional land-area unit) and remains the ordinary everyday word for "tall/high" in Vietnamese itself, unlike most Sino-Vietnamese readings covered in this sweep that stay confined to a learned register. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 갓 (gau, from Middle Chinese 見母 k + 豪韻 ɑu) diverges from Sino-Korean 고 in the coda, Dan'a'yo retaining a final consonant Korean lacks.

**Real homophone found, a genuine phonetic family**: `注音 ㄍㄚㄨ`/`갓` is also carried by [[稿]] ("manuscript, copy, rough draft") — already listed among 高's own Derived Characters, a direct phonetic descendant rather than a coincidental convergence.
