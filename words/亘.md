## Notes
- [亘 (char)](../characters/亘%20(char).md) "swirl, whirlpool"

亘's original, now-archaic sense in Chinese was literally "to revolve, whirlpool" — the glyph is thought to combine 二 ("two," here representing horizon lines) with an enclosing/circular element (囘), depicting something turning within bounds. This original sense fell out of use, and 亘 came to be identified with the visually similar character 亙 ("to extend all the way through, span continuously" — as in 亘古/亙古, gèngǔ, "since time immemorial"), the sense in which the character survives in ordinary modern use.

Japanese has three on'yomi (セン/カン/コウ, sen/kan/kō) alongside native わた(る) (wata(ru), "to span, extend over" — e.g. 全国に亘って, "spanning the whole country"), reflecting the "extend/span" sense rather than the archaic "whirlpool" one. Korean 긍 (geung) and Vietnamese cắng — one of three genuinely attested Hán Việt readings for this character (cắng, hoàn, tuyên), verified via dictionary search rather than guessed among five other implausible near-homophone candidates stored on the character page — both correspond to the "extend, continuous" sense as well; no attested reading in any daughter language distinguishes the archaic "whirlpool" sense from the later "extend" sense.

This word is part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read hwan/환/ㄏ⺢ㄋ): [[喚]] ("summon") and [[環]] ("ring"), both still awaiting their own turn in this sweep — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed on each when it comes up.
