## Notes
- [平](../characters/平.md) "even" + [坦](../characters/坦.md) "flat; level" — this word is the stand-in for [坦](../characters/坦.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

平坦 describes physical flatness or evenness — level ground, a smooth road (平坦な道, "an even/flat road").

Japanese へいたん and Korean 평탄 both preserve this literal "physically flat/level" sense directly, and extend it figuratively to a smooth, untroubled life or career (平坦な人生, "an untroubled life"). **Vietnamese bình thản has drifted away from the physical sense entirely**: it now names a state of inner calm, serenity, and equanimity of mind — psychological composure — rather than literal flatness, having generalized from the same figurative "untroubled" extension that Japanese/Korean still keep alongside the literal sense, while Vietnamese kept only the figurative branch. No homophones (注音 ㄅ⼶ㄫㄊㄚㄋ unique across both words and characters).
