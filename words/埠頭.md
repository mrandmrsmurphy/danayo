## Notes
- [埠](../characters/埠.md) "wharf" + [頭 (char)](../characters/頭%20(char).md) "head"
- This word is the viable form of [埠](../characters/埠.md): the character's meaning (wharf, quay) is identical, but 埠 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 埠頭 is its `stand_in` compound.

埠 is a 形声 character built on the phonetic 阜 with the earth radical 土: originally a landing place or embankment on a waterway, and later the port or trading town itself. The bound 埠 survives in Chinese place-name compounds (Cantonese 埗 is its variant form, listed as an alias here). 頭 "head" is the common noun suffix meaning "end, extremity, front": the quay is the head of the land where it meets the water.

The word is a shared Sinitic term across the sphere: Mandarin bùtóu, Cantonese bou6 tau4, Japanese futō (ふとう), Korean 부두 (North Korean pronunciation identical). In Dan'a'yo it covers the whole range of the English glosses, a structure where ships tie up and load: pier, wharf and quay. It keeps the shared CJKV sense, with no divergence needed. Its lexipedia entry is under [Water](../lexipedia/Water.md).
