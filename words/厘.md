## Notes
- [厘 (char)](../characters/厘%20(char).md) "centi-"

厘 (alternate form 釐) originally named a small unit of weight/length in the traditional Chinese decimal measurement system (one-thousandth of a 尺, or one-tenth of a 分), and by extension came to serve as the modern metric prefix "centi-" (厘米, límǐ, "centimeter"). It also names a small monetary/interest unit — 厘 as "0.1%" in interest-rate contexts (年利率一厘, "an annual interest rate of 0.1%").

Mandarin lí, Cantonese lei4, and Japanese りん/てん (rin/ten, on'yomi, largely confined to formal/technical and historical-currency contexts) all preserve the sense. Korean 리 (ri) parallels the Sino-xenic reading. Vietnamese has two spelling variants of the same reading, li and ly (quốc ngữ orthographic variants rather than distinct words), both attested in this metric/measurement sense.

This word is part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read li/리/ㄌㄧ): [[浬]] ("nautical mile," still awaiting its own turn) and [[里]] ("village," already perfected — its own callout has been added alongside this iteration).
