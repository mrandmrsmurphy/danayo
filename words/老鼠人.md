## Notes
- [老 (char)](../characters/老%20(char).md) "old; prefix" + [鼠](../characters/鼠.md) "rat" (the compound [[老鼠]] "rat, mouse") + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person"

老鼠人 (rat person) is a Chinese internet slang term describing a person in a state of chronic low energy and low motivation: sleeping through the day, awake at night, subsisting on takeout, scrolling without purpose, vaguely anxious about the future but unable to act on it. The name comes from the behavioral analogy to a rat — scurrying at the margins, avoiding light, hiding in small spaces, largely invisible to the productive world.

The term has a background in 鼠鼠文学 (rat literature), a self-deprecating meme that became popular in 2023 and began as a take on lower-income people working long hours in low-paid jobs (Xinbang, as cited by the marketing site Digitaling). An earlier version of this page said it came from a phonetic substitution of 鼠 for 叔 in 叔叔我呀 and that it had a parallel Russian source, крысиные люди; I could not verify either, so neither is stated here. The sources I found say the 老鼠人 label itself was first used jokingly by Chinese students in the UK who stayed in through its gloomy winters, and that short-video platforms then spread it across China.

In 2025 老鼠人 became a recognized lifestyle persona; Xinbang data cited by Digitaling put the #老鼠人 topic at nearly two billion views across platforms, about one billion of them on Douyin. Vlogs documenting daily rat-person existence — instant noodles at 3am, curtains drawn, a phone as the primary social contact — went viral. A variant phrase, 低能量老鼠人 (low-energy rat person), circulated particularly among Chinese students abroad, describing the isolation of a gloomy winter: hiding from the cold, avoiding sunlight, making no plans. Some viewers argue that the vlogs are performative or mass-produced for traffic and advertising.

老鼠人 sits on the same spectrum as [[臥平]] and [[放腐]] but occupies a distinct register. Where 躺平 is a political stance (a deliberate rejection of the 996 grind) and 摆烂 is an active nihilism (tanking deliberately), 老鼠人 describes something more diffuse and less chosen: a general state of being ground down, of days dissolving without intention. The rat person is not necessarily refusing the system — they may simply lack the energy to engage with it at all. The term is used both self-deprecatingly, by people who identify with the lifestyle, and descriptively, as a compassionate or rueful observation about a generation that has quietly withdrawn from public life.

In Dan'a'yo 老鼠人 names the low-energy, withdrawn state described above, as distinct from the deliberate refusal of [[臥平]] and the active nihilism of [[放腐]]; it is placed beside those two on the Efforts lexipedia page.
