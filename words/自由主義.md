## Notes
- [自](../characters/自.md) "self" + [由](../characters/由%20(char).md) "from, by" + [主](../characters/主.md) "master, main" + [義](../characters/義.md) "principle" — "doctrine of freedom," built on [[自由]] ("freedom") and [[主義]] ("-ism"). None of the characters' own `stand_in` points here, so 自由主義 is an independent compound.

自由主義 is liberalism, the political creed that puts individual liberty first. Mandarin zìyóu zhǔyì, Cantonese zi6 jau4 zyu2 ji6, Japanese じゆうしゅぎ and Korean 자유주의 use it; Vietnamese chủ nghĩa tự do reverses the order. In American English "liberal" has drifted toward the centre-left, while in much of Europe and East Asia 自由主義 still means classical, market-friendly liberalism; Dan'a'yo takes the broad sense. Compare [[保守]] (conservative) and [[共産主義]] (communism).
