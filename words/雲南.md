## Notes
- [雲 (char)](../characters/雲%20(char).md) "cloud" + [南](../characters/南.md) "south" — of unclear origin, first attested as a Han county. No stand-in relationship applies (雲 is its own stand-in; 南's own is [[南方]]). 云 is the alias of 雲, so 云南 is kept as the word's alias.

The older and non-province senses come first. English Wiktionary says the etymology of the Chinese name is unclear and that it is first attested in the Book of Han as a county under Yizhou commandery, located in the modern Xiangyun County of Yunnan. English and Chinese Wiktionary also list Unnan, a city in Shimane Prefecture, Japan, read うんなん; English Wiktionary analyses the Japanese name as 雲 (a clipping of 出雲, Izumo) + 南, "the southern part of Izumo Province".

Yunnan, the province, is in the southwest of China, bordering Myanmar, Laos and Vietnam, and its capital is [[昆明]] (from memory, not re-checked). The Japanese field holds うんなん, the shared reading of the Chinese province and the Shimane city. The Korean field holds the Sino-Korean 운남, matching the Dan'a'yo 諺文 exactly; the usual South Korean name for the province today is 윈난 (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Vân Nam is the Sino-Vietnamese name. Cantonese wan4 naam4 is the Jyutping on English Wiktionary. No homophones (注音 ㄨㄋㄋㄚㄇ unique across both words and characters).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English and Chinese Wiktionary (Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese sections) list the senses above.

1. Yunnan, a former county of the Han dynasty, now Xiangyun County.
2. Unnan, a city in Shimane Prefecture, Japan.
3. Yunnan, the province of China.
