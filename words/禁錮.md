## Notes
- [禁](../characters/禁.md) "forbid, prohibit" + [錮 (char)](../characters/錮%20(char).md) "confine, imprison" — literally "forbid-confine."

禁錮 means to imprison or confine, pairing 禁 (the broad "prohibit" root, bound to [[禁止]]) with 錮, whose own etymology is concretely physical — originally "to solder or seal metal into cracks," extended to "to block, confine, imprison." Compare [[監禁]] and [[軟禁]] (house arrest), other 禁-compounds for confinement, and 錮's own standalone use as a word in its own right.

Mandarin jìngù, Cantonese gam3 gu3, Korean 금고 (geumgo), and Japanese きんこ (kinko) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real legal/everyday term for imprisonment in its language. Vietnamese cấm cố is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 禁's cấm (one of three attested readings on its own page) and 錮's own cố. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 김고 diverges from Sino-Korean 금고 in the first syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄍㄧㄇㄍㄛ unique among both words and characters).
