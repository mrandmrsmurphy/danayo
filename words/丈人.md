## Notes
- [丈 (char)](../characters/丈%20(char).md) "elder, unit of length" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" — an honorific term for an elderly man, narrowed in the family context to specifically "wife's father."

丈 itself carries the honorific "respected elder" sense independently of its literal "a unit of length" meaning (老丈, "an old gentleman"), and 丈人 preserves both readings side by side even today: a general honorific for an old man, and — the sense foregrounded in this word's own gloss — a wife's father specifically, standing alongside near-synonyms like 岳父/岳丈 in both Mandarin and Vietnamese. Vietnamese trượng nhân (confirmed via Hán Nôm dictionary sources) carries exactly this same double sense, though everyday spoken Vietnamese normally uses the fully native bố vợ ("wife's father") instead, leaving trượng nhân in a more literary/classical register. Japanese じょうじん is likewise a literary/classical reading rather than the everyday word, which instead uses 舅 (しゅうと, shūto) or 義父 (ぎふ, gifu) for the same relationship. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 창닌 diverges from Sino-Korean 장인, the coda/onset pattern already documented repeatedly this stretch of the sweep. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄚㄫㄋㄧㄋ unique among both words and characters).
