## Notes
- [圏 (char)](../characters/圏%20(char).md) "encircled area"

圏 (Japanese shinjitai of 圈) means "an encircled area, zone, sphere" — an abstract enclosure or region, distinct from a plain physical ring (北極圏, "Arctic Circle"; 首都圏, "metropolitan area").

**Content corrected**: `korean` was stored as the literal string `"null"` — corrected to `권`, matching the character page's own value.

Mandarin quān and Cantonese hyun1 preserve the sense directly, though in Mandarin/Cantonese 圈 is more often the concrete "ring, circle" than this word's abstract "zone" sense. Japanese ケン (KEN, on'yomi) is fully productive in exactly this abstract "zone, sphere" sense (大気圏, taikiken, "atmosphere"; 極圏, kyokuken, "polar circle") — the sense this word's own gloss reflects. Korean 권 (gwon) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, likewise productive in the abstract sense (극권, "polar circle"). Vietnamese quyển is the Hán Việt reading for this specific abstract "sphere, zone" sense, well attested in khí quyển (氣圈, "atmosphere," an extremely common everyday word) and cực quyển (極圈, "polar circle/region," directly paralleling Japanese 極圏). A separate Hán Việt reading, khuyên, covers 圈's other, more concrete sense — "ring, circle, to encircle/mark" (lan khuyên, "an enclosure, pen") — and was not used here since it belongs to the physical-ring sense rather than this word's abstract-zone sense.

This word shares its reading (gwen/권/ㄍ⼔ㄋ) with [[巻]] ("roll; roll up") and [[絹]] ("silk"), both still unperfected and awaiting their own turn in this sweep.
