## Notes
- [九 (char)](../characters/九%20(char).md) "nine"

九 is the numeral "nine," 象形 (pictographic) in origin though its original picture is no longer transparent. Like the other low numerals, it has a dedicated anti-forgery/financial variant sharing its exact reading, [[玖]], used on financial documents for the same reason as [[一]]/[[壱]] and [[七]]/[[漆]] — many more strokes, much harder to alter.

Mandarin jiǔ, Cantonese gau2, and Vietnamese cửu are all the direct Sino-xenic reading in ordinary use. Japanese has two on'yomi, きゅう (kyū, the everyday reading, as in 九月 "September") and く (ku, an older reading now largely confined to certain fixed compounds and superstition — く is avoided in some contexts for its homophony with 苦, "suffering"), alongside the native reading ここのつ (kokonotsu) for casual object-counting. Korean similarly pairs Sino-Korean 구 (gu) with the native numeral 아홉 (ahop).

This word shares its Dan'a'yo reading (kyu/큐/ㄎ⼜) with [[丘]] ("hill") — a coincidental homophone with no semantic connection.
