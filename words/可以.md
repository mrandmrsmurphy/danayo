## Notes
- [可 (char)](../characters/可%20(char).md) "able to, may" + [以 (char)](../characters/以%20(char).md) "by means of, with" — Dan'a'yo's preposition for permission, distinct from the bare prefix [可](../characters/可%20(char).md) which marks ability/capacity. 可以 is one of the most common words in Mandarin, where it does the same double duty (能力 and 許可) that Dan'a'yo splits between the bare prefix and this preposition.

Mandarin kěyǐ and Cantonese ho2 ji5 are the standard readings. Vietnamese khả dĩ is a real, if formal and literary, Sino-Vietnamese word ("can be, is acceptable"). Japanese and Korean have no corresponding word built from these characters; both use native grammatical constructions for "can/may" (Japanese ～できる, Korean -을/ㄹ 수 있다), so the `japanese` and `korean` fields are blank. Compare [[不但]] and [[分之]].
