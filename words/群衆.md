## Notes
- [群](../characters/群.md) "flock, crowd" + [衆](../characters/衆.md) "public, crowd"
- This word is the stand-in for [群](../characters/群.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

群 is a 形声 character with 羊 "sheep" (a flock) and 君 as the phonetic, and means a flock, herd or group moving together, as in [群島](群島.md) "archipelago, a group of islands"; 衆 "the many, the multitude" makes the compound a gathered mass of people. 群衆 is a crowd, in two related senses: the physical crowd at a spot (a crowd in a square, at a station, at a match) and the masses or the public in the social and political sense (the people as a body, as in 群衆運動 "a mass movement").

It differs from [集団](集団.md) "a group, a collective," which is an organized or defined body, in that a 群衆 has little internal structure and is defined by the numbers and the shared place or moment. Because it carries both the physical and the political sense, it is deliberately cited on three lexipedia pages: [Society](../lexipedia/Society.md) for the general "crowd" sense, [Government](../lexipedia/Government.md) for "the masses, the public" as a governed population, and [Kinship](../lexipedia/Kinship.md) for its place among the words for groups of people. The readings are Mandarin qúnzhòng (aliases 群眾 and 群众), Cantonese kwan4 zung3, Japanese gunshū (ぐんしゅう), Korean 군중 and Sino-Vietnamese quần chúng. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared senses, "crowd, masses, the public."
