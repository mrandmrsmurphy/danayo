## Notes
- [青 (char)](../characters/青%20(char).md) "blue" + [銅 (char)](../characters/銅%20(char).md) "copper" — no `#cranberry`: neither constituent's own `stand_in` points here (both are themselves). Named for the blue-green patina copper develops over time.

青銅 (qīngtóng) is the standard Mandarin word for "bronze," and Cantonese 青銅 (cing1tung4) likewise. Japanese せいどう (seidō) and Korean 청동 (cheongdong) are the standard terms in their languages as well.

Vietnamese đồng điếu is the standard technical term for bronze (copper-tin alloy), though etymologically distinct from 青銅 itself. A more directly cognate Sino-Vietnamese term also exists: đồng thanh, built from the same morphemes as 青銅 (thanh 青 + đồng 銅) but reordered into native noun-then-modifier syntax — a further instance of the word-order-reversal pattern already seen in [[限定詞]], [[除外]], [[電灯]], and [[電車]].

**Fixed a real bug**: `cantonese` had a spurious comma-joined second variant `ceng1 tung4`, the same erroneous alternate reading of 青 already corrected on [[青色]] — trimmed to 青's own stored jyutping `cing1` alone.
