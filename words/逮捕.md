## Notes
- [逮](../characters/逮.md) "to reach; to catch up to; to apprehend" + [捕 (char)](../characters/捕%20(char).md) "to catch; to grab" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the bound character [逮](../characters/逮.md); 逮 is too bounded to appear alone, so it surfaces in Dan'a'yo through 逮捕 (the other half, 捕, has its own stand-in [捕](捕.md)).

逮捕 combines 逮 — "to reach, to catch up with" (辵 "movement" as the semantic component, with 隶, a hand grasping at something from behind, as the phonetic) — with 捕 "to catch, to seize" (扌 "hand" + 甫, phonetic). Together they give the standard legal verb-noun for arrest: to catch up with a person and take hold of them. As a 事詞 it serves both as the act ("to arrest") and the event ("an arrest, apprehension"), and in Japanese it takes する like other Sino-compound verbs.

Wiktionary lists the same sense in Chinese and Japanese ("to arrest a person for a crime; to apprehend") and gives a Korean entry (체포) as a hanja form, but has no Vietnamese entry for the compound, so the `vietnamese` field is omitted rather than guessed. The legal scope differs by jurisdiction. In Mainland China, per Wiktionary's usage note, 逮捕 refers to pretrial detention authorized by the procuratorate (the equivalent of 羈押 in Taiwan and Macau), while the physical apprehension of a suspect, in flagrante or not, is called 刑事拘留 (short form 刑拘). In Taiwan, 逮捕 refers specifically to arresting wanted suspects or those caught in the act; Taiwan also has a variant reading dǎibǔ alongside the standard dàibǔ. In Japanese, 逮捕 is the ordinary criminal-procedure term; Wiktionary attests 逮捕状 (arrest warrant), 緊急逮捕, 不当逮捕, 誤認逮捕 and 別件逮捕, and (from memory, not re-checked) 現行犯逮捕 for a warrantless arrest of someone caught in the act. Wiktionary's Japanese entry cross-references 拘束 "detention" as a related term; see also the Dan'a'yo words [拘束](拘束.md) and [拘禁](拘禁.md) for restraint and detention. Also compare [拿捕](拿捕.md) and [捕獲](捕獲.md), which share 捕 but apply to seizing ships or catching prey.

Cross-linguistic fields: Mandarin dàibǔ, Cantonese dai6 bou6, Japanese たいほ and Korean 체포 are all the compound's own readings, each compositional from its two characters. Dan'a'yo's sense is the shared CJKV sense of "arrest"; no narrowing or shift is claimed. The Vietnamese verb for arrest, bắt giữ (from memory, not re-checked), is a different, non-Sinitic-compound construction and is not a reading of this word.

Syllable boundary: the 注音 ㄉㄚㄧㄅㄛ is written without a boundary dot. The only way the string could be read differently is as ㄉㄚ + ㄧ + ㄅㄛ (with a bare ㄧ syllable), which is not the intended reading; ㄉㄚㄧㄅ + ㄛ and ㄉㄚ + ㄧㄅㄛ are not available because ㄅ is not a legal final and no ㄧㄅㄛ syllable exists. This matches the vault's treatment of other words with a ㄉㄚㄧ syllable followed by an initial ㄅ-, such as 大夫 (ㄉㄚㄧㄅㄨ).
