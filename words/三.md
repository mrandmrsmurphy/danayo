## Notes
- [三 (char)](../characters/三%20(char).md) "three"

三 is the numeral "three," 指事 (indicative): three stacked horizontal strokes, a straightforward triplication of [[一]] ("one," one stroke) alongside [[二]] ("two," two strokes). In real Chinese convention, 参/叁 serves as 三's anti-forgery/financial complex-form variant, the same role [[壱]] and [[漆]] play for [[一]] and [[七]] — but unlike those two completed pairs, this vault's `characters/参.md` has its own `stand_in` pointing to [[参加]] rather than to itself, and no `words/参.md` yet exists; so 三 does not (yet) have a working Dan'a'yo anti-forgery counterpart the way 一 and 七 do. Flagged here rather than asserted as already implemented.

Mandarin sān, Cantonese saam1, and Vietnamese tam are all the direct Sino-xenic reading in ordinary use. Japanese has the on'yomi さん (san, used in formal counting and compounds like 三月 "March," 三人 "three people") alongside the native reading みっつ (mittsu), preferred for casual object-counting. Korean similarly has Sino-Korean 삼 (sam) alongside the native numeral 셋 (set) — Sino-Korean 삼 dominates in compound words and higher counting, while 셋 is used for counting small numbers of everyday objects.
