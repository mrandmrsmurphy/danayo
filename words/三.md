## Notes
- [三 (char)](../characters/三%20(char).md) "three"

三 is the numeral "three," 指事 (indicative): three stacked horizontal strokes, a straightforward triplication of [[一]] ("one," one stroke) alongside [[二]] ("two," two strokes). In Chinese convention 参/叁 is 三's anti-forgery (financial) variant, the same role [[壱]] and [[漆]] play for [[一]] and [[七]]. Here 参 is a character page whose stand-in word is [[参加]], so the anti-forgery "three" appears only inside [[参拾]] "thirty"; there is no separate word 参.

Mandarin sān, Cantonese saam1, and Vietnamese tam are all the direct Sino-xenic reading in ordinary use. Japanese has the on'yomi さん (san, used in formal counting and compounds like 三月 "March," 三人 "three people") alongside the native reading みっつ (mittsu), preferred for casual object-counting. Korean similarly has Sino-Korean 삼 (sam) alongside the native numeral 셋 (set) — Sino-Korean 삼 dominates in compound words and higher counting, while 셋 is used for counting small numbers of everyday objects.
