## Notes
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) "one" + [半](../characters/半.md) "half" — legitimizing note: 半's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, so 半 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word; 一's own `stand_in` is 一 itself, so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply.

Mandarin yībàn ("half, one half") is the standard form; the erhua-suffixed colloquial variant yībànr (Beijing-dialect, "-r" added) is not used in the field but is recorded among the `aliases`. Fixed a real comma-joined contamination bug where both forms had been jammed into a single `mandarin` value without a separator. Cantonese jat1 bun3 and Korean 일반 are standard compositional readings. Vietnamese nửa is the real, everyday native word for "half" (rather than a Sino-Vietnamese compositional form). `kwin` is false (AND-rule: 一 false, 半 true) — corrected from a stale `true`.

**A genuine cross-language coincidental homophone, not just a Dan'a'yo one**: Japanese いっぱん is the phonologically regular sokuon-assimilated reading of いち + はん (半's own on'yomi HAN, with h→p devoicing after gemination) — but this happens to be the exact same pronunciation as [[一般]] ("general, common"), mirroring the coincidental Dan'a'yo-level homophone between the two words already flagged above.
