## Notes
- [新 (char)](../characters/新%20(char).md) "new" + [年 (char)](../characters/年%20(char).md) "year" — literally "new year."
- Technically just two words juxtaposed: <ruby>[新](../words/新.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄋ</rt></ruby> + <ruby>[年](../words/年.md)<rt>ㄋㄝㄋ</rt></ruby>
1. New Year - ambiguous as to Gregorian or Lunar
2. New Year's Day - ambiguous as to Gregorian or Lunar

Mandarin xīnnián, Cantonese san1 nin4, Japanese しんねん (shinnen), and Korean 신년 are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 新's and 年's own stored citations exactly. Vietnamese năm mới ("new year," literally "year new") is a native phrase using Vietnamese's own word order rather than a Sino-Vietnamese compound. **Found and fixed two real bugs**: `japanese` contained a stray zero-width space corrupting the text; and `korean` was stored as "신년, 새해," mixing the real compositional Sino-Korean word (신년) together with a native vernacular alternative (새해, "new year," built from native 새 "new" + native 해 "year") — the latter belongs in Notes prose, not the `korean` field.

`kwin` is false: 年 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page, which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 新 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄋㄋㄝㄋ unique among both words and characters).
