## Notes
- [舌 (char)](../characters/舌%20(char).md) "tongue; clapper" — stand-in for [[舌 (char)|舌]], which cannot appear independently

舌 is 象形 of a snake's forked tongue emerging from a mouth ([[口]]) — the oracle-bone form showed the fork directly, with later variants adding a horizontal stroke at the base that itself often evolved into a second, illusory fork. The character page documents its whole phonetic family (活, 刮, 括, 筈, 蛞, 聒) as descending from an unlinkable ancestor glyph 𠯑, a recognizable descendant with no page for the source. Beyond the anatomical organ, 舌 extends to "clapper" (the tongue-like striking piece inside a bell) and to speech itself — 舌根 names both the physical root of the tongue and, in traditional East Asian medicine/Buddhist philosophy, the taste faculty as one of the six sense-roots.

Japanese carries two on'yomi with a real functional split: ぜつ (ZETSU, the dominant reading — 舌戦 zessen "war of words," 毒舌 dokuzetsu "malicious tongue, backbiting") and rarer せつ, alongside the fully productive native 訓読み した (shita), the everyday word for the physical organ. Vietnamese thiệt is the reading used here (thiệt đài "tongue coating," a term from traditional medicine), rather than the character's other stored variant thịt, which is the ordinary modern Vietnamese word for "meat, flesh," an unrelated homophone and not a reading of 舌.
