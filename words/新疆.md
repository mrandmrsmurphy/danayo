## Notes
- [新](../characters/新%20(char).md) "new" + [疆](../characters/疆.md) "boundary, frontier, territory" — "new territory". No stand-in relationship applies (新's own is [[新]]; 疆's is [[辺疆]]).

This name has an older general sense, listed first. English Wiktionary says it was originally a general term for newly acquired territories, used since the Southern Song, and that in the Qing it was applied to various non-Han-inhabited territories (it names western Guizhou, Wumeng in Yunnan and Jinchuan among them) as well as to the region now called Xinjiang; the first Qing emperor to apply it to that region was the Daoguang Emperor, in 1821. The same entry quotes Zuo Zongtang (1877), where it is the region. Xinjiang is a region of north-western China whose capital is [[烏魯斉]] (Ürümqi) (from memory, not re-checked).

The Japanese field holds しんきょう, the Korean field the Sino-Korean 신강, and the Vietnamese field Tân Cương, all as given in English Wiktionary's descendants line; Korean differs from the Dan'a'yo 諺文 신갼 in the vowel of 疆, and the usual South Korean name today is 신장 (from memory, not re-checked). Cantonese san1 goeng1 is the Jyutping on English Wiktionary. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄋㄍ⼘ㄋ unique across both words and characters).

**How the official name is written.** The full official name 新疆維吾爾自治區 is written as separate words: 新疆 <ruby>[維吾爾](../words/回紇.md)<rt>ㄏㄛㄧㄏㄜㄊ</rt></ruby> <ruby>[自治](../words/自治.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄜㄑㄧ</rt></ruby> <ruby>[区域](../words/区域.md)<rt>ㄎㄨ·ㄨㄧㄎ</rt></ruby>. 維吾爾 is not a separate word here: it is already an alias of [[回紇]] "Uighur", so that word carries the ethnonym. The official name has no 族 after 維吾爾, so no segmentation of 族 was needed.
