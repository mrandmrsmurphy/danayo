## Notes
- [蛾 (char)](../characters/蛾%20(char).md) "moth" (stand-in for 蛾)

蛾 names the moth, the night-flying counterpart to 蝶 ("butterfly") in the East Asian folk taxonomy of Lepidoptera — the two are treated as a natural pair, distinguished chiefly by moths' drab coloring, thicker bodies, and attraction to lamplight, a behavior widely used as a metaphor for reckless self-endangerment (as in the idiom 飛蛾撲火, "a moth flying into the flame").

Cross-linguistically the daughter languages mostly continue the character's own reading directly: Mandarin é, Cantonese ngo4, and Sino-Korean 아 all track the character's ㄚ syllable, and Vietnamese ngài is likewise a direct Sino-Vietnamese continuant. Japanese が (ga) is the on'yomi used in entomological and compound contexts (夜蛾 yaga "noctuid moth"), while everyday speech more often reaches for native names of specific common species rather than this single umbrella term.
