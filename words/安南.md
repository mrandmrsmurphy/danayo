## Notes
- [安](../characters/安.md) "peaceful" + [南](../characters/南.md) "south" — no stand-in relationship applies (安's own is [[平安]]; 南's own is [[南方]]). The name means "the pacified south" or "to pacify the south".

This name began as a Chinese name for Vietnam and then passed back into Vietnamese. The Chinese senses come first, in the order English Wiktionary lists them (historical Annam, then dated Vietnam), with the Tang protectorate, the origin of the name, ahead of both.

1. The Tang protectorate of Annam, 安南都護府, "Protectorate General to Pacify the South", the Tang administrative region for the south, with its seat in what is now northern Vietnam (Chinese Wikipedia: set up by the Tang at Jiaozhi, in the north of modern Vietnam, and made the independent state 安南國 in the time of the Southern Han). The protectorate was created in the 7th century (Tang, 679 by the usual dating, from memory, not re-checked) and gave the name to the region. See [[交址]] for the older name of the same land.
2. The old Chinese name for Vietnam, and for Vietnamese rulers: Chinese Wikipedia says that from the later 12th century into the early 19th the Vietnamese rulers were invested by the Chinese emperors as kings of Annam (安南國王) and the country's foreign name was 安南國; English Wiktionary marks the use for Vietnam as a whole "dated" and quotes Lu Xun in 1927, naming Annam and Korea together. In 1804 the Qing agreed to the new name 越南 (from memory, not re-checked); see [[越南]]. Hvdic's Nguyễn Quốc Hùng dictionary says the same: the name of the Vietnamese country in the Lý period (see [[李朝]]). Chinese Wikipedia also has the "Annam under Ming" period, the Ming rule of 1407–1427 (the dates are from memory, not re-checked).
3. Annam, the central region of Vietnam under French colonial rule: a protectorate under the Nguyễn emperors (see [[阮朝]]) with its capital at Huế (see [[順化]]), one of the three parts of French Indochina (see [[印度支那]]), beside Tonkin in the north and Cochinchina in the south (see [[交址]]). The Vietnamese name for this region is Trung Kỳ (中圻) (Japanese Wikipedia). Annamite is still the name of the language group and the mountain range (Japanese 安南山脈, the Annamite Range) in English and Japanese.
4. In Vietnamese, An Nam is the Hán-Việt reading of the same name and appears in Vietnamese dictionaries as the historical name of the country. Many Vietnamese reject the name as a colonial-era exonym (from memory, not re-checked).

Two other homographs of this spelling in English Wiktionary are set aside here: Annan, a district of Tainan in Taiwan, and a transliteration of the Akan surname Annan.

Readings. Mandarin Ānnán and Cantonese on1 naam4 are as given at English Wiktionary; Japanese あんなん, Korean 안남 and Vietnamese An Nam are as given for the historical name (English Wiktionary: Japanese Annam "the former name of Vietnam", Korean hanja form of 안남, Vietnamese chữ Hán form of An Nam). Chinese Wiktionary has the same Chinese senses (安南國, "dated" 越南), plus the Japanese, Korean, Okinawan and Vietnamese sections. Vietnamese Wiktionary has a page for 安南 whose content is a Chinese entry reading "交址: An Nam", an odd page that nevertheless spells the Giao Chỉ name with 址 (not otherwise verified).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11. English Wiktionary, Chinese Wiktionary: as above. Japanese and Korean Wiktionaries have no 安南 page of their own (the Japanese and Korean entries are in the English and Chinese Wiktionaries). hvdic.thivien.net: 安南 an nam. No homophones (注音 ㄚㄋㄋㄚㄇ unique across both words and characters).
