## Notes
- [目 (char)](../characters/目%20(char).md) "list, category" (a secondary sense of the same character, distinct from its primary "eye" meaning) + [階](../characters/階.md) "level, tier, storey" — literally "category-level," the taxonomic rank of order.

Mandarin mù, Cantonese muk6, Japanese もく (moku), and Korean 목 are all the real, standard bare-character terms used in taxonomy, matching 目's own stored citations exactly. Vietnamese departs furthest here: the real term is bộ, which is not even the same character — Vietnamese taxonomy borrows [[部]] ("part, section, ministry") for this rank rather than 目, a cross-character substitution unique to this rank in the whole hierarchy. This is also the rank where Dan'a'yo's 階-suffixing pays off most directly: bare [[目]] is already a separate, unrelated Dan'a'yo word meaning "eye," so 目階 is not a stylistic nicety here but the only way to name this rank without colliding with an existing word.

**Why append 階 at all, when none of the four source languages do**: Dan'a'yo appends 階 uniformly across all eight ranks of the taxonomic hierarchy ([[域階]], [[界階]], [[門階]], [[綱階]], [[目階]], [[科階]], [[属階]], [[種階]]) as a purely internal disambiguation device — natural languages rely on context alone to read 目 as "order" rather than "eye" inside a taxonomy chart, but a constructed language benefits from removing that ambiguity outright, especially since Dan'a'yo already independently uses bare [[目]] for "eye." In casual or space-constrained usage, the bare character (e.g. 目 alone) remains an understood abbreviation of the full 階-suffixed word, the same way scientific Latin abbreviates "Order" to "O." — but the full form is what a dictionary entry or first mention should use.

`kwin` is false: 目 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page, which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 階 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄇㄨㄎㄍ⼶ㄧ unique among both words and characters).
