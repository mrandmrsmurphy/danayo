## Notes
- [域 (char)](../characters/域%20(char).md) "domain, region" + [内](../characters/内.md) "inside" — stand-in for neither: 域's own stand-in is the bare word [[域]], and 内's is [[内部]].

域内 is "within the region": inside a bounded area, and by extension "intra-regional", as opposed to what lies outside it. It is used in the writing-system chapter in 域内可読 "legible across the region" and for a script that is usable across the nations of the East Asian sphere. The build is plain: 域 "domain, region" + 内 "inside". It is parallel to [[校内]] "on-campus", with the area named by 域 in place of 校, and it is a near neighbour of [[区域]] "area" and [[地域]] "district", which name the area itself where 域内 names being inside it.

The word is attested in Japanese and Korean and is, in practice, a term of regional organisation. Japanese いきない (*ikinai*) and Korean 역내 (*yeoknae*, North Korean 역내 also; the 두음법칙 does not touch it) mean "within the area", and both are most at home in economics and politics: 域内貿易 / 역내 무역 "intra-regional trade", and especially the European Union ("EU member states within the bloc", opposed to 域外 / 역외 "outside the bloc"). Mandarin yùnèi (Cantonese wik6 noi6) is also found in the sense "within the domain or territory" (older dictionaries gloss 域内 and 域中 alike as "within the domain"), but I could not confirm how current it is in modern Chinese usage relative to 区域内 or 境内, and the Cantonese reading is unverified. Vietnamese has no matching Sino-Vietnamese word in the dictionaries, so the `vietnamese` key is omitted: the ordinary expressions are *trong khu vực* "in the region" and *nội khối* "within the bloc".

No meaning clash exists in any of the languages. Dan'a'yo's sense is exactly the shared one, with the scope of "region" set by context: the East Asian sphere itself, a country, or a bloc. The Cantonese reading is the compositional value of the two characters (域 wik6, 内 noi6) and was not separately confirmed in a Cantonese dictionary.
