## Notes
- [神 (char)](../characters/神%20(char).md) "god, deity, spirit" + [道 (char)](../characters/道%20(char).md) "way" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (神's is [[神]] itself, 道's is [[道]] itself), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 道 is individually `kwin: false`.

神道 is a classical philosophical term, "the way of the gods/spirits," used in early Chinese texts (notably the Yijing: 觀天之神道，而四時不忒, "observe Heaven's divine way, and the four seasons do not err") to mean the mysterious, unerring natural order underlying the cosmos — the sense documented here as "natural law." This is distinct from [[神道教]] ("Shintoism"), the codified Japanese native religion, even though Shinto's own name derives from this exact same two-character term — Japanese しんとう on its own overwhelmingly refers to the religion in ordinary modern usage, and 神道教 exists mainly as a rarer, more explicit formal variant.

Mandarin shéndào, Cantonese san4 dou6, Japanese しんとう (shintō), Korean 신도 (sindo), and Vietnamese Thần đạo are all standard, fully compositional readings of the two characters, though a reader should keep the classical-philosophy/religion sense split in mind rather than assuming either meaning is the only one.
