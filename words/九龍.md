## Notes
- [九 (char)](../characters/九%20(char).md) "nine" + [龍 (char)](../characters/龍%20(char).md) "dragon" — literally "nine dragons," a genuine multi-referent name rather than a single concept with several translations.

九龍 (Kowloon) is the district of Hong Kong opposite Hong Kong Island, traditionally said to take its name from the eight surrounding hills plus a boy-emperor of the Southern Song, together making "nine dragons." Mandarin jiǔlóng, Cantonese gau2 lung4 (Kowloon's own English name transliterates this Cantonese reading directly), Japanese きゅうりゅう, and Korean 구룡 are all standard, fully compositional readings of the Hong Kong place name. Vietnamese cửu long, however, is far better known for an entirely different referent: **Cửu Long** is the standard Vietnamese name for the Mekong River delta and its nine tributary mouths (九龍江, "the Nine Dragons River"), a major geographic and agricultural region of southern Vietnam — the same "nine dragons" naming logic applied to a different set of nine, unrelated to Hong Kong's Kowloon.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 큐룡 diverges from Sino-Korean 구룡, since 九's own Dan'a'yo reading 큐 doesn't match Sino-Korean 구, even though 龍's own reading matches exactly (龍 is independently `kwin: true`).
