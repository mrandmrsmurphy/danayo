## Notes
- [属](../characters/属.md) "belong, category" + [階](../characters/階.md) "level, tier, storey" — literally "belong-level," the taxonomic rank of genus.

Mandarin shǔ, Cantonese suk6, Japanese ぞく (zoku), and Korean 속 are all the real, standard bare-character terms used in taxonomy, matching 属's own stored citations exactly. Vietnamese uses chi instead of 属's own Sino-Vietnamese readings (thuộc/chúc) — a native word literally meaning "branch" (of a tree), an organic metaphor distinct from, but adjacent to, 属's own core sense of "belonging, category."

**Why append 階 at all, when none of the four source languages do**: Dan'a'yo appends 階 uniformly across all eight ranks of the taxonomic hierarchy ([[域階]], [[界階]], [[門階]], [[綱階]], [[目階]], [[科階]], [[属階]], [[種階]]) as a purely internal disambiguation device — natural languages rely on context alone to read 目 as "order" rather than "eye" inside a taxonomy chart, but a constructed language benefits from removing that ambiguity outright, especially since Dan'a'yo already independently uses bare [[目]] for "eye." In casual or space-constrained usage, the bare character (e.g. 目 alone) remains an understood abbreviation of the full 階-suffixed word, the same way scientific Latin abbreviates "Order" to "O." — but the full form is what a dictionary entry or first mention should use.

`kwin` is false: 属 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page, which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 階 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄛㄎㄍ⼶ㄧ unique among both words and characters).
