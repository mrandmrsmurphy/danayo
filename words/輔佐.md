## Notes
- [輔](../characters/輔.md) "assist" + [佐](../characters/佐.md) "help" — stand-in for [輔](../characters/輔.md), which cannot appear independently.

輔 originally named the boards bound to a cart wheel for support, and from that "to support, to aid"; 佐 is "to help, to be at someone's side." Both already mean assisting, so 輔佐 is a synonym compound, and historically it is the word for the high officials who help a ruler govern (the sense behind the title 輔佐 and the grouping of ministers around a throne). Its close companion is [輔弼](輔弼.md), likewise a ruler's counselor-aide, and the less specific [佐理](佐理.md).

Here the sphere's usage diverges from this page's gloss. In modern Chinese and Japanese the word has widened to assisting anyone in a position of authority: in Japanese it is normally written 補佐 (ほさ), as in 補佐官 "an aide, an assistant officer," since 輔 is outside the common-use characters, and this page's 輔佐 is the older spelling. Dan'a'yo deliberately keeps the narrower classical sense, "to assist a ruler, to serve as an aide," as the Government domain page records, so that general "help" vocabulary stays in other words. Readings: Mandarin fǔzuǒ, Cantonese fu6 zo3, Japanese hosa, Korean 보좌, Sino-Vietnamese phụ tá.
