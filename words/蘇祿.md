## Notes
- [蘇](../characters/蘇.md) "revive" + [祿](../characters/祿.md) "wealth" — a transliteration of the name, so the characters carry no meaning of their own; no stand-in relationship applies (蘇's own stand-in is [[蘇生]], 祿's is [[俸祿]]). The aliases 苏禄 and 蘇洛 are the simplified and variant spellings.

蘇祿 is a proper noun with two senses at English Wiktionary.

1. Historical: the Sultanate of Sulu, the Muslim state that ruled the islands of the Sulu Archipelago, parts of Mindanao in today's Philippines, parts of Palawan and north-eastern Borneo.
2. Sulu, a province of the Philippines.

English Wiktionary gives the etymology as loaned through Hokkien, from Tausug Sulug, the unelided form of Sūg ("Sulu"), from sulug, the unelided form of sūg ("current"), and compares Maranao Solog and Malay Suluk. Its derived terms 蘇祿群島, 蘇祿海 and 蘇祿人 are not words in the vault. Zhang Xie's *Dongxi yang kao* (1618) lists Sulu among the eastern ocean countries of Luzon ([[呂宋]]).

Mandarin Sūlù and Cantonese sou1 luk6 are as at English Wiktionary. Japanese スールー and Korean 술루 are the ordinary names (from memory, not re-checked), so `kwin` is false. The `vietnamese` field holds the compositional Hán-Việt reading tô lộc (each character as its page gives it; not attested for this name).

No homophones (注音 ㄙㄛㄌㄛㄎ unique across words and characters, checked 2026-10-12). The 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot.
