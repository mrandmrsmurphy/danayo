## Notes
- [宇](../characters/宇.md) "universe; space" + [宙 (char)](../characters/宙%20(char).md) "eternity" — this word is the stand-in for [宇](../characters/宇.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

宇宙 is a classical philosophical coinage, not a modern scientific loan: the ancient definition (recorded in texts like the Shizi and echoed in the Huainanzi) glosses 宇 as "the four directions and up-and-down" (space) and 宙 as "what extends from past to present" (time) — the two characters were paired specifically to name space and time as the twin dimensions constituting the cosmos, centuries before any Chinese contact with modern astronomy. Both characters share the same graphemic root sense of "roof, eaves" (宇's phonetic 于 and 宙's phonetic 由 both drift from architectural imagery — eaves and roof-beams as a synecdoche for an entire dwelling), which 20th-century scholarship (Meyer 2010) argues was metaphorically extended outward from "the whole space of a house" to "the whole extent of space and time."

Japanese うちゅう, Korean 우주, and Vietnamese vũ trụ (all Wiktionary-verified) preserve the same unified space-time cosmological sense as the classical Chinese coinage, with no comparable semantic narrowing across any of the four languages — all are the standard modern word for "universe, outer space, cosmos." No HSK level is given for the compound. No homophones (注音 ㄨㄐㄨㄛ unique across both words and characters).
