## Notes
- [紳](../characters/紳.md) "gentry, sire" + [士 (char)](../characters/士%20(char).md) "scholar" — 紳's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, but 士's own is [[士]] itself, so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply; 紳士 legitimizes 紳 alone. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 士 is individually `kwin: false`.

紳士 means a gentleman — a man of refined manners and gentry standing, from 紳's original sense of the sash worn with formal dress.

Mandarin shēnshì, Cantonese san1 si6-2 (with the tone-2 change common to this kind of compound noun), Japanese しんし (shinshi), Korean 신사 (sinsa), and Vietnamese thân sĩ are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 紳士 is itself a common attested word for "gentleman" in all five languages. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
