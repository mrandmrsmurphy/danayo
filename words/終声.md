## Notes
- [終](../characters/終.md) "to end, final" + [声](../characters/声.md) "sound, voice" — no stand-in relationship applies (終's own stand-in is [[終了]]; 声's is [[発声]]).

終声 is the "final sound" of a syllable: the position after the vowel, and the consonant that occupies it. It is the third member of the three-part analysis of a Korean syllable that the *Hunminjeongeum* itself lays down: 初聲 (the initial), 中聲 (the middle, the vowel) and 終聲 (the final). That makes it the most natural word for the coda in a language whose phonology claims Korean authority (朝鮮正音, 発音権威): Dan'a'yo's retained syllable-final contrasts, which Vietnamese tends to neutralize ([[中和]]), are 終声 contrasts, and the velar nasal ㄫ is allowed in 終声 but never in an initial.

It overlaps with [[韻尾]] and is not identical. 韻尾 is the Chinese tradition's term, the "tail of the rhyme": the last element within a 声母/韻母 analysis, so a coda that belongs to a final. 終声 is the Korean tradition's term, the final part of a three-part syllable, and it names the position as well as the sound. Dan'a'yo keeps both: 韻尾 when speaking of the structure of the 韻母, 終声 when speaking of what ends the syllable.

Wiktionary records 終聲 in Chinese (Mandarin zhōngshēng, Cantonese zung1 sing1) and Korean (종성, jongseong) with the same meaning, "(phonology) final", the final part of a syllable. It has no Japanese or Vietnamese entry, so the `japanese` field gives the compositional on'yomi しゅうせい (not separately verified) and `vietnamese` is omitted. Dan'a'yo's own reading 중싱 differs from Korean 종성, so `kwin` is false.

The word is filed as 終声, with 声, the form used by every other 声-word in the vault (声母, 声調, 声門, 発声). The traditional 終聲, the form in which the term is written in the *Hunminjeongeum* and in Korean, is kept as an alias, together with the simplified 终声. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄨㄫㄙㄧㄫ unique across both words and characters).
