## Notes
- [聘 (char)](../characters/聘%20(char).md) "betroth; hire" + [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "money; metal" — bride-price.

聘金 is the bride-price: money or gifts the groom's family gives to the bride's family on betrothal. It is "betroth-money." Mandarin pìnjīn (pìngjīn in Taiwan) and Cantonese ping3 gam1 are confirmed on Wiktionary, which glosses it "betrothal gifts from the groom's family to the bride's family; bride price," and also "money paid when hiring." It is the formal, written-standard term (dialects use 彩禮, 聘禮, 財禮), and is also used in Taiwan and Singapore Mandarin and some Hokkien. Japanese and Korean were not confirmed, so those fields are left blank. It is the counterpart of [[嫁資]] (dowry), which flows from the bride's side. The act of betrothing is [[聘]], and the engagement itself is [[婚約]].
