## Notes
- [青 (char)](../characters/青%20(char).md) "blue" — stand-in for [[青 (char)|青]], which cannot appear independently

青 is 形声: phonetic [[生]], the original form depicting 丹 ("cinnabar, mineral pigment") beneath 生 — a color-naming character built directly on a pigment, "blue-green, verdant." This vault's own compound family already reflects the classical CJKV color-boundary looseness this character is famous for: 青色 is glossed "blue-green; cyan; grue" in a single breath, the character straddling what English treats as two separate colors (blue and green) — the same phenomenon behind Japanese traffic signals being popularly called 青 (blue) despite being physically green, and behind 青蛙 ("frog," an animal English speakers would call green) sitting comfortably in this vault's own Words list. Beyond color, 青年 ("youth, young man") extends 青 metaphorically to freshness/immaturity, paralleling English "green" in the sense of inexperienced, and the vault's own periodic-table convention uses 青 as the prefix for caesium via [[青素]].

The character's phonetic family is unusually large and productive: 請/晴/情/清/猜/睛/精/錆/靖/鯖 all descend from 青 phonetically, spanning senses from "ask" and "clear weather" to "feelings" and "mackerel" with no unifying semantic thread — a purely phonetic family, similar in scale to [[老]]'s or [[米]]'s own descendant sets covered earlier in this sweep. Japanese carries two on'yomi, せい (SEI, used here — 青年 seinen) and rarer しょう, alongside the native 訓読み あお (ao), the ordinary everyday word for blue/green covering the same wide color range as the Sino-Japanese layer. Vietnamese thanh is the character's sole stored reading, alive in both literal-color and figurative-youth registers (thanh niên "youth," directly paralleling 青年). `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 청 (ceng, from Middle Chinese 清母 t͡sʰ + 青開韻 eŋ) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 청.
