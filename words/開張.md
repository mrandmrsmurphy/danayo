## Notes
- [開](../characters/開.md) "open" + [張](../characters/張.md) "stretch, extend"

開張 means the grand opening of a business or establishment. Mandarin 開張 (kāizhāng), Cantonese 開張 (hoi1zoeng1), Korean 개장 (gaejang), and Sino-Vietnamese khai trương all share this everyday commercial sense — announcing a shop, restaurant, or facility (such as a swimming beach or market) as newly open for business.

Japanese diverges sharply: 開張 (かいちょう) is not the everyday word for a business opening — real attested senses are "to spread out widely" (e.g. an insect's wingspan when its wings are open) and, historically, a variant of 開帳, the unveiling of a hidden Buddhist icon (extended colloquially to opening an illegal gambling table). For the "grand opening" sense that the other CJKV languages share, Japanese instead uses 開業 (kaigyō, "opening a business/practice") or 開幕 (kaimaku, "opening/inauguration," for events and ceremonies) — the reason this word's Dan'a'yo page carries an editorial note to prefer 開幕 or 開業 over coining 開張 for that meaning.
