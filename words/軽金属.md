## Notes
- [軽 (char)](../characters/軽%20(char).md) "light" + [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "metal" + [属](../characters/属.md) "category, kind" — light metals; in materials science and engineering, the metals of low density (roughly below 5 g/cm³), principally aluminum, magnesium, titanium, and beryllium.

The term contrasts directly with 重金属 (heavy metals). 軽金属 appears in formal metallurgical classification and industrial discourse: 軽金属工業 (light metals industry), 軽金属学会 (the Japan Institute of Light Metals). The category is economically and strategically significant, since aluminum, magnesium and titanium are essential to aerospace and automotive applications.

Korean 경금속 and Chinese 轻金属 follow the same compound structure and domain. Vietnamese commonly uses the analytic phrase *kim loại nhẹ* (metal + light/not-heavy) rather than the Sino-Vietnamese *khinh kim thuộc*, reflecting the tendency of Vietnamese technical vocabulary to favor descriptive native phrasing over calqued Sino-Vietnamese forms.

Of the three characters, 属's own `stand_in` is 所属, so this word is not its legitimizer; 軽's and 金's are themselves.  Mandarin qīngjīnshǔ, Cantonese hing1 gam1 suk6, Japanese けいきんぞく and Korean 경금속 are the same compound, and the alias 輕金屬 is the traditional form.  `kwin` is false because the Dan'a'yo 킹김족 differs from the Korean 경금속.  The 注音 needs no syllable dot, and no other word shares it.
