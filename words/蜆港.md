## Notes
- [蜆](../characters/蜆.md) "basket clam" + [港](../characters/港.md) "harbor, port" — no stand-in relationship applies (蜆 is a 名専字; 港's own is [[港湾]]). The name is more often written today 峴港, kept as an alias, but 蜆港 is the original form and is the base spelling here (see below).

蜆港 (Vietnamese Đà Nẵng, "Da Nang") is the largest city of central Vietnam, a centrally governed city on the coast at the mouth of the Hàn River, with a deep-water port, and the gateway to the old trading port [[会安]] and the former province of Quảng Nam (see [[広南]]; the province was folded into Đà Nẵng in the 2025 reorganization, as stated at English Wiktionary's 廣南 entry; the rest is from memory, not re-checked). In Chinese writing the city is called 峴港 (simplified 岘港) or 蜆港; the Vietnamese name Đà Nẵng is the one everyday speakers use.

The two spellings. English Wiktionary's 峴港 entry (read 2026-10-11) defines it as "Đà Nẵng, a city in Vietnam" and calls it a corruption of 蜆港 ("mussel port"), the name referring to the nearby Sơn Trà island, which is shaped like a mussel (蜆), and which ancient Chinese vessels used as a navigation point to Đà Nẵng. Chinese Wikipedia (read 2026-10-11) is less sure: it offers that the name may come from the shape of the Sơn Trà peninsula, which looks like a river clam, or from the area once producing many clams; it also records that Chinese speakers may have transliterated the Vietnamese "Hàn" (the river, as in Cửa Hàn) as 蜆, later changed to 峴, and that the historian Võ Văn Dật argues instead that "Hàn" itself comes from a Hainanese pronunciation of 蜆港. So the clam etymology is plausible but not settled. By decision, 蜆 is the base character here because it is the original name and the more productive character (it also writes the basket clam and the butterfly chrysalis); 峴港 is the alias form, the one used in modern print, and 峴 itself has no character page in this vault, nor is it an alias of any character, so the alias is read with the same ㄏㄝㄋㄏㄛㄫ.

The Vietnamese name Đà Nẵng is not Hán-Việt. It is usually said to be Cham in origin (from memory, not verified), a name for the river mouth or the great river, and the Chinese characters record the older Chinese-language name rather than translating it. The `vietnamese` field therefore holds the Hán-Việt reading hiện cảng (hvdic.thivien.net gives 蜆 as hiện; 港 is cảng), the way a Vietnamese reader would read the hanzi, not the everyday name. Mandarin Xiǎngǎng is the compositional reading of 蜆港 (English Wiktionary gives Xiàngǎng and Cantonese jin6 gong2 for 峴港; for 蜆港 the characters give xiǎn + gǎng and hin2 + gong2). Japanese けんこう and Korean 현항 are compositional Sino-Xenic readings (蜆 KEN/현, 港 KOU/항, North Korean form); Japanese actually says ダナン and Korean 다낭 (from memory, not re-checked).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 峴港 as a Chinese proper noun (Mandarin Xiàngǎng, Cantonese jin6 gong2; "Đà Nẵng, a city in Vietnam") that lists 蜆港 and 蚬港 as alternative forms; its 蜆 entry lists 蜆港 / 蚬港 only as a red-linked compound, with no entry of its own. Chinese Wiktionary's 蜆 page likewise has 蜆港 as a red link. Chinese Wikipedia discusses both 峴港 and 蜆港 in the city's etymology section. Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 蜆港 entry. The 注音 ㄏㄝㄋㄏㄛㄫ is shared with no other word or character.

Dan'a'yo keeps the Chinese-sphere name: it is the proper noun for the city, with no other sense.
