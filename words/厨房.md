## Notes
- [厨](../characters/厨.md) "kitchen; cook" + [房](../characters/房%20(char).md) "room" — stand-in for [厨](../characters/厨.md), which cannot appear independently
- 厨房 names the room where 厨 — "kitchen, cook" — happens, the most literal kind of compound: the function plus the space it occupies. The word keeps this plain transparency across the Sinosphere as the standard term for a kitchen, whether domestic or professional: Mandarin chúfáng and Cantonese cyu4 fong4, Sino-Korean 주방 (jubang, the modern, slightly more technical word favored for restaurant and appliance-equipped kitchens, set against the older native 부엌), Sino-Japanese ちゅうぼう (chūbō, likewise the more formal or commercial register, beside native daidokoro 台所), and Sino-Vietnamese trù phòng, which sits alongside the everyday native nhà bếp. Across all four living readings, the same pattern recurs: the Sino-Xenic compound supplies the formal or institutional word for "kitchen," while each language keeps its own native term for the cozier, more domestic version of the same room.

厨 is a 形声 character (the 厂 "shelter" radical with a phonetic 豆 and 寸 element in the full form 廚), recorded as an alias here, and originally named a cookhouse or kitchen, and 房 "room" completes the compound, which sits with [房室](房室.md) "room, chamber" among the room words. The Buildings page lists it as the plain entry "kitchen," and Dan'a'yo keeps that plain sense, "kitchen," as the general word for the room, with no divergence from the shared Sino-xenic term.
