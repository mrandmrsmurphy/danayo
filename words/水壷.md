## Notes
- [水 (char)](../characters/水%20(char).md) "water" + [壷](../characters/壷.md) "pot" — this word is the viable form of [壷](../characters/壷.md): the character's meaning (pot; vessel) is identical, but 壷 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 水壷 is its `stand_in` compound.

水壷 names a portable water container — a kettle, canteen, or water bottle — as distinct from a large fixed vessel. In Japanese it's especially associated with school and outdoor-activity culture: 水筒 (suitō, using a different second character) is actually the more common modern everyday term for a personal water bottle, with 水壷/水壺 leaning toward the older or more general "water jug/kettle" sense.

Japanese すいこ and Korean 수호 are both standard compositional Sino-readings, and `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 수호 matches Sino-Korean 수호 exactly. **`vietnamese` intentionally left blank**: no attested Sino-Vietnamese compound for this exact word could be confirmed; everyday Vietnamese instead uses the native bình nước ("water bottle/vessel"). No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄨㄏㄛ unique across both words and characters).
