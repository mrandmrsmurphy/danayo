## Notes
- [南](../characters/南.md) "south" + [京](../characters/京.md) "capital" — "southern capital". No stand-in relationship applies (南's is [[南方]]; 京's is [[京城]]).

南京 (Nanjing) is the capital of Jiangsu province in eastern China, on the lower Yangtze (from memory, not re-checked). The name is literally "southern capital", and the city served as the capital of several dynasties and of the Republic of China (dates from memory, not re-checked). English Wiktionary also lists a historical Chinese sense: the Nanjing of the Liao dynasty, a former city in the southwest of modern Beijing and one of that dynasty's capitals.

In Japanese the ordinary sense comes first: なんきん is also a noun "pumpkin, squash" (synonyms 南瓜 かぼちゃ and 唐茄子 とうなす), and the city name ナンキン is the same spelling. Compounds such as 南京錠 (padlock), 南京虫 (bedbug) and 南京豆 (peanut) are listed under it, but they are compounds, not senses of the bare word, and are not claimed here. A different Japanese word, なんきょう (same characters, Go-on reading), means "a southern capital" and formerly referred to Nara; it is not covered by this entry. The pumpkin sense is attested in Japanese only; Chinese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionary give the city alone, and in Dan'a'yo the pumpkin sense is carried over from Japanese and placed on [Food](../lexipedia/Food.md). The Korean field holds the Sino-Korean 남경 (Wiktionary Korean: alternative name of Nanjing); the usual South Korean name today is 난징 (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Nam Kinh is the Sino-Vietnamese name. Attestation: Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionary (English edition). No homophones (注音 ㄋㄚㄇㄍ⼶ㄫ unique across both words and characters).

1. (Japanese) pumpkin, squash.
2. (historical) the Liao-dynasty southern capital, a former city at modern Beijing.
3. Nanjing, the city.
