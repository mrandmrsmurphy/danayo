## Notes
- [啄 (char)](../characters/啄%20(char).md) "peck at"

啄 is 形声: semantic [[口]] ("mouth") + phonetic [[豕]] ("pig") — the mouth striking repeatedly like a rooting pig, extended to birds pecking. 啄木鳥 ("woodpecker," literally "wood-pecking bird") is the most common compound.

Mandarin zhuó and Cantonese doek3 preserve the sense directly. Japanese タク (TAKU, on'yomi, confined mostly to 啄木鳥 takuboku-dori and personal/place names — this character is classified in this vault's own data as a name-use kanji) alongside native ついば(む) (tsuiba(mu), "to peck," the everyday verb) cover the sense. Korean 탁 (tak) parallels the Sino-xenic reading. Vietnamese trác is the Hán Việt reading, attested in ẩm trác ("drinking and pecking," of feeding birds), trác mễ ("to peck rice"), and cited in classical poetry (Đỗ Phủ's "hương đạo trác dư anh vũ lạp," "the parrot pecked and left behind fragrant rice grains"); the character page's other stored candidates, trốc ("head," a Central Vietnamese dialect word) and chác ("to barter, trade," as in đổi chác), are unrelated Nôm phonetic-loan readings with no connection to pecking, and were excluded.

This word shares its reading (tog/톡/ㄊㄛㄎ) with [[禿]] ("bald"), still unperfected and awaiting its own turn in this sweep — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed when it comes up.
