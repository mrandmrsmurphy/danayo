## Notes
- [怪](../characters/怪.md) "strange" + [石 (char)](../characters/石%20(char).md) "rock; stone"

**怪石** is an ordinary word for a strangely shaped rock, of the kind prized in Chinese gardens and scholars' studios. As a calligraphy term it is the figurative name of the first of the eight principles of the 永字八法, the dot ([[点]]): the dot is imagined as a weathered rock set down at the head of the character.

Li Puguang (李溥光), writing in the Yuan dynasty in his 永字八法解, gave each of the eight strokes of the 永字八法 a figurative two-character name; 怪石 is the dot's name, joined by 玉案 for the horizontal ([[玉案]]), 鉄柱 for the vertical, 蟹爪 for the hook, 虎牙 for the rising stroke, 犀角 for the curve, 鳥啄 for the left-falling stroke and 金刀 for the right-falling stroke. They are listed with the modern names on the [[Stroke]] page.

The Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and North Korean forms are the ordinary compound readings of the word. No Vietnamese form is given.
