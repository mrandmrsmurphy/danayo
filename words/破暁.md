## Notes
- [破 (char)](../characters/破%20(char).md) "rend, break" + [暁](../characters/暁.md) "daybreak" — 暁's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 破's own is [[破]] itself; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 暁 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word.

破暁 means daybreak or dawn — literally "the breaking [of night into] daybreak," the moment darkness gives way to light. It carries a more literary register than plainer words for "morning" or "sunrise," often used in poetic or narrative description.

Mandarin pòxiǎo and Cantonese po3 hiu2 are compositional. Japanese and Korean and Vietnamese here are likewise compositional (はぎょう, 파효, phá hiểu), though the everyday word in each of these languages for "dawn" is more often a native or alternate compound (Japanese 夜明け yoake, Korean 새벽 saebyeok, Vietnamese bình minh), with 破暁 itself surviving mainly in literary or classical-flavored writing.
