## Notes
- [彩](../characters/彩.md) "vibrant, multicolored" + [色](../characters/色.md) "color" — literally "vibrant-color."

彩色 describes something colored or polychromatic, contrasting directly with [[単色]] ("monochrome, single-color"). It's the character-reversed near-synonym of [[色彩]] (the general noun "color"), the two words sharing the same two characters in opposite order — 彩色 functioning as the adjective, 色彩 as the noun.

Mandarin cǎisè, Cantonese coi2 sik1, and Korean 채색 (chaesaek) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese has two attested on'yomi-based readings, さいしき (saishiki, the more traditional/artistic register, as in traditional Japanese painting) and さいしょく (saishoku, the more general reading) — kept as a real two-item list rather than collapsed to one. Vietnamese thái sắc is likewise compositional, from 彩's thái (one of four attested readings) and 色's own sắc. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 채식 diverges from Sino-Korean 채색 in the second syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄑㄚㄧㄙㄧㄎ unique among both words and characters).
