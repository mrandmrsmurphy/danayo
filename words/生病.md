## Notes
- [生](../characters/生.md) "life, to produce, to arise" + [病 (char)](../characters/病%20(char).md) "sickness" — literally "for sickness to arise," a verb-object construction meaning "to fall ill, to get sick."

Mandarin shēng bìng, Cantonese sang1 beng6, and Vietnamese sinh bệnh (verified, an attested Hán Việt cognate) all carry the general verb sense "to fall ill, to catch a disease" — the everyday way to say someone got sick. This is the sense Dan'a'yo adopts as primary.

Korean 생병, though the same compositional compound, has narrowed in real usage to a distinct sense: a self-inflicted or unnecessary illness (from overexertion, or an illness "brought on oneself for no good reason"), or colloquially "to feign sickness" — not the general "to fall ill" of Mandarin/Cantonese/Vietnamese. This is documented rather than corrected, matching this vault's practice of noting genuine cross-linguistic sense divergence (cf. [[愛人]]) rather than papering over it.

Japanese せいびょう is the compositional Sino-Japanese reading (SEI + BYOU) but is not independently confirmed as live Japanese vocabulary — Japanese instead says 病気になる (byōki ni naru) for "to fall ill." `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 상병 diverges from Sino-Korean 생병 in the first syllable. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄚㄫㄅ⼶ㄫ unique among both words and characters).
