## Notes
- [狭](../characters/狭.md) "narrow; cramped" + [窄](../characters/窄.md) "narrow; tight" — stand-in for both [[狭]] and [[窄]] (transitivity holds: A=B=AB), hence the `#cranberry` tag

Neither constituent character can stand alone in Dan'a'yo: 狭's own `stand_in` and 窄's own `stand_in` both point to this exact compound. Mandarin xiázhǎi, Cantonese haap6 zaak3, and Japanese きょうさく (kyōsaku) are all real compositional readings for "narrow, constricted," with きょうさく specifically favored in technical/medical registers (脊柱管狭窄症, "spinal stenosis"). Korean 협착 (hyeopchak) likewise carries a genuine medical sense ("stenosis, constriction") alongside the general "narrow" meaning. Vietnamese hẹp hòi is a real, attested compound, though it has drifted to a figurative sense — "narrow-minded, petty" — rather than literal physical narrowness, for which Vietnamese instead uses the native hẹp alone. `kwin` is false: neither constituent's Dan'a'yo reading coincides with its Sino-Korean counterpart (狭's 합 vs 협; 窄's 작 vs 착).
