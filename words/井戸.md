## Notes
- [井](../characters/井.md) "well" + [戸](../characters/戸.md) "door, household" — いど (ido), the everyday Japanese word for a water well, most familiar in 井戸端 (idobata, "by the well" — the traditional gathering spot for neighborhood gossip, giving 井戸端会議, idobata kaigi, "well-side chat," i.e. idle conversation among neighbors).

**Content note, already flagged directly on this page before this iteration**: this is a deliberately "very Japanese" entry — 井戸 isn't a real Chinese compound (Mandarin/Cantonese simply use 井 alone for "well," not combined with 戸/戶), so `mandarin`/`cantonese` were left blank rather than fabricated. This word was created specifically to occupy this Dan'a'yo syllable slot (a crowded one at jing/징) with a genuine Japanese vocabulary item, the same kind of language-specific fill this vault uses elsewhere when a syllable needs spreading out. `korean: 우물`/`vietnamese: giếng` are each language's own native word for "well," included for completeness rather than as translations of 井戸 itself. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 징호 diverges entirely from the native-word-based Korean field. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄧㄫㄏㄛ unique among both words and characters).
