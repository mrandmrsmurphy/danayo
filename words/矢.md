## Notes
- [矢 (char)](../characters/矢%20(char).md) "arrow" — stand-in for [[矢 (char)|矢]], which cannot appear independently

矢 is 象形 of an arrow, the fletching and shaft visible in the character's upper strokes over the arrowhead below.

The character survives today mostly in a narrower, more classical register than many other characters: 矢 rarely appears as the everyday word for "arrow" in modern Mandarin (箭 has largely displaced it there), but persists in fixed idiomatic and technical registers — 矢志 ("to vow, to pledge," the arrow standing for an unwavering, straight-flying resolve) and in the vault's own already-documented three-way homophone relationship with [[四]] ("four") and [[視]] ("look at, inspect"), both coincidental convergences unrelated to 矢's own meaning.

Japanese keeps a single on'yomi し (SHI), used mainly in compounds like 一矢 (isshi, "a single arrow," as in the idiom 一矢を報いる "to fire back a single retaliatory shot"), while the native 訓読み や (ya) remains the ordinary everyday word for a physical arrow. Vietnamese thỉ survives almost exclusively in learned/classical compounds rather than everyday speech, which instead uses the native mũi tên ("arrow," literally "arrow-tip").
