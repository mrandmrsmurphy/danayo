## Notes
- [矢 (char)](../characters/矢%20(char).md) "arrow" — stand-in for [[矢 (char)|矢]], which cannot appear independently

矢 is 象形 of an arrow, the fletching and shaft visible in the character's upper strokes over the arrowhead below. **Note**: `characters/矢 (char).md` remains genuinely unperfected — no `date-last-perfect`, and its `## Notes` holds only two dangling, un-bulleted Middle Chinese initial/final wikilinks plus a leftover "Components:"-style bullet naming 𠂉/大 without any real graphemic write-up — flagged here for the separate character-perfection sweep rather than rebuilt inside this word-creation pass, though its individual scalar fields remained trustworthy ground truth for this page.

The character survives today mostly in a narrower, more classical register than most of this sweep's other body-of-vocabulary characters: 矢 rarely appears as the everyday word for "arrow" in modern Mandarin (箭 has largely displaced it there), but persists in fixed idiomatic and technical registers — 矢志 ("to vow, to pledge," the arrow standing for an unwavering, straight-flying resolve) and in the vault's own already-documented three-way homophone relationship with [[四]] ("four") and [[視]] ("look at, inspect"), both coincidental convergences unrelated to 矢's own meaning.

Japanese keeps a single on'yomi し (SHI), used mainly in compounds like 一矢 (isshi, "a single arrow," as in the idiom 一矢を報いる "to fire back a single retaliatory shot"), while the native 訓読み や (ya) remains the ordinary everyday word for a physical arrow — the same Sino-technical/native-everyday split recurring throughout this sweep. Vietnamese thỉ survives almost exclusively in learned/classical compounds rather than everyday speech, which instead uses the native mũi tên ("arrow," literally "arrow-tip"). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 싀 (siǝ, from Middle Chinese 書母 ɕ + 脂B三開韻 ɣiɪ) diverges from Sino-Korean 시 in the vowel, Dan'a'yo preserving a diphthongal glide Korean has smoothed to a plain vowel.
