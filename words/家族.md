## Notes
- [家](../characters/家.md) "house; family" + [族](../characters/族.md) "tribe; clan" — this word is the stand-in for [族](../characters/族.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

家族 names the family as a lineage or kin-group, distinct from [[家庭]] ("household"), which names the shared living space and its immediate occupants — the two overlap heavily in casual use but 家族 is the wider, more anthropological term, extending outward to 親族 ("relatives"), 氏族 ("clan"), and 九族 ("nine generations of kin"), all built on the same 族 "tribe, clan" root. This breadth is also why 族 is so productive as an ethnonym suffix in Mandarin (漢族 "Han," 満族 "Manchu," 苗族 "Hmong," 壮族 "Zhuang"), naming an entire ethnic group as a kind of vast extended kin-group — a metaphor that doesn't carry over into Japanese or Korean usage of 家族 itself.

Japanese かぞく (kazoku) is comparatively narrow by contrast: Wiktionary records it as referring specifically to one's *own* immediate family — parents, children, siblings — with the honorific ご家族 (go-kazoku) required when referring to someone else's family. The word itself is also not ancient in Japanese; it isn't attested before 1808, and appears to have been borrowed from modern written Chinese rather than inherited from any earlier native usage, unlike many other basic kinship terms.

Korean 가족 and Vietnamese gia tộc are both used as plainly and broadly as Mandarin/Cantonese 家族, with no comparable narrowing to "one's own family only." No HSK level is given for the compound on Wiktionary, though both constituent characters are independently HSK 1.
