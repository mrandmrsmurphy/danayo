## Notes
- [久 (char)](../characters/久%20(char).md) "long time (ago)"

久 is 象形, likely originally a pictogram of an arrow tip (accounts vary and the exact visual relationship to the modern form is uncertain), long since bleached of any such literal meaning and used exclusively for "a long time; long-lasting." It appears widely in compounds emphasizing duration and permanence: [[悠久]] ("long-lasting, time-honoured"), [[恒久]]/[[永久]] ("eternal, perpetual"), [[久闊]] ("a long period of separation," a formal greeting used after not seeing someone for a long time), and the chengyu [[天長地久]] ("as enduring as heaven and earth").

Mandarin jiǔ, Cantonese gau2, and Vietnamese cửu all preserve the same "long time" sense directly. Japanese has two on'yomi, きゅう/く (kyū/ku), alongside the native kun'yomi ひさ(しい) (hisa(shii), the everyday adjective for "long-lasting," as in お久しぶり o-hisashiburi, "long time no see"). Korean 구 (gu) parallels the Sino-xenic reading directly.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (gyu/규/ㄍ⼜) with [[球]] ("sphere"), now completed together with this page.
