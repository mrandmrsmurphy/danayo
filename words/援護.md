## Notes
- [援](../characters/援.md) "aid, assist" + [護](../characters/護.md) "protect, safeguard" — support, backing, aid. Mandarin/Cantonese treat this compound as a Japanese borrowing rather than native vocabulary (Chinese Wiktionary lists it only under its Japanese entry), but Korean genuinely and independently attests it: 원호, "to help and look after," used specifically for state support of disabled veterans and national merit recipients (원호 기금, 상이군인 원호).
The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese fields, which were blank, now hold the compositional readings of the two characters (yuánhù, wun4 wu6, viện hộ), following the vault's convention that these fields record the character readings even where the word is not an everyday word in that language, as the paragraph above says is the case for Chinese. Because the Korean 원호 differs from the Dan'a'yo 諺文 온호 in the first syllable's final, `kwin` is false.

The Society page lists the word as "support, backing (especially for veterans)" and the Directions page as "support, backing, aid (especially for veterans)." Dan'a'yo keeps that sense, with the veterans' welfare use as its characteristic. It is distinct from the existing words [援助](援助.md) "aid, assistance," [救援](救援.md) "relief, rescue" and [保護](保護.md) "protection," which have no specific veterans' sense. The Japanese 援護 has the same veterans' sense in 戦傷病者戦没者遺族等援護法, but I recall that from memory and have not checked it against a source.
