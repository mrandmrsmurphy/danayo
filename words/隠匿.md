## Notes
- [隠](../characters/隠.md) "hide" + [匿](../characters/匿.md) "hide"
- This word is the viable form of [匿](../characters/匿.md): the character's meaning (to hide, conceal) is identical, but 匿 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 隠匿 is its `stand_in` compound.

隠 is to hide from view, keep out of sight, and 匿 is to hide away, lie concealed, so 隠匿 is a synonym compound for deliberate concealment of a person, object or fact. It is the word of the courtroom and the official report: in Japanese law 犯人隠匿 "harboring a criminal" and 証拠隠匿 are standard offence names, and in Chinese and Korean it is likewise used for hiding evidence, assets or fugitives. The concealment is intentional and usually illicit, so it leans negative.

The Sensation page notes that 隠匿, [隠蔵](隠蔵.md) and [隠蔽](隠蔽.md) are near-synonyms with no sharp distinction recorded. A useful rule of thumb is that 隠蔽 is covering something up so that it cannot be seen (the "cover-up"), 隠蔵 is storing something away out of sight, and 隠匿 emphasizes keeping a person or thing hidden from those seeking it, though the three overlap heavily and Dan'a'yo does not force a hard line. The readings are Mandarin yǐnnì, Cantonese jan2 nik1, Japanese intoku (いんとく) and Korean 은닉. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "conceal, hide, cover up."
