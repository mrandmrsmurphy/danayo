## Notes
- [穿 (char)](../characters/穿%20(char).md) "penetrate, pierce, drill" + [山 (char)](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain" + [甲 (char)](../characters/甲%20(char).md) "armor, first heavenly stem" — none of the three characters' own `stand_in` points here, so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: true` per the AND-rule: all three constituents are individually `kwin: true`.

穿山甲 names the pangolin, the scale-covered mammal — literally "mountain-piercing armor," describing both its overlapping scaled armor plating and its habit of burrowing through the ground (traditionally imagined as boring through mountains). It is heavily poached for its scales in traditional medicine and meat, making it one of the most trafficked mammals in the world.

Mandarin chuānshānjiǎ, Cantonese cyun1 saan1 gaap3, Japanese せんざんこう (senzankō), and Korean 천산갑 (cheonsangap) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese xuyên sơn giáp is likewise compositional; everyday Vietnamese more often uses the native tê tê.
