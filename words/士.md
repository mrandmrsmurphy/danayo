## Notes
- [士 (char)](../characters/士%20(char).md) "scholar"

士 originally depicted a war axe, later coming to mean "soldier" and eventually "officer, scholar, learned gentleman." It survives as a common suffix for professions and roles requiring skill or standing (博士, "doctor, PhD"; 紳士, "gentleman"; 武士, "warrior, samurai").

Mandarin shì and Cantonese si6 preserve the sense directly. Japanese シ/ジ (SHI/JI, on'yomi, fully productive: 武士道, bushidō, "the way of the warrior") alongside native さむらい (samurai, a specific extended sense, "warrior retainer") cover the sense. Korean 사 (sa) parallels the Sino-xenic reading. Vietnamese sĩ is the standard Hán Việt reading, extremely common as a professional/role suffix (bác sĩ, "doctor"; chiến sĩ, "soldier, warrior"; nghệ sĩ, "artist"). The character page's other stored candidates are Nôm phonetic loans for unrelated native words, not doublets of "scholar/warrior": sãi ("Buddhist monk," as in sư sãi); sõi ("fluent," as in nói sõi, "to speak fluently"); sỡi, more marginal still, with no independent corroboration beyond the same single source as sõi.
