## Notes
- [巴 (char)](../characters/巴%20(char).md) "tomoe" + [基](../characters/基.md) "foundation" + [斯](../characters/斯.md) "this" + [坦](../characters/坦.md) "flat" — pure phonetic rendering of *Pākistān*

巴基斯坦 is Pakistan: a phonetic rendering of *Pākistān*, with four characters used for sound alone (巴 "tomoe", 基 "foundation", 斯 "this", 坦 "flat"). English Wiktionary lists the Chinese name (Mandarin Bājīsītǎn, Cantonese baa1 gei1 si1 taan2), Japanese パキスタン and Korean 파키스탄 as the country Pakistan; Japanese and Korean write the name in sound, so the Japanese and Korean fields here are those real names, and Vietnamese Pakistan is the same.

English Wikipedia credits Choudhry Rahmat Ali with coining the name in 1933, in the pamphlet *Now or Never*, first as "Pakstan", used as an acronym for the homelands Panjab, Afghania, Kashmir, Sindh and Baluchistan, and says "Pakistan" is both a Persian and an Urdu word. It gives Pakistan's independence, after the partition of British India, as 14 August 1947.

The region includes the ancient Buddhist region of Gandhara. English Wikipedia places it in present-day northwestern Pakistan and northeastern Afghanistan, with its core in the Valley of Peshawar, and says the Chinese monk Faxian visited Taxila in Gandhara and that Xuanzang visited and worshipped its holy Buddhist sites. Compare [[孟加拉]], Bangladesh, and [[獅子国]], Sri Lanka.
