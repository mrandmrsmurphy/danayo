## Notes
- [盛](../characters/盛.md) "flourish, full" + [衰](../characters/衰.md) "decrease, weaken"

盛衰 pairs two opposite states — flourishing and decline — into a single word for the cyclical rise and fall of fortunes, dynasties, nations, or any state of affairs. It carries a strong classical and philosophical resonance, closely tied to the idiom 盛者必衰 ("what flourishes must fade"), a Buddhist-inflected expression of impermanence found across East Asian literature — famously opening Japan's *Tale of the Heike* (祇園精舎の鐘の声、諸行無常の響きあり). The related compound 盛衰栄辱 extends the pairing into a fuller meditation on the vicissitudes of fortune, adding glory and humiliation.

Mandarin shèngshuāi, Cantonese sing6 seoi1, Japanese せいすい (seisui), Korean 성쇠 (seongsoe), and Vietnamese thịnh suy are all fully compositional and carry the same weighty, reflective register across the sphere.
