## Notes
- [尤](../characters/尤.md) "especially; particularly" + [其 (char)](../characters/其%20(char).md) "that, it (pronoun)" — this word is the stand-in for [尤](../characters/尤.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

尤其 is a Mandarin/Cantonese grammatical adverb marking a specific case singled out from a broader group ("especially, particularly, above all") — 尤 alone already carries this "especially, exceptionally" sense (as in the character's own extended meaning), with 其 functioning as a light grammatical pronoun rather than contributing independent semantic content.

**None of the other three daughters actually use this character compound**: Japanese has no 尤其 kanji reading at all — とりわけ (recorded here) is a purely native Japanese word serving as the closest translation-equivalent, not a reading of these characters. Korean likewise defaults to the native 특히, not a Sino-Korean 우기. Vietnamese does have a genuine, if comparatively literary, Sino-Vietnamese reading vưu kỳ (attested), though everyday speech prefers the native đặc biệt (là)/nhất là. No homophones (注音 ⼜ㄛㄍㄜ unique across both words and characters).
