## Notes
- [広 (char)](../characters/広%20(char).md) "wide" + [場](../characters/場.md) "market, open space" — literally "wide space."

広場 names a plaza or square — a large open public space, distinguished from the narrower "market" sense that 場 alone carries in most of its other compounds ([[市場]], [[工場]]). 場's own core sense (an open threshing floor, later a market or venue) makes 広場 a natural, transparent extension: simply a *wide* version of that open space, emphasized for public gathering rather than commerce.

Mandarin guǎngchǎng, Cantonese gwong2 coeng4, and Korean 광장 (gwangjang) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese ひろば (hiroba) is likewise fully compositional, but built from native kun'yomi rather than Sino-Japanese on'yomi — 広's own ひろ + 場's own ば, both already independently stored as their respective characters' `japanese_native` values — and is itself the real, standard everyday Japanese word for "plaza, open square." Vietnamese quảng trường is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 広's quảng and one of 場's two attested readings, trường (the other, tràng, is not used here). `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 광장 matches Sino-Korean 광장 exactly. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄍ⺢ㄫㄐㄚㄫ unique among both words and characters).
