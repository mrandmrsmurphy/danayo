## Notes
- [開](../characters/開.md) "open" + [示](../characters/示.md) "show, reveal, indicate" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes 示 as a bound character (開's own stand-in is [[開放]]; transitivity fails, so no `#cranberry`).

開示 means to reveal, disclose, or instruct. Mandarin 開示 (kāishì) and Japanese 開示 (かいじ) both carry a modern legal/financial "disclosure" sense (情報開示 jōhō kaiji, "information disclosure," used of corporate reporting), alongside an older Buddhist sense of a teacher revealing or expounding the dharma to a student. Korean 개시 (gaesi) happens to be spelled identically to [[開始]]'s own Korean reading — a real coincidental Korean homograph (示's own Sino-Korean reading is 시, giving 개+시), unrelated to any Dan'a'yo collision since the two words' own 注音/羅馬字/諺文 differ entirely.

Sino-Vietnamese khai thị is best known through Buddhist usage, especially the four-stage formula khai, thị, ngộ, nhập (開示悟入) from the Lotus Sutra's Expedient Means chapter, describing the Buddha's purpose in appearing in the world: to open (khai) sentient beings' eyes, reveal (thị) the true nature of reality, lead them to understanding (ngộ), and bring them to enter (nhập) the path. In contemporary colloquial use, khai thị also refers more broadly to a teacher or monastic giving spoken guidance or instruction to disciples.
