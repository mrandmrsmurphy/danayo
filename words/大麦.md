## Notes
- [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) "big" + [麦](../characters/麦.md) "wheat, barley"
- This word is the viable form of [麦](../characters/麦.md): the character's meaning (grain; barley/wheat) is identical in this compound, but 麦 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 大麦 is its `stand_in` compound.
- Japanese reads this おおむぎ (ōmugi), a native-Japanese compound (大 = おお, 麦 = むぎ) rather than the Sino-Japanese on-yomi concatenation.

大麦 is barley (*Hordeum vulgare*), literally "big wheat". It is paired in Chinese with 小麦 "small wheat", the name for wheat itself, so the two cereals are named as a large-and-small couple of one kind of grain rather than as unrelated crops. In Dan'a'yo 大麦 is the word for barley specifically; the vault has no word for wheat yet, so the bound character [[麦]] is left covering both grains.

Barley has long been a staple and a brewing grain across East Asia. It survives most visibly in the drinks [[大麦茶]] and [[麦茶]], roasted-barley tea, and in Korean 보리 (the native word for barley) and Japanese むぎ, which are the everyday words. The compound reading 대맥 and Sino-Vietnamese đại mạch are the formal and scientific forms.
