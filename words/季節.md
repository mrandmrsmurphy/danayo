## Notes
- [季](characters/季.md) "season" + [節 (char)](characters/節%20(char).md) "node" — stand-in for [[季]], which cannot appear independently.

季節 combines [[季]] ("season, quarter, youngest sibling") with [[節]] ("node, joint, festival") to mean "season" in the periodic/climatic sense (季節性, "seasonal"). 季 alone cannot stand as an independent word — this compound is its stand-in.

Mandarin jìjié and Cantonese gwai3 zit3 preserve the compositional sense directly. Japanese きせつ (kisetsu) is the ordinary, everyday Japanese word for "season," fully productive (季節感, "seasonal feel"). Korean 계절 (gyejeol) parallels the Sino-xenic reading and is likewise the everyday word for "season" (Korean does not have as strong a competing native term here as it does elsewhere).

Vietnamese quý tiết is dictionary-attested (quý from 季, tiết from 節) but marginal in practice — it surfaces mainly as a character-by-character Hán Việt gloss rather than a word Vietnamese speakers actually reach for; everyday Vietnamese uses the native word mùa for "season" instead. On the constituent characters: 季's other stored candidates (quí, a pre-reform spelling variant of quý rather than a separate reading; quỳ, an unrelated homophone-adjacent word; cuối, a native Nôm gloss for 季's "last, final" sense rather than a phonetic reading) are excluded as noise. 節's tiết is the standard Hán Việt reading, and tết (the Lunar New Year holiday) is a genuine Old Sino-Vietnamese doublet of the same character from an earlier borrowing layer — not a coincidence, but also not part of this compound's reading; tét/típ/tít/tịt are Nôm readings of unrelated native words and are excluded.

kwin is false: [[節]] (char) is kwin, but [[季]] is not, and the AND-rule requires every constituent to be kwin for the compound to qualify.
