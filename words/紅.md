## Notes
- [紅 (char)](../characters/紅%20(char).md) "crimson, red" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 紅 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

紅 means crimson, red: the color of blood and ripe fruit (紅色, "red"; 紅茶, "black tea", literally "red tea"; 紅包, "red envelope"), and by extension flourishing or popular (走紅, "become popular"; 紅人, "favorite"). The simplified form 红 is listed as an alias. It is a 形声 compound of 糸 "silk" with the phonetic 工, since red was first a silk dye.

Mandarin hóng, Cantonese hung4, Japanese こう (紅茶/こうちゃ, "black tea"; 紅葉/こうよう, "autumn leaves"), Korean 홍 (홍차), and Vietnamese hồng (the primary of the character's two stored candidates, hường being a rarer variant) are all standard, fully compositional readings. The character's own etymology traces a semantic drift: 紅 originally denoted lighter shades of red specifically, but gradually displaced [[赤]] as the general-purpose word for "red" in most of the modern sphere — Mandarin hóngsè is now the everyday word for "red," while 赤 survives mainly in older or more literary compounds (赤道 "equator," 赤字 "deficit, literally 'red characters'"). Japanese, however, kept 赤 (あか, aka) as its own everyday native word for "red," reserving 紅 for a narrower, more vivid "crimson" register (口紅 kuchibeni "lipstick," 紅葉 momiji "autumn leaves") — the opposite division of labor from the rest of the sphere.
