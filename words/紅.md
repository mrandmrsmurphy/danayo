## Notes
- [紅 (char)](../characters/紅%20(char).md) "crimson, red" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 紅 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

紅 means crimson, red. Mandarin hóng, Cantonese hung4, Korean 홍, and Vietnamese hồng (the primary of the character's two stored candidates, hường being a rarer variant) are all standard, fully compositional readings. The character's own etymology traces a semantic drift: 紅 originally denoted lighter shades of red specifically, but gradually displaced [[赤]] as the general-purpose word for "red" in most of the modern sphere — Mandarin hóngsè is now the everyday word for "red," while 赤 survives mainly in older or more literary compounds (赤道 "equator," 赤字 "deficit, literally 'red characters'"). Japanese, however, kept 赤 (あか, aka) as its own everyday native word for "red," reserving 紅 for a narrower, more vivid "crimson" register (口紅 kuchibeni "lipstick," 紅葉 momiji "autumn leaves") — the opposite division of labor from the rest of the sphere. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 홍 (hong, from Middle Chinese 匣母 ɣ + 東一韻 uŋ) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 홍. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄏㄛㄫ unique across both words and characters).
