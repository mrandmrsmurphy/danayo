## Notes
- [小 (char)](../characters/小%20(char).md) "small" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" — no stand-in relationship applies (both 小's and 人's own `stand_in` fields point to themselves).

小人 is a moral category from Confucian ethics — literally "small person," a person of narrow, petty, self-interested character, standing in explicit opposition to 君子 (the noble/superior person of cultivated virtue). This ethical-contrastive pair (小人 vs. 君子) runs throughout the Analects and remains the primary living sense in modern usage — a mean, small-minded, or underhanded person — rather than any literal reference to physical stature or social rank (its older, now secondary sense as a self-deprecating first-person pronoun, "your humble servant," survives in classical/formal registers).

Japanese しょうじん, Korean 소인, and Vietnamese tiểu nhân (all standard, non-drifted readings) preserve the same moral-contrastive sense, including the classical self-deprecating pronoun usage in formal Japanese/Korean historical contexts. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄛㄋㄧㄋ unique across both words and characters).
