## Notes
- [以 (char)](../characters/以%20(char).md) "by means of; (as a boundary marker) from X onward" + [外](../characters/外.md) "outside" — "outside the limit set by X," hence "other than, except for, apart from." Neither character's own `stand_in` points here (以's is itself, 外's is [[外部]]), so no legitimizer relationship applies. `kwin` is false because Dan'a'yo 이왜 differs from Sino-Korean 이외.

以外 belongs to the same family as [[以前]] and [[以後]], in which 以 marks a reference point and the second character says which side of it is meant: "before," "after," and here "outside." Placed after a noun phrase, it picks out everything that falls outside that phrase: 日本語以外 "languages other than Japanese." That is the postposed pattern the Dan'a'yo grammar uses for exactly this phrase. The noun it follows is the excluded set. The sentence normally goes on to say something about whatever is left over, as in Japanese 関係者以外立入禁止, "no entry except for persons concerned."

All five languages have the word or a close relative of it. Japanese いがい, Korean 이외, and Mandarin yǐwài are ordinary, fully compositional readings. Japanese pairs it with the antonym 以内 ("within"). Japanese 以上 and 以下 conventionally include the number they are anchored to ("100 or more," "100 or fewer"), while 以外 excludes the thing named. Mandarin often brackets the noun with 除了…以外 ("apart from…"), a frame that can mean either "except for" or "in addition to" depending on what follows, a split that [[除外]] ("exclude") avoids by keeping only the exclusive reading. Vietnamese dĩ ngoại is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form, confirmed by hvdic.thivien.net as "besides." In everyday Vietnamese speakers reach for the native ngoài (itself cognate with 外 as ngoại) instead. Japanese also has the native ほか "other than" and Korean 외에 / 밖에, so this is a place where the Sino-Xenic compound is the register-neutral, written-leaning choice.

Dan'a'yo uses 以外 in the same sense as the shared CJKV word and does not narrow, shift, or broaden it. It sits beside the contrastives [[別]] "other, distinct" and [[他]] "another" and the comparative particle [[過]] "than."
