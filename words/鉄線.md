## Notes
- [鉄 (char)](../characters/鉄%20(char).md) "iron" + [線](../characters/線.md) "line, clue" — wire.

鉄線 is the broad Dan'a'yo word for physical wire: a drawn strand of metal, whether for fencing, binding, reinforcement or crafts. Iron stands for metal here in the same way it does in [[鉄板]]. Wire made for carrying current is [[電線]], and thread of fibre is [[糸線]]. Mandarin tiěxiàn, Cantonese tit3 sin3 and Japanese てっせん are the standard readings of 鐵線/鉄線 as "iron wire"; Korean 철선 is the same compound, though everyday Korean prefers 철사 (鐵絲) and Mandarin prefers 铁丝, both built on 絲 "thread." Japanese 鉄線 (てっせん) also names the clematis flower, a sense Dan'a'yo does not take over. Wire can be drawn from any ductile metal; see [[延性]]. For gold wire, 金線 would be the literal reading.
