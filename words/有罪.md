## Notes
- [有](../characters/有%20(char).md) "have" + [罪](../characters/罪%20(char).md) "sin, crime" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (both are bare self-standing words), so 有罪 is an independent compound. Note that Japanese uses 罪's on'yomi ZAI here (ゆうざい), not its native kun-reading つみ used when 罪 stands alone. `kwin: true` per the AND-rule: `characters/有 (char).md` and `characters/罪 (char).md` are both individually `kwin: true`.

有罪 means "guilty" — the direct antonym of [[無罪]] above. Mandarin yǒuzuì, Cantonese jau5 zeoi6, Japanese ゆうざい (yūzai), and Korean 유죄 (yujoe) are all standard, fully compositional readings.

**A genuine markedness asymmetry, worth flagging.** [[無罪]]'s own Vietnamese field, vô tội, really is the living, standard Vietnamese word for "innocent" — but Vietnamese does *not* mirror this with a parallel Sino-Vietnamese "hữu tội" for "guilty." Living usage instead says có tội, pairing 罪's Hán Việt tội with the native verb có ("to have") rather than Sino-Vietnamese hữu ("to have"). The negative member of the pair (vô-) stayed fully Sino-Vietnamese while the positive member didn't — hữu tội above is the mechanical compositional reading, kept for consistency with this vault's citation method, but is not the word a Vietnamese speaker would actually reach for.
