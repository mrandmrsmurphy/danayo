## Notes
- [有](../characters/有%20(char).md) "have" + [罪](../characters/罪%20(char).md) "sin, crime". Japanese uses 罪's on'yomi ZAI here (ゆうざい), not its native kun-reading つみ used when 罪 stands alone.

有罪 means "guilty" — the direct antonym of [無罪](無罪.md). Mandarin yǒuzuì, Cantonese jau5 zeoi6, Japanese ゆうざい (yūzai), and Korean 유죄 (yujoe) are all standard, fully compositional readings.

Vietnamese shows a markedness asymmetry. [無罪](無罪.md)'s own Vietnamese field, vô tội, really is the living, standard Vietnamese word for "innocent" — but Vietnamese does *not* mirror this with a parallel Sino-Vietnamese "hữu tội" for "guilty." Living usage instead says có tội, pairing 罪's Hán Việt tội with the native verb có ("to have") rather than Sino-Vietnamese hữu ("to have"). The negative member of the pair (vô-) stayed fully Sino-Vietnamese while the positive member didn't — hữu tội is the mechanical compositional reading, kept for consistency with this vault's citation method, but is not the word a Vietnamese speaker would actually reach for.
