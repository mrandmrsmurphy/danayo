## Notes
- [津](../characters/津.md) "haven" + [波](../characters/波.md) "wave" — this word is 津's own `stand_in`, since it's too bounded to stand alone.
- Japanese reading つなみ is a fused form (津 = つ "inlet", 波 = なみ "wave"), not a concatenation of the on'yomi.

津波 is originally a Japanese word, a compound of 津 "cove, inlet, port" and 波 "wave", so named because tsunamis do the most damage at harbours. Wiktionary attests it from the early 1600s, and it is the source of English "tsunami" and the same loan in many other languages. Its senses in Japanese also include, rarely, a tidal bore and a storm surge, though the spelling 海嘯 is specific to the tidal-bore sense. Mandarin and Cantonese speakers read the characters as jīnbō and zeon1 bo1, and the Korean 진파 is likewise the Sino-Korean reading; these are readings of the borrowed spelling, not the native names of the thing in those languages.

In Dan'a'yo the word is the general term for a tsunami, as placed on the [Nature](../lexipedia/Nature.md) page. The near-duplicate [海粛](海粛.md) is recorded there as a phonetic-substitute spelling of 海嘯.
