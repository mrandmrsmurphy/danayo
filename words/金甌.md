## Notes
- [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "gold; metal" + [甌](../characters/甌.md) "small bowl, cup" — 甌 is a 名専字 listed separately at [[List of 名専字]], not legitimized by this word; 金's own is the single-character word [[金]]. The simplified form 金瓯 is kept as an alias because 瓯 is an alias of 甌 on its character page.

This spelling serves two unrelated uses, an ordinary literary noun and the Chinese-character name of a Vietnamese province and city, so the ordinary senses come first. The word is typed as a noun (名詞), the part of speech of its primary ordinary sense; the Cà Mau sense is a second sense of the same noun, as with [[大力]] and [[広南]], not a second part of speech.

1. A golden chalice, a gold cup or bowl (literary). This is the literal sense and the one English Wiktionary lists first (literary: "golden chalice").
2. The nation, the country (literary, figurative). A flawless gold vessel is the image of a state that is whole and unbroken, and the sense is nearly always used with the idea of territorial integrity. English Wiktionary lists it as "the country; the nation", and hvdic.thivien.net (the Nguyễn Quốc Hùng dictionary) glosses Vietnamese kim âu as "a gold vessel, a gold basin; refers to the nation". The image comes from a famous remark of Emperor Wu of Liang, who said his state was "like a golden chalice with not one flaw", recorded in the 南史 (in the life of 朱異), the 梁書 (in the life of 侯景) and the 資治通鑑; the story, with the quotations as checked against those texts, is told at [金甌無缺](../chengyu/金甌無缺.md), the four-character idiom built on this word. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense: [[国家]] is the plain everyday word for a country, and 金甌 the literary, figurative one that stresses its unbroken territory (compare [[領土]] "territory").
3. Cà Mau, the southernmost province and city of Vietnam, in the Mekong Delta. English Wiktionary records both the province and the city under a Chinese proper-noun entry, derived-terms 金甌角 (Cape Cà Mau). The Vietnamese name Cà Mau is not Hán-Việt: it is usually derived from Khmer <i>Tuk Khmau</i>, "black water" (from memory, not re-checked), and the Chinese spelling is a later device. English Wiktionary says the Chinese entry is borrowed from Vietnamese and is "probably a phono-semantic matching of Cà Mau, likely via Cantonese": the existing word 金甌 was used because its Cantonese reading gam1 au1 is close to Cà Mau (the auspicious meaning of the word as "the nation" may also have helped, which is my own inference, not stated at Wiktionary). So the place name is probably derived from the existing word, not the word from the place. The southernmost tip, Cape Cà Mau (Mũi Cà Mau), is 金甌角 in Chinese. The province was merged with the neighbouring Bạc Liêu province in the 2025 reorganization of Vietnam's provinces (from memory, not re-checked).

The `vietnamese` field holds the Hán-Việt reading kim âu (kim 金, âu 甌; hvdic.thivien.net gives 金甌 as kim âu), which is also the reading of the Han form of the Vietnamese word for sense 2; English Wiktionary adds the calque âu vàng "gold bowl". It is not the name of the place, which is Cà Mau. Mandarin jīn'ōu and Cantonese gam1 au1 are as at English Wiktionary. Japanese きんおう and Korean 금구 are the compositional Sino-Xenic readings (金 KIN, 甌 OU; 金 김, 甌 구, North Korean forms); the Japanese word 金甌 in the sense "nation" is, I believe, an ordinary literary word and so is the Korean one (from memory, not re-checked). The actual Japanese and Korean names of the city are カマウ and 까마우 (from memory, not re-checked).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 金甌 as a Chinese entry with both etymologies above and a Vietnamese Han-form entry for kim âu ("gold cup, the nation, stability"), listing 固金甌, 名覆金甌, 名動金甌, 金甌藏名 and 金甌無缺 as derived terms. Chinese Wiktionary has a stub (Mandarin jīn'ōu, Cantonese gam1 au1) whose only gloss is "Ca Mau, Vietnam". Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 金甌 page (the parse API returned no page for each). No homophones (注音 ㄍㄧㄇ·ㄛㄨ unique across both words and characters).
