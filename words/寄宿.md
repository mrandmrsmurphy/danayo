## Notes
- [寄 (char)](../characters/寄%20(char).md) "approach; send; rely on" + [宿](../characters/宿.md) "lodging" — this word is the stand-in for [宿](../characters/宿.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

寄宿 names the act of lodging or boarding away from one's own home — staying at another's house, renting a room, or (its most common modern sense) a student or worker living in dormitory housing provided by a school or employer. It is also used, informally, as a shortened form of its own extension [[寄宿舎]] ("dormitory, boarding house") itself.

Korean 기숙 (as in 기숙사, "dormitory") and Japanese きしゅく are both plain, everyday, non-drifted readings matching the Mandarin/Cantonese sense directly. Vietnamese ký túc (as in ký túc xá, "dormitory") is likewise attested — with a striking twist: sources trace ký túc xá as a borrowing of the *Japanese* compound 寄宿舎 (kishukusha) rather than a direct Sino-Vietnamese coinage from Chinese, despite being written with ordinary Sino-Vietnamese characters throughout. No HSK level is given for the compound; no homophones (注音 ㄍㄨㄧㄙㄨㄎ unique across both words and characters).
