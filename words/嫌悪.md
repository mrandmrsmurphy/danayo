## Notes
- [嫌](../characters/嫌.md) "detest, hate" + [悪 (char)](../characters/悪%20(char).md) "bad, evil" — this word is the viable form of [嫌](../characters/嫌.md): the character's meaning (to detest; to loathe) is identical, but 嫌 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 嫌悪 is its `stand_in` compound.

嫌悪 names visceral dislike or disgust — a stronger, more instinctive aversion than simple dislike, closer to English "revulsion" than to plain "dislike." In Japanese, 嫌悪感 (ken'okan, "a feeling of disgust/aversion") is the everyday psychological/emotional-vocabulary form, describing the felt reaction itself rather than a judgment about the object.

The 悪 here uses its secondary reading wù (to hate), not è (evil); hence the Mandarin xiánwù and Cantonese wu3 rather than the "bad/evil" readings. This same polyphony recurs across several 悪-compounds meaning "to hate/loathe" rather than "evil" (e.g. 憎悪, zēngwù, "hatred, abhorrence").

Korean 혐오 and Vietnamese hiềm ác are both standard compositional Sino-readings. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 험악 diverges from Sino-Korean 혐오 in both syllables. No specific HSK level is confirmed.
