## Notes
- [里 (char)](../characters/里%20(char).md) — this word is the stand-in for its own character, 会意 of [[田]] ("field") + [[土]] ("soil, earth").

里 carries two distinct, both long-standing senses: an administrative/settlement sense, "village, hamlet" (the smallest traditional unit of local organization, headed by a 里長 "village head"), and a measure sense, "li," the traditional East Asian unit of distance (roughly 500m in modern China, though the exact value has varied by era and country). For the unrelated Western "mile," this vault reserves 英里 instead (not yet its own page), keeping the two units distinct rather than conflating them the way English "mile" sometimes gets used loosely for 里 in translation. 里 also serves as this vault's periodic-table abbreviation letter for curium (Cm) in [[居里金]] — a purely graphic reuse, unconnected to either of its ordinary senses.

Mandarin lǐ, Cantonese lei5, Korean 리 (ri), Japanese り (ri, on'yomi), and Vietnamese lý are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 里 is itself the real, everyday word for both senses in each source language. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 리 matches Sino-Korean 리 exactly. This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (li/리/ㄌㄧ) with [[厘]] ("centi-") and [[浬]] ("nautical mile") — a three-way homophone group, already cross-referenced bidirectionally on all three pages.
