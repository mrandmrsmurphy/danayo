## Notes
- [腐](../characters/腐.md) "rotten; corrupt" + [敗](../characters/敗.md) "be defeated" — stand-in for [[腐]], which cannot appear independently

腐敗 means to rot, spoil, or be corrupt: for organic matter to decay (食物腐敗, "food spoils"; 防腐, "preserve against decay") and, figuratively, for people, institutions or customs to be morally corrupt (官員腐敗, "corrupt officials"; 腐敗政治, "corrupt politics"; 反腐敗, "anti-corruption"). Both halves independently carry the sense of decay or failure, a near-synonym doubling.

Mandarin fǔbài, Cantonese fu6 baai6, Japanese ふはい (fuhai), and Korean 부패 (bupae) all cover both the literal sense (food/organic matter rotting) and the figurative one (moral or institutional corruption, 腐敗政治/부패정치, "corrupt politics") evenly, in ordinary everyday register. Vietnamese hủ bại is a real, attested Sino-Vietnamese compound, but has leaned more heavily toward the figurative, formal/literary sense — moral decline, corrupt customs (phong tục hủ bại) — reserved for journalism and literature rather than everyday speech about spoiled food.
