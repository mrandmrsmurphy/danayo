## Notes
- [臘](../characters/臘.md) "Laba" + [八 (char)](../characters/八%20(char).md) "eight" + [節 (char)](../characters/節%20(char).md) "holiday"

The Laba Festival falls on the 8th day of the 12th lunar month ([[臘八]]), and English Wikipedia describes two roots that fused over time.

The older is the year-end sacrifice: according to that article the "eight" referred to sacrifices to eight gods at the end of the year, and 臘 (la) originally referred to these sacrifices. The twelfth month is called 臘月; I did not verify when or why it was so named, and English Wiktionary's entry for the festival gives no etymology for 臘.

The Buddhist layer comes after Buddhism reached China: the festival came to commemorate the Buddha's enlightenment, and the date, the eighth day of the twelfth month, was fixed in the Northern and Southern dynasties (English Wikipedia). It is the day the Buddha is said to have reached enlightenment under the Bodhi tree. The festival is associated with [[臘八粥]] (*Laba zhōu*), a thick porridge of grains, legumes, nuts and dried fruits; the same article gives one legendary account in which the porridge began in the Song dynasty with Buddhist monasteries giving congee to people. The festival is the prelude to the Chinese New Year, about three weeks later, as the proverb 過了臘八就是年 ("after Laba, it's already the New Year") puts it, though I did not find that proverb in the sources I checked. Regional 臘八粥 recipes vary widely.

English Wikipedia says Laba corresponds directly to the Japanese Rōhatsu, whose observance is 成道会 (じょうどうえ, *jōdōe*); the page's Japanese field ろうはちせつ is the compound reading of the Chinese name, not the Japanese festival's own name. I did not check the Vietnamese observance.
