## Notes
- [朝 (char)](../characters/朝%20(char).md) "morning" + [鮮](../characters/鮮.md) "fresh" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" — [朝鮮](朝鮮.md) "Joseon; Korea" + [人](人.md) "person"

朝鮮人 is "a person of Joseon; a Korean," the historical sense that this word has in period texts such as the 1919 Korean Declaration of Independence ([Korea](../lookup/Korean/Korea.md)), where 朝鮮 is the name of the whole country and 朝鮮人 are its people. The word [朝鮮](朝鮮.md) changes meaning over time: today it points to North Korea, and 朝鮮人 then means a North Korean person; Dan'a'yo keeps both, and the sense depends on the period of the text. According to Wiktionary, Chinese 朝鮮人 (cháoxiǎnrén, Cantonese ciu4 sin1 jan4) is "(north) Korean (person)," with 韓國人 as the synonym; Japanese ちょうせんじん is "a Korean (person)" and, more specifically, "a North Korean (person)," first cited in 1635, and it carries a strongly derogatory tone when used of South Koreans out of context; Korean 조선인 is the North Korean usage. `vietnamese` người Triều Tiên is the ordinary Vietnamese phrase (from memory, not re-checked).
