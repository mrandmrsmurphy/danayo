## Notes
- [域 (char)](../characters/域%20(char).md) "realm, domain" + [階](../characters/階.md) "level, tier, storey" — literally "realm-level," the taxonomic rank of domain.

Mandarin yù and Cantonese wik6 are the real, standard bare-character terms used in taxonomy, matching 域's own stored citations exactly. Korean 역 is likewise the real bare term (역 > 계 > 문 > 강 > 목 > 과 > 속 > 종 is the standard Korean rank sequence), also matching 域's own citation. Japanese is the outlier: because "Domain" is a genuinely modern rank (proposed by Carl Woese in 1990, decades after 界–種 were already established), Japanese never coined a native Sino-Japanese term for it and instead borrows the English word directly as ドメイン (domein) — stored here rather than a fabricated on'yomi reading of 域, since ドメイン is the real word Japanese biologists actually use. Vietnamese vực is the real term, standard in Vietnamese biology curricula.

**Why append 階 at all, when none of the four source languages do**: Dan'a'yo appends 階 uniformly across all eight ranks of the taxonomic hierarchy ([[域階]], [[界階]], [[門階]], [[綱階]], [[目階]], [[科階]], [[属階]], [[種階]]) as a purely internal disambiguation device — natural languages rely on context alone to read 目 as "order" rather than "eye" inside a taxonomy chart, but a constructed language benefits from removing that ambiguity outright, especially since Dan'a'yo already independently uses bare [[目]] for "eye." In casual or space-constrained usage, the bare character (e.g. 目 alone) remains an understood abbreviation of the full 階-suffixed word, the same way scientific Latin abbreviates "Order" to "O." — but the full form is what a dictionary entry or first mention should use.

`kwin` is false: 域 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page, which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 階 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄨㄧㄎㄍ⼶ㄧ unique among both words and characters).
