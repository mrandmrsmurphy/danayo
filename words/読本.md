## Notes
- [読](../characters/読.md) "read" + [本 (char)](../characters/本%20(char).md) "root, foundation; book" — 本 here in its secondary "book, volume" sense (compare [[書本]] "book"), not its more literal "foundation" sense; literally "reading-book."

読本/讀本 names a reader — a book compiled specifically for reading practice or instruction, historically most associated with school primers and language-learning textbooks (the term is especially well-attested as a genre label in Japanese education, where 読本 series have long been a staple of literacy instruction).

Mandarin dúběn, Cantonese duk6 bun2, Korean 독본 (dokbon), and Japanese とくほん (tokuhon) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 読本/讀本/독본/とくほん is itself the real, standard word for "reader" (in the textbook sense) in all four. Vietnamese độc bản is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 読's đọc and 本's bản. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 독본 matches Sino-Korean 독본 exactly. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄉㄛㄎㄅㄛㄋ unique among both words and characters).
