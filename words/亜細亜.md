## Notes
- [亜](../characters/亜.md) "Asia" + [細 (char)](../characters/細%20(char).md) "fine, detailed" + 亜 (repeated) — a phonetic transliteration of the name "Asia" (亜細亜 reads roughly a-sei-a), not a compositional coinage; the characters were chosen for their sound rather than their meaning. Legitimizing note: 亜's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, so 亜 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word.

亜細亜 is the fuller, more classical/formal transliteration of "Asia," historically used in a broader cultural and human sense (Asian peoples, Asian civilization), as distinct from the shorter [[亜洲]] ("Asia" as a geographic landmass, technically an abbreviation of 亜細亜洲). Japanese preserves 亜細亜 mainly in historical, literary, and compound contexts (アジア主義, "Pan-Asianism"), while everyday Japanese uses the katakana loanword アジア (ajia) for "Asia" in ordinary speech — used here as the `japanese` field value, since アジア is overwhelmingly the real, everyday Japanese word, not 亜細亜's own on'yomi reading. Mandarin Yàxìyà, Cantonese aa3 sai3 aa3, and Korean 아세아 are all compositional transliterations, though modern everyday usage in both languages likewise favors the shorter 亚洲/亜洲/아주 forms. Vietnamese châu Á is the standard everyday term, not distinguishing the cultural-vs-geographic split this vault draws between 亜細亜 and 亜洲.
