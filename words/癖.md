## Notes
- [癖](../characters/癖%20(char).md) "idiosyncrasy, habit" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 癖 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

癖 names a fixed, often quirky personal habit or idiosyncrasy — not a vice exactly, but a peculiarity of behavior distinctive enough to be worth naming, from a harmless quirk to a compulsive fixation. It is highly productive as a suffix in all these languages, attaching to nouns to name a specific fixation or -mania: 潔癖 ("cleanliness obsession"), 収集癖 ("collecting habit"), 浪費癖 ("spendthrift habit").

Mandarin pǐ and Cantonese pik1 both use 癖 this way in ordinary speech. Japanese くせ (kuse, the native kun'yomi) is the everyday word for a personal habit or mannerism, while the on'yomi へき (heki) used here survives mainly in Sino-Japanese compounds like 潔癖 (keppeki). Korean 벽 (byeok) likewise appears chiefly in compounds rather than standing alone, with native 버릇 preferred for "habit" in casual speech — a pattern where the Sino-form supplies the compound-forming register and a native word covers everyday usage, paralleling 癒 and several other single-character stand-ins in this vault.
