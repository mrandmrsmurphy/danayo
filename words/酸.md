## Notes
- [酸](../characters/酸%20(char).md) "acidic, sour" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 酸 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo. Its 形声 etymology (酉, "wine vessel" + phonetic 夋) reflects the sharp, sour taste of fermenting wine. In chemistry, it also names an "acid" as a class of compound (酸性, "acidity, acidic").

Mandarin suān, Cantonese syun1, and Korean 산 (san) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese uses the native kun-reading すっぱい (suppai, "sour") rather than the on-reading サン (SAN) for the bare adjective sense, though SAN remains standard in the chemistry sense (酸性, さんせい). Vietnamese toan is the standard Sino-Vietnamese reading. This word is a genuine Dan'a'yo homophone of [[算]] ("to calculate") — coincidental, unrelated in meaning.
