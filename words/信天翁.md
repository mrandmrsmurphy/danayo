## Notes
- [信](../characters/信.md) "trust" + [天 (char)](../characters/天%20(char).md) "sky, heaven" + [翁 (char)](../characters/翁%20(char).md) "old man" — literally "one who trusts the sky, old man," a poetic Chinese name for the albatross, evoking the bird's effortless long-distance gliding. Mandarin xìntiānwēng and Cantonese seon3 tin1 jung1 use this literal compound; Korean 신천옹 parallels it as the Sino-Korean reading. Japanese instead uses the native あほうどり (ahōdori, literally "foolish bird," from the albatross's clumsy, easily-caught behavior on land) — a different word for the same animal, not built from 信天翁's own characters (corrected a typo in the stored reading, あはうどり → あほうどり). `vietnamese: hải âu` is the general everyday word for "seabird, albatross" (the more taxonomically precise "họ hải âu mày đen" names one specific species, black-browed albatross, rather than the bird generally). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 신턴옹 diverges from Sino-Korean 신천옹 in the second syllable. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄋㄊㄝㄋㄛㄫ unique among both words and characters).
