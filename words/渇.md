## Notes
- [渇 (char)](../characters/渇%20(char).md) "thirst" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the character 渇 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry. The character is also the Dan'a'yo abbreviation for tantalum in periodic-table formulae, via [[渇金]].

渇/갈/khát all stay remarkably consistent across the family as the plain, everyday word for physical thirst — khát nước ("thirsty for water") is exactly as ordinary in Vietnamese as 喉が渇く (nodo ga kawaku, "the throat gets thirsty") is in Japanese, with no register split the way some body-sensation words show. All four languages also extend the same root metaphorically to non-physical craving or yearning (渇望/갈망/khát vọng, "aspiration, yearning"), a semantic extension that runs in parallel rather than diverging.

English Wiktionary (at the traditional form 渴) explains the character as 水 plus the phonetic 曷, "dry, exhausted (of water)". It gives the Chinese senses "thirsty" (口渴, 渴望 "to thirst for, to long for") and, in Hakka, "tired", and an obsolete variant of 竭, "to exhaust". Its Japanese entry glosses it "thirsty; parched; yearn; pine", with the on readings かつ, かち, けつ, げち and かい and the kun かわ-く. The Food page lists the plain thirst sense.

Mandarin kě and Cantonese hot3 are as at English Wiktionary.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄎㄚㄊ (checked 2026-10-12); character pages may.
