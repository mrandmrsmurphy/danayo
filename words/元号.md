## Notes
- [元](../characters/元.md) "origin, first" + [号](../characters/号.md) "name, title" — literally "founding title," the name proclaimed at the start of an emperor's or era's reign (compare [[元年]], "the first year of an era," and [[改元]], "to change the era name").

元号/元號 names the era name — the title an East Asian monarch (or, in modern Japan, each reigning era) adopts to mark a period of rule, as in [[明治]], [[大正]], [[昭和]], etc. The near-synonym 年号/niên hiệu (using 年 "year" instead of 元) is the more common everyday term for the same system in Vietnamese historical writing.

Mandarin yuánhào, Cantonese jyun4 hou6, Korean 원호 (wonho), and Japanese げんごう (gengō) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday term for "era name" in its language. Vietnamese nguyên hiệu is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 元's nguyên and 号's own hiệu (as opposed to hào, its other reading, used in unrelated senses like ordinal numbering). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 원핫 diverges from Sino-Korean 원호 in the second syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ⼔ㄋㄏㄚㄨ unique among both words and characters).
