## Notes
- [九 (char)](../characters/九%20(char).md) "nine" + [十 (char)](../characters/十%20(char).md) "ten" — ninety, following the regular multiplicative numeral pattern (N × 十).

九十 is the standard formal/compositional numeral for "ninety" across the sphere: Mandarin jiǔshí, Cantonese gau2 sap6, Japanese きゅうじゅう, and Korean 구십 are all fully compositional and used interchangeably with native-numeral alternatives in each language (Japanese also has きゅうじゅう alongside no distinct native form at this magnitude; Korean 아흔 is the native alternative). Vietnamese cửu thập is the classical/formal Sino-Vietnamese numeral, distinct from the everyday native chín mươi ("nine tens") used in ordinary counting.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 큐십 diverges from Sino-Korean 구십, since 九's own Dan'a'yo reading 큐 doesn't match Sino-Korean 구, even though 十's own reading matches exactly (十 is independently `kwin: true`).
