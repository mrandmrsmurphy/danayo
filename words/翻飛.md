## Notes
- [翻](../characters/翻%20(char).md) "flip, turn over" + [飛](../characters/飛%20(char).md) "fly" — "flip-fly," to fly with a turning, beating motion. Their own `stand_in` values: 翻's is itself; 飛's is itself; none points here, so 翻飛 is an independent compound.

翻飛 is to flutter: to fly in a turning, beating motion, as a bird hovers over its nest or a butterfly drifts. It is a neologism for Dan'a'yo's purposes. It is a real Chinese word (Mandarin fānfēi, Cantonese faan1 fei1), but it could not be confirmed in a second language: Japanese and Korean have no entry for it in the dictionaries checked, so those two fields give the readings of the two characters. It was coined for the Hebrew *meraḥepet* (מְרַחֶפֶת) in Genesis 1:2, the Spirit "hovering" over the waters like a bird over its young (compare Deuteronomy 32:11). Compare [[飛]] (to fly), [[飛翔]] (to soar) and [[揺]] (to shake), which loses the bird.
