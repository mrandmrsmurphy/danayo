## Notes
- **Genuine `#cranberry`**: both [蝙](../characters/蝙.md) and [蝠](../characters/蝠.md) — each carries the "bat" sense in its own right, even though 蝙 is never used solo in running text — have their own `stand_in` pointing to this exact compound (full transitivity; tag already present on both character pages).

蝙蝠 is the bat, the standard word. [[天鼠]] ("heaven's rat") is a poetic or dialectal synonym with exactly the same sense, and the two are interchangeable. Neither is the flying squirrel, which is [[飛鼠]], a rodent that glides on a membrane between its legs and is unrelated to the bat.

Mandarin has two readings, biānfú and biǎnfú, of which biānfú is kept. Japanese こうもり is the standard native word for "bat", matching both constituents' own native readings. Korean 편복 is the Sino-Korean compositional reading; the native 박쥐 is the everyday term, mentioned here in prose rather than in the field. Vietnamese biên bức is compositional.
