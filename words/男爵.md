## Notes
- [男](../characters/男.md) "male; man" + [爵](../characters/爵.md) "baron; noble rank"
- This word is the viable form of [爵](../characters/爵.md): the character's meaning (noble rank, baron) is identical, but 爵 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 男 ("male") specifies the gendered form of the title, making this the standard term for a baron — the parallel 女爵 "baroness" would follow the same pattern (女 "female" + 爵) but is not yet created as its own word in this vault.

男爵 is a real historical/modern title across the Sinosphere: the lowest rank of the Five Ranks of Nobility (五等爵) in classical Chinese and Japanese peerage systems, and still used today as the standard translation for European baronial titles. Mandarin nánjué, Cantonese naam4 zoek3, Japanese だんしゃく, Korean 남작, and Vietnamese nam tước are all standard, fully compositional readings across the CJKV sphere.
