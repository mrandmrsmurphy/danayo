## Notes
- [処 (char)](../characters/処%20(char).md) "place; to dwell" + [女 (char)](../characters/女%20(char).md) "woman"

処女 means "virgin, maiden" — an unmarried woman, by extension the constellation/zodiac sign Virgo (処女座), and attributively "maiden, inaugural" (処女作, "a debut work"; 処女航海, "maiden voyage"). The compound is built here on the canonical character 処 — itself an alias-cluster covering 処/处/處, all variants of the same morpheme meaning "place, to reside, to be situated" — with the more common real-world spellings 处女 (simplified) and 處女 (traditional) recorded as word-level aliases, following the same alias-parent pattern used for [[坐位]].

女, a 象形 pictograph of a kneeling figure with folded arms (detailed in [[女 (char)]]'s own Notes), is one of the most productive characters in the script, anchoring [[魔女]] ("witch"), [[女優]] ("actress"), and the [[妓女]]/[[舞妓]] pair. 処女's literal sense — "a woman who stays [at home], unmarried" — reflects the traditional Chinese explanation of the word, in which an unmarried woman is one who remains in the parental household (処 as "to remain, to stay").

The Reproduction page lists it as "a virgin, maiden," also "Virgo," and that is the whole range Dan'a'yo keeps. The readings are Mandarin chǔnǚ, Cantonese cyu2 neoi5, Japanese shojo (しょじょ), Korean 처녀 (where the word means "unmarried young woman," with 총각 as the male counterpart) and Sino-Vietnamese xử nữ. In Japanese and Chinese the word has a strong physical sense of virginity, while the Korean 처녀 is the ordinary word for a young unmarried woman.
