## Notes
- [方](../characters/方.md) "side; manner, way" + [式](../characters/式.md) "style; formula"
- This word is a viable form of [式](../characters/式.md) alongside [様式](様式.md): 式 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word, and 方式 is one of the compounds its page lists (the other being its `stand_in`, 様式).

方式 is "a way of doing something," the fixed manner or system by which an action is carried out: 生活方式 "way of life," 思維方式 "way of thinking," 生産方式 "mode of production," 支払い方式 "payment system." The 方 is the "way, method" sense of 方 (as in 方法), and 式 supplies the idea of an established pattern or formula, so the compound names a method that has settled into a standard form.

It is closest to [方法](方法.md) "method," but the two are not interchangeable. 方法 is the particular means used to get a result (a method for solving a problem, a way to open a jar), whereas 方式 is the overall scheme or mode in which a thing is regularly done, so it is the natural word for systems, protocols, and modes (the interview 方式 of a survey, a payment 方式). It also differs from [様式](様式.md) "style, type": 様式 describes the form a finished thing takes (a period or school of art, a formal style), and 方式 describes the manner of proceeding.

According to Wiktionary (checked 2026-10-11 through the parse API), Chinese 方式 (fāngshì, Cantonese fong1 sik1) is a noun glossed "way; means; fashion; mode; style; pattern; manner; method," with synonym 方法 and derived terms 生活方式, 生產方式, 表達方式 and 聯繫方式. Japanese 方式 (ほうしき) is a noun "method; system," and the Japanese Wiktionary defines it as a fixed form or procedure; its example is an interview-method survey. Korean 방식 is attested as the hanja form on the English, Chinese and Korean Wiktionaries (the Korean entry 방식 itself is only an empty draft stub there). Vietnamese does not use 方式 as a separate word: the Vietnamese Wiktionary has no 方式 page, but its phương thức entry gives 方式 as the Sino-Vietnamese source ("manner and method, in general"), and the English Wiktionary treats 方式 in Vietnamese as the Han form of phương thức. The `vietnamese` field is thus the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading phương + thức (式 is stored as sức and thức in the character page).

Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "way, mode, manner; method, system." No homophones (注音 ㄈㄚㄫㄙㄧㄎ unique across words and characters).
