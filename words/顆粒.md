## Notes
- [顆](../characters/顆.md) "granule" + [粒](../characters/粒.md) "particle, grain"
- This word is the stand-in for [顆](../characters/顆.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

顆 is a small round object (a pearl, a seed, a tooth) and is also the measure word for such things in Chinese (一顆珠 "a pearl"), and 粒 is a grain or a single particle, as of rice, also used as a counter for grains. The compound 顆粒 is therefore the small solid piece as a thing: a granule, a pellet, a single grain. It is the word for the form that powders and foods take when formed into small bits, as in 顆粒狀 "granular," 顆粒劑 "granules (a drug form)," and the granulated products of cooking, farming and industry (sugar, fertilizer, instant coffee).

It is related to [粒子](粒子.md) "particle," the physicist's word, in that 粒 supplies the "small bit" idea, but 顆粒 names a tangible granule, not an abstract particle. In Chinese the modern spelling is 颗粒 (the alias), and in Japanese かりゅう and Korean 과립 the word is a technical term, especially in medicine (granules) and materials. The readings are Mandarin kēlì, Cantonese fo2 nap1, Japanese karyū (かりゅう) and Korean 과립. The Food page lists it among the words for food forms. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "granule, pellet, grain."
