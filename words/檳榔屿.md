## Notes
- [檳](../characters/檳.md) "betel nut" + [榔](../characters/榔.md) "betel palm" + [屿](../characters/屿.md) "islet" — "betel-nut islet", that is [[檳榔]] (the stand-in for both 檳 and 榔) plus 屿, whose own stand-in is [[島屿]]. The alias 檳榔嶼 is the traditional spelling, since 嶼 is an alias of 屿.

檳榔屿 is a proper noun with two referents, both islands named for the betel palm.

1. Penang, the island of the Malaysian state of Penang, in the Strait of Malacca. English Wiktionary dates the Chinese name to the Mao Kun map in volume 240 of the *Wubei zhi* (1621), and quotes Karl Gützlaff's *Maoyi tongzhi* (c. 1840), as quoted in Wei Yuan's *Haiguo tuzhi* (1849), which lists "Malacca, Penang and other places" among the places held by the English company. The Malay name Pulau Pinang itself means "betel-nut island", so the Chinese name is a translation, not a sound-for-sound transliteration. English Wiktionary gives 檳島 as a synonym and the Hokkien reading Pin-nâng-sū.
2. Binlang Islet, a small islet in Lieyu Township, [[金門]] (Kinmen) County, Taiwan, listed at English Wiktionary as the second sense of the same spelling (I did not check the islet's own article).

Mandarin Bīnlángyǔ (read with bīn, not bīng as in the common noun 檳榔) and Cantonese ban1 long4 zeoi6 are as at English Wiktionary. Japanese ペナン and Korean 페낭 are the ordinary transliterations of the Malay name, as with [[旧金山]], so `kwin` is false. The `vietnamese` field holds the compositional Hán-Việt reading tân lang dữ (each character as its page gives it; not attested for this name).

No homophones (注音 ㄅㄧㄋㄌㄚㄫㄙ⼄ unique across words and characters, checked 2026-10-12). The 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot.
