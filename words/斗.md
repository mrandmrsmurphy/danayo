## Notes
- [斗 (char)](../characters/斗%20(char).md) "peck; Dipper" — stand-in for [[斗 (char)|斗]], which cannot appear independently

斗 is a 象形 pictograph of a ladle-shaped dipper or measuring cup for grain, held by a hand — the same vessel-shape that gave the Big Dipper constellation its Chinese name, 北斗 ("northern dipper"), a naming logic parallel to the Western asterism's own dipper/ladle association despite arising independently. As a unit, 斗 named a dry-measure volume (roughly a decaliter historically, though exact values shifted across dynasties) standing above 升 ("liter-scale unit") and below 石 in the traditional Chinese volumetric hierarchy — this vault already documents the related [[熨斗]] "flatiron," whose own name literally repurposes this same ladle/scoop shape for the ancient hot-coal iron.

Vietnamese preserves an unusually crowded four-reading set on the character itself (tẩu, điếu, đấu, đẩu) — đẩu is the one independently attested for this measure/constellation sense, confirmed via the compound 熨斗 (uất đẩu). Japanese keeps two on'yomi, と (TO, the common reading — 北斗 Hokuto, 一斗 itto "one peck") and とう (TOU, rarer, confined to a smaller set of technical/classical compounds) — but no native 訓読み exists at all (`japanese_native: ø`), leaving 斗 entirely within the borrowed Sino-Japanese layer despite naming an everyday-enough historical measure. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 돗 (dou, from Middle Chinese 端母 t + 侯韻 əu) diverges from Sino-Korean 두 in the coda, Dan'a'yo retaining a final consonant Korean has dropped. 斗 shares its Dan'a'yo reading with two other standalone words, [[投]] ("throw") and [[豆]] ("bean") — a three-way coincidental convergence, unrelated etymologically to any of them.
