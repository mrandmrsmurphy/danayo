## Notes
- [新 (char)](../characters/新%20(char).md) "new" + [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "gold" + [山 (char)](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain" — "New Gold Mountain"; no stand-in relationship applies (新's own stand-in is the single-character word [[新]]; 金 and 山 are their own stand-ins).

新金山 is a dated Chinese name for Melbourne, the capital of Victoria in Australia. English Wiktionary gives the etymology: the name refers to the gold rush around Melbourne and contrasts with 舊金山 "Old Gold Mountain", San Francisco ([[旧金山]]), 金山 being used loosely for any place of great chances of wealth, like the English "gold mine" (see [[金山]]). The same entry gives a second dated sense, "Australia", marked as Taishanese; the vault's word for the country is [[豪洲]], so this sense is a historical remark, not a competing word.

Mandarin Xīnjīnshān and Cantonese san1 gam1 saan1 are as at English Wiktionary. The `vietnamese` field holds the compositional reading Tân Kim Sơn (each character as its page gives it; not attested for this name). Japanese メルボルン and Korean 멜버른 are the ordinary transliterations of the English name, as with 旧金山, so `kwin` is false.

The modern Chinese name of Melbourne is 墨爾本, a phonetic spelling that is not a word in the vault; 新金山 is the translated name.

No homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄋㄍㄧㄇㄙㄚㄋ unique across words and characters, checked 2026-10-12). The 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot.
