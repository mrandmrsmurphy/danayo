## Notes
- [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) "great" + [王 (char)](../characters/王%20(char).md) "king" — an honorific title appended to the names of especially revered monarchs, as in 世宗[[大王]] (King Sejong the Great) or 廣開土大王 (King Gwanggaeto the Great). Also used historically in Japan for early sovereigns before the title 天皇 was adopted (大王/オオキミ), and in Vietnamese folk religion for deified kings and generals (đại vương). Attested across Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese (だいおう), Korean (대왕), and Vietnamese (đại vương).
The Government page lists it as "a great king," and Dan'a'yo keeps that sense, an honorific for especially revered monarchs, with the Japanese 大王 (ōkimi) as the pre-天皇 title for early sovereigns and the Vietnamese deified-king usage as regional specializations. It is a step up from the plain [王](王.md) "king" and [国王](国王.md) "king of a country," and a different register from [帝王](帝王.md) "emperor, sovereign." The Korean 대왕 is the same as the Dan'a'yo 諺文 (대왕), so `kwin` is true. The readings are Mandarin dàwáng, Cantonese daai6 wong4, Japanese daiō (だいおう), Korean 대왕 and Sino-Vietnamese đại vương. The examples above (世宗大王, 廣開土大王) are the page's existing account; they match what I know but have not been re-checked here.
