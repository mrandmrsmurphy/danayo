## Notes
- [便 (char)](../characters/便%20(char).md) "cheap"

便 is 会意: [[人]] ("person") + [[更]] ("to change") — a person changing their posture to make themselves comfortable, hence "convenient." The character has since split into two distinct reading clusters across the daughter languages: the "convenient, plain, cheap" cluster this word belongs to (方便, "convenient"; 便宜, "cheap," the price sense; also "plain, ordinary," as in 便服, "everyday clothes"), and a separate "excrement, to relieve oneself" sense (大便, "feces"; 便意, "the urge to go") that this word does not cover.

Mandarin biàn, Cantonese bin6, and Japanese べん/びん (ben/bin, on'yomi, spanning both senses in Japanese) all carry the reading. Korean actually splits the two senses into two distinct Sino-Korean readings — 편 (pyeon) for "convenient, cheap" (the sense this word covers) and 변 (byeon) for "excrement." **Content removed/corrected**: this word's own `korean` field previously stored 변, which is a coincidental resemblance to the Dan'a'yo syllable's own 변 spelling rather than the real Korean loanword for this sense — corrected to 편. Vietnamese shows the same two-way split: tiện for "convenient" (方便, phương tiện) and biền for specifically "cheap" (便宜 read as biền nghi, "cheap price") — this word's "cheap" sense uses biền; tiện, previously listed alongside it, belongs to the other sense and was removed.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (byen/변/ㄅ⼶ㄋ) with another word still awaiting its own turn in this sweep, [[変]] ("change") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed when it comes up.
