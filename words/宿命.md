## Notes
- [宿](../characters/宿.md) "lodging; to stay overnight" + [命](../characters/命.md) "life; fate" — a fate one has "lodged in" from a previous life, carried forward into this one.

宿命 originates as a Buddhist technical term: 宿 here carries its extended sense of "former, prior" (as in 宿世, "previous existence"), so 宿命 names a destiny fixed by karma accumulated in past lives, distinct from more neutral fate-words like [[運命]] (命's own stand-in, "fortune, destiny" more broadly). This origin survives directly in Japanese 宿命論 (shukumei-ron, "fatalism") and the Buddhist doctrine 宿命通 ("knowledge of past lives," one of the six supernatural powers), showing the term's religious register more plainly than the modern secular Mandarin/Cantonese usage, which has broadened to mean simply "destiny" or "the inescapable," often with a fatalistic or tragic connotation (as in 宿命的 "fated, doomed").

Korean 숙명 and Vietnamese túc mệnh (Wiktionary-confirmed) both preserve the same weighty, often tragic register as the Chinese-language usage, rather than the more neutral secular sense. `kwin` is `false`, not `true` as the frontmatter previously (incorrectly) stated — the AND-rule requires every constituent's own `kwin` to be `true`, and 宿's is `false` even though 命's is `true`. No HSK level is confirmed for the compound; no homophones (注音 ㄙㄨㄎㄇ⼶ㄫ unique across both words and characters).
