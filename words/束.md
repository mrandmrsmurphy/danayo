## Notes
- [束 (char)](../characters/束%20(char).md) "bundle of" — stand-in for [束 (char)](../characters/束%20(char).md), which is too bound to appear independently; this word gives it a usable form.

束 is the Dan'a'yo classifier for bundled or bound objects — 量詞, counting flowers, vegetables, straw, firewood, papers, and any collection of discrete objects tied or gathered together. Following the grammar's rule (NP + numeral + classifier; see [文法 - 04句法](../grammar/文法%20-%2004句法.md)), the noun comes first and the numeral plus 束 follow it without a space: 花一束 (one bunch of flowers), 稻草三束 (three sheaves of straw), 蔬菜兩束 (two bundles of vegetables), 光一束 (one beam of light). These example phrases are illustrative, not drawn from a corpus. In the grammar's classifier table 束 is the class for "bundles: groups, bouquets, bales." The character is classified 指事 on its own page (following Wiktionary: a bag tied at both ends, earlier forms often vertically symmetric), so the visual structure of a tied bundle directly encodes what it classifies. From the concrete sense of "a bound bundle," 束 extended to the verb "to bind; to constrain" (拘束, 結束, 束縛, 約束) and to abstract restraint, but Dan'a'yo 束 as a classifier preserves the original material sense.

Senses, following Wiktionary's Chinese entry for 束:
1. Classifier for bundles, bunches, bouquets and beams (the sense of this word).
2. Bundle, bunch, bale (as a noun); in Mandarin also a geometric "pencil" (a family of objects sharing a property), in compounds such as 光束 (light beam).
3. To bind, to tie up; in compounds, to control or restrain (拘束, 束縛), and "end" (結束 in Mandarin). In Dan'a'yo these verbal senses live in the compounds [[束縛]], [[拘束]], [[結束]] and [[約束]], not in the bare 束.
4. Surname (Mandarin); not used as a Dan'a'yo word.

The CJKV classifier landscape for bundles shows moderate convergence. Mandarin 束 (shù) is listed on Wiktionary as a classifier for objects in bunches or bundles, such as bouquets of flowers: 一束花 (one bunch of flowers), 一束光 (one beam of light). From memory, not re-checked: 一束头发 (one lock of hair) is also said, and 捆 (kǔn) covers tightly bound bundles of firewood and rope. Cantonese 束 (cuk1) is likewise a bunch-classifier; from memory, not re-checked, 紮 (zaak3) is the colloquial classifier for tightly bound bundles, as in 一紮蔥 (one bundle of spring onions). Japanese has 束 as a counter in two readings per Wiktionary: the native たば (taba, "counter for bundles," as in 一束 hitotaba) and the Sino-Japanese そく (soku, a counter for large bundles); the Sino-Xenic そく recorded in this page's `japanese` field is the latter, and the native たば (also noun "bundle, bunch, sheaf") is the everyday word. Korean 속 (束) appears in Sino-Korean compounds such as 약속 (約束, promise); from memory, not re-checked, 묶음 (mukkeum, "a tied-together set") and 다발 (dabal, "bunch, bouquet") are the native Korean equivalents. Vietnamese bó is the native bundle word (một bó hoa, one bunch of flowers; một bó củi, one bundle of firewood; from memory, not re-checked); Sino-Vietnamese thúc (束) is the attested Hán-Việt reading, appearing in compounds such as kết thúc (結束, to conclude) and câu thúc (拘束, to restrain; both from memory, not re-checked).

束 shares its Dan'a'yo syllable ㄙ⼄ㄎ with the character [続](../characters/続.md); no other word shares it. 束 is among the twelve classifiers of the grammar's table and is listed on [Measurement](../lexipedia/Measurement.md) under "Bound Sets & Pairs" beside 冊 and 双, as the collective classifier. Its Dan'a'yo sense is the same as the shared CJKV sense.
