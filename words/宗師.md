## Notes
- [宗](../characters/宗.md) "religion; ancestor; venerable founder" + [師](../characters/師.md) "expert; master"

宗師 names a grandmaster or patriarch — a founding or supremely accomplished master whose authority a school, sect, or tradition traces itself back to, combining 宗's sense of "ancestral founder/venerated source" with 師's sense of "master, teacher." It is honorific and somewhat literary, reserved for a figure of towering, often founder-level stature within a martial, religious, or scholarly lineage, rather than an ordinary teacher.

The compound has no independently attested Sino-Vietnamese reading (no dictionary entry for 宗師 as a fixed unit), so the concept is expressed periphrastically in Vietnamese rather than through a direct loanword. In Japanese そうし is a learned, mostly written-register term, and Sino-Korean 종사 carries the same honorific weight, most often encountered today in the context of traditional Korean martial arts and religious lineages.
