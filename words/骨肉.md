## Notes
- [骨 (char)](../characters/骨%20(char).md) "bone" + [肉 (char)](../characters/肉%20(char).md) "flesh" — no `#cranberry`: both 骨's and 肉's own `stand_in` point to themselves; neither points here.

Mandarin gǔròu, Cantonese gwat1juk6, Japanese こつにく, Korean 골육, and Sino-Vietnamese cốt nhục are all standard, fully compositional readings — used figuratively for blood relations/kinship (as in 骨肉相連, "bone of my bone and flesh of my flesh," and 骨肉相殘/cốt nhục tương tàn, "flesh and blood fighting each other"), directly paralleling the Biblical idiom already noted on [[骨]].
