## Notes
- [正 (char)](../characters/正%20(char).md) "correct" + [体](../characters/体.md) "form; body" + [字 (char)](../characters/字%20(char).md) "character" — "characters in their correct form," built on [[正体]] plus 字.

正体字 is a character written in its standard form, the shape that conforms to an official or historical orthography and not one of its variants ([[異体]]). Wiktionary lists three Chinese senses: chiefly in Taiwan, the traditional Chinese characters; a character, traditional or simplified, that conforms to an official or historical standard, as the opposite of variant characters, with 正字 as a synonym; and, in typography, characters set in their regular form instead of bold or italic. Japanese せいたいじ is the regular or traditional form of Chinese characters in a Chinese-language context.

Dan'a'yo takes the second sense. A 正体字 is a character in the form the vault treats as standard, close to [[正字]] "correct character" but naming the form and not the judgment. Because the vault's standard set is the [[新字体]] forms, 正体字 does not mean [[繁体字]] here, though it does in Taiwan; the traditional forms are the [[旧字体]] or 繁体字, and the simplified mainland forms are [[簡体字]]. Mandarin zhèngtǐzì, Cantonese zing3 tai2 zi6 and Japanese せいたいじ are listed on Wiktionary; Korean 정체자 and Vietnamese chính thể tự are the compositional readings and were not checked against a dictionary.
