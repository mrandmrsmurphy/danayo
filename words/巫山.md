## Notes
- [巫](../characters/巫.md) "shaman; witch; sorceress" + [山 (char)](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain" — no stand-in relationship applies (巫's own `stand_in` is [[巫女]], 山's is itself).

巫山 (Wushan) is a mountain range on the Yangtze River, at the border of modern Chongqing and Hubei, where the river cuts through to form Wu Gorge (巫峡), one of the Three Gorges — traditionally described as the "Twelve Peaks of Wushan." The place-name origin of 巫, "shaman," reflects the mountain's deep association with legend and myth in classical Chinese poetry (most famously, the Chu ci tradition's tale of the Goddess of Wushan).

Japanese ふざん (with rendaku voicing, san → zan), Korean 무산, and Vietnamese Vu Sơn are all standard readings for this place name. No homophones (注音 ㄇㄨㄙㄚㄋ unique across both words and characters).
