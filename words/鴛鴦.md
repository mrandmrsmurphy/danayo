## Notes
- [鴛](../characters/鴛.md) "male mandarin duck" + [鴦](../characters/鴦.md) "female mandarin duck" — **`#cranberry`**: both 鴛's and 鴦's own `stand_in` point to [[鴛鴦]] itself, and both characters already independently mean "mandarin duck" on their own — genuine transitivity (A=B=AB), a "dead paired-gender" compound listed on [[lookup/List of 連綿詞]].

Mandarin yuānyāng, Cantonese jyun1joeng1, Korean 원앙 (also the everyday Korean word), and Sino-Vietnamese uyên ương are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese をし (an older/classical kana spelling of おし, as in おしどり oshidori) reflects 鴛's own native gloss.
