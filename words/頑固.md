## Notes
- [頑](../characters/頑.md) "stubborn" + [固](../characters/固.md) "solid, strong"
- [[頑]] is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo; 頑固 is its legitimizing compound (the `stand_in` for 頑).

頑固 describes someone (or something) that will not yield: stubborn, obstinate, set in one's ways. The two characters reinforce each other, 頑 "obdurate, unyielding" and 固 "firm, hard", so the compound reads as hardness of will. It is usually mildly or openly critical when applied to a person, though Japanese 頑固 (がんこ) can be said with some admiration of a craftsman or elder who holds to his standards (頑固親父, the stubborn old father of the stock character).

Mandarin 顽固 additionally carries a political sense of die-hard resistance to change (顽固派, the diehards), and the word also describes things that resist removal, such as a stubborn stain or a persistent illness. In Dan'a'yo it keeps the shared CJKV sense of stubborn and obstinate, covering both the personal-temperament and the resistant-thing uses, with no divergence.
