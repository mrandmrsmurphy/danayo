## Notes
- [三 (char)](../characters/三%20(char).md) "three" + [界](../characters/界.md) "realm; world" — a translation of Sanskrit *trailokya* (*triloka*), "the three worlds."

三界 are the three realms of Buddhist cosmology: the realm of desire ([欲界](欲界.md)), the realm of form ([色界](色界.md)) and the formless realm ([無色界](無色界.md)). Beings caught in the cycle of birth and death are reborn within them, which is why they are also called 三有, the "three existences". The realms are ranked: the desire realm lies lowest and the formless realm highest, each a finer way of existing than the one below. Wiktionary and the Chinese and Japanese encyclopaedias agree on the Buddhist sense. In Chinese the Pure Land teachers add that Amitābha's land lies outside the three realms. Daoism borrowed the idea as 三界二十八天, the three realms with their twenty-eight heavens. The same Sanskrit word is also rendered 三世, the three times, past, present and future. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Japanese readings are the attested ones; the Korean follows the North Korean readings of the characters, and the Vietnamese is the standard Sino-Vietnamese.

Compare [世界](世界.md), "the world", and [欲望](欲望.md), "desire", the quality of the lowest realm.
