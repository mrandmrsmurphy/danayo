## Notes
- [嵌](../characters/嵌.md) "inlay" + [入 (char)](../characters/入%20(char).md) "enter" — this word is the viable form of [嵌](../characters/嵌.md): the character's meaning (to inlay; to embed) is identical, but 嵌 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 嵌入 is its `stand_in` compound.

嵌入 means to embed or inlay: to set one thing into another so that it sits fixed within it. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 嵌入 (Mandarin qiànrù, Cantonese ham3 jap6, also ham6 jap6) as "to embed; to inlay; to implant; to let into", and Japanese かんにゅう as "inlaying" and "to inlay". It has no Korean or Vietnamese entry, so Korean 감입 here is built from the characters, and the Vietnamese field is left blank.

The two characters are 嵌, to inlay, and the 入 of entering, "set in".
