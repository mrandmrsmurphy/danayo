## Notes
- [朱](../characters/朱.md) "cinnabar, vermilion" + [錫](../characters/錫.md) "tin" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 錫 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry (bare 錫 cannot stand alone).

Mandarin xī and Cantonese sek3 are the real everyday words for "tin" in those languages, using bare 錫 alone rather than the two-character compound — matching 錫's own stored citations exactly, since Dan'a'yo prefixes 朱 specifically to give the character a legitimizing compound rather than reflecting a real cross-linguistic compositional reading. Japanese すず (suzu, native kun'yomi) and Vietnamese thiếc are both real, independently attested native words for tin in their languages, unrelated in form to either 錫 alone or the 朱錫 compound. Korean 주석 is the exception: it genuinely is compositional (朱's own 주 + 錫's own 석), matching Dan'a'yo's own derivation exactly, which is why `kwin` is true here despite the word not being a real compositional term in most of the other source languages.

`注音 ㄐㄨㄙㄝㄎ`/`주석` is a real, reciprocally documented homophone with [[主席]] ("chairman, chairperson") — coincidental, unrelated in meaning. See [Metals](../lexipedia/Metals.md) and [Periodic Table](../lexipedia/Periodic%20Table.md).
