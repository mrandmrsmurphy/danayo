## Notes
- [今 (char)](characters/今%20(char).md) "now" + [朝 (char)](characters/朝%20(char).md) "morning" + [安](characters/安.md) "peace, safety" — literally "now-morning-peace." A Dan'a'yo-original greeting, part of a family built on the same 今+[time of day]+安 pattern alongside [[今昼安]] ("good afternoon") and [[今夜安]] ("good evening") — see also [[今朝]] ("this morning").

**Content corrected**: `mandarin` was `zǎochen` — the bare noun "morning," not itself a greeting. Corrected to `zǎoshang hǎo` (matching the alias already stored), the standard everyday Mandarin "good morning" greeting; `zǎo'ān` is a fully equivalent alternative, more common in Taiwan. `cantonese` was entirely blank; filled with `zou2 san4` — the same characters as the bare noun "morning," but genuinely used as the everyday Cantonese greeting itself, a real cross-dialect divergence from Mandarin rather than an error.

Since this is an invented Dan'a'yo greeting rather than a natural cross-linguistic loanword, the `mandarin`/`cantonese`/`japanese`/`korean`/`vietnamese` fields each hold that language's own real "good morning"-equivalent greeting, not a compositional reading of 今朝安's own three characters. Japanese おはようございます/おはよう (formal/casual) and Vietnamese chào buổi sáng are both standard morning greetings. Korean 안녕하세요 is the standard general-purpose Korean greeting used at any time of day — Korean has no separate "good morning"-specific greeting the way English or Vietnamese do, so the same phrase correctly appears here and on the [[今夜安]] ("good evening") sibling entry.
