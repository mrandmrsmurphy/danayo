## Notes
- [河](../characters/河.md) "stream, river" + [南](../characters/南.md) "south" — no stand-in relationship applies (河's own is [[小河]]; 南's own is [[南方]]).

This name is a collision between a Chinese province and a Vietnamese one, so the Chinese sense comes first.

1. Henan, the province of central China whose capital is Zhengzhou, "south of the (Yellow) River"; the historical 河南 was also the region south of the lower Yellow River (Henan Commandery, Henan Prefecture and the Tang Henan Circuit are listed at English Wiktionary) and the area south of the upper bend of the river (the Ordos and Hetao).
2. Hà Nam, a province of northern Vietnam south of Hanoi (see [[河内]], whose name 河内 "inside the river" belongs to the same river-name pattern, Hà Nam being literally "south of the river"). English and Chinese Wiktionary both record it as a province of Vietnam; it was merged into Ninh Bình in the 2025 reorganization (from memory, not re-checked).

Other places also called 河南 include a county in Qinghai, a town (かなん, Kanan) in Osaka prefecture, and a city (하남, Hanam) in Gyeonggi province, South Korea, all listed at Chinese or English Wiktionary. The Japanese かなん and the Korean 하남 are the established readings of the Chinese province and match the character readings exactly.

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 河南 as a Chinese proper noun, with Japanese かなん and Korean entries and a Vietnamese Han-form entry; Chinese Wiktionary lists the province, the historical regions, the Vietnamese province and the Korean, Japanese and Qinghai places; Vietnamese Wiktionary has an entry that glosses it as the Chinese province. Japanese and Korean Wiktionaries have no entry. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄚㄋㄚㄇ unique across both words and characters).
