## Notes
- [七 (char)](../characters/七%20(char).md) "seven" + [情](../characters/情.md) "feeling, emotion" — a foundational enumeration in East Asian philosophy/medicine of the basic human emotional states, with a real and interesting divergence in exactly which seven are counted depending on tradition:
- ([儒教](words/儒教.md)) [喜](words/喜.md)、[怒](words/怒.md)、[哀傷](words/哀傷.md)、[恐惧](words/恐惧.md)、[愛](words/愛.md)、[悪](words/悪.md)、[欲望](words/欲望.md)。
- ([佛教](words/佛教.md)) 喜、怒、[憂](characters/憂.md)、[懼](characters/惧.md)、愛、[憎](characters/憎.md)、欲。
- ([[漢方]][[医学]]) 喜、怒、憂、[思](characters/思.md)、[悲](words/悲.md)、[恐](characters/恐.md)、[驚](characters/驚.md)。

These three traditions genuinely differ, not just in translation but in content: Confucianism's set matches the version most widely cited in Korean Neo-Confucian philosophy too — 七情 (칠정) is a central term in the famous Four-Seven Debate (四端七情論, 사단칠정론) between Yi Hwang and Gi Daeseung, one of the most significant intellectual controversies in Korean philosophical history. Buddhism's set swaps in 憂 ("worry, distress") for 哀傷, and Traditional Chinese Medicine's set differs most from the other two, adding 思 ("pensiveness, overthinking") as its own distinct pathological emotion — TCM treats each of the seven as capable of directly injuring specific internal organs when experienced in excess, a diagnostic framework with no real equivalent in the Confucian or Buddhist enumerations.

Vietnamese thất tình (confirmed via web search) is best known today paired with 六欲 in the set phrase thất tình lục dục ("the seven emotions and six desires"), a common Buddhist-inflected idiom for the full range of worldly feeling and craving to be mastered or transcended — the Vietnamese Buddhist enumeration of the seven (hỷ, nộ, ai, cụ, ái, ố, dục) matches this page's own Buddhist-tradition list exactly. `kwin` is false: both syllables diverge from their Sino-Korean counterparts (七's Dan'a'yo 칟 vs. Korean 칠; 情's Dan'a'yo 칭 vs. Korean 정), the same coda/vowel-divergence pattern already documented repeatedly this stretch of the sweep. No homophones (注音 ㄑㄧㄊㄑㄧㄫ unique among both words and characters).
