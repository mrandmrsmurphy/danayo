## Notes
- [運](../characters/運.md) "move; transport" + [動 (char)](../characters/動%20(char).md) "move"

**運動** spans two clusters of meaning united by the idea of directed motion. In its physical sense it means exercise, sport, or athletic activity — the movement of the body. In its political or social sense it means a movement or campaign — coordinated collective action toward a goal. The two readings coexist in all five CJKV languages, though the balance differs: in Mandarin, 运动 is strongly associated with political campaigns (especially in the language of the Communist period), while in Japanese うんどう more readily suggests physical exercise or electioneering.

The compound also names named historical movements: the 五四運動 (May Fourth Movement, 1919) and the 新文化運動 (New Culture Movement) in China, the 三一運動 (March First Movement, 1919) in Korea, and independence movements of the kind cited on the Geography page ([[独立]]運動). Physical-sense compounds include 運動会 (sports day, Japanese *undōkai*) and 運動場 (sports ground).

Vietnamese *vận động* has both senses as well: physical movement or exercise, and "to campaign; to lobby" (*vận động bầu cử*, to campaign for election). Korean 운동 is the everyday word for exercise and sport (운동하다) and for a movement (독립운동, 民主化運動).

In Dan'a'yo 運動 covers both senses in a single word, as in the rest of the CJKV sphere; the context (sport versus campaign) disambiguates, and the person is [[運動家]] in either sense.
