## Notes
- [飢](../characters/飢.md) "hungry" + [饉](../characters/饉.md) "famine"
- This word is the stand-in for [饉](../characters/饉.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

飢 is hunger, and 饉 is a year of scarcity, a famine, and the old dictionary tradition even distinguishes them by what failed: a failed grain harvest was 饑 (the alias form of 飢) and a failed vegetable crop 饉, so 飢饉 joins the two failures into the general word for famine, in effect "the hunger of a year when the harvest failed." It names a widespread scarcity of food with starvation as the result, a collective calamity and not an individual's hunger.

The word is the history-book term for famine, used of the great famines recorded in each country's chronicles, and it is paired with [災害](災害.md) "disaster" and similar words in the vocabulary of calamity. It differs from [飢餓](飢餓.md) "hunger, starvation," which is the condition of the hungry person or population, where 飢饉 is the event of a failed food supply that causes it. The Food page lists it as "a famine, crop failure." The aliases 饑饉 and 饥馑 are the traditional-variant and simplified Chinese spellings. The readings are Mandarin jījǐn, Cantonese gei1 gan2, Japanese kikin (ききん) and Korean 기근. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "famine, crop failure."
