## Notes
- [長 (char)](../characters/長%20(char).md) "long" + [牙](../characters/牙.md) "fang" — stand-in for [[牙]], which is never used on its own — literally "long tooth," a tusk.

Mandarin 長/长 reads cháng ("long") in this compound's noun sense "tusk", but the same written form 长牙 is also a common verb meaning "to teethe, to grow teeth", where 長/长 reads zhǎng ("to grow"). The two are distinguished only by context. (From memory, not re-checked.)

Cantonese coeng4 ngaa4 parallels the Mandarin noun sense, but none of Japanese, Korean or Vietnamese appears to use a 長牙-based compound for "tusk". The Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese fields (ちょうが, 장아, trường nha) are compositional Sino-Xenic readings, not claims of real usage. In practice Japanese uses 牙 alone (きば, kiba), Korean uses the native 송곳니 (songgotni, "awl-tooth", canine tooth) and Vietnamese uses the native răng nanh (also from memory, not re-checked).

尖牙 ("canine tooth") is not listed as an alias: it names a narrower concept than "tusk" and would need its own entry if the vault ever creates one.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄐㄚㄫㄚ (checked 2026-10-13).
