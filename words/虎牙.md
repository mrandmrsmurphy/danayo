## Notes
- [虎 (char)](../characters/虎%20(char).md) "tiger" + [牙](../characters/牙.md) "fang; tooth"

**虎牙** is an ordinary word for a tiger's fang, and by extension a canine tooth. As a calligraphy term it is the figurative name of the fifth of the eight principles of the 永字八法, the rising flick (提, also written 挑; classically 策): the short, sharp upward stroke is imagined as a fang.

Li Puguang (李溥光), writing in the Yuan dynasty in his 永字八法解, gave each of the eight strokes of the 永字八法 a figurative two-character name; 虎牙 is the rising stroke's name. See [[怪石]] for the full set, and the [[Stroke]] page. The flick comes in a level and a slanting form ([[平提]], [[斜提]]).

The Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and North Korean forms are the ordinary compound readings of the word. No Vietnamese form is given.
