## Notes
- [竟](../characters/竟%20(char).md) "finally, after all, at last" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 竟 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

竟 originally meant "to finish, to end" (a person finishing speaking, per its 会意 etymology), extended to the adverbial sense "finally, after all, at last" — often carrying a note of surprise or contrary-to-expectation outcome, as in 畢竟 ("after all, in the end").

Mandarin jìng, Cantonese ging2, and Korean 경 (gyeong) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese uses the native kun-reading おわる (owaru, "to end, finish") rather than the on-readings ケイ/キョウ (KEI/KYOU) for the bare character alone. Vietnamese cánh is the standard Sino-Vietnamese reading.
