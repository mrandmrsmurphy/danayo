## Notes
- [檀](../characters/檀.md) "sandalwood" + [香](../characters/香.md) "fragrance" + [山 (char)](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain" — "sandalwood mountain", that is [[檀香]] "sandalwood" plus [[山]]; no stand-in relationship applies (檀's own stand-in is [[檀木]], 香's is [[香気]], and 山 is its own stand-in).

檀香山 is a proper noun with two senses at English Wiktionary.

1. Honolulu, the capital of the state of Hawaii in the United States.
2. Dated: Hawaii, the Hawaiian Islands, the Sandwich Islands. Wiktionary quotes Bridgman's *Chinese Chrestomathy in the Canton Dialect* (1841), which describes the islands as lying eighty degrees due east of Canton, thirteen in number, the largest being Hawaii, and Xue Fucheng's diary of 1891, which says the Hawaiian islands once had four hundred thousand natives.

The name means "sandalwood mountain", a translation of the trade in Hawaiian sandalwood, not a transliteration (from memory, not re-checked). English Wiktionary lists 火奴魯魯 (Honolulu) and 夏威夷 (Hawaii) as synonyms; neither is a word in the vault.

Compare [[旧金山]] and [[新金山]], the other Chinese city names built on 山.

Mandarin Tánxiāng Shān and Cantonese taan4 hoeng1 saan1 are as at English Wiktionary. Japanese ホノルル and Korean 호놀룰루 are the ordinary names of the city (from memory, not re-checked), so `kwin` is false. The `vietnamese` field holds the compositional Hán-Việt reading đàn hương sơn (each character as its page gives it; not attested for this name).

No homophones (注音 ㄉㄚㄋㄏ⼘ㄫㄙㄚㄋ unique across words and characters, checked 2026-10-12; 檀香 begins the same way, but its 注音 ends after ㄫ). The 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot.
