## Notes
- [暖 (char)](../characters/暖%20(char).md) "warm" + [簾](../characters/簾.md) "curtain"
- This word is the viable form of [簾](../characters/簾.md): the character's meaning (screen, curtain) is identical, but 簾 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 暖簾 is its `stand_in` compound.

暖簾 is the Japanese のれん (noren): a short cloth curtain hung across the entrance of a shop, restaurant or inn, usually bearing the shop's name or crest, hung out when the business is open and taken in when it closes. The written form 暖簾 is a Japanese jukujikun spelling of the word (the characters mean "warm curtain"); the Mandarin nuǎnlián and Korean 난렴 readings given in the frontmatter are just the Sino-Xenic readings of the characters, not the word's own pronunciation, and in Korean the word is normally borrowed as 노렌 (noren) directly from Japanese.

Because the curtain carries the shop's name, のれん also came to mean the shop's good name, credit and goodwill: 暖簾に傷がつく ("the curtain is stained", i.e. the shop's reputation is damaged) and 暖簾分け ("dividing the curtain", an employee setting up a branch under the old master's name).

In Dan'a'yo, it is the term for the object and its commercial-reputation sense, filed in the Trade domain beside [[商店]] and [[舗]] (the shop itself).
