## Notes
- [韻](../characters/韻.md) "rhyme" + [図](../characters/図.md) "diagram" — literally "rhyme diagram."

韻図/等韻圖 names the rime table, the classical Chinese phonological charting system that organizes syllables by initial consonant, rhyme class, tone, and division — the very apparatus this vault's own Middle Chinese reconstructions and `聲`/`韻` lookup pages ultimately derive from. The two-syllable form 韻図 is a modern abbreviation of the fuller classical term 等韻圖 ("rime table of the [phonological] divisions," kept here as an alias), reflecting how rime tables organize syllables into 等 ("divisions"), a further phonological distinction beyond initial/final/tone.

Mandarin yùntú, Cantonese wan6 tou4, and Korean 운도 (undo) are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 韻's and 図's own stored citations. Japanese いんず (inzu) is likewise compositional, drawing IN from 韻's two attested on'yomi and ズ from 図's two attested on'yomi — the specific combination used in Japanese historical-linguistics scholarship for this term. Vietnamese vận đồ is likewise compositional, from 韻's vận (one of two attested readings) and 図's own đồ. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 운도 matches Sino-Korean 운도 exactly. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄨㄋㄉㄛ unique among both words and characters).
