## Notes
- [目 (char)](../characters/目%20(char).md) "eye" + [的](../characters/的.md) "target" + [語](../characters/語.md) "word" — "the target word," built on [[目的]] "target, objective" (the stand-in that legitimizes 的) plus 語. 語's own `stand_in` is [[言語]], so no `#cranberry` applies to this compound.

目的語 is the grammatical term for the object of a verb, the word or phrase that receives the action: in 神 創造 世界 ("God created the world"), 世界 is the 目的語. It is a Japanese grammatical term, taken over into Korean as 목적어; the vault's own grammar pages use it in the gloss 主語–動詞–目的語 ("subject–verb–object") when describing the SVO order Dan'a'yo takes from modern East Asian languages (`文法 - 04句法`). The vault does not yet have a page for 主語.

**Mandarin and Vietnamese do not use it this way.** Mandarin 目的语 (mùdìyǔ) means "target language," the language one is learning or translating into, the opposite of the source language (see [[原言語]]); Cantonese is the same. The Chinese grammatical term for "object" is 宾语/賓語. Vietnamese likewise uses tân ngữ, the Sino-Vietnamese reading of 賓語, for the grammatical object (tân ngữ trực tiếp "direct object", tân ngữ gián tiếp "indirect object"), so `vietnamese` gives this real term rather than a compositional reading. A Mandarin reader meeting 目的語 in Japanese or Korean grammar must not take it as "target language." The `mandarin` and `cantonese` fields record the compositional readings only.

**How it fits Dan'a'yo's own grammar.** Dan'a'yo syntax is topic-comment (`文法 - 04句法`), but the object still has a firm place: the grammar says a [[事詞]] (eventive, "basically transitive verbs") requires both subject and object and is incomplete without its complement, and that an absent object may be licensed by the passive prefix 被 (世界 被創造 "The world was created"). Objects are not case-marked: the grammar has no accusative case, and a direct object is identified by the sentence structure that 事詞 is built around (see the Semantic Range Notes on [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md)). This is the same relation that [[他動詞]] ("transitive verb") names from the verb's side. It is not the same as the nominalizer －物, which the morphology page glosses "object, result" (the thing produced by an action, as in 食物). The related word [[対象]] is the general "object, target" of attention or study.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 묵덕요 differs from Sino-Korean 목적어 in all three syllables. No homophones (注音 ㄇㄨㄎㄉㄝㄎ·⼄ is unique). The dot marks a genuine fork: ㄇㄨㄎ|ㄉㄝㄎ|⼄ could be re-read as ㄇㄨㄎ|ㄉㄝ|ㄎ⼄, as on [[中国語]] and [[外国語]].
