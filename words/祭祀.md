祭祀 is the general term for ritual sacrifice and ancestor/deity worship in traditional East Asian religion — offerings of food, incense, and ceremony made to gods, spirits, or ancestors to seek their favor or fulfill filial/religious obligation. It underlies the broader category of ritual practice distinguished from casual prayer by its formal, often periodic and communal, structure (as in seasonal ancestral rites or state sacrifices to Heaven).

Mandarin jìsì, Cantonese zai3 zi6, Japanese さいし (saishi), and Korean 제사 (jesa, still the everyday term for ancestral memorial rites in Korean households) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese tế tự is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form for formal sacrificial rites.
