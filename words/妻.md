## Notes
- [妻 (char)](../characters/妻%20(char).md) "wife" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes 妻(char) as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

妻 is "wife" — a 会意 compound depicting a hand seizing a woman's hair, traditionally read as symbolizing marriage by capture or ownership; a possible (though historically doubted) connection to 齊 ("equal") would instead read the character as "equal to her husband," and Schuessler has proposed an Austroasiatic origin (comparing Middle Khmer's word for "wife") rather than a native Sinitic one. The character heads a small family of set compounds: [[妻子]] ("wife and children," a collective term for one's family), 結髪夫妻 (a couple married since youth, lifelong devotion), and 糟糠之妻 (a wife who shared the hardships of poverty, "a wife through thick and thin").

Mandarin qī, Cantonese cai1, Japanese さい (sai, on'yomi, formal/written register) alongside native つま (tsuma, the everyday word for "wife"), Korean 처 (cheo, Sino-Korean, more formal than native 아내, anae), and Vietnamese thê (Hán Việt, literary, alongside the everyday native vợ) all preserve the sense — in every daughter language, the Sino-Xenic reading given here sits in a more formal or literary register than the language's own native everyday word for "wife."

kwin is false: Dan'a'yo 체 does not match Korean 처. 妻 reads 체 (cei/ㄑㄝㄧ) in Dan'a'yo — MC 清母 (t͡sʰ, aspirated affricate) + 齊開韻 (ei) — while Sino-Korean gives 처 (cheo), a monophthong -eo. Dan'a'yo preserves the MC diphthong quality of 齊開韻 as -ei where Sino-Korean simplifies it to a single vowel.
