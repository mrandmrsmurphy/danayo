## Notes
- [行 (char)](../characters/行%20(char).md) "deeds, conduct" + [伝](../characters/伝.md) "to transmit; a record, biography" — "a record of deeds," best known as the second element of **使徒行傳/[[使徒]]行伝** (Acts of the Apostles), the New Testament book recounting the deeds of the early Church following Jesus's ascension.

Here 傳/伝 carries its "biography, chronicle" sense (as in 傳記, *zhuànjì*, "biography") rather than its more common "transmit, propagate" sense — both senses derive from the same root meaning "to pass something on," whether a message (transmission) or a life's deeds (a written record passed down to posterity). 行傳 as "chronicle of acts/conduct" thus parallels titles like 水滸傳 (*Water Margin*, lit. "chronicle of the water's edge") and the many other works whose titles end in 傳.

In Japanese Bible translations, 使徒行伝 (*Shito Gyōden*) was the standard Protestant title for Acts for over a century; the title has more recently been rendered 使徒の働き in some modern translations, while Japanese Catholic Bibles use 使徒言行録. Korean retains **사도행전** (使徒行傳) as the standard title across denominations. The split between [[宗徒]] (Catholic) and [[使徒]] (Protestant/Orthodox) for "apostle" does not carry through as cleanly to 行傳/行伝 itself, which remains widely recognized as "Acts" regardless of which term for "apostle" precedes it.

The 基督敎 page records the word as the second half of 使徒行伝 ("Acts"), noting that 行伝 itself has its own page here and is no longer an uncoined placeholder. Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "Acts (biblical book); record of deeds," with the general sense, a record of someone's deeds, as the base meaning and the biblical title as its best-known use. The readings are Mandarin xíngzhuàn, Cantonese hang4 zyun6, Japanese gyōden (ぎょうでん) and Korean 행전. The aliases 行傳 and 行传 are the traditional and simplified spellings.
