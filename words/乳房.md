## Notes
- [乳](../characters/乳.md) "milk, breast" + [房 (char)](../characters/房%20(char).md) "chamber, room" — literally "milk chamber," the breast (of a human or, of a mammal generally, the udder).

乳房 is the standard anatomical/medical term across the Sinosphere: Mandarin rǔfáng, Cantonese jyu5 fong4, Japanese にゅうぼう (in clinical usage; everyday speech more often says 胸/むね for the general chest area), and Korean 유방 (유방암, "breast cancer," the most common clinical collocation) are all fully compositional. **Vietnamese content note**: filled with vú, the real, everyday native Vietnamese word for "breast" — preferred over a forced Sino-Vietnamese compositional calque (乳's own nhũ + 房's own phòng, not independently attested as a fixed term) since this is a concrete everyday body-part word, where Vietnamese typically favors its own native vocabulary.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 누방 diverges from Sino-Korean 유방, since 乳's own Dan'a'yo reading doesn't match Sino-Korean, even though 房's own reading matches exactly (房 is independently `kwin: true`).
