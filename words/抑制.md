## Notes
- [抑](../characters/抑.md) "to press down; to restrain" + [制](../characters/制.md) "to regulate; to control; system" — stand-in for [制](../characters/制.md), which cannot appear independently
- A near-synonym compound — "to press down" plus "to bring under control" — denoting the act of holding something back before it can run its course: an emotion, an impulse, a disease, a chemical reaction, an enemy's advance. The pairing is a workhorse of formal and technical registers across the Sinosphere — Mandarin yìzhì, Cantonese jik1 zai3, Japanese よくせい (yokusei), Sino-Korean 억제 (eokje), and Sino-Vietnamese ức chế — appearing equally in psychology (impulse suppression), medicine (inhibiting a pathogen or response), and physics or chemistry (restraining a reaction), wherever a force needs to be checked from the outside rather than allowed to run free.

The Government page pairs it with [抑止](抑止.md), which also means "to suppress, restrain, inhibit"; the two are close, and in practice 抑止 is the form that leans toward deterrence (as in 核抑止 "nuclear deterrence") where 抑制 leans toward holding down a rate, a feeling or a process, as in 感情を抑制する "to hold one's feelings in check." It also differs from [制御](制御.md) "to control, regulate," which implies steering something within a range, and from [制限](制限.md) and [制約](制約.md), which are limits imposed. It is placed on the Government page for the sense of authority holding something in check. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "to suppress, restrain, inhibit."
