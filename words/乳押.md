## Notes
- [乳](../characters/乳.md) "breast, milk" + [押 (char)](../characters/押%20(char).md) "to press, to hold down" — a transparent Dan'a'yo-internal descriptive coinage, "breast-holder," for a garment none of the source languages name with a native Sino-CJKV compound. This word is the `stand_in` legitimizer for [[押]], which cannot appear independently.

**A genuine coinage, not a translated loan**: unlike most words in this vault, 乳押's own reading doesn't correspond to a real word borrowed or calqued into Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean, or Vietnamese — each of those languages' own stored fields instead document *their own real, independently existing word* for the garment rather than a reading of these two specific characters. Mandarin uses 乳罩/胸罩 (nǎizhào/xiōngzhào, "milk/chest cover"), Cantonese 胸罩 (hung1 zaau3), Japanese ちちおさえ (a native compound, "milk/breast holder" — conceptually close to Dan'a'yo's own coinage but built from different morphemes), and Korean simply borrows the English loanword ブラジャー-style as 브래지어 (beurae-ji-eo, "brassiere"). Vietnamese áo ngực ("chest garment," the standard modern term, favored over the older French loanword xu chiêng from *soutien-gorge*) fits the same pattern: a real, independently-formed word rather than any translation of 乳押 itself. No homophones (注音 ㄋㄨㄚㄆ unique among both words and characters).
