## Notes
- [洲 (char)](../characters/洲%20(char).md) "islet" — stand-in for [[characters/洲 (char)|洲]], which cannot appear independently

洲 is [[州]] itself with 水 ("water") added as a semantic marker — a phono-semantic derivative distinguishing "land formed in water" (a river islet or sandbank) from the unrelated later sense 州 had specialized into, "administrative division." The distinction then generalized: 洲 became the standard word for the largest landmasses of all, the continents (亜洲/亚洲 Asia, 欧洲 Europe, 美洲 the Americas, 大洋洲 Oceania) — geographically the polar opposite of a small river islet, but sharing the same "land surrounded by water" logic at every scale. Modern Japanese orthography complicates the picture: 洲 fell outside the postwar jōyō kanji list, so continent names and other 洲-compounds are now conventionally written with 州 instead in everyday Japanese, even though the two characters remain semantically distinct in Chinese and in this vault.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 줏 (juo) diverges from Sino-Korean 주 (ju) — 洲 reads with MC 章母 (t͡ɕ) + 尤韻 (ɨu), and Dan'a'yo preserves the compound-vowel structure of 尤韻 as a uo-diphthong (as also seen on [[宙]], [[州]], and [[由 (char)|由]]) where Sino-Korean monophthongizes to a plain -u. This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo pronunciation with [[宙]] and [[州]] — a real three-way homophone cluster, cross-linked above.
