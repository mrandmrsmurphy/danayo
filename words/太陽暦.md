## Notes
- [太 (char)](../characters/太%20(char).md) "great" + [陽 (char)](../characters/陽%20(char).md) "sun, yang" + [暦 (char)](../characters/暦%20(char).md) "calendar"

太陽暦 names a calendar based purely on the Earth's orbit around the sun, its months an arbitrary administrative division unrelated to the moon's phases — as opposed to [[太陰暦]] (a purely lunar calendar) and [[太陰太陽暦]] (the lunisolar hybrid both traditional East Asian calendars actually used). The Gregorian calendar, in nearly universal use today for civil purposes, is the paradigm case of 太陽暦.

Japan formally adopted the solar (Gregorian) calendar for civil use in 1873, replacing its own traditional lunisolar calendar — a reform bound up with the Meiji era's broader push toward Western-style modernization. China, Korea, and Vietnam made similar official transitions to the solar calendar in the following decades, though all three retain their traditional lunisolar calendars for holidays and cultural observances (Chinese New Year, Seollal, Tết).

Japanese たいようれき and Korean 태양력 are both standard compositional Sino-readings. Vietnamese dương lịch is the real, standard term — the everyday contraction that drops 太, the same simplification already seen in [[太陰暦]]'s own âm lịch. No specific HSK level applies to a term this specialized. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄚㄧ⼘ㄫㄌㄝㄎ unique across both words and characters).
