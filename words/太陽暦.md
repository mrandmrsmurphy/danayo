## Notes
- [太 (char)](../characters/太%20(char).md) "great" + [陽 (char)](../characters/陽%20(char).md) "sun, yang" + [暦 (char)](../characters/暦%20(char).md) "calendar"

太陽暦 is a solar calendar: one whose dates follow the seasons, or equivalently the apparent position of the sun, and whose months do not follow the moon's phases. English Wiktionary glosses both Japanese たいようれき and Korean 태양력 as "solar calendar". For Chinese it lists the shorter 陽曆 (Mandarin yánglì, Cantonese joeng4 lik6) as "solar calendar" and, informally and specifically, "Gregorian calendar", and for Vietnamese dương lịch it gives "solar calendar; Gregorian calendar", so the shorter forms are the ones that are attested there. It has no entry for the full 太陽暦 in Chinese, so Mandarin tàiyánglì and Cantonese taai3 joeng4 lik6 here are built from the characters.

English Wikipedia gives the Gregorian calendar as an example of a solar calendar, sets it against lunar and lunisolar calendars, whose months follow the moon, and says the Egyptians appear to have developed the first solar calendar. The two others are the [[太陰暦]], the lunar calendar, and the [[太陰太陽暦]], the lunisolar calendar.
