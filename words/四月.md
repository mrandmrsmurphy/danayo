## Notes
- [四 (char)](../characters/四%20(char).md) "four, fourth" + [月 (char)](../characters/月%20(char).md) "month" — literally "fourth month," April.

Mandarin sìyuè, Cantonese sei3 jyut6, Japanese しがつ (shigatsu), and Korean 사월 are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 四's and 月's own stored citations exactly — unlike the weekday names, Chinese month names genuinely are formed this same compositional way in every source language, with no irregularity (April, unlike June's 유월, has no euphonic contraction in Korean). **Found and fixed a real bug**: `cantonese` was stored as "sei3jut6," missing both a space and the "y" in 月's own jyut6 reading — corrected to "sei3 jyut6." Vietnamese tháng tư ("month four") is the real native word, using Vietnamese's own number-based month naming rather than a Sino-Vietnamese compound.

`kwin` is false: both 四 and 月 individually diverge from Korean on their own character pages, so the AND-rule fails on both counts. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄜ·⼔ㄊ unique among both words and characters).
