## Notes
- [田](../characters/田.md) "field; paddy field" + [鼠](../characters/鼠.md) "rat; mouse"

田鼠 is a vole or field rat, a rodent of open farmland rather than of the house (compare [家鼠](家鼠.md)). English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 田鼠 (Mandarin tiánshǔ, Cantonese tin4 syu2) as "vole; field rat". Its Japanese entry lists でんそ with several senses, among them 野鼠 (field mouse), the Tanezumi rat and 畑鼠 (vole). Korean 전서 and Vietnamese điền thử are built from the readings of the two characters.

The rat addressed in the 《詩經》 poem 碩鼠 ("Big Rat") eats the millet of the people: 碩鼠碩鼠，無食我黍. The 毛詩序 says the poem satirizes heavy taxation. Rats were also one of the four targets of the Four Pests Campaign (1958–1962), alongside sparrows, flies and mosquitoes.
