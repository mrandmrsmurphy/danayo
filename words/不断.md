## Notes
- [不 (char)](../characters/不%20(char).md) "not" + [断](../characters/断.md) "to cut, to sever" — literally "not cut off," hence "continuous, unending, without interruption."

Vietnamese bất đoạn (confirmed via web search, attested directly in Buddhist scripture: 相續苦不斷, "suffering continuous and unbroken," from the Lotus Sutra) uses 斷's "to cut, to sever" reading đoạn rather than its other Sino-Vietnamese reading đoán ("to judge, to decide," as in phán đoán) — the same character carrying two genuinely distinct Vietnamese readings by sense, the way [[行]]/[[朝]] split earlier in this sweep.

**A striking real-world homophone worth documenting**: Japanese ふだん (fudan) for 不断 is not an error or coincidence but a confirmed true homophone of 普段 (also ふだん, "usual, everyday") — the two are historically related, with 普段 originally an ateji (phonetic-only kanji) spelling of the same word later reinterpreted as its own distinct kanji form; modern Japanese keeps them formally separate by meaning (普段 "usual/everyday" vs. 不断 "constant/ceaseless," as in 優柔不断, "wishy-washy indecisiveness") while both are pronounced identically. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 볻돤 diverges from Sino-Korean 부단 in both syllables. No homophones at the Dan'a'yo level (注音 ㄅㄛㄊㄉ⺢ㄋ unique among both words and characters).
