## Notes
- [土 (char)](../characters/土%20(char).md) "earth, soil" + [着 (char)|著](../characters/着%20(char).md) "rooted, fixed in place" (an alias of [[着 (char)|着]], per its own `aliases` field) — literally "rooted in the soil." No stand-in relationship applies (土's own `stand_in` is itself; 着's own is itself).

土著 means aborigine, aboriginal, indigenous — the original inhabitants of a land. Modern colloquial Mandarin also uses it humorously for anyone long-settled in a place regardless of ancestry (北京土著, "a born-and-bred Beijinger") — the closest existing sense to English "native (of a place)," though the word's primary, formal meaning stays "aborigine."

**A real reading nuance**: Mandarin tǔzhù and Cantonese tou2 zyu6 both use 著's own distinct classical "zhù/zyu6" reading ("rooted, fixed in place, notable") rather than [[着 (char)|着]]'s stored "wear" reading — a genuine per-word divergence Dan'a'yo's own citation doesn't capture, since 著 is folded into 着 as a blanket alias regardless of sense. Japanese どちゃく and Korean 토착, however, spell this word with 着 directly (not 著) and use that character's ordinary ちゃく/착 reading, matching this vault's own citation exactly. Vietnamese thổ trước is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form. `kwin` is false: 土 is `kwin: true`, 着 is `kwin: false`. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄛㄐ⺢ㄎ unique across both words and characters).
