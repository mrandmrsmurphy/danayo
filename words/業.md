## Notes
- [業 (char)](../characters/業%20(char).md) "profession; business" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the character 業 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

業/업/nghiệp all carry a genuine double life across the family: an ordinary, secular sense ("occupation, business, undertaking" — 卒業/졸업/tốt nghiệp, "graduation") sitting alongside the Buddhist technical sense, "karma" (Sanskrit karma, the law that intentional action brings its own consequence). Japanese is the one language here that marks this split with two separate readings of the same character: ぎょう for the everyday "occupation/business" sense (授業, 卒業, 業界), and ごう specifically for the Buddhist "karma" sense — even 自業自得, "you reap what you sow," is read jigōjitoku using ごう, not ぎょう, despite using the same character. Mandarin, Cantonese, Korean, and Vietnamese don't split the reading this way; context alone distinguishes "profession" from "karma."
English Wiktionary lists many Chinese senses of 業: "profession; business; trade" (農業, 商業), "merits and achievements; work", "course of study" (學業, 畢業 "to graduate"), "property; estate" (家業), an obsolete-in-standard "lofty; large" (in the *Shijing*), the Buddhist "karma", a literary "already" (業經), a literary "to engage in", and a historical sawtooth ornamental board on a bell-stand. It gives the glyph origin as a beam hung with bells and chimes, a person lifting that beam, or a "great attack" compound, and notes the character was written doubled until the Warring States period. The Trade page lists the Dan'a'yo word as the bound or abstract "profession; business" sense, which is the first of these.

Mandarin yè and Cantonese jip6 are as at English Wiktionary. The Japanese ぎょう/ごう reading split in the paragraph above is not from Wiktionary and I have not re-checked it.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄝㄆ (checked 2026-10-12).
