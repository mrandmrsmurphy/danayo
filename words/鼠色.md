## Notes
- [鼠](../characters/鼠.md) "rat/mouse" + [色](../characters/色.md) "color" — no `#cranberry`: 鼠's own `stand_in` is [[熊鼠]], 色's is [[色彩]]; neither points here. `kwin` is false (AND-rule: both constituents individually false).

An old-fashioned name for "dark grey" — a traditional "mouse-colored" color term paralleled across several daughter languages. Japanese ねずみいろ (nezumi-iro, "mouse color") and Korean 쥐색 (jwi-saek, native 쥐 "mouse" + Sino 색 "color") are both real, attested compounds built the same way as the Dan'a'yo headword. Mandarin huīsè and Cantonese fui1 sik1 instead use the real, everyday word for "grey" (灰色) rather than a literal "mouse-color" calque, since 鼠色 isn't the naturally used term in those languages. Vietnamese xám chuột ("mouse grey") is the real native color term, following the established native word-order pattern (color-adjective + noun) rather than a Sino-Vietnamese compositional form.
