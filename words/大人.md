## Notes
- [大 (char)](characters/大%20(char).md) "big" + [人 (char)](characters/人%20(char).md) "person" — a compound found across the Sinosphere for "adult, grown-up," literally "big person."

Mandarin dàrén and Cantonese daai6 jan4 preserve the compositional sense directly, though in colloquial Mandarin 大人 is now more often reserved for addressing officials/superiors in historical contexts, with 成人 or 大人物 covering plainer "adult" senses. Japanese おとな (otona, kun'yomi, the everyday word for "adult") is a native reading assigned to this same character pair (ateji-like), while たいじん (taijin, on'yomi) is comparatively rare and mostly literary/honorific ("great man"). Korean 대인 (daein) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, most often as a formal/literary register (Korean prefers 어른 or 성인 in everyday speech).

Vietnamese đại nhân is attested as the genuine Hán Việt reading (confirmed via Hán Nôm dictionary sources and Wiktionary), but its dominant living sense in Vietnamese is the archaic honorific "lord, Your Excellency" — a term of address for officials, nobles, or venerated elders, surviving mainly in historical/period fiction rather than everyday speech. "Adult" is listed only as a secondary, classical dictionary sense; modern Vietnamese normally says người lớn for "adult." Included here as the correct compositional reading, with this semantic divergence flagged rather than glossed over.
