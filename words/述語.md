## Notes
- [述](../characters/述.md) "to state, to relate" + [語](../characters/語.md) "word, language" — no stand-in relationship applies (述's own stand-in is [[陳述]]; 語's is [[言語]]).

述語 is the grammatical predicate: the part of a clause that says something about its topic, the verb or stative and what goes with it. In Dan'a'yo it is the comment of the topic–comment sentence, and the grammar's word order is fixed around it: frame particles, then the 主題 topic, then the 述語, then any 語気助詞 sentence-final particle. Every clause must have one, the grammar's principle of obligatory predication (述語必須), and a noun predicate takes the copula 是. On the phonology page the 述語 of an utterance is where its pitch is highest.

Mandarin shùyǔ, Cantonese seot6 jyu5, Japanese じゅつご (jutsugo) and Korean 술어 (sureo) are all recorded with the same grammatical meaning on Wiktionary. Mandarin grammars more usually say 谓语 (謂語) for the predicate, which is why the simplified 述语 is kept as an alias but is not the everyday Mandarin form; Japanese and Korean use 述語 and 술어 as the standard term. Wiktionary and hvdic have no Vietnamese entry, so that field is omitted. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄨㄊ⼄ unique across both words and characters).
