## Notes
- [盆](../characters/盆%20(char).md) "basin" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 盆 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

盆 names a wide, shallow open container — a basin, tub, or pot — used for washing, holding water, or growing plants. It is highly productive as a compound element: 盆栽 (bonsai, "tray planting"), 臉盆 (washbasin), 骨盆 (pelvis, literally "bone basin"). Mandarin pén, Cantonese pun4, Japanese ぼん (bon), Korean 분 (bun), and Vietnamese bồn are all standard, fully compositional readings — Japanese ぼん is additionally well known from お盆 (Obon), the Buddhist mid-summer festival honoring ancestral spirits, whose name derives from a different, unrelated etymology (a transliteration of Sanskrit ullambana) that happens to share the same character. No Dan'a'yo homophones — a stale reciprocal callout claiming a shared reading with [[紛]] ("numerous, disorderly") had been added to both pages in error (紛's real reading is fǝn/쁜/ㄈㄜㄋ, not this word's pun/푼/ㄆㄨㄋ), removed here.
