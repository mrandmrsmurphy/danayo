## Notes
- [邦](../characters/邦.md) "federation, country" + [美](../characters/美%20(char).md) "beautiful" + [蜀](../characters/蜀.md) "Shu kingdom" — no stand-in relationship applies (邦's own is [[連邦]]; 蜀's own is [[蜀国]], the Shu kingdom, which is unrelated to this place; 美 is its own stand-in).

邦美蜀 (Vietnamese Buôn Ma Thuột) is a city of the Central Highlands (Tây Nguyên) and the capital of [[得楽]] province (English Wiktionary: "provincial capital of Đắk Lắk"). It is the largest city of the highlands and the centre of the Vietnamese coffee trade, and it is the historical heartland of the Rhade (Ê Đê) people (from memory, not re-checked); it is also where the 1975 offensive that opened the final campaign of the Vietnam War began (from memory, not re-checked).

The characters are a Hán-Việt style rendering of a Rhade name, not a translation: "federation, beauty, Shu" is not what the name means. English Wiktionary (read 2026-10-11) derives Buôn Ma Thuột from Rhade ƀuôn "village" + ama "father" + the personal name Y Thuôt, literally "Y Thuôt's father's village", and lists the variants Buôn Mê Thuột and Ban Mê Thuột (a "mother's village" form). The `vietnamese` field holds the Hán-Việt reading bang mỹ thục (hvdic.thivien.net gives 邦 as bang, 蜀 as thục, and 美 as mỹ, also written mĩ), a loose match for the real name Buôn Ma Thuột. Mandarin Bāngměishǔ and Cantonese bong1 mei5 suk6 are as given at English Wiktionary. The Japanese ほうびしょく (邦 HOU, 美 BI, 蜀 SHOKU) and Korean 방미촉 are compositional Sino-Xenic readings; Japanese actually says ブオンマトゥオト and Korean 부온마투옷 (from memory, not re-checked).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 邦美蜀 as a Chinese proper noun (a city in Vietnam, Buôn Ma Thuột; Mandarin Bāngměishǔ, Cantonese bong1 mei5 suk6), and its English entry for Buôn Ma Thuột gives 邦美蜀 as the Mandarin translation. Chinese Wiktionary's Vietnamese entry for Buôn Ma Thuột links 邦美蜀 with no definition. Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 邦美蜀 page. The 注音 ㄅㄚㄫㄇㄧㄙㄛㄎ is shared with no other word or character.

Dan'a'yo keeps the Chinese-sphere name: it is the proper noun for the city, with no other sense.
