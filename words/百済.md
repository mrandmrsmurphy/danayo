## Notes
- [百 (char)](../characters/百%20(char).md) "hundred" + [済 (char)](../characters/済%20(char).md) "help, ferry"

百済 (Baekje, c. 18 BC – 660 AD) was one of the Three Kingdoms of Korea, alongside [[高句麗]] (Goguryeo) and [[新羅]] (Silla), occupying the southwestern Korean peninsula. It was a major conduit for the transmission of Chinese writing, Buddhism, and continental culture to Japan, maintaining especially close diplomatic and cultural ties with the Yamato court — Baekje scholars and craftsmen are traditionally credited with introducing Buddhism and literacy to Japan in the 6th century. The kingdom fell to a joint Silla–Tang invasion in 660 AD.

Mandarin Bǎijì, Cantonese baak3 zai3, Korean 백제 (Baekje), and Vietnamese Bách Tế are all standard compositional renderings of the name. Japanese uniquely preserves the old native reading くだら (Kudara) rather than the on'yomi ひゃくさい, reflecting the kingdom's deep historical relationship with Japan — the reading survives independently of the character reading, much as "Peking" survives in English alongside "Beijing."
