## Notes
- [冠](../characters/冠.md) "crown" + [冕](../characters/冕.md) "ceremonial crown"
- This word is the viable form of [冕](../characters/冕.md): the character's meaning (ceremonial crown) is identical, but 冕 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 冠冕 is its `stand_in` compound.

冠冕 is the classical compound for the ceremonial headwear of rulers and high officials: 冠 is a cap or crown in general, and 冕 the specific ritual crown of the Zhou ritual system, worn on state occasions. Mandarin guānmiǎn, Cantonese gun1 min5 and Korean 관면 (gwanmyeon) are all the same literary word. Because the headwear stood for rank, 冠冕 also came to mean high office and its dignity, and the idiom 冠冕堂皇 ("dignified and imposing," often with the sense of a lofty pretext hiding something less lofty) is built on it.

Dan'a'yo-specific sense: the same as the shared sinitic one. In the Government domain it is kept distinct from [[王冠]], which is the ordinary word for a monarch's crown as a physical object; 冠冕 is the more literary, ritual and rank-bearing word, used for the insignia of office rather than a particular crown.
