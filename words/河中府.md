## Notes
- [河](../characters/河.md) "river" + [中](../characters/中.md) "middle" + [府](../characters/府.md) "prefecture" — "prefecture in the midst of the rivers". No stand-in relationship applies, and no `#cranberry` tag: 河's own stand-in is [[小河]], 中's is [[中心]], and 府's is [[政府]].

河中府 is Samarkand, the city in Uzbekistan. The name is the one given in the *Xiyou lu* (西游录) of Yelü Chucai, which the Chinese Wikipedia article on the book summarizes: after Otrar one comes to the great city Xunsigan (尋思干, Samarkand), "fertile land", and "the Western Liao called it Hezhong Prefecture". Chinese Wikipedia's article on the Western Liao likewise lists "河中府 (Samarkand)" among the main towns of that state, so the name belongs to the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, the period of the Qara Khitai and the Mongol conquest. It is a Chinese administrative translation of the place: 河中 "between the rivers" is the Chinese name for Transoxiana, the land between the Amu Darya and the Syr Darya, and 府 "prefecture" gives it the rank of a Chinese prefecture.

The same spelling was also a prefecture of Song China in what is now Shanxi, on the Yellow River near Yongji (from memory, not re-checked); that is a different place, and context chooses. English Wiktionary has no page for 河中府, and I found no dictionary that lists it, so the Samarkand sense rests on the two Wikipedia articles above.

Mandarin Hézhōngfǔ and Cantonese ho4 zung1 fu2 are the compositional readings. Japanese サマルカンド and Korean 사마르칸트 are the ordinary transliterations of the name of the city, as with [[旧金山]], so `kwin` is false. The `vietnamese` field holds the compositional Hán-Việt reading Hà Trung phủ (each character as its page gives it; not attested for this name).

No homophones (注音 ㄏㄚㄐㄨㄫㄈㄨ unique across words and characters, checked 2026-10-12). The 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot.
