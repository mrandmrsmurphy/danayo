## Notes
- [肉 (char)](../characters/肉%20(char).md) "meat" + [桂](../characters/桂.md) "cinnamon, cassia" — 桂's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, but 肉's own is [[肉]] itself, so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply; 肉桂 legitimizes 桂 alone. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`.

肉桂 names cinnamon, or more precisely cassia bark (Cinnamomum cassia).

Mandarin ròuguì, Cantonese juk6 gwai3, Japanese にっけい (nikkei), and Korean 육계 (yukgye, the pharmacological Sino-Korean term for cinnamon bark) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 肉桂 is itself a common attested word for "cinnamon" in all four languages. Vietnamese nhục quế is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading. **Fixed a real bug**: `korean` had 계피, the real Korean word for cinnamon but built from the unrelated compound 桂皮 (gyepi, "cinnamon bark") rather than 肉桂's own compositional reading — corrected to 육계. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
