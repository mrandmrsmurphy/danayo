## Notes
- [瓦 (char)](../characters/瓦%20(char).md) "tile" — stand-in for [[瓦 (char)|瓦]], which cannot appear independently

瓦 is 象形, depicting either fired earthenware pottery or a curved roof tile — the character is itself [[Radical 098|瓦]], one of the small set of radicals that name an everyday manufactured object rather than a natural form or body part. Beyond the physical roofing material, the character extended early to a measurement/scientific sense it keeps in the modern language: 瓦 (and 瓦特, its fuller transliteration) names the watt as a unit of electrical power, an unrelated 20th-century phonetic loan riding on the same syllable, distinct from [[佤]] ("Wa," the ethnic group name, an actual phonetic derivative of 瓦 rather than a coincidental homophone).

Japanese has only the on'yomi が (GA) for this word, used almost entirely in the compound 瓦解 (gakai, "to collapse, disintegrate" — literally "to shatter like roof tiles," an idiom-turned-ordinary-word), while the native かわら (kawara) is the everyday word for a physical roof tile itself. Vietnamese shows the same kind of split found elsewhere in this vault between a formal Sino-Vietnamese layer and its nativized descendant: the character's own stored field lists both the more classical ngoa/ngoã and the fully nativized ngói, the last of which is the word actually alive in everyday Vietnamese (nhà ngói, "a tile-roofed house") — ngói was used here as the reading matching this word's own concrete "tile" gloss, over the more archaic ngoa/ngoã forms used in the character's Sino-Vietnamese compounds. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 와 ('wa, from Middle Chinese 疑母 ŋ + 麻二合韻 ɣua) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 와, the same reading also shared with [[臥]] ("lie down, crouch") — an unrelated character coincidentally converging on the identical syllable.
