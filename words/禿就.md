## Notes
- [禿 (char)](../characters/禿%20(char).md) "bald" + [就 (char)](../characters/就%20(char).md), here used as this vault's borrowed stand-in glyph (借代字) for the unlinked character 鷲 ("eagle, vulture") — no stand-in relationship applies (both 禿's and 就's own `stand_in` fields point to themselves). `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`.

禿就 (禿鷲) names an Old World vulture — a bald-headed vulture, the bare head being an adaptation for hygiene when feeding on carrion. 就 here does **not** use its own regular Dan'a'yo reading (cwai/쵀/ㄑ⺢ㄧ); [[就]]'s own Notes explicitly document that when borrowed for 鷲's sense, it deliberately switches to the alias reading juo/줏/ㄐㄨㄛ (matching 鷲's own real Chinese pronunciation, jiù) instead — the same substitution already established on [[就鳥]] ("vulture" generally).

Japanese はげわし (hagewashi, literally "bald eagle/vulture," fully compositional from はげ "bald" + わし "eagle") and Korean 독수리 (doksuri, the standard everyday word for vulture/eagle) are standard readings. Mandarin tūjiù and Cantonese tuk1 zau6 are fully compositional. Vietnamese thốc thứu is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form, built on 就's own alias reading thứu (as established on [[就鳥]]); everyday Vietnamese instead prefers the native kền kền.
