## Notes
- [聖](../characters/聖.md) "holy; sacred" + [誕](../characters/誕.md) "to be born; birth" + [節 (char)](../characters/節%20(char).md) "seasonal festival; holiday; joint; node". None of the three characters has this word as its `stand_in` (聖's is [[神聖]], 誕's is [[誕生]], and 節 is its own stand-in via [[節]]).

聖誕節 — "holy birth festival" — is the sinographic compound for Christmas, the Christian feast of the birth of Jesus. The three characters are transparent: 聖 "holy", 誕 "to be born; birth" (誕 also carries a sense of "fantastic, outlandish" in other compounds, but here the birth sense is the operative one), and 節 "festival, holiday" (from the bamboo node, generalised to the "nodes" of the calendar year). The compound reads directly as "the festival of the holy birth". It is the Christian feast listed beside [[復活節]] (Easter) in [基督敎](../lexipedia/基督敎.md); like 復活節 and [[春節]] it is formed on the 節 "festival" pattern. The simplified spelling 圣诞节 is kept as an alias, since no separate word file exists for it.

Cross-linguistic note. The `japanese`, `korean` and `vietnamese` fields hold the Sino-Xenic reading of the compound, not the everyday word. Wiktionary lists 聖誕節 with Mandarin Shèngdànjié, Cantonese sing3 daan3 zit3, Japanese せいたんせつ and Korean 성탄절, and has no Vietnamese entry for it; the Vietnamese field is therefore the regular Sino-Vietnamese reading thánh đản tiết (thánh, đản and tiết are the readings on the 聖, 誕 and 節 character pages; 誕 = đản was re-checked on hvdic.thivien.net). The everyday words are different: Japanese overwhelmingly says クリスマス (from English "Christmas"), Korean has 크리스마스 alongside 성탄절, and Vietnamese says Giáng sinh (降生, "descend-be born") or lễ Giáng sinh — this last point is from memory, not re-checked. These borrowings are not readings of 聖誕節 and are deliberately left out of the fields. I did not verify who first coined the Chinese compound or when, so no origin story is asserted here.

Pronunciation: 注音 ㄙㄧㄫㄉㄚㄋㄐㄝㄊ is the plain concatenation of ㄙㄧㄫ + ㄉㄚㄋ + ㄐㄝㄊ, with no syllable-boundary dot. Checked against `syllables/` and the syllable structure in the phonology chapter: at 聖|誕 the ㄫ coda is followed by ㄉ, but no syllable begins with ㄫ and ㄉ is not a legal coda, so ㄙㄧㄫ·ㄉㄚㄋ has only one parse; at 誕|節 the alternative ㄉㄚ|ㄋㄐㄝㄊ fails because ㄋ would need a vowel or glide before ㄐ. There are no homophones.

Dan'a'yo uses the shared CJKV sense (Christmas, the Christian feast), with no narrowing or shifting.
