## Notes
- [例](../characters/例.md) "example, precedent, custom" + [外](../characters/外.md) "outside" — "outside the precedent": an exception. Neither character is free on its own in Dan'a'yo (例 is carried by [[実例]], 外 by [[外部]]).

例外 is literally "outside the 例", where 例 is the established pattern or precedent (as in 恒例 and 比例), so an exception is a case that falls outside a rule. Wiktionary records it as a Japanese-coined Sino-compound (wasei kango) that was taken back into Chinese and Korean in the modern period, and it is now ordinary in all five languages: Mandarin 例外, Japanese 例外 (as in 例外なく "without exception"), Korean 예외 in the South and 례외 in the North, Cantonese lai6 ngoi6. Vietnamese *lệ ngoại* is the Sino-Vietnamese order, but the usual modern word is the reversed *ngoại lệ*, which hvdic.thivien.net also lists; the field here gives the straight reading *lệ ngoại*, because the reversed form is no longer a compositional reading.

The word is neighbours with [[除外]] "to exclude" and [[以外]] "other than". 以外 is a postposition that marks what lies outside a set, 除外 is an act of taking something out of the set, and 例外 is the thing that was left outside, regarded as a case against the rule. Computing and logic use it in the same way (an "exception" that is raised or handled).

In Dan'a'yo it is the head of 例外規定 ("exception rules"), the section of [[文法 - 03文字法]] listing the character pairs where the extended Shinjitai policy is overridden (龍/竜, 圓/円, 佛/仏 and so on). Here a 例外 is a departure from a general rule that has its own explicit, listed provision, which is why the heading is 例外 + [[規定]], "a stipulation of exceptions". This is the shared CJKV sense; no narrowing is intended.

Readings: Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and Vietnamese from Wiktionary and hvdic.thivien.net; Korean is the North Korean 례외 (no initial-sound law), derived from the stored readings of 例 and 外 (the South Korean form is 예외). No homophones (注音 ㄌㄝ⺢ㄧ unique across words, chengyu and characters).
