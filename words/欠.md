## Notes
- [欠 (char)](../characters/欠%20(char).md) "yawn" — stand-in for [[欠 (char)|欠]], which cannot appear independently

欠 is a 象形 pictograph of a kneeling person with mouth open — the character page documents this as the root image behind a whole family of "open-mouth" pictographs built the same way: 次 ("next," originally speaking/spitting while speaking), 吹 ("blow"), 歌 ("sing"), 欲 ("desire"), 歐 ("vomit"). From the literal yawn, the character generalized to "a gap, a lack, a deficiency" — the mouth's open space read metaphorically as something missing — giving 欠缺 ("lack, be deficient in") and 欠乏 ("deficiency," already documented in this vault as its own word).

Japanese keeps only the single on'yomi けつ (KETSU) for this word, but the native verb readings preserved on the character page (か-ける "to lack, be missing," か-く "to lack, be short of") show the same literal-yawn-to-abstract-lack shift as an independent development within Japanese itself, not merely inherited from the Chinese semantic range — 欠席 (kesseki, "absence") and 欠点 (ketten, "a fault, a flaw") both draw on this deficiency sense rather than the original yawn. Vietnamese khiếm survives in the same abstract register (khiếm khuyết "shortcoming, deficiency," paralleling 欠缺 exactly) rather than the literal bodily sense, which Vietnamese names with its own native word (ngáp, "to yawn"). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 캼 (kyam, from Middle Chinese 溪母 kʰ + 嚴韻 ɨam) diverges from Sino-Korean 흠 in both onset aspiration realization and vowel, a wider divergence than most `kwin: false` pairs in this corpus.
