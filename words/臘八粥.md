## Notes
- [臘](../characters/臘.md) "Laba" + [八 (char)](../characters/八%20(char).md) "eight" + [粥 (char)](../characters/粥%20(char).md) "congee"

臘八粥 is the ritual porridge eaten on [[臘八節]]. English Wikipedia says it is usually made with eight or more ingredients, which stand for good luck, since eight is a lucky number and the *ba* of Laba means eight. Its Beijing version is given as barley, maize, millet, sorghum, glutinous rice, azuki beans, jujubes, lotus seeds, chestnuts and pine seeds; the article adds that versions vary by region and may include tofu, potato, meat and vegetables.

The same article gives the history in outline: in the Han dynasty the congee was used in worship and not eaten; the festival date was fixed on the eighth day of the twelfth lunar month in the Northern and Southern dynasties; in the Song dynasty it was eaten widely by common people, officials and aristocrats; and in the Qing dynasty the emperor and nobles gave it to officials and servants.

Korean 팥죽 (red bean porridge), eaten on the winter solstice (冬至) rather than on Laba, is the nearest Korean parallel, and Japanese お粥 (o-kayu, [[粥]]) is structurally the same dish type but an everyday and sick-day food; I did not check either comparison against a source.

In Dan'a'yo 臘八粥 names this specific festival dish, not congee in general, for which the word is [[粥]]; it is placed on the Food lexipedia page with the other named dishes, and its festival is [[臘八節]].
