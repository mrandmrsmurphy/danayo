## Notes
- [祭](../characters/祭.md) "festival, sacrifice" + [日 (char)](../characters/日%20(char).md) "day"

祭日 names a day set aside for ritual observance or festival celebration — rooted in 祭's core sense of ceremonial sacrifice/worship, extended to festival days more broadly. In Japan it historically named a category of state ritual observance days distinct from [[祝日]] (the modern legally-designated national holidays); the two categories were merged into the single 祝日 system after WWII, so 祭日 today survives mainly as an older or informal way of referring to public holidays.

Korean 제일 is a standard compositional Sino-Korean reading. Vietnamese tế nhật is attested, literally "ritual/sacrificial day." No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄝ·ㄋㄧㄊ unique across both words and characters).
