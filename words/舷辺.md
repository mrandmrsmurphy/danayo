## Notes
- [舷](../characters/舷.md) "side of a boat, gunwale" + [辺](../characters/辺.md) "side, edge, border" — stand-in for [舷](../characters/舷.md), which cannot appear independently

A compound describing the gunwale — the upper edge of a ship's side where hull meets deck. 舷 (ふなばた funabata in Japanese) is the specific nautical term for the ship's side, and figures in directional compounds: 左舷 (さげん sagen, port) and 右舷 (うげん ugen, starboard). In Chinese, 舷 similarly appears in 舷窗 (xiánchuāng "porthole") and 舷梯 (xiántī "gangway"). The addition of 辺 "edge, side" reinforces the boundary sense — the margin where boat ends and sea begins. Korean 현변 is the hanja reading; the native term is 뱃전 (baetjeon "ship's side").

The form 舷辺 is a straightforward compound of the bound 舷 with the common "edge" element, and it is the Chinese form (舷边 in the simplified spelling, listed as an alias) that is most at home in ordinary use. The Japanese けんへん, Korean 현변 and Sino-Vietnamese huyền biên recorded in the frontmatter are the Sino-xenic readings of the two characters, not an everyday word in those languages, which use their own terms (Japanese 舷側 or ふなばた, Korean 뱃전). The word is placed under [Water](../lexipedia/Water.md) as a part of a vessel, beside words such as [船尾](船尾.md) "stern" and [船舶](船舶.md) "ship." Dan'a'yo keeps the sense, "gunwale, side of a boat, ship's side."
