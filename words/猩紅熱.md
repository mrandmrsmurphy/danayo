## Notes
- [猩](../characters/猩.md) "orangutan; vivid red" + [紅 (char)](../characters/紅%20(char).md) "crimson" + [熱 (char)](../characters/熱%20(char).md) "fever" — scarlet fever (*Streptococcus pyogenes* infection)

The name goes back to the 猩猩 (orangutan, or the legendary ape of that name), whose blood was proverbially a vivid red dye: 猩紅 is the deep scarlet of that dye, deeper than 紅 alone.  Adding 熱 names the fever marked by a rash of precisely that color.  The disease name is the standard modern medical term, matching English "scarlet fever", and it is shared by Japanese (猩紅熱, しょうこうねつ, shōkōnetsu) and Korean (성홍열) with identical meaning.  Whether it first arose in Chinese or Japanese medical translation was not verified here.

Scarlet fever is caused by group A *Streptococcus pyogenes* producing an erythrogenic toxin. The disease presents with sudden high fever, sore throat (strep throat), and within one to two days a characteristic sandpaper-textured rash spreading from the trunk — the scarlet that gives the disease its name. The tongue develops the "strawberry tongue" appearance. Before antibiotics, scarlet fever was a leading cause of childhood death in East Asia and Europe; it remains treatable with penicillin but has not been eradicated.

猩's own `stand_in` is 猩猩, so this word is not its legitimizer.  Mandarin xīnghóngrè, Cantonese sing1 hung4 jit6, Japanese しょうこうねつ and Korean 성홍열 are the readings, and the alias 猩红热 is listed.  Vietnamese bệnh tinh hồng nhiệt is the Sino-Vietnamese name with the word bệnh ("disease") in front.  `kwin` is false because the Dan'a'yo 성홍녇 differs from the Korean 성홍열.
