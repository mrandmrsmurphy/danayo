## Notes
- [負 (char)](../characters/負%20(char).md) "negative, minus" + [数](../characters/数.md) "number" — literally "negative number," a real number less than zero.

Mandarin fùshù, Cantonese fu6 sou3, and Japanese ふすう are standard, fully compositional readings of 負數/負数, matching 負's and 数's own stored citations. **Korean does not follow this pattern**: rather than a direct 負數 calque (which would collide with 부수, the Sino-Korean term for a Kangxi radical, 部首), Korean instead uses 음수 (陰數/음수, literally "yin number"), built on the yin–yang polarity rather than 負 itself — filled here as the real attested term per this vault's cross-linguistic-field convention, not a compositional guess. Vietnamese số âm ("negative/yin number") mirrors the same yin-based logic as Korean rather than a direct calque of 負數 — the everyday, and only attested, Vietnamese term.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 뷰수 does not correspond to Korean 음수 at all, since Korean uses an entirely different root for this concept. No homophones (注音 ㄅ⼜ㄙㄨ unique among both words and characters).
