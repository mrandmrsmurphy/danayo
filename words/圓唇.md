## Notes
- [圓 (char)](../characters/圓%20(char).md) "round" + [唇 (char)](../characters/唇%20(char).md) "lips" — no stand-in relationship applies (圓 and 唇 are both their own stand-ins).

圓唇 is "rounded", of a sound produced with the lips pushed into a round shape: the term for lip rounding in vowels and, by extension, the vowels that have it, such as ㄨ /u/ and ㄛ /o/. As a modifier it is a 性詞, and it takes 無 for its opposite, so 無圓唇 is "unrounded", as in 無圓唇後舌母音, the "unrounded back vowel" that Korean inserts to break up a consonant cluster, and 中舌無圓唇母音, the unrounded central vowel that Mandarin inserts. Japanese's epenthetic vowel is described as a bilabialized (両唇化) version of that same unrounded back vowel.

Wiktionary lists 圓唇 as a variant of 圓脣 and defines it as the linguistic adjective "(of a vowel) rounded": Mandarin yuánchún and Cantonese jyun4 seon4. Japanese 円唇 (えんしん) and Korean 원순 are the matching terms in those languages and are given here from the compositional readings of the characters, not separately verified in a dictionary. Korean writes the second element with 脣 (순), a variant of 唇 whose own Sino-Korean reading is 진, which is why the `korean` field holds 원순 and why `kwin` is true. Wiktionary and hvdic have no Vietnamese entry, so that field is omitted. No homophones (注音 ⼔ㄋㄙㄨㄋ unique across both words and characters).

Dan'a'yo spells the first element with 圓, the traditional form, and does not use 円: that character is, in Japanese, too closely tied to the yen.
