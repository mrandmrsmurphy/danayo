## Notes
- [漢](../characters/漢.md) "Han (Chinese)" + [蔵](../characters/蔵.md) "Tibet" (here read *zàng*, a distinct sense from the character's primary "hide, store" reading, cognate with 西藏 Xīzàng, "Tibet")

漢蔵 (Sino-Tibetan) names the language family that includes Chinese and the Tibeto-Burman languages, one of the world's largest language families by number of speakers. As a modern academic classification term rather than an inherited word, its rendering varies more than ordinary vocabulary across the CJKV sphere: some languages built a term directly from the Chinese hanzi compound 漢藏/汉藏, while others coined a parallel calque instead.

Mandarin and the other Sinitic-adjacent traditions use 漢藏語系 (hànzàng yǔxì) directly, since the compound is native to Chinese scholarship on the subject. Japanese linguistics, by contrast, generally favors the katakana loan-calque シナ・チベット語族 (Shina-Chibetto gozoku, "Sino-Tibetan language family") over a hanzi reading — 漢蔵 does not circulate as spoken Japanese vocabulary for this concept, even though the kanji compound is recognizable as the Chinese term. Korean linguistics similarly tends toward 중국티베트어족 (Jungguk-Tibet eojok, "China-Tibet language family") rather than a Sino-Korean 한장 reading. Vietnamese linguistic writing, drawing on its stronger tradition of Hán-Việt technical vocabulary, does use Hán-Tạng directly, paralleling the Chinese form.

This pattern — a technical or scientific classification term inheriting unevenly across the daughter languages, sometimes as a direct Sino-Xenic compound and sometimes as an independently coined calque — recurs across modern academic vocabulary more broadly, distinguishing it from the older, fully inherited vocabulary layer where a single Sino-Xenic reading reliably surfaces in all four traditions.
