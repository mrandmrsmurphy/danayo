## Notes
- [廬](../characters/廬.md) "shack" + [舎](../characters/舎.md) "inn; hut"
- This word is the viable form of [廬](../characters/廬.md): the character's meaning (hut, shack) is identical, but 廬 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 廬舎 is its `stand_in` compound.

廬 is a 形声 character with the 广 "shelter" radical and the phonetic 盧, and names a simple, temporary or rustic dwelling: a field hut, a hermit's cottage, a hut put up for mourning. In Classical Chinese it is most familiar in 草廬 "thatched hut," as in the story of 三顧草廬, in which Liu Bei visits Zhuge Liang's thatched cottage three times. 舎 adds the generic "dwelling, lodging," so 廬舎 reads as "hut-dwelling," a plain doubling of the idea of a humble home.

This is a literary word and rare in ordinary speech. The reading is Mandarin lúshè, Cantonese lou4 se3 and Korean 려사 (North Korean keeps the initial 려). In Japanese 廬 is a Sino-Japanese ro/ryo bound element, and the native word is いおり (庵/廬); the vault's Japanese field is left blank for this word rather than invented. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense "hut, cottage, thatched dwelling" with no divergence.
