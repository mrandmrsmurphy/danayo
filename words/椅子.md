## Notes
- [椅](../characters/椅.md) "chair" + [子](../characters/子.md) "child" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 椅 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

Mandarin yǐzi, Cantonese ji2 zi2, Japanese いす (isu), and Korean 의자 are the standard readings. Japanese いす is the Sino-Japanese reading of the whole compound, with 子 read す as in 扇子 (せんす), rather than a native word. The compound is a Mandarin-style noun built with the suffix 子, which marks everyday objects (as in 桌子, 帽子).

椅 was originally the name of a tree, appearing in the Shijing as 椅桐梓漆; the furniture sense developed after the chair itself came into Chinese life, earlier called 胡床 ("barbarian bed"), a folding seat of northern origin. 椅子 became the general word for a chair with a back, while 凳 / 凳子 names a backless stool; Dan'a'yo glosses the word as both "chair" and "stool" but with the sense of a chair first.
