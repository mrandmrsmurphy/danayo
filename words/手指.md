## Notes
- [手 (char)](../characters/手%20(char).md) "hand" + [指](../characters/指.md) "finger, toe" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the bound character [指](../characters/指.md), which cannot appear independently.

Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, and Korean all keep 手指/수지/しゅし as the real, standard word for "finger." Vietnamese is the outlier: ngón tay, a fully native compound, is the everyday word in speech, while the Sino-Vietnamese reading thủ chỉ survives mainly as word trivia — a well-known riddle asks "bàn tay là thủ, ngón tay là chỉ, vậy cánh tay là gì?" ("the hand is thủ, the finger is chỉ, so what's the arm?"), treating the Sino-Vietnamese body-part vocabulary as a puzzle rather than everyday usage.
English Wiktionary gives the Chinese noun two senses: the anatomical "finger" (measure words 根 and 隻; 交叉手指, "to cross one's fingers") and, in informal Hong Kong Cantonese, "flash drive; thumb drive". It lists the finger hyponyms 大拇指, [[食指]], [[中指]], 無名指 and [[小指]], and the derived terms 手指甲, 手指頭 and 金手指, among others. A second, Coastal Min entry means "finger ring". The Body page lists the Dan'a'yo word as "finger".

Mandarin shǒuzhǐ and Cantonese sau2 zi2 are as at English Wiktionary.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄙ⼜ㄐㄧㄜ (checked 2026-10-12).
