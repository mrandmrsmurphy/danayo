## Notes
- [百 (char)](../characters/百%20(char).md) "hundred" + [万](../characters/万.md) "ten-thousand" — one million, 1,000,000.

Unlike English, which gets a new word at "million," the Sinitic system reaches 1,000,000 by counting myriads: 百万 is literally "a hundred myriad." 百 here is the bare word [[百]], so no 一 is needed. Japanese ひゃくまん, Korean 백만, Mandarin bǎiwàn and Cantonese baak3 maan6 are the plain concatenations, and 百万 is also the ordinary way to say "million" in Japanese. Vietnamese is the exception: it says triệu (the Sino-Vietnamese reading of 兆, a character whose numeric value has shifted across periods; see [[一兆]]) for a million, so `vietnamese` is left blank rather than given a calque. Compare [[十万]], [[千万]] and [[一億]].
