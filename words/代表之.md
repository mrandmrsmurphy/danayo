## Notes
- [代表](../characters/代.md) "to represent" + [之](../characters/之.md) (classical object particle, "it/them") — literally "representing it," i.e. "on behalf of." A Dan'a'yo-internal grammatical coinage explicitly modeled on the ancient Chinese co-verb 爲 (wéi, "for, on behalf of, for the sake of"), not itself an attested compound in any single source language — hence each language field instead documents that language's own natural way of expressing "on behalf of": Mandarin/Cantonese lean on 代表 used prepositionally (我代表大家, "I, on behalf of everyone..."), Korean uses ...을 대표하여, and `vietnamese: thay mặt` is the standard everyday Vietnamese phrase (thay mặt công ty, "on behalf of the company"). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 대뱟티 diverges entirely from the phrase-based Korean field. No homophones (注音 ㄉㄚㄧㄅ⼘ㄨㄊㄧ unique among both words and characters).
