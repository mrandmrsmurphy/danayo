## Notes
- [醍](../characters/醍.md) "essential oil" + [醐](../characters/醐.md) "purest cream"
- This word is the viable form of [醍](../characters/醍.md): the character's meaning (essential oil, clarified butter) is identical, but 醍 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 醍醐 is its `stand_in` compound.

醍醐 is a Buddhist-era loan-compound, a Chinese transcription-and-translation of an Indian dairy term for the purest product of refined milk. The Mahayana *Nirvana Sutra* sets out five flavors in order of refinement (milk, cream, curds, butter, then 醍醐), and uses the last as an image for the highest teaching. That image is the source of the figurative sense "the essence, the very best," and of the idiom 醍醐灌頂 "to be doused with 醍醐," meaning a sudden flash of enlightenment.

The Japanese reading daigo (だいご) is the best known outside the literal sense: 醍醐味 (daigo-mi) is everyday Japanese for the real pleasure or best part of something (読書の醍醐味 "the real joy of reading"), and Daigo is also a place and a name. The other readings are Mandarin tíhú, Cantonese tai4 wu4, Korean 제호 and Sino-Vietnamese đề hồ (the 醍 character carries two Vietnamese readings, thể and đề, and the compound uses đề). Dan'a'yo keeps the shared senses as the Food page records them: literally "ghee, finest cream," and figuratively "the essence of Buddhism." It is placed under [Food](../lexipedia/Food.md).
