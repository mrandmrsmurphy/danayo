## Notes
- [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "gold; metal" + [刀](../characters/刀.md) "blade; knife"

**金刀** is an ordinary word for a gold-colored blade, or a blade of metal. As a calligraphy term it is the figurative name of the eighth of the eight principles of the 永字八法, the right-falling stroke ([[捺]], classically 磔, and also called 波): the long stroke that thickens toward its end and finishes in a clean foot is imagined as the edge of a blade.

Li Puguang (李溥光), writing in the Yuan dynasty in his 永字八法解, gave each of the eight strokes of the 永字八法 a figurative two-character name; 金刀 is the right-falling stroke's name. See [[怪石]] for the full set, and the [[Stroke]] page.

The Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and North Korean forms are the ordinary compound readings of the word. No Vietnamese form is given.
