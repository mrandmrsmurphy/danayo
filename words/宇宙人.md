## Notes
- [宇](../characters/宇.md) "universe, space" + [宙 (char)](../characters/宙%20(char).md) "eternity" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" — one who travels through or comes from outer space; both astronaut and extraterrestrial life

The compound [[宇宙]] (universe) is built on an architectural metaphor: 宇 originally meant the eaves of a building, 宙 the roof-beams — together they denote the totality of space and time, the structure that contains everything. Adding 人 ("person") produces a word that can mean either "cosmonaut" (a person who enters that structure) or "alien" (a person from beyond it).

All the major East Asian languages have the same compound: Mandarin 宇宙人 (yǔzhòurén), Japanese 宇宙人 (うちゅうじん), Korean 우주인, Vietnamese vũ trụ nhân. Its meaning shifts by language, however. In Japanese and Mandarin the everyday sense is "alien", an extraterrestrial; the astronaut is a different word there (Japanese 宇宙飛行士, Mandarin 宇航員 or 太空人). In Korean 우주인 covers both, and is the common word for an astronaut as well as an alien. This is the same split that the English glosses show.

In Dan'a'yo 宇宙人 keeps both senses, "alien" and "astronaut", as the word's meaning, with context telling them apart.
