## Notes
- [主](../characters/主.md) "lord; master; principal" + [語](../characters/語.md) "word, language" — "the governing word," the term that names the subject of a clause. No stand-in relationship applies (主's own stand-in is [[主人]]; 語's is [[言語]]).

主語 is the grammatical subject: the word or phrase a clause is about and that the verb is said of, as in 神 創造 世界 ("God created the world"), where 神 is the 主語 and 世界 the [[目的語]]. The name takes 主 as the governing element, the same logic as [[主格]] (the nominative case, which marks the subject) and [[主題]] (the topic, the governing element of discourse).

In Dan'a'yo the subject is not the same thing as the topic. The grammar is topic–comment: a sentence puts its [[主題]] before the [[述語]], and a noun may be a topic with no marking at all. The 主語 is the argument the predicate itself requires. The syntax page says a [[事詞]] "requires both subject and object" and is incomplete without them, and that an active clause's subject becomes the "former subject" (旧主語) after the passive prefix 被, which may reintroduce it with an adverbial phrase marked 以. A subject can therefore be the topic as well, but it need not be.

Mandarin zhǔyǔ (主语), Cantonese zyu2 jyu5 and Japanese しゅご are all recorded with this meaning on Wiktionary, and Korean 주어 is the hanja-based form. Vietnamese does not read the characters at all and says chủ ngữ for the subject, the real grammatical term, so `vietnamese` holds that and not a compositional reading. Wiktionary adds a Japanese slang sense, the subject of a blunt, unqualified categorical statement; Dan'a'yo uses only the grammatical sense.
