## Notes
- [吸 (char)](../characters/吸%20(char).md) "suck, inhale" + [血 (char)](../characters/血%20(char).md) "blood" + [鬼](../characters/鬼.md) "devil, demon" — literally "blood-sucking demon," the standard Mandarin/Japanese/Korean calque of the Western "vampire." **Content bug found and fixed**: `mandarin` had three variant readings for 血 (xuè/xuě/xiě) crammed comma-joined into a single string, and `japanese` used a non-standard romaji-like spelling instead of proper hiragana — corrected `mandarin` to the standard xīxuèguǐ (血's own primary reading is xuè; xiě is a colloquial variant sometimes heard in casual speech, e.g. 流血 liú xiě) and `japanese` to きゅうけつき (吸血鬼). **Genuine cross-linguistic mismatch documented**: Vietnamese has no Sino-Vietnamese calque of this compound in everyday use — the real Vietnamese word for "vampire" is the native folkloric term **ma cà rồng**, unrelated to the 吸血鬼 hanzi; a compositional "hấp huyết quỷ" would not be real usage (domain knowledge, this session's search quota exhausted). Filled blank `cantonese: kap1 hyut3 gwai2`. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 힙훧귀 diverges from Sino-Korean 흡혈귀 in the first syllable's vowel. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄧㄆㄏ⼔ㄊㄍㄨㄧ unique among both words and characters).
