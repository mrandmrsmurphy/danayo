## Notes
- [忍](../characters/忍.md) "endure" + [耐 (char)](../characters/耐%20(char).md) "endure"
- This word is the viable form of [忍](../characters/忍.md): the character's meaning (to endure) is identical, but 忍 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 忍耐 is its `stand_in` compound.

Both halves already mean "endure," so 忍耐 is a synonym compound that reinforces the idea. 忍 is traditionally read as a 形声 character with 心 "heart" under a blade-like phonetic, and so pictures holding one's feelings in check; 耐 means to withstand, bear, or hold out against hardship (as in 耐久 "durable"). The two shades differ slightly: 忍 stresses suppressing one's own impulses, 耐 stresses holding up under external pressure, and 忍耐 covers both, which gives the English glosses "patience," "endurance" and "perseverance."

In East Asian ethics 忍耐 is a prized virtue, praised as a mark of maturity, and it is one of the most common words in schooling and moral instruction. The reading is shared across the sphere: Mandarin rěnnài, Cantonese jan2 noi6, Japanese nintai (にんたい), Korean 인내. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense with no divergence.
