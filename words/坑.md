## Notes
- [坑 (char)](../characters/坑%20(char).md) "pit; hole"

坑 means "pit, hole, hollow" — a depression in the ground, whether natural or dug (坑道, "tunnel, gallery"). It appears in the historical episode 焚書坑儒 ("burning books and burying scholars alive," a Qin-dynasty act of ideological repression).

Mandarin kēng and Cantonese haang1 preserve the sense directly. Japanese コウ (KOU, on'yomi, formal register) covers the sense with no dedicated native kun'yomi. Korean 갱 (gaeng) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 구덩이 (gudeong-i, "pit, hole"). Vietnamese khanh is the Hán Việt reading, phonologically regular from Middle Chinese kʰæŋ (溪-initial → kh-, 庚-rhyme division II → -anh, the same pattern as 更→canh, 杏→hạnh, 冷→lãnh), and directly attested in phần thư khanh nho (焚書坑儒, matching the historical episode above). The character page's other stored candidate, ganh, could not be confirmed as a genuine reading of 坑 — no regular sound-change path connects it to 坑's Middle Chinese source, and the real Vietnamese words ganh/gánh ("to carry on a shoulder pole") and gành ("rocky shoal, outcrop") bear no plausible semantic link to "pit"; flagged here as likely spurious rather than confidently excluded, since this session's search tooling was unavailable to fully verify.
