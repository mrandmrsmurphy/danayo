## Notes
- [突](../characters/突.md) "abruptly, suddenly" + [厥](../characters/厥.md) "they" — 厥's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 突's own is [[突然]]; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 厥 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`.

突厥 (Tūjué) is the historical Chinese name for the Turkic peoples — most specifically the Göktürks, the nomadic confederation that founded the first Turkic Khaganate in Central Asia in the 6th century CE, ancestral to many later Turkic groups and states.

Mandarin Tújué, Cantonese dat6 kyut3, Japanese とっけつ (tokketsu), and Korean 돌궐 (dolgwol) are all standard, fully compositional readings, each the historical/scholarly term for the Göktürks and early Turkic peoples in its language. Vietnamese Đột Quyết is likewise compositional.
