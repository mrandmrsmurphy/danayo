## Notes
- [乎 (char)](../characters/乎%20(char).md) "did; do"

乎 is 会意/形声 in origin (Old Chinese \*ɢaː: "wind, air" as semantic + phonetic 丂), originally the same character as [[呼]] ("to call, to cry") before the two later graphically differentiated. In its surviving classical usage, 乎 functions purely as a grammatical particle — most often a sentence-final question marker (equivalent to modern 嗎/呢), as in the circumfix [[不亦V乎]] ("is that not...?", from the Analects line 不亦樂乎, "is that not a joy?"). It also serves as a preposition equivalent to 於 ("in, at, than") in some classical constructions.

Mandarin hū, Cantonese fu4, and Japanese こ (ko, on'yomi, classified in this vault's own data as a name-use kanji rather than everyday vocabulary) all preserve the reading with little living use outside classical/literary registers. Korean 호 (ho) is likewise confined to classical contexts. Vietnamese hồ is the Hán Việt reading, attested from the same Analects line (bất diệc lạc hồ, "is that not a joy?"); the character page's other stored candidate, hô, actually belongs to the historically related but graphically differentiated character [[呼]] ("to call, shout"), not to 乎 itself — removed from this word's own field as a mismatch.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (ho/호/ㄏㄛ) with two other words still awaiting their own turn in this sweep, [[呼]] ("call") and [[虎]] ("tiger") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed on each when it comes up.
