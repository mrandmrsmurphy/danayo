## Notes
- [講](characters/講.md) "lecture" + [演 (char)](characters/演%20(char).md) "unfold; perform" — stand-in for [[講]], which cannot appear independently.

**Content corrected**: `cantonese` was the malformed string `"gong2 jin2, gong2 jin5"`; the second candidate's `jin5` doesn't match [[演]]'s own established Cantonese reading (`jin2`, the only value stored on that character's own page) — corrected to `gong2 jin2`, dropping `jin5` as unsupported.

講演 means "a lecture; to give a speech." 講 means "to lecture, explain, discuss"; 演, originally "water spreading out over a long course" (形声, semantic 水 "water" + phonetic 寅), was extended to "unfold, develop, perform" over time.

Mandarin jiǎngyǎn and Cantonese gong2 jin2 preserve the sense directly. Japanese こうえん (kōen) is standard, everyday vocabulary (講演会, "lecture event"). Korean 강연 (gang'yeon) parallels the Sino-xenic reading and is likewise standard vocabulary. Vietnamese giảng diễn is directly attested (giảng 講 + diễn 演, matching the character order exactly), though in living Vietnamese usage it leans toward a narrower, more technical pedagogical sense — "the lecture method" of teaching, presenting a lesson systematically start to finish — rather than "a lecture" as a general event; the reversed compound diễn giảng is also separately attested, closer to "public speaking, delivering a speech." Everyday spoken Vietnamese more often reaches for diễn thuyết ("to give a speech/address") or bài giảng/giảng bài ("a lecture," academic sense) instead of either order of this compound.
