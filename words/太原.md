## Notes
- [太](../characters/太%20(char).md) "great, supreme" + [原](../characters/原.md) "plain, original" — no stand-in relationship applies (太's own is the single-character word [[太]]; 原's own is [[原始]]).

This name is a collision between a Chinese city and a Vietnamese province, so the Chinese sense comes first.

1. Taiyuan, the capital and largest city of Shanxi province (see [[山西]]), northern China, on the Fen river; one of the oldest continuously named cities of China, the seat of the ancient Taiyuan commandery (from memory, not re-checked).
2. Thái Nguyên, a province and city of northern Vietnam, north of Hanoi, a centre of Vietnamese tea growing and an old Viet Minh base area (from memory, not re-checked). English Wiktionary lists both the province and the city.

The characters are "great" + "plain": 太原 is "the great plain" or "great steppe", the Fen valley's broad basin (from memory, not re-checked). Mandarin Tàiyuán and Cantonese taai3 jyun4 are as in Wiktionary; Japanese たいげん is attested for the Chinese city at English Wiktionary; Korean 태원 is the compositional reading; Vietnamese Thái Nguyên is the Hán-Việt reading and the real name of the Vietnamese sense (thái 太, nguyên 原, per hvdic.thivien.net).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 太原 as a Chinese proper noun (Taiyuan; Thái Nguyên province and city), a Japanese entry (たいげん, Taiyuan) and a Vietnamese Han-form entry for Thái Nguyên; Chinese Wiktionary has a stub with Mandarin tàiyuán. Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no entry. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄚㄧ⼔ㄋ unique across both words and characters).
