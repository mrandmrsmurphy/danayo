## Notes
- [事 (char)](../characters/事%20(char).md) "thing; action" — the same character as the free word [事](事.md); －事 is its bound, fully productive suffix form.

－事 is the second of Dan'a'yo's **nominalizing suffixes**, the one that marks **the action or event itself** as an abstract noun ([[文法 - 05形態]], §名詞化). Attached to a verb phrase, it returns a noun for *the act of X-ing*: 食事 "the act of eating, a meal", 学習事 "the act of studying, study", 絵画事 "the act of drawing". Where [[－物]] names the product, －事 names the process, so 絵画物 is "a drawing" and 絵画事 is "drawing". An 事詞 may take either suffix, since it can denote both result and action. A 性詞 takes only －事, because a quality has no object: [[大事]] "importance, a matter of greatness", from the stative 大.

Attached to a plain noun, －事 reads as "the affairs of X", a use the Chinese-derived vocabulary already shows: [[軍事]] "military matters", [[家事]] "housework", [[人事]] "personnel matters", and [[行事]] "event" and [[従事]] "to engage in" on the verb side. All are valid and stay enumerated on their own pages. Both uses are one idea, *the matter of X*, whether X is an action, a quality or a domain.

－事 is a **派生辞**, productive in the plain sense: any transparent *stem + 事* is valid Dan'a'yo without its own page, and a derivative earns a page only when its meaning is lexicalized. Its derivatives can take further affixes, as any new noun can. Full productivity rests on the grammar's own nominalization system and on living use in the written source languages: Japanese 食事 (shokuji), 行事 (gyōji) and 大事 (daiji); Korean 식사, 행사 and 대사; and Mandarin 军事 (jūnshì), 家事 (jiāshì) and 国事 (guóshì) for the "affairs of X" sense, though Mandarin does not use it for the act of eating or similar action nouns. Vietnamese sự works the other way round, as a free nominalizing word placed *before* the verb (sự phát triển, "development"), so it is productive but in a different position.

The same character is also the free word [[事]] "thing; action", which can stand alone and take case markers; the two are different words with different pages, and the filename tells them apart, as for [[－物]] and 物. －事 shares its syllable (ji, ㄐㄧ) with [[字]] "character" as well as with the free word 事; the homophone callout cross-references them, and a bound suffix is never confused with either in practice.

The page filename uses the fullwidth hyphen `－` (U+FF0D) to mark a word that cannot stand alone but is fully productive; the ASCII form `-事` is kept as an alias.
