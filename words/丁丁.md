## Notes
- [丁 (char)](../characters/丁%20(char).md) "nail; fourth in a series" reduplicated — an onomatopoeia for a sharp, repeated striking sound: chopping wood, metal clanging, or jade pieces colliding, with no connection to 丁's own more common senses.

丁丁 traces back to 伐木丁丁 ("the chopping of trees, thud-thud"), an image from the Book of Songs (詩經) describing woodcutting — from there it generalized to any sharp, rhythmic striking sound, including horse hooves, pipa music, and jade colliding. **Content note**: classical commentary traditions record 丁 in this specific reduplicated onomatopoeic reading as historically pronounced zhēng (giving 丁丁 as zhēngzhēng, not dīngdīng) in the original Shijing context — a polyphonic reading distinct from 丁's everyday dīng — but this vault's own stored fields (mandarin, Dan'a'yo 諺文/羅馬字/注音) are all built consistently on dīng, matching the modern dictionary convention many contemporary sources also use for this onomatopoeia (e.g. a woodpecker's pecking sound). Flagged here for completeness rather than overhauling the whole entry's phonetic basis on a single classical-commentary detail.

Japanese ちょうちょう (chōchō) reduplicates 丁's on'yomi チョウ rather than a rarer alternate reading, and Vietnamese đinh đinh reduplicates 丁's own Hán Việt đinh, both following the same dīng-based reading this word's other fields already use. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 덩 (from 丁's own reading) diverges from Sino-Korean 정 in the vowel. No homophones (注音 ㄉㄝㄫㄉㄝㄫ unique).
