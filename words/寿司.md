## Notes
- [寿](../characters/寿.md) "long life" + [司](../characters/司.md) "govern; officer" — no stand-in relationship applies (寿's own `stand_in` is [[寿命]], 司's is [[公司]]).

寿司 is Japanese ateji: 寿 and 司 were chosen purely for their sound (su-shi), carrying no semantic connection to vinegared rice at all — a genuine coincidence that the "long life" sense of 寿 makes the spelling feel auspicious for a food word, but the choice was phonetic first. Modern Japanese also historically wrote sushi as 鮨 or 鮓 (both genuinely fish-related characters, semantically on-target unlike 寿司), but 寿司 is by far the dominant modern spelling and the only one this vault uses.

Mandarin shòusī and Cantonese sau6 si1 are the borrowed forms used across the Chinese-speaking world for this specifically Japanese food, read with each character's own ordinary reading rather than any special loanword phonology. **Korean strikingly does not borrow the Japanese spelling's reading at all**: 초밥 (chobap) is a native Korean calque — 초 "vinegar" + 밥 "rice" — literally "vinegared rice," bypassing the Sino-Japanese ateji entirely and translating the dish's defining ingredient instead. `vietnamese` is omitted: Vietnamese uses the direct loanword *sushi* itself rather than any Sino-Vietnamese reading of 寿司. No homophones (注音 ㄙ⼜ㄙㄚ unique across both words and characters).
