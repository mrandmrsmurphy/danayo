## Notes
- [八 (char)](../characters/八%20(char).md) "eight" (stand-in for 八)

八 is the Dan'a'yo numeral for "eight." As a single free digit rather than a coined compound, its word page simply licenses the character's own independent, unbound use — the stand-in relationship runs from 八 to itself.

Cross-linguistically the daughter languages preserve the numeral essentially unchanged: Mandarin bā, Cantonese baat3, and Vietnamese bát all continue the character's own reading directly, and Sino-Korean 팔 functions as the ordinary counting numeral alongside the native Korean count-word 여덟 used for concrete quantities of objects. Japanese is the outlier — the on'yomi はち (hachi) covers arithmetic and most compounds (八月 hachigatsu "August," 八十 hachijū "eighty"), but before certain counters it undergoes euphonic gemination to はっ- (八本 happon, "eight long objects"), a sound change distinct from its ordinary reading.
