## Notes
- [科](../characters/科.md) "branch, section, academic subject" + [階](../characters/階.md) "level, tier, storey" — literally "branch-level," the taxonomic rank of family.

Mandarin kē, Cantonese fo1, Japanese か (ka), and Korean 과 are all the real, standard bare-character terms used in taxonomy, matching 科's own stored citations exactly. Vietnamese uses họ instead of 科's own Sino-Vietnamese reading (khoa) — a native word that is, strikingly, the very same word Vietnamese uses for a kinship "family" or "clan," a nice cross-linguistic echo of English's own identical double duty for "family" (household vs. taxonomic rank).

**Why append 階 at all, when none of the four source languages do**: Dan'a'yo appends 階 uniformly across all eight ranks of the taxonomic hierarchy ([[域階]], [[界階]], [[門階]], [[綱階]], [[目階]], [[科階]], [[属階]], [[種階]]) as a purely internal disambiguation device — natural languages rely on context alone to read 目 as "order" rather than "eye" inside a taxonomy chart, but a constructed language benefits from removing that ambiguity outright, especially since Dan'a'yo already independently uses bare [[目]] for "eye." In casual or space-constrained usage, the bare character (e.g. 目 alone) remains an understood abbreviation of the full 階-suffixed word, the same way scientific Latin abbreviates "Order" to "O." — but the full form is what a dictionary entry or first mention should use.

`kwin` is false: 科 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page, which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 階 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄎ⺢ㄍ⼶ㄧ unique among both words and characters).
