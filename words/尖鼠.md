## Notes
- [尖](../characters/尖.md) "sharp; pointed" + [鼠](../characters/鼠.md) "rat; mouse"

尖鼠 is the shrew: the small, mouselike mammal of the family Soricidae. The name is "pointed mouse", 尖 sharp plus 鼠 mouse. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 尖鼠 (Mandarin jiānshǔ, Cantonese zim1 syu2) as "shrew (Soricidae)", Japanese とがりねずみ as "a shrew (mouselike animal)", and lists Korean 첨서 for the same word. It has no Vietnamese entry, so the Vietnamese field is left blank.

English Wikipedia says that shrews, in the order Eulipotyphla, look like long-nosed mice but are not rodents: they are much closer relatives of hedgehogs and moles, and have sharp, spike-like teeth where rodents have gnawing incisors. It adds that some species are venomous, the venom reaching the wound by grooves in the teeth, not by fangs, and names the northern short-tailed shrew, *Blarina brevicauda*, among the species with venomous saliva. The related Chinese word 鼩鼱 (qújīng) is also glossed "shrew".
