## Notes
- [来 (char)](../characters/来%20(char).md) "come" + [賓](../characters/賓.md) "guest"
- This word is the viable form of [賓](../characters/賓.md): the character's meaning (visitor, guest) is identical, but 賓 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 来賓 is its `stand_in` compound.

賓 is "guest" in the formal, ceremonial sense, the counterpart of 主 "host" (主賓 "host and guest"; the pair is old in ritual texts), and 来 "come" makes 来賓 "a guest who has come." It is the word for an invited guest at a ceremony, wedding, conference, opening or speech, and in Japanese the stock phrase 来賓挨拶 means the guests' speeches at such an event. The register is formal and public: a dinner guest in a private home would normally be called by an everyday word, not 来賓.

The reading is shared across the sphere: Mandarin láibīn, Cantonese loi4 ban1, Japanese raihin (らいひん), Korean 래빈 (the North Korean form, keeping initial 래). Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "a guest, visitor at an event," with no divergence, and lists it under both [Society](../lexipedia/Society.md) (as a social role) and [Events](../lexipedia/Events.md) (as a participant).
