## Notes
- [迷 (char)](../characters/迷%20(char).md) "lost, confused" + [路](../characters/路.md) "road, path" — literally "a lost/confusing road." No stand-in relationship applies (迷's own `stand_in` is itself; 路's own is [[道路]]).

迷路 shows a genuine cross-linguistic part-of-speech split rather than one shared meaning. In Mandarin, 迷路 is overwhelmingly used as a common everyday **verb**, "to get lost, to lose one's way" (我迷路了, "I got lost") — the associated verb to [[迷宮]]'s noun above. In Japanese and Korean, however, めいろ/미로 function purely as **nouns** naming a maze with a guaranteed, findable exit (a garden maze, an amusement-park maze) — distinct from [[迷宮]]'s "true labyrinth, exit not guaranteed" sense. Dan'a'yo keeps both senses on this one entry rather than splitting it further, matching the character-level Chinese/Japanese-Korean divergence already documented elsewhere in this vault.

Cantonese mai4 lou6 and Vietnamese mê lộ are the compositional readings from each character's own attested value. `kwin` is false: 迷 is `kwin: false`, 路 is `kwin: true`. No homophones (注音 ㄇㄝㄧㄌㄛ unique across both words and characters).
