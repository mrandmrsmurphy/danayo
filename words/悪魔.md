## Notes
- [悪 (char)](../characters/悪%20(char).md) "bad, evil" + [魔](../characters/魔.md) "demon"

悪魔 names the devil or a demon — an evil supernatural being, generalizing the specifically Buddhist sense of 魔 (from Sanskrit *māra*, a tempter/obstructor of enlightenment) into the broader Judeo-Christian-influenced sense of a personified evil spirit or Satan figure. It is the everyday word across the sphere for "devil" in both religious and colloquial use (e.g. calling a cruel or merciless person 悪魔 figuratively, "a devil").

Cross-linguistically the compound is a stable Sino-Xenic loan: Sino-Korean 악마 and Japanese あくま both directly concatenate the component readings and carry the same sense, with あくま additionally being the standard Japanese translation for "devil/Satan" in Christian contexts. Vietnamese ác ma is likewise an ordinary, freely used word for a devil or evil spirit, built the same way from ác ("evil") + ma ("ghost, demon").
