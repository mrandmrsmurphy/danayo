## Notes
- [繁](../characters/繁.md) "numerous, lush, complicated" + [体](../characters/体.md) "form, body" + [字 (char)](../characters/字%20(char).md) "character" — literally "complicated-form characters."

繁体字/繁體字 names Traditional Chinese characters, as used in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau, directly contrasting with [[簡体字]] ("Simplified Chinese characters," used in mainland China and Singapore). This vault treats 繁体字 as equivalent to [[旧字体]] ("old character forms," the term for pre-shinjitai character forms in Japan) — a deliberate simplification, since the two labels arise from genuinely separate reform histories (China's mid-20th-century simplification vs. Japan's own, differently-scoped shinjitai reform) that happen to converge on largely the same unsimplified forms for most characters.

Mandarin fántǐzì, Cantonese faan4 tai2 zi6, Korean 번체자 (beoncheja), and Japanese はんたいじ (hantaiji) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real term used to describe this character set in its language. Vietnamese phồn thể tự is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 繁's phồn (one of three attested readings — phiền/phồn/tấn) and 体's own thể. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 판테지 diverges from Sino-Korean 번체자 in all three syllables. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄆㄚㄋㄊㄝㄧㄐㄧ unique among both words and characters).
