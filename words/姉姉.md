## Etymology
- [姉 (char)](../characters/姉%20(char).md) "elder sister" reduplicated — the extremely common Mandarin/Cantonese colloquial term for "(elder) sister," written 姐姐/姊姊 in the source languages, folded here into Dan'a'yo's single bound character 姉 (which stands in for 姊/姐 alike).

## Notes
- Unlike the Mandarin/Cantonese case, this reduplication does not naturalize into Japanese or Korean or Vietnamese — each instead uses its own native address term, which additionally varies by the speaker's own gender in Korean: Japanese お姉さん (oneesan); Korean 언니 (a female speaker addressing her own elder sister) or 누나 (a male speaker addressing his); Vietnamese chị. `japanese`/`vietnamese` deliberately left blank, and `korean: 자자` (already stored) is flagged as compositional-only rather than real usage — none of the three are forced into a fabricated reduplicated calque, the same pattern already established on [[伯伯]]/[[叔叔]]/[[女女]]. The previous stray note "this word should come with a warning label!" likely flagged exactly this trap — a naive learner might assume simple reduplication transfers across languages the way it does in Mandarin/Cantonese. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 자자 matches the compositional Sino-Korean 자자 exactly, though as explained above this compositional Korean form is not actually the word Koreans use for this relationship. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄚㄐㄚ unique among both words and characters).
