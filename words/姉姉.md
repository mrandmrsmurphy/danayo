## Notes
- [姉 (char)](../characters/姉%20(char).md) "elder sister" reduplicated — the extremely common Mandarin/Cantonese colloquial term for "(elder) sister," written 姐姐/姊姊 in the source languages, folded here into Dan'a'yo's single character 姉 (which stands in for 姊/姐 alike).

姉姉 is the intimate, colloquial address-register form of [姉](姉.md), covering the same sense: an older female relative of the same generation, not strictly a literal sibling.

Unlike the Mandarin/Cantonese case, this reduplication does not naturalize into Japanese, Korean or Vietnamese — each uses its own native address term, which in Korean also varies with the speaker's gender: Japanese お姉さん (oneesan); Korean 언니 (a female speaker addressing her own elder sister) or 누나 (a male speaker addressing his); Vietnamese chị. The `japanese` and `vietnamese` fields are therefore omitted rather than filled with a fabricated reduplicated calque, the same pattern as [伯伯](伯伯.md), [叔叔](叔叔.md) and [女女](女女.md). Korean 자자 is the compositional reading only, not the form Koreans actually use. A learner should not assume that simple reduplication transfers across languages the way it does in Mandarin and Cantonese.
