## Notes
- [障](../characters/障.md) "shield, barricade" + [碍](../characters/碍.md) "hinder" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes 障 as a bound character (碍's own stand-in is [[妨碍]]; transitivity fails, so no `#cranberry`).

障碍 means an obstacle, barrier, or handicap — a hindrance to progress. Mandarin 障礙/障碍 (zhàng'ài), Cantonese 障礙 (zoeng3ngoi6), Korean 장애 (jangae, extremely common in both the "obstacle" and modern "disability" senses), and Sino-Vietnamese chướng ngại all preserve this everyday meaning across the CJKV sphere.

Japanese 障碍/障礙 (しょうげ) has an unusual doubled-reading history: it was originally read しょうげ (Go-on) as a Buddhist term for obstacles to enlightenment from the late Heian period through the Meiji era, when an alternate Kan-on reading しょうがい also arose. To resolve the ambiguity, Japanese eventually settled on distinguishing the spellings — しょうげ stayed tied to 障碍/障礙, while しょうがい migrated to a different spelling entirely, 障害 (using 害 "harm" instead of 碍/礙), which became the standard postwar orthography and the modern word for "disability" (障害者, "person with a disability").
