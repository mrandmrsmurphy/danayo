## Notes
- [提](../characters/提.md) "to raise, present" + [出 (char)](../characters/出%20(char).md) "to exit, go out"

提出 means "to submit, to bring forward, to put forward" — submitting a document, an application, a proposal, or a resignation to an authority. 提 contributes its core sense of "to raise up, to bring to someone's attention" (as in [[提供]] and [[提案]] "proposal," cross-linked from [[提]]'s Words section); 出, a 会意 character combining 止 ("foot") with 凵 ("cave, pit") — the image of a foot stepping out of a cave — contributes the directional sense of "out, forth," seen throughout [[出 (char)]]'s large Words family ([[出版]] "to publish," [[出席]] "to attend," [[抽出]] "to extract").

提出 literally "raises [something] out [into view]" — the bureaucratic and academic act of formally producing a document so that it leaves the submitter's hands and enters official consideration. Its Vietnamese form đề xuất (also meaning "to propose, to suggest" in everyday speech) shows the same compound surviving in a slightly broadened sense outside the strictly bureaucratic "submit a form" usage dominant in Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, and Korean.

It differs from [提示](提示.md) "to present, to show," where the thing is displayed to someone, and from [提案](提案.md) "a proposal," the thing itself that is put forward, since 提出 is the act of handing it in. The Directions page places it for its outward, forward-moving image. The readings are Mandarin tíchū, Cantonese tai4 ceot1, Japanese teishutsu (ていしゅつ), Korean 제출 and Sino-Vietnamese đề xuất. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "to submit, to bring forward, to put forward."
