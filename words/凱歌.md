## Notes
- [凱](../characters/凱.md) "triumphant song" + [歌](../characters/歌.md) "song"
- This word is the stand-in for [凱](../characters/凱.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

凱 is the music played by an army on its return from victory, and 歌 is "song," so 凱歌 is the song of victory sung or played at the triumphal homecoming. The classical ritual setting is the army's return and the sacrifice at the ancestral shrine; the same 凱 appears in [凱旋門](凱旋門.md) "triumphal arch." The word is used literally of a victory song, and far more often figuratively, in set phrases such as 高奏凱歌 and 高唱凱歌 "to sing a paean," meaning to celebrate a great success or victory.

It is a more specific and more elevated word than the general [歌曲](歌曲.md) "song," and its center of meaning is triumph, so it is close in sense to [勝利](勝利.md) "victory" (the event) while naming the celebration of it. The readings are Mandarin kǎigē (alias 凯歌, the simplified form), Cantonese hoi2 go1, Japanese gaika (がいか) and Korean 개가. Dan'a'yo keeps the sense, "triumphant song, victory song," placed under [Art](../lexipedia/Art.md) for the song.
