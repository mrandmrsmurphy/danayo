> [[概然]] "generally so, roughly true"

## Notes
- [蓋](../characters/蓋.md) "cover; probably" + [然](../characters/然%20(char).md) "so" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (蓋's own is [[覆蓋]]; 然's own is itself); 蓋然 is an independent compound. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: `characters/蓋.md` is `kwin: true`, `characters/然 (char).md` is `kwin: false` → compound `false`.

蓋然 means highly probable, likely, plausible — real, quite likely, but stopping short of certain. It is the base of 蓋然性 (Japanese がいぜんせい gaizensei, Korean 개연성 gaeyeonseong, Mandarin gàiránxìng), the standard term across philosophy, statistics, economics, and law for epistemic probability — "not completely certain, but quite likely," distinct from bare possibility ([[可能]]) on one side and necessity ([[必然]]) on the other. Cantonese koi3 jin4 and Vietnamese cái nhiên are the straightforward compositional readings.

**A genuine four-way homophone worth flagging explicitly**: 蓋 and [[概]] share the exact same reading in Mandarin (gài), Japanese on'yomi (GAI), Korean (개), *and* Dan'a'yo (gai/개/ㄍㄚㄧ) — so 蓋然 and [[概然]] are true homophones in all four of those languages, a real and often-asked source of confusion (蓋然性 vs. 概然性) in Japanese and Chinese usage. Vietnamese is the one daughter language that actually keeps them apart: 蓋's Hán Việt is *cái*, while 概's is *khái* — see [[概然]]'s own Notes for the fuller writeup and for how Dan'a'yo uses this real ambiguity as license to split the two senses cleanly rather than treating them as true synonyms.
