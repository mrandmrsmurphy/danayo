## Notes
- [蹠](../characters/蹠.md) "sole of the foot; to tread" + [骨 (char)](../characters/骨%20(char).md) "bone" — stand-in for [蹠](../characters/蹠.md), which cannot appear independently
- A transparent anatomical compound — "sole-bone" — naming the five long bones of the midfoot that bridge the ankle and the toes, more commonly called 中足骨 ("mid-foot bones") in everyday Japanese medical usage, with 蹠骨 (しょこつ, shokotsu) standing as its more literal, classically-rooted synonym. Mandarin zhígǔ, Cantonese zek3 gwat1, and Sino-Korean 척골 (cheokgol) preserve the same literal "tread-bone" image — 蹠 itself, the character for the sole of the foot, survives almost nowhere outside this kind of technical compound, anchored here the way many bound anatomical terms are anchored by their Latin or Greek counterparts in English.

The readings on this page, Japanese しょこつ and Sino-Korean 척골, are the literal Sino-xenic readings of the two characters, and the everyday clinical words differ: Japanese uses 中足骨, and Korean medical Korean uses 중족골 (中足骨) for the metatarsals. The Korean sound 척골 is also the standard reading of 尺骨 (the ulna, a bone of the forearm), a coincidence of sound with no connection in sense. The Body page lists it with the plain gloss "metatarsal bone," and Dan'a'yo keeps that anatomical sense. The alias 跖骨 is the modern Chinese spelling (跖 being the variant of 蹠), and the neighboring skeleton words include [骨格](骨格.md) "skeleton, frame" and [骨盤](骨盤.md) "pelvis."
