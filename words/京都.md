## Notes
- [京](../characters/京.md) "capitol" + [都](../characters/都.md) "capital"

京都 is Kyoto, written "capital city". The same two characters mean "capital" in the abstract in classical Chinese, and in Japanese the word was applied specifically to Heian-kyō (平安京), the capital founded in 794. The reading きょうと is the on-reading of both characters. In Mandarin the second character is read dū "city", not dōu "all".

Kyoto was the seat of the imperial court from 794 until the Meiji restoration moved it to Tokyo in 1868, and it is Japan's principal city of temples, shrines and old craft. Its sites include Kinkaku-ji, Kiyomizu-dera and Fushimi Inari, and many of them are UNESCO World Heritage. It is one of the two "urban prefectures" (府) with Osaka.

In Dan'a'yo 京都 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a 府, an urban prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 교토 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
