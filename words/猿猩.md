## Notes
- [猿](../characters/猿.md) "ape, monkey" + [猩](../characters/猩.md) "orangutan" — this word is the stand-in compound that legitimizes the bound character 猿 (per its own stored `stand_in`).

猿猩 serves as this vault's general word for "monkey, ape," pairing two characters that are each independently bound morphemes in real Chinese/Japanese/Korean — neither 猿 nor 猩 stands alone as an everyday word in any source language, the same pattern documented across this domain's primate/mammal vocabulary (see Animals.md's Semantic Range Notes). 猩 more narrowly denotes the orangutan specifically (as in [[猩猩]] itself, and 猩紅熱 "scarlet fever," named for a blush-red color associated with the animal), while 猿 is the broader "ape/monkey" root, also standing in for gibbons specifically in classical usage.

Mandarin yuánxīng, Cantonese jyun4 sing1, and Korean 원성 (wonseong) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese えんせい (ensei) is likewise compositional from 猿's on'yomi EN + 猩's on'yomi SEI; everyday Japanese instead uses the native kun'yomi さる (saru, already 猿's own stored `japanese_native` value) as the ordinary word for "monkey," since 猿 is not bound in Japanese the way it is in this vault's Dan'a'yo system. Vietnamese viên tinh is likewise compositional, from 猿's Hán Việt viên (one of three attested readings, alongside native vượn and regional ươi) + 猩's tinh. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 온성 diverges from Sino-Korean 원성 in the first syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄛㄋㄙㄝㄫ unique among both words and characters).
