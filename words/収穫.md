## Notes
- [収](../characters/収.md) "gather, collect" + [穫](../characters/穫.md) "harvest, cut grain"
- This word is the stand-in for [穫](../characters/穫.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

収 (traditional 收) is to gather in and take in, and 穫 is to reap grain, so 収穫 is the gathering-in of the crop: the act of harvesting, and by extension the harvest itself, the crop and its yield. It is thus both a verb ("to harvest") and a noun ("a harvest; a crop"), as the English glosses show. The word belongs to the agricultural year, with seasonal phrases like 秋の収穫 "the autumn harvest."

In all the languages it also has a strong figurative sense, "gains, results, what one comes away with" (今日の収穫 "today's catch," or in Chinese 收穫 for the fruits of a trip, a study or an effort), the same image as "reaping what one has sown." This sense is part of the shared meaning and not a Dan'a'yo extension. It is related to, but different from, [収集](収集.md) "to collect, gather" and [収入](収入.md) "income, revenue." The readings are Mandarin shōuhuò (aliases 收穫 and 收获), Cantonese sau1 wok6, Japanese shūkaku (しゅうかく), Korean 수확 and Sino-Vietnamese thu hoạch. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "harvest, crop, yield," placed under both [Food](../lexipedia/Food.md) and [Nature](../lexipedia/Nature.md).
