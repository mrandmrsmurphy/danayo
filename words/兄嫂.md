## Notes
- [兄 (char)](../characters/兄%20(char).md) "older brother" + [嫂](../characters/嫂.md) "sister-in-law" — this word is the stand-in for [嫂](../characters/嫂.md), which is never used on its own.

兄嫂 names one's older brother's wife, a specific position in the traditional East Asian kinship system. English Wiktionary glosses it for Chinese as "one's elder brother and his wife" and, in indirect address, "sister-in-law (elder brother's wife)". The compound is mildly redundant by design: 嫂 alone already means this relationship, and 兄 is prefixed to make the reference explicit.

The Japanese field holds the compositional Sino-Japanese reading けいそう (keisō), built from the on'yomi of 兄 and 嫂 (not checked against a dictionary). The ordinary Japanese word for the relationship is the native あによめ (aninoyome, 兄 + 嫁), which belongs here in the prose and not in the field. Korean 형수 (hyeongsu) is the hanja-form reading listed on English Wiktionary. Mandarin xiōngsǎo and Cantonese hing1 sou2 concatenate transparently. Vietnamese huynh tẩu is reported to be rare in everyday speech, with tẩu tẩu used for direct address (from memory, not re-checked).

No other word shares the 注音 ㄏ⼄ㄫㄙㄚㄨ (checked 2026-10-13).
