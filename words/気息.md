## Notes
- [気 (char)](../characters/気%20(char).md) "feeling" + [息](../characters/息.md) "breathe"
- This word is the viable form of [息](../characters/息.md): the character's meaning (breath; news) is identical, but 息 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 気息 is its `stand_in` compound.

気 is the vital energy or breath-stuff (qi), and 息 is the breath drawn in and out, so 気息 is the breath in its fullest sense: the breathing of a living body and the vital energy that goes with it. It is the word for breathing in descriptions of vitality or its failure (気息奄奄 in Japanese and Chinese, "barely breathing, at death's door"), and in the sense of "spirit" it is the qi or vital-breath, set apart from the soul-words on the Religion page. By extension it also names the air or atmosphere that something gives off, a trace of a presence or a scent, as in the Chinese 生活氣息 "the flavor of everyday life."

The lexipedia places the word accordingly. On [Religion](../lexipedia/Religion.md) it is the main sense, "breath; spirit." On [Sensation](../lexipedia/Sensation.md) it is listed for the weak, tertiary "smell" sense, where the Sensation page itself marks the primary meaning as "breath," and the Emotions page treats its sibling [気](気.md) separately as the broader "feeling, energy" root. The readings are Mandarin qìxī (alias 氣息), Cantonese hei3 sik1, Japanese kisoku (きそく) and Korean 기식. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared primary sense, "breath, vital breath," with "smell" and "spirit" as the secondary uses recorded.
