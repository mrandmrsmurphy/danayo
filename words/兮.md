## Notes
- [兮 (char)](../characters/兮%20(char).md) "pshaw"

兮 is 會意: [[丂]] ("a fork in a tree, breath catching on an obstruction") + [[八]] ("dispersal, breath escaping outward like wind through trees") — together depicting an exhaled sigh or exclamatory breath. It functions purely as a classical exclamatory/rhythmic particle, marking a pause or emphasis in verse, most famous from Xiang Yu's 垓下歌 ("力拔山兮氣蓋世," "My strength plucked up mountains, my spirit surpassed the world") and throughout the 楚辭 (Verses of Chu) tradition.

Japanese has on'yomi けい (kei, confined entirely to classical/literary contexts, since 兮 has no living use in the modern language at all) — no native kun'yomi exists for this character. Korean 혜 (hye) is likewise confined to classical contexts, alongside native 어조사 ("grammatical particle," a descriptive gloss rather than a translation). Vietnamese hề is the Hán Việt reading, used in Vietnamese translations of classical Chinese poetry featuring this particle.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (hei/헤/ㄏㄝㄧ) with another word still awaiting its own turn in this sweep, [[奚]] ("how") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed when it comes up.
