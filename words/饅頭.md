## Notes
- [饅](../characters/饅.md) "steamed bun" + [頭 (char)](../characters/頭%20(char).md) "head" — 饅頭 is 饅's own legitimizing compound (饅's `stand_in` points here, already annotated in its own citation), but since 頭's own `stand_in` is 頭 itself instead, transitivity fails and no `#cranberry` tag applies.

饅頭 (mántou) is the standard Mandarin word for the plain, unfilled steamed bun, and Cantonese 饅頭 (maan6tau4) likewise. Japanese まんじゅう (manjū) usually refers instead to a filled steamed/baked bun (typically sweet, with a bean-paste filling) rather than the plain Chinese type. Korean 만두 (mandu) has diverged further still — it denotes dumplings generally (often filled and boiled or pan-fried, closer to Chinese 餃子/jiǎozi) rather than a steamed bun specifically. Sino-Vietnamese màn thầu, a direct loanword from Cantonese/Mandarin, keeps the original plain-bun sense.

**Fixed a real bug**: `japanese` had まんぢゆう, mixing the historical kana spelling ぢ with an outright typo (big ゆ instead of small ゅ) — corrected to the modern standard まんじゅう.
