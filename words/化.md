## Notes
- [化 (char)](../characters/化%20(char).md) "-ification"

化 is 会意: two 人 ("person") figures, one upright (亻) and one upside down (𠤎) — reversal, transformation, change (Shuowen alternatively treats 𠤎 as purely phonetic). It functions as a highly productive suffix meaning "-ification, -ize, to become/render [X]": [[強化]] ("to strengthen"), [[悪化]] ("to worsen"), [[進化]] ("to evolve"), [[液化]] ("to liquefy"), and the disciplinary noun [[化学]] ("chemistry").

Mandarin huà, Cantonese faa3, and Japanese か/け (ka/ke, on'yomi, fully productive: 文化 ぶんか "culture") alongside native ば(ける) (ba(keru), "to transform, turn into [something supernatural]") all preserve the sense. Korean 화 (hwa) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 되다 (doeda, "to become"). Vietnamese hoá is the Hán Việt reading, extremely well attested (văn hoá, "culture"; biến hoá, "to transform"); the character page's other four stored candidates (goá "widow," hoa "flower" — itself the reading of the derived character [[花]], not 化 — hoé, huế) are unrelated or contamination and were not used here.

This word is part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read hwa/화/ㄏㄨㄚ): [[火]] ("fire") and [[禾]] ("grain"), both still awaiting their own turn in this sweep — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed on each when it comes up.
