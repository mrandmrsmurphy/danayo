## Notes
- [頁 (char)](characters/頁%20(char).md) "page; sheet" — stand-in for [[頁 (char)|頁]], which cannot appear independently

頁 is 象形, originally depicting a head (still visible in its role as the semantic radical for head-related characters like 頭, 顔, 額), and only later acquiring the modern "page, leaf [of a book]" sense via an unrelated phonetic loan for [[葉 (char)|葉]] ("leaf") — Mandarin *yè* and Cantonese *jip6* both directly preserve 葉's own borrowed pronunciation. Korean actually splits the two senses onto two distinct hanja readings rather than reusing one across both meanings: 혈 for the original "head" sense (the character page's own `korean_native` gloss, 머리, documents this older sense) versus 엽, the reading proper to "page" and the one used here, matching this word's own primary gloss.

Japanese preserves an unusually wide three-reading on'yomi set — けつ (KETSU, used here), よう (YOU), and the rarer ゲチ (GECHI, 呉音) — inherited from the character's older "head" role, but modern everyday Japanese overwhelmingly reaches for the English loanword ページ (pēji) for "a page" instead of any native kanji reading, leaving 頁's on'yomi confined mostly to compound/technical contexts (頁岩 ketsugan, "shale," a geological term unrelated to the page sense entirely). Vietnamese hiệt is the reading matching this word's own gloss, picked over the character's other stored variant hệt, which is actually the unrelated Vietnamese word for "identical, exact" (as in giống hệt) rather than a genuine second reading of 頁 — though hvdic.thivien.net does list both hiệt and hệt as genuine, sense-undifferentiated readings of 頁 itself, so hệt may not be purely a coincidental homograph after all. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 헏 (hed, from Middle Chinese 匣母 ɣ + 屑開韻 et) diverges from Sino-Korean 엽 entirely, since Dan'a'yo derives its syllable from 頁's own native "head"-lineage Middle Chinese reading rather than the borrowed "page" pronunciation. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄝㄊ shared only by [[歇]], whose own `stand_in` is 間歇, not itself).
