## Notes
- [頁 (char)](../characters/頁%20(char).md) "page; sheet" — stand-in for [[頁 (char)|頁]], which cannot appear independently

頁 is 象形, originally depicting a head (still visible in its role as the semantic radical for head-related characters like 頭, 顔, 額), and only later narrowing in modern usage to "page, leaf [of a book]" — a semantic shift the character page's own korean_native gloss ("머리," "head") still preserves as the older sense even though the frontmatter's primary English gloss reflects the newer one. **Note**: `characters/頁 (char).md` remains genuinely unperfected (blank `pos`, `## Notes` holding only two dangling CC-initial/final wikilinks, no `date-last-perfect`) — an eighth character page found in this state this sweep — flagged for the character-perfection sweep rather than rebuilt here.

Japanese preserves an unusually wide three-reading on'yomi set — けつ (KETSU, used here), よう (YOU), and the rare けち (KECHI) — inherited from the character's older "head" role, but modern everyday Japanese overwhelmingly reaches for the English loanword ページ (pēji) for "a page" instead of any native kanji reading, leaving 頁's on'yomi confined mostly to compound/technical contexts (頁岩 ketsugan, "shale," a geological term unrelated to the page sense entirely). Vietnamese hiệt is the reading matching this word's own gloss, picked over the character's other stored variant hệt, which is actually the unrelated Vietnamese word for "identical, exact" (as in giống hệt) rather than a genuine second reading of 頁. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 헏 (hed, from Middle Chinese 匣母 ɣ + 屑開韻 et) diverges from Sino-Korean 혈 in the vowel. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄝㄊ shared only by [[歇]], whose own `stand_in` is 間歇, not itself).
