## Notes
- [檸](characters/檸.md) "lemon" + [檬](characters/檬.md) "lemon" — stand-in for both [[檸]] and [[檬]], neither of which can appear independently. This is a genuine cranberry pair: 檸 and 檬 exist solely to spell this one word, together.

檸檬 is purely phonetic, a transliteration of the Western word "lemon" — 檸's reading was deliberately overridden (2026-07-12) from its Middle-Chinese-derived value to ㄌㄝ, and 檬's likewise to ㄇㄛㄋ, specifically so that the compound spells "lemon" directly. Neither character is attested in the Classical Chinese corpus (`mc_id: 0` on both), and the daughter languages show no shared inherited reading at all: Japanese レモン (remon) and Korean 레몬 (remon) both borrow the Western word directly rather than using this character compound; Vietnamese uses its own native word chanh (quả chanh, "a lemon," with the fruit classifier quả) rather than any Sino-Vietnamese rendering of 檸檬.

**Content corrected**: `kwin` was `false`; both [[檸]] (char) and [[檬]] (char) are individually kwin, so the AND-rule was actually satisfied — corrected to `true`. Also cleaned up the malformed `vietnamese` value (previously the comma-joined string "quả chanh, chanh") down to the single native word chanh, with the fuller phrase quả chanh noted in prose.
