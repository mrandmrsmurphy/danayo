## Notes
- [雲](../characters/雲%20(char).md) "cloud" (phonetic borrowing) + [呑](../characters/呑.md) "swallowing" (stand-in for 雲呑) — a folk-etymological Cantonese respelling of the dish, evoking clouds floating in soup.
- This term is originally written as 餛飩／馄饨 (húntun) and the two terms are homophones in Cantonese. But 餛飩／馄饨 (húntun) usually refers to northern-style wontons while 雲吞／云吞 (yúntūn) refers to Cantonese-style wontons.

Mandarin yúntūn and Cantonese wan4 tan1 are the real, standard compositional readings, matching 雲's and 呑's own stored citations exactly — this dish name genuinely originates in Cantonese. Japanese ワンタン (wantan) and Korean 완탕 are both real, direct phonetic loans from the Cantonese pronunciation (wan4 tan1) rather than Sino-Xenic compositional readings of the characters — the same borrowing pattern found in English "wonton." Vietnamese hoành thánh is likewise a real, standard loanword.

`kwin` is false: Korean 완탕, a phonetic loan, does not match the compositional Sino-Korean stacking of 雲's own 운 + 呑's own 탄 (운탄) — despite both characters individually being kwin-true, there is no real Sino-Korean compound to match against. No homophones (注音 ㄨㄋㄊㄚㄋ unique among both words and characters).
