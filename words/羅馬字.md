## Notes
- [羅](../characters/羅.md) "Rome" + [馬 (char)](../characters/馬%20(char).md) "horse" + [字 (char)](../characters/字%20(char).md) "character" — none of the three characters' own `stand_in` points here (羅's is [[羅馬]], 馬's is [[馬]] itself, 字's is [[字]] itself), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 羅 and 字 are each individually `kwin: false`.

羅馬字 names Latin letters, the Roman alphabet, or romanization more generally — literally "Roman characters."

Mandarin luómǎzì, Cantonese lo4 maa5 zi6, and Korean 로마자 (romaja) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 羅馬字 is itself a common attested word for "Roman alphabet" in all three. Japanese uses the loanword-plus-character form ローマ字 (Rōmaji, literally "Rome" written in katakana + 字), the standard term for Japanese romanization systems. Vietnamese la mã tự is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
