## Notes
- [世](../characters/世.md) "world, generation" + [宗](../characters/宗.md) "ancestor, founder" — a posthumous temple name (廟號) granted to a monarch in the Sinitic tradition, most famously Korea's 世宗[[大王]] (King Sejong the Great, r. 1418–1450), the fourth king of Joseon and creator of the Korean alphabet. The same temple name was also borne by several Chinese emperors, including the Han Wudi (Emperor Wu of Han), the Ming Jiajing Emperor, and the Qing Yongzheng Emperor — temple names are a fixed stock of titles reused across dynasties, not unique to a single monarch.

世宗 is also the short name of the city 世宗特別自治市 (세종특별자치시), Sejong Special Self-Governing City, the administrative capital of South Korea in the central part of the country, built from 2007 and officially established in 2012 on land taken from the old 忠清 territory (see [[忠清]]), and named for King Sejong; many ministries moved there from [[漢城]] (Seoul) (from memory, not re-checked). The long form carries the status title 特別自治市 "special self-governing city" in place of the 廣域市 "metropolitan city" of the other large cities, and the bare name 世宗 is used for the city. The royal and the city sense share one word because the city is named directly for the king.
