## Notes
- [一 (char)](../characters/一%20(char).md) "one" + [端](../characters/端.md) "end, beginning, cause" — literally "one end/beginning," hence "one aspect [of a larger matter]" or, idiomatically, "a person of some accomplishment" (一端の人, an individual who has attained a recognized level of skill or standing).

一端 covers a real spread from the concrete "one end/tip [of something]" to the abstract "one aspect, one side [of an issue]" — the same end-of-a-thing-as-facet-of-an-issue metaphor found in English "one end of the argument." 端 itself carries the sense "origin, cause" independently (見端, "to see the first sprouts/beginnings of something"; 造端, "to instigate, to originate"), which this compound's "beginning" reading draws on directly.

Vietnamese nhất đoan (confirmed via web search) uses đoan in its "beginning, origin, cause" sense specifically — the same word family that gives đầu mối ("a starting point, a lead," in fully native Vietnamese vocabulary) its conceptual shape, though đầu mối itself isn't derived from 端 directly. Japanese いったん shows the same sokuon gemination (いち→いっ- before an unvoiced obstruent) already documented on [[一体]]/[[一定]]/[[一斉]]/[[一旦]] earlier in this stretch of the sweep — this word happens to share its Japanese reading exactly with [[一旦]] despite the two being semantically unrelated and written with different second characters (端 vs 旦), a coincidental homophony in Japanese specifically that doesn't carry over to Dan'a'yo, Mandarin, Cantonese, Korean, or Vietnamese. `kwin` is false, the same [[一 (char)]] root-cause pattern confirmed across every 一-initial compound checked this stretch. No homophones at the Dan'a'yo level (注音 ㄧㄊㄉ⺢ㄋ unique among both words and characters).
