## Notes
- [墨](../characters/墨.md) "ink" + [西](../characters/西.md) "west" + [哥](../characters/哥.md) "elder brother" — phonetic rendering of *México*; the characters carry no semantic content

墨西哥 is Mexico: a phonetic rendering of the Spanish *México*, with 墨, ink, plus 西, west, plus 哥, elder brother, used only for their sounds. English Wiktionary lists it for Chinese (Mandarin Mòxīgē, Cantonese mak6 sai1 go1) as the country in North America, and lists Japanese ぼくせいか only as an obsolete spelling of メキシコ, the present Japanese name. For Korean it lists 멕시코, with no character spelling, and Vietnamese Mê-hi-cô is likewise a phonetic form, so those names are the real words and the Japanese reading here is built from the characters. The alias 墨 is the usual one-character abbreviation for Mexico in Chinese.

English Wikipedia gives several Nahuatl etymologies for the name, including *Mexitli*, "place in the middle of the century plant", and *Mēxihco*, "place in the navel of the moon". It describes the Manila galleon trade, between Manila and Acapulco in New Spain, as in use from 1565 to 1815.
