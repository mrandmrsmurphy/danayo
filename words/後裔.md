## Notes
- [後 (char)](../characters/後%20(char).md) "after" + [裔](../characters/裔.md) "descendant" — descendant, posterity; stand-in for [裔](../characters/裔.md), which cannot appear independently. Attested across all five languages: Mandarin hòuyì, Cantonese hau6 jeoi6, Japanese こうえい, Korean 후예, Vietnamese hậu duệ.

The Kinship page lists 後裔 as "descendant, posterity — a more formal/literary near-synonym of [子孫](子孫.md)," and its duplicate-terminology audit confirms the pair as a real distinction, concrete versus literary "descendants": 子孫 is the plain word for children and grandchildren, while 後裔 is the register of genealogies and history, used for a line of descent over many generations, often of a famous ancestor (the 後裔 of a dynasty or a sage). Its counterpart for the other direction is [祖先](祖先.md) "ancestor." Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "descendant, posterity," with the formal register as its characteristic. The Korean field 후예 differs from the Dan'a'yo 諺文 훗여 in both syllables, so `kwin` is false. The usage description of 後裔 beyond the Kinship page's own wording is from memory and has not been checked against a source.
