## Notes
- [香](../characters/香.md) "incense, fragrant, aroma" + [気 (char)](../characters/気%20(char).md) "feeling" — legitimizing note: 香's own `stand_in` is [[香気]] itself, already annotated in its own citation; 気's own `stand_in` is 気 itself, so transitivity fails — no `#cranberry`.

香気 carries a distinctly positive connotation — a pleasant fragrance or aroma, as opposed to a neutral smell (匂い) or an unpleasant one (臭気, "stench"). Mandarin xiāngqì, Cantonese hoeng1hei3, Japanese こうき, and Korean 향기 are all standard, fully compositional terms with this same pleasant-scent sense. Sino-Vietnamese hương khí is attested (notably in classical/literary usage, e.g. Truyện Kiều's "hương khí ngạt ngào"), though everyday modern Vietnamese more often reaches for the native compound mùi hương or hương thơm.
