## Notes
- [神 (char)](../characters/神%20(char).md) "god, deity, spirit" + [話 (char)](../characters/話%20(char).md) "speak on, talk about" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (神's is [[神]] itself, 話's is [[話]] itself), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 話 is individually `kwin: false`.

神話 literally means "talk of the gods" — the traditional stories, legends, and cosmologies of a culture concerning deities, creation, and the origins of the world, distinct from 伝説 ("legend," typically about semi-historical human heroes) and 童話 ("fairy tale," a children's story without religious weight, though "fairytale" appears as a loose secondary English gloss here). It is highly productive as a modifier: 神話学 ("mythology, the academic study of myth"), ギリシャ神話 ("Greek mythology"), 都市伝説 is related but distinct (urban legend, not 神話 proper).

Mandarin shénhuà, Cantonese san4 waa2, Japanese しんわ (shinwa), Korean 신화 (sinhwa), and Vietnamese thần thoại are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the ordinary everyday word for "myth/mythology" in its language.
