## Notes
- [従 (char)](../characters/従%20(char).md) "obey, follow, collateral" + [兄 (char)](../characters/兄%20(char).md) "older brother" + [弟 (char)](../characters/弟%20(char).md) "younger brother" — spelling out [[従兄]] and [[従弟]] together, the same coordinating pattern as [[兄弟姉妹]].

従兄弟 is the collective/generic form: male cousins as a class, or "cousin" used without specifying older or younger. Classically restricted to a father's brother's sons; modern living Japanese generalizes it to cousins of either parent's side, and — notably — 従兄弟 is one of the most common ways to write plain いとこ (itoko) in Japanese, the everyday word for "cousin" full stop, more often than the fully kanji-differentiated じゅうけいてい reading. Cantonese cung4 hing1 dai6 and Mandarin cóngxiōngdì are both directly attested on Wiktionary as "cousin (father's brother's son)," though modern colloquial Mandarin reaches for 堂兄弟 instead. Korean 종형제 and Vietnamese tòng huynh đệ (anh em họ con nhà bác/chú) are both real Sino compounds, secondary to native/other-Sino alternatives (사촌 형제; đường huynh đệ).
