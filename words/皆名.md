## Notes
- [皆 (char)](../characters/皆%20(char).md) "all; every; universal" + [名 (char)](../characters/名%20(char).md) "name; polite person classifier"

皆名 is the collective (universal) correlative in the 名 ("name; polite person") column of the correlative table: "everyone (polite), all of you or them taken together, ladies and gentlemen", the deferential counterpart of [皆人](皆人.md). The character [皆 (char)](../characters/皆%20(char).md) (Dan'a'yo *gye*, ㄍ⼶) is the quantifier "all; every", the free word being [皆](皆.md); [名 (char)](../characters/名%20(char).md) (Dan'a'yo *ming*, ㄇㄧㄫ) is "name", and the word page [名](名.md) records its second job as a polite classifier for counting people (五名 "five persons, polite", 何名様 "how many guests, very honorific"). Where the distributive [毎名](毎名.md) goes through the honoured persons one at a time, 皆名 treats them as one group: the predicate holds of the whole company at once, which makes it suited to addressing an audience. Its part of speech is 代詞 (pronoun), like the rest of the 皆 row ([皆事](皆事.md), [皆人](皆人.md), [皆物](皆物.md), [皆処](皆処.md) and so on) and like [此名](此名.md), [其名](其名.md), [彼名](彼名.md), [何名](何名.md) and [某名](某名.md); only [毎名](毎名.md) in this column is adverbial (副用名詞). Illustrative only (invented, not from `grammar/`): 皆名、歓迎 "welcome, everyone (polite)".

Against its column neighbours: the plain collective is [皆人](皆人.md), and 皆名 is its polite counterpart, the same register swap as [此人](此人.md) against [此名](此名.md). The distributive is [毎名](毎名.md) "each person (polite)". [某名](某名.md) is the indefinite "someone (polite)", naming an unspecified individual, not a universal, and [何名](何名.md) is the interrogative "who?" at the very polite end of the ladder "誰 is normal, 何人 is rude, 何名 is very polite" (`grammar/文法 - 97品詞.md`, line 154). The deictic cells [此名](此名.md), [其名](其名.md) and [彼名](彼名.md) each point at one particular honoured person, whereas 皆名 covers all of them. Note that [名](名.md)'s own prose glosses 此名 as "this name; what this is called"; that conflicts with the documented function of the 名 column ("polite person"), which this page follows.

The false friend is [皆様](皆様.md). In this vault 皆様 is the 様 ("manner") cell of the same row, "in every way; in all respects". Japanese 皆様 / 皆さま (みなさま) and 皆さん (みなさん), by contrast, mean "everybody, everyone", the honorific pronoun used to address a group, and that sense belongs here to 皆名 (polite) and [皆人](皆人.md) (neutral), not to 皆様. The overlap exists because Japanese uses 様 (*sama*) as an honorific suffix, while Dan'a'yo uses 様 as the manner column and 名 as the polite person column.

Cross-linguistic fields were checked against Wiktionary on 2026-10-11. Wiktionary has no entry for 皆名, so no `mandarin`, `cantonese`, `japanese`, `korean` or `vietnamese` field is entered: the word is a Dan'a'yo compositional construction, and none of the readings below is an attested reading of the compound. The earlier fields (Mandarin gèwèi, Japanese 皆様, Korean 여러분) were real equivalents of the meaning, not readings of 皆名, and are removed for that reason. Established equivalents: Mandarin 各位 (gèwèi, Cantonese gok3 wai6-2) is a pronoun, polite, "ladies and gentlemen; everybody" (examples 各位來賓 "distinguished guests", 多謝各位 "thank you everyone"), and has its own vault page, [各位](各位.md), with 各 "each" rather than 皆 "all"; Japanese 皆さん (みなさん) is a pronoun "everybody, everyone", used when addressing a group, formed from 皆 (mina) plus the honorific suffix さん; Japanese 皆さま (spelled 皆様, みなさま) is an honorific pronoun "everybody, everyone"; Korean 여러분 is a noun from 여러 "various" + 분 "persons", used when addressing a group, "ladies and gentlemen; everyone (present)", and also a polite plural "you". 분 plays the role 名 plays here as the polite person classifier; the vault has no word page for 여러분, so it is mentioned unlinked. Vietnamese is omitted: from memory *quý vị* or *các vị* would be the polite equivalent, not re-checked, and hvdic.thivien.net covers single characters, not phrases. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared CJKV sense "everyone (polite)"; no narrowing is needed.

On [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md)'s correlative table (line 126 is the 皆 ("all") row; the same chart is in `grammar/文法 - 97品詞.md`, line 147 in the 名 ("name; polite person") row, with the column header cell 皆 on line 142), 皆名 is the fourth cell of the 皆 row, after [皆事](皆事.md) "all matters", [皆物](皆物.md) "all things" and [皆人](皆人.md) "all people", and before [皆処](皆処.md) "all places", [皆時](皆時.md) "all times", [皆様](皆様.md) "all manners", [皆多](皆多.md) "the whole amount" and [皆類](皆類.md) "all kinds". The table is 7 determiners (此, 其, 彼, 何, 毎, 某, 皆) × 9 noun classes (事, 物, 人, 名, 処, 時, 様, 多, 類), 63 forms, so 皆名 is parsed compositionally: 皆 = collective, 名 = polite person. A learner who knows the 人 column can derive the polite form by swapping 人 for 名.

The spellings are the plain concatenation of the two character pages: 羅馬字 *gye* + *ming* = gyeming, 諺文 겨 + 밍 = 겨밍, 注音 ㄍ⼶ + ㄇㄧㄫ = ㄍ⼶ㄇㄧㄫ, each checked letter by letter against 皆 (char) and 名 (char). The 注音 carries a syllable-boundary dot, ㄍ⼶·ㄇㄧㄫ, because the string has a second complete partition: `grammar/文法 - 02音韻論.md` allows ㄇ as a syllable-final consonant, and both ㄍ⼶ㄇ (*gyem*, whose syllable page glosses it "cocoon") and ㄧㄫ exist in `syllables/`, so ㄍ⼶ㄇ + ㄧㄫ would also be a legal reading. The dot marks the intended ㄍ⼶ + ㄇㄧㄫ. The same applies to [皆人](皆人.md) (ㄍ⼶ㄋ + ㄧㄋ), and contrasts with [皆事](皆事.md) and [皆処](皆処.md), whose second syllables begin with a consonant that cannot close a syllable and so need no dot.
