## Notes
- [入 (char)](../characters/入%20(char).md) "enter"

入 is 象形, depicting an inward-pointing wedge or arrowhead — entry, going in, as opposed to [[出]] ("exit, go out"). It is one of the most basic and productive verbs in the language, heading compounds for physical entry ([[入口]] "entrance," [[入場]] "to enter a venue"), intrusion ([[侵入]] "to invade," [[闖入]] "to break in"), and joining/incorporation ([[入籍]] "to naturalize, enter a household register," [[参入]] "to enter, join [a market]"), and appears in the chengyu [[単刀直入]] ("to go straight to the point," literally "a single blade entering directly").

Mandarin rù, Cantonese jap6, and Japanese にゅう/じゅ/じゅう (nyū/ju/jū, on'yomi, fully productive: 入学 にゅうがく "school enrollment") alongside native い(る)/はい(る) (i(ru)/hai(ru), "to enter," the everyday verb) all preserve the sense. Korean 입 (ip) is the Sino-Korean reading used throughout compounds, alongside native 들다 (deulda, "to enter," the everyday native verb). Vietnamese nhập is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in nhập khẩu ("to import") and nhập cảnh ("to enter a country"); the character page's other three stored candidates (nhạp, nhép, nhẹp) are tonal/orthographic near-misses and look like corpus noise.
