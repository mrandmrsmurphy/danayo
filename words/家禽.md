## Notes
- [家](../characters/家.md) "house, family" + [禽](../characters/禽.md) "fowl" — domesticated birds raised for meat, eggs, or feathers; a bird-specific subset of livestock (家畜, which covers mammals too).

家禽/가금/gia cầm/かきん all keep the same precise scope across the family — birds only, distinct from four-legged livestock. Japanese carries one register note: かきん reads as a bit formal or technical in casual conversation, appropriate for writing and formal explanation, while everyday spoken Japanese more often names the specific bird (鶏, "chicken") or says 養鶏 ("poultry farming") instead of the general category term.

English Wiktionary gives the Chinese noun as "domestic fowls; poultry", with the measure word 隻, the Hokkien synonym 雞鴨 and the antonym 野禽 ("wild fowl"). Its Japanese entry has the same gloss and the same antonym, and names [[家畜]] ("livestock; domesticated animals") as the hypernym, which agrees with the first bullet's contrast. Korean 가금 and Vietnamese gia cầm are listed with the same gloss, "poultry".

Mandarin jiāqín and Cantonese gaa1 kam4 are as at English Wiktionary. The register note about Japanese in the paragraph above is not from Wiktionary and I have not re-checked it.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄍㄚ·ㄎㄧㄇ (checked 2026-10-12).
