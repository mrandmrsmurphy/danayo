## Notes
- [勤](../characters/勤.md) "diligent; work" + [番 (char)](../characters/番%20(char).md) "turn, shift" — serving a duty shift.

勤番 is serving a turn of duty: taking one's [[番]] (shift) of work as an act, not the shift itself. Japanese きんばん is confirmed on the Japanese Wiktionary with two senses: "serving a duty in rotation" and, in the Edo period, a daimyo's retainers taking turns to stay at their lord's residences in Edo and Osaka. Chinese and Korean are not confirmed (the proposed readings qínfān and geunbeon are unverified), so those fields are left blank. Dan'a'yo takes the general sense. Compare [[勤務]] (duty, work at a post) and [[当番]]. Like the other shift words, it belongs to the 番 family, a systematic schema that Dan'a'yo imposes for predictability rather than a claim about the ancient usage.
