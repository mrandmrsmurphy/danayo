## Notes
- [白 (char)](../characters/白%20(char).md) "white" + [熊 (char)](../characters/熊%20(char).md) "bear" — literally "white bear."

Mandarin báixióng and Cantonese baak6 hung4 are straightforward Sino compounds, but Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese all break the pattern with native calques instead: Japanese しろくま (shirokuma, 白い+熊 in native kun'yomi), Korean 흰곰 (huingom, native 흰 "white" + 곰 "bear," not Sino 백웅), and Vietnamese gấu trắng (native "bear white," word order reversed from the Sino/English pattern). Polar bears have no traditional presence south of the Sinosphere's temperate zone, so unlike most everyday animal names these languages coined descriptive native terms rather than adopting the Chinese compound — the reverse of the usual pattern where Sino vocabulary dominates technical or exotic referents.
