## Notes
- [机](../characters/机.md) "machine" + [関 (char)](../characters/関%20(char).md) "close" — the spelling follows the Dan'a'yo forms of the characters; the alias 機關 is the traditional spelling.

机関 is "machine; mechanism; organ; organization". English Wiktionary (at the traditional spelling 機關) gives the Chinese word three senses: a government organization, institution or agency (金融機關, "financial institution"; 司法機關, "judicial authorities"; 最高立法機關, "top legislative body"); a mechanism (啟動機關, "starting mechanism"); and, figuratively, a scheme or intrigue. As an attributive adjective it means "machine-operated". Derived terms include 機關槍 ("machine gun") and 國家機關 ("state organ"). Japanese 機関 (きかん, kikan) is the same word, and Korean 기관 ("engine; machine; agency") and Vietnamese cơ quan ("organ") are its Sino-Korean and Sino-Vietnamese forms.

The word straddles two domains, which is why it sits on both pages: on Tools it is the mechanism, the moving working part, and on Government it is the institution that carries out a function of the state. The Tools page marks the institutional register as distinct from the plain physical machine.

Mandarin jīguān and Cantonese gei1 gwaan1 are as at English Wiktionary.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄍㄧㄜㄍ⺢ㄇ (checked 2026-10-12).
