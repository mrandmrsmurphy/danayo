## Notes
- [只 (char)](../characters/只%20(char).md) "only; just; merely" — 只 is also the `stand_in` of its own character page, i.e. the word that legitimizes the bound character 只

只 is the restrictive (exclusive) topic-retaining particle of Dan'a'yo, "only; just; merely; simply". Like all 関詞 it is a **suffix**: it attaches directly after a noun phrase and restricts the predicate to that noun alone. The vault's grammar gives 一人只 "only one person" ([[文法 - 04句法]]), and 連傑只 市場於 購買 "Only Jet buys at the market" and 我之描只 寺院於 睡眠 "Only my cat sleeps at the temple" ([[文法 - 05形態]]). Further illustrative examples (invented for this page, not taken from a source text): 我只 "only I; it's only me", 一冊只 "just one volume". Per the grammar, a restrictive suffix such as 只 does not turn the noun phrase into an adverb: the phrase stays the topic/subject and merely carries the restriction. Because case particles and restrictive particles never stack, 只 is not attached after a case-marked phrase (no Japanese-style には/でも sequences); Dan'a'yo restructures the phrase instead. Its siblings are [[且]] "also" (inclusive, widening the scope) and [[就]] "just, merely" (delimitative); 只 narrows to exactly the marked noun.

Do not confuse 只 with [[唯]] "only", which is a 修飾語 (prefixing modifier) rather than a suffix, nor with [[不過]] and [[僅僅]]. [[Dimensions]] distinguishes the four "only" words by force and register: 只 is the everyday, neutral "just, only", 唯 more literary and emphatic, 不過 the mildest ("nothing more than"), 僅僅 stressing bare sufficiency. Dan'a'yo-specific sense: none beyond the shared CJKV "only"; the only Dan'a'yo-specific feature is the suffixing syntax.

Character and spelling. 只 is the Dan'a'yo form; the character page lists the aliases 衹 and 隻. 衹 is a traditional variant spelling of this same "only" word (Wiktionary treats 衹/祇/祗 as traditional forms of 只, zhǐ). 隻 is a different word: Mandarin zhī, Cantonese zek3, "single; classifier for birds, boats, one of a pair" (Japanese セキ, Korean 척), unrelated in etymology, which merges into 只 only under mainland Simplified Chinese orthography; it is aliased here only to mirror the character page, and the "only" sense of this word is not the 隻 sense. Mandarin 只 is thus read zhǐ ("only", the sense used here) while the simplified-form classifier is read zhī. The separate character [[祇]] (qí, "earth spirit", as in 天神地祇) is a distinct word and is not an alias. No separate word file exists for 隻 or 衹.

Cross-linguistic fields. The `japanese` (し, go-on/kan-on both し per Wiktionary), `korean` (지, North Korean reading) and `vietnamese` (chỉ, listed on Wiktionary as the Sino-Vietnamese form meaning "only; just; simply; merely") fields give the character's Sino-Xenic bound readings, not the native equivalents. The native/functional equivalents are: Mandarin 只 (also 仅, 光), Cantonese 淨係 (zing6 hai6) and 只係 (zi2 hai6), Japanese だけ (dake) and しか…ない (shika…nai), with のみ in literary register, Korean 만 (man) and 뿐 (ppun), 오직 "solely" as an adverb, and Vietnamese chỉ (the same Hán-Việt syllable is the everyday word: chỉ có một "there is only one"). Mandarin examples 只有, 只是, 只能 are standard (the first and third are on Wiktionary's compound list; 只好 "have no choice but to" is given from memory, not re-checked).

只 reads ce (ㄑㄝ, 처): Middle Chinese 章母 + 支三開韻, as on the [character page](../characters/只%20(char).md).
