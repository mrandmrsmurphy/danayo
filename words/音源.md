## Notes
- [音](../characters/音.md) "sound" + [源](../characters/源.md) "source, origin" — "the source of a sound".

Wiktionary: Japanese 音源 (おんげん) "source of a sound; (computing) a device or chip that produces sound, tone generator; (music) audio, recording"; Chinese 音源 (yīnyuán) "(music) audio, recording", with fandom senses (chart performance; vocal-synth voicebank). The Dan'a'yo sense below is a different, specialised one.

Dan'a'yo uses the word in a different, specialised way, as the heading 音源推定 of [[文法 - 03文字法]]. The chapter says that the pronunciation system of Dan'a'yo is based on 中古漢語 (Middle Chinese), with the 鄭張尚芳韻表 (the Zhengzhang Shangfang rime table) as the 本表, the primary table, and that a rule-of-thumb table for initials and finals is given in an appendix. From these rules each character gets a rule-derived pronunciation (規則発音, a rule-derived [[発音]] in the chapter's wording), adjusted where the syllable would be too crowded. In that heading the 音源 is therefore the source from which a character's Dan'a'yo reading is derived: the Middle Chinese reading. The chapter does not define the term separately; this page records only what the heading and its section say, so the gloss "source reading" is an interpretation of that heading, not a stated definition. Read with its partner [[推定]], 音源推定 is the "derivation (reconstruction) of the sound source".

The word is related to, but distinct from, [[字源]] "etymology of a character" and [[根源]] "root, origin": 字源 is where a *character* comes from, whereas this 音源 is where a character's *sound* comes from. It also stands beside [[正音]] "correct pronunciation" and [[発音]] "pronunciation", the outputs of the derivation rather than its input.
