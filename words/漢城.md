## Notes
- [漢](../characters/漢.md) "Han (Chinese)" + [城](../characters/城.md) "castle, wall" — no stand-in relationship applies (漢's own stand-in is [漢族](漢族.md); 城's own is [城郭](城郭.md)).

漢城 (Hanseong) was the official name of Seoul throughout the Joseon dynasty (from 1395's 漢城府, "Hanseong Prefecture," onward), and remained the standard Chinese-language exonym for the city until 2005, when Seoul's city government formally requested Chinese-language media switch to the phonetic transliteration 首爾 (Shǒu'ěr) instead — since 漢城, like [東京](東京.md)/[北京](北京.md), names the city by a historical Sinitic compound rather than rendering Seoul's own actual name, 서울 (a native Korean word meaning "capital," with no traditional hanja spelling of its own at all). Dan'a'yo follows this vault's established convention for East Asian capital names ([東京](東京.md), [北京](北京.md)) in using the real historical exonym rather than a transliteration, fully aware that this specific exonym is one the city itself has asked the world to retire.

Mandarin Hànchéng, Cantonese hon3 sing4, Japanese かんじょう (kanjō, attested — see 漢城府 in Japanese historical sources), Korean 한성 (Hanseong, the city's own historical name for itself), and Vietnamese Hán Thành (attested, used of the five Joseon-era palaces still standing in Seoul) are all real, historically attested readings, not compositional guesses.


**Not to be confused with** [京城](京城.md) (Gyeongseong/Keijō), the Japanese colonial-era name for the same city (1910–1945) — that word is reserved on this vault for its plain compositional meaning, "capital city, metropolis," rather than assigned to Seoul specifically, precisely to keep this history straight.
