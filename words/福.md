## Notes
- [福](../characters/福%20(char).md) "blessing, happiness, good fortune, prosperity" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 福 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

**Fixed a real bug**: `羅馬字`/`諺文` previously stored pug/푹, but the character page's own authoritative reading is fug/뿍 (`注音` ㄈㄨㄎ was already correct) — corrected to match. This completes the three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group already documented on [[副]]'s own page: 福, 副, and [[腹]] all share the reading fug/뿍/ㄈㄨㄎ, a deliberate override keeping an f-initial and -k coda across all three phonetic siblings despite most real languages deriving a plain p-initial for this character family.

福 names blessing, happiness, good fortune, and prosperity broadly — one of the most visually and culturally prominent characters in East Asian folk tradition, displayed inverted (倒福) on doors during Chinese New Year as a pun on 到 ("arrive," since "upside-down fortune" sounds like "fortune arrives"). It is highly productive: 幸福 ("happy, blessed"), 福祉 ("welfare, well-being"), 祝福 ("to bless, blessing").

Mandarin fú, Cantonese fuk1, and Korean 복 (bok, extremely common in given names and auspicious symbolism) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese さいはひ (saiwahi, archaic kana spelling of modern さいわい, "happiness") is a native kun-reading rather than the on-reading ふく (fuku) seen in compounds like 幸福 (こうふく). Vietnamese phúc/phước are both attested Hán Việt readings (phúc more Northern/formal, phước more Southern/colloquial), with Phúc also extremely common as a given name.
