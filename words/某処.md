## Notes
- [某 (char)](../characters/某%20(char).md) "some; a certain; indefinite" + [処 (char)](../characters/処%20(char).md) "place; location"

某処 is the indefinite place correlative — "somewhere; at some unspecified location." Like all 某-row entries, it refers to a place the speaker either does not know or chooses not to specify: the location is presumed to exist, but its identity is withheld. This contrasts with the interrogative 何処 (asking for the location's identity) and the distal 彼処 (pointing to a known but distant place). 某処 sits between them: the place exists and may even be known, but the speaker declines to name it.

Mandarin 某處 (mǒu chù) is the direct compositional equivalent and is well attested in formal and journalistic Chinese: 藏在某處 ("hidden somewhere"); 發生於某處 ("occurring at a certain location"); 駐紮在某處的軍隊 ("troops stationed at some undisclosed location"). Japanese どこか (dokoka, "somewhere" — 何処+か indefinite suffix) is the natural spoken equivalent; Vietnamese ở chỗ nào (at some place or other) is the analytic equivalent. Korean 어떤 곳 (eotteon got, "a certain place") or 어딘가 (eodin-ga, "somewhere — indefinite") cover the same ground.

某処 fills a real lexical gap visible in the source languages: none has a single dedicated indefinite place pronoun that fits a systematic paradigm the way Dan'a'yo's correlative system provides one (Chinese 某處 is the nearest, but is formal). Japanese どこか is the closest but is formed by suffix (どこ + the particle か), not a separate compound; Korean uses circumlocution. In Dan'a'yo, 某処 is as compact and compositionally transparent as 此処, 其処, or 何処 — the indefiniteness is simply a property of the 某 row, applied uniformly across all nine semantic columns.
The Grammar page sets 某処 in its correlative table, in the 処 ("place") column and the 某 ("a certain") row, beside [某事](某事.md), [某物](某物.md) and [某人](某人.md); the same row has [某名](某名.md). The word is tagged `neologism`, so no compositional readings were added; its `japanese`, `korean` and `vietnamese` fields hold the spoken equivalents (どこか, 어떤 곳, ở chỗ nào) rather than Sino-Xenic readings, as on the sibling correlative pages. The Mandarin phrases (藏在某處, 發生於某處, 駐紮在某處的軍隊) are illustrative constructions, not attested quotations.
