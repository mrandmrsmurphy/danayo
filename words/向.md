## Notes
- [向 (char)](../characters/向%20(char).md) "toward; -ward" — 向 is also the `stand_in` of its own character page, i.e. the word that legitimizes the bound character 向

向 is the allative case particle (向格) of Dan'a'yo, marking the direction of motion or orientation: "toward; in the direction of." Like every Dan'a'yo adposition it is a **suffix**: it follows the noun phrase it governs ([[文法 - 04句法]], 関詞). Illustrative examples (invented for this page, not taken from a source text): 東向 "toward the east", 城向走 "walk toward the city", 師向問 "inquire of the teacher, toward the teacher", and, from the 躺平 draft sentence in [[China]], 霊魂 宇宙 向 漂 "the soul drifts toward the universe". The grammar's own case chart lists 向 as the allative ([[文法 - 97品詞]]) and the language booklets gloss it as Mandarin 朝/往, Japanese へ, Korean 으로/-쪽으로 and Vietnamese về phía. An older version of this page wrote the examples prefix-style (向城走, 向西行, 向師問); those were rewritten here to follow the suffixing rule.

Among the case particles, 向 marks the direction of orientation without implying arrival: it can express intent or facing without completion. Contrast [[于]] (dative, an animate recipient), [[至]] (terminative, the endpoint actually reached), [[於]] (locative, static location) and [[由]] (ablative, the source). A noun phrase takes at most one case particle, so 向 is not stacked with another ([[文法 - 05形態]]). The derived noun [[向格]] names the case itself; other words built on the character are [[方向]] "direction", [[傾向]] "tendency" and [[左向鉤]] "left-facing hook". Dan'a'yo-specific sense: none beyond the shared CJKV "toward"; the Dan'a'yo-specific feature is the suffixing syntax and its place in the case system.

Cross-linguistic fields. The `japanese` (こう), `korean` (향, North Korean reading) and `vietnamese` (hướng) fields give the character's Sino-Xenic bound readings (the character page also lists Japanese キョウ and ショウ), not the native equivalents. The functional equivalents are: Mandarin 向 (xiàng) itself, a preposition "toward; facing", overlapping 往 and 朝 (向北走 "walk northward", 向他請教 "ask him for advice"); Cantonese 向 (hoeng3, from memory, not re-checked); Japanese へ and に (北に向かう, using the native verb 向かう "to face, head toward"); Korean 향해(서) and (으)로 (서울을 향해 달리다 "run toward Seoul"); Vietnamese về, hướng (đi hướng bắc "go northward", hướng tới tương lai "move toward the future"; the Sino-Vietnamese hướng is also the everyday word). Wiktionary spellings 嚮 and 曏 were not re-checked this session; the character page lists both as aliases (曏 and 嚮, the latter descending from the original form of 響), and neither has its own word file.

向 reads hyang (ㄏ⼘ㄫ, 향): Middle Chinese 曉母 + 陽開韻, as on the [character page](../characters/向%20(char).md).
