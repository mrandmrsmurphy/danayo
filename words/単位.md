## Notes
- [単](../characters/単.md) "single, simple" + [位](../characters/位.md) "position, place"

単位 is the everyday word for "unit" in the broadest sense — a unit of measurement (meters, kilograms, yen), a unit in an organization, or, in Japanese and Korean academic usage, a unit of academic credit earned toward a degree (the very credits that accumulate toward a [[学位]]). 単 is the shinjitai simplification of 單/单 — a 象形 character whose traditional form depicts a forked hunting tool — that in modern usage means "single, simple, mere," already seen in [[単独]] ("alone, solo") and [[単刀直入]] ("to go straight to the point," both cross-linked from [[単]]'s Chengyu section).

単位 literally combines "single [thing]" with 位 ("place, slot") — a single, standardized slot into which quantities are divided for counting and comparison. The word sits naturally alongside [[単身]] ("alone, by oneself, unaccompanied") in 単's compound family: both extend 単's core sense of "one, alone" into more abstract domains, one into being on one's own and the other into measurement.

The word has a notable Chinese divergence: in Mandarin 单位 (dānwèi) is also the standard word for a person's work unit or organization (a school, factory or office, as in 工作单位), a sense that grew in the planned-economy era and is part of everyday Chinese, where Japanese and Korean use the word for the academic-credit sense instead. The Knowledge, Society and Measurement pages each cite the word for a different one of these senses. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared core, "a unit," and the credit sense on Knowledge, and the work-unit sense is understood as the Chinese organizational extension of "a unit." Readings: Mandarin dānwèi, Cantonese daan1 wai6, Japanese tan'i (たんい), Korean 단위, Sino-Vietnamese đơn vị.
