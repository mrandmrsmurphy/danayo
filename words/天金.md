## Notes
- [天 (char)](../characters/天%20(char).md) "heaven, sky" (short for [[天王星]] "Uranus," the planet/god) + [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "metal" — literally "heaven-metal," a Dan'a'yo neologism for uranium, following the pattern of naming this element after the planet Uranus (compare the real Mandarin/Japanese naming rationale below).

Mandarin yóu (铀), Cantonese jau4 (with a tone-sandhi variant jau4-2 in some contexts), Korean 우라늄, Japanese ウラン (uran), and Vietnamese urani are all the real, standard words for uranium in each language — not compositional readings of 天 and 金 — matching this vault's periodic-table convention of storing the genuine external word rather than a coined compositional reading. Mandarin 铀 and Japanese ウラン both independently derive their own naming from "Uranus" (铀 itself uses 由 as a phonetic-adjacent component, while ウラン is a direct transliteration), the same motivating idea behind Dan'a'yo's own coinage 天金.

`kwin` is false: 天 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page (天's own Sino-Korean 천 does not match Dan'a'yo's 턴), which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 金 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄝㄋㄍㄧㄇ unique among both words and characters).
