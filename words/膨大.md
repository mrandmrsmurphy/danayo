## Notes
- [膨](../characters/膨.md) "swell, inflate" + [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) "big" — huge, enormous, swollen; this word is the stand-in for 膨, which cannot appear independently.

膨大 is "huge, enormous" and, of a thing that expands, "swollen." It is [膨](../characters/膨.md) "swell" plus 大 "big," and its sense is size that has grown past the ordinary: a 膨大 budget, a 膨大 amount of data. Japanese ぼうだい is the common adjective for an enormous quantity (first attested in 1872 as "swollen and large"; the later "huge" sense is often written 厖大), and Mandarin péngdà and Cantonese paang4 daai6 are the same compound, glossed on Wiktionary as "swollen; big, great, huge" and, as a verb, "to swell, distend, expand." Korean was not confirmed, so `korean` is left blank. It differs from [[膨脹]], the verb "to swell, inflate," which names the act where 膨大 names the resulting size, and from the other "huge" words ([[巨大]], [[碩大]], [[宏大]], [[浩大]]) in its sense of having become large by expansion or accumulation. This word is the stand-in that legitimizes [膨](../characters/膨.md), which cannot appear independently.
