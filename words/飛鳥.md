## Notes
- [飛 (char)](../characters/飛%20(char).md) "fly" + [鳥 (char)](../characters/鳥%20(char).md) "bird" — no `#cranberry`: 飛's own `stand_in` is 飛 itself, 鳥's is 鳥 itself; neither points here.

飛鳥 (fēiniǎo) is a real Mandarin word for "a bird in flight," and Cantonese 飛鳥 (fei1niu5) likewise. Korean 비조 (bijo) is the standard compositional term. Japanese has three distinct readings for this same written form: ひちょう (hichō, the formal on'yomi compound), とぶとり (tobutori, a native descriptive phrasing, "a flying bird"), and あすか (Asuka, a special place-name reading — Asuka, the historical capital region and namesake of the Asuka period, is conventionally written with these exact characters as an ateji). Sino-Vietnamese phi điểu is likewise a real, attested literary term.
