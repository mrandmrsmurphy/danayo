## Notes
- [国](../characters/国.md) "nation" + [家](../characters/家.md) "house; family" — `characters/国.md`'s own `stand_in` points to this word (国家), meaning 国 is a bound morpheme that never appears independently outside this compound (in this Dan'a'yo sense). `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: `characters/国.md` is `kwin: false`, `characters/家.md` is `kwin: true` → compound `false`.
- All cross-linguistic fields were already correct, standard readings: Mandarin guójiā, Cantonese gwok3 gaa1, Japanese こっか, Korean 국가, and Vietnamese quốc gia are all everyday words for "country; nation."

**No longer a homophone of [[国歌]]**: this word's own reading (gogga/곡가/ㄍㄛㄎㄍㄚ) is independently correct and unchanged, but 国歌 was found to have been storing an erroneous "ga" reading of its own 歌-syllable — corrected to its true gǝ/그/ㄍㄜ, so the two words no longer collide at the Dan'a'yo level (they remain real-world homophones in Japanese only, both こっか, a coincidence unrelated to Dan'a'yo phonology).
