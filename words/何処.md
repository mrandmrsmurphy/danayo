## Notes
- [何 (char)](../characters/何%20(char).md) "what; which; interrogative" + [処 (char)](../characters/処%20(char).md) "place; location"

何処 is the interrogative place correlative — "where?" — and is one of the most cross-linguistically grounded entries in the entire correlative table. In Japanese, 何処 is written with these exact two characters and read どこ (doko) — "where" in ordinary everyday speech. The Dan'a'yo reading *haco* differs from the Japanese reading, but the grapheme is identical, making 何処 one of the few correlatives that is not purely a Dan'a'yo coinage but overlaps directly with an existing Japanese orthographic compound.

Mandarin 哪裡 (nǎlǐ, "which-interior → where?") and the Beijing colloquial 哪兒 (nǎr, with r-suffix — listed as an alias) are the standard *everyday* equivalents, distinct from 何処's own literary/compositional héchǔ. Cantonese uses 邊度 (bin1 dou6, "which place?") colloquially, distinct from 何処's own compositional ho4 cyu2. Korean 어디 (eodi, "where") is the standard everyday interrogative place word, distinct from 何処's own Sino-Korean compositional 하처; Vietnamese đâu (where?) is the everyday monosyllabic form, distinct from 何処's own compositional hà xử. All five languages have a dedicated everyday "where" pronoun, confirming 何処's Swadesh list membership (entry 13) and its status as universal basic vocabulary — even though the Dan'a'yo headword itself instead uses each language's own literary/compositional reading, per the vault's convention for correlatives with genuine classical attestation.

Unlike most 何X correlatives (which are Dan'a'yo neologisms or classical Chinese compounds), 何処/どこ (doko) is in active everyday use in modern Japanese — not a literary or formal form but the standard spoken word. This means Japanese speakers encounter 何処 as an entirely familiar compound with a different reading (*haco* in Dan'a'yo vs *doko* in Japanese), analogous to the way ateji readings work: same characters, different phonology, same meaning — and unlike the other four languages, `japanese: どこ` is genuinely and directly 何処's own reading, kept as-is. The alias 哪兒 marks the Beijing r-suffix colloquial form that is less formal than 哪裡 but equally standard in northern Mandarin.
