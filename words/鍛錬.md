## Notes
- [鍛](../characters/鍛.md) "forge" + [錬](../characters/錬.md) "smelt, refine" — literally "to forge and refine," the physical act of forging metal by repeated heating and striking.

鍛錬 names the literal process of forging metal — heating and repeatedly hammering it into shape — and, by a well-established figurative extension in living Japanese and Mandarin usage, the disciplined "tempering" of a person's body, skill, or character through rigorous training and practice (体を鍛錬する, "to train one's body"). In modern speech the figurative sense dominates; the literal metalworking sense survives mainly in historical, literary, or technical contexts. This distinguishes it from [[鍛冶]] (built on the same leading character 鍛), which names the trade and craft of metalworking itself without any figurative drift.

Mandarin duànliàn, Cantonese dyun3 lin6, Japanese たんれん, and Korean 단련 are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 鍛's and 錬's own stored citations. No Vietnamese reading is independently attested for this specific two-character form.
