## Notes
- [小 (char)](../characters/小%20(char).md) "small" + [指](../characters/指.md) "finger" — no stand-in relationship applies (小's own `stand_in` is itself, 指's is [[手指]]).

小指 is the smallest digit — the pinky (finger or toe). It fits into the classical Chinese five-finger naming set alongside 巨指/拇指 (thumb), 食指 (index), 將指 (middle finger, literally "leading finger"), and 無名指 (ring finger, literally "nameless finger").

**Japanese has a genuine register split**: こゆび (koyubi, native kun'yomi) is the everyday word, while しょうし (the on'yomi reading stored here) is the specifically medical/anatomical register term — both are correct, just in different contexts, the same everyday-vs-technical split documented on [[私讐]]/[[小冊子]]. Korean 소지 and Vietnamese tiểu chỉ (verified) are both standard Sino-form readings, though Vietnamese everyday speech more often uses the native ngón út. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄛㄐㄧㄜ unique across both words and characters).
