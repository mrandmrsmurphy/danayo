## Notes
- [広](../characters/広%20(char).md) "wide" + [島](../characters/島%20(char).md) "island"

広島 is Hiroshima, written "wide island", with 広 the Japanese shinjitai form of 廣. The city was founded by the warlord Mōri Terumoto in 1589 on the delta of the Ōta River. The reading ひろしま is native: 広 (ひろ, "wide") plus 島 (しま, "island").

Hiroshima was the target of the first atomic bomb on 6 August 1945, and the Atomic Bomb Dome and the Peace Memorial Park commemorate it. The prefecture is also known for the island shrine of Itsukushima (Miyajima), oysters and its own style of okonomiyaki.

In Dan'a'yo 広島 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 히로시마 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
