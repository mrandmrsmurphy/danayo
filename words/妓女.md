## Notes
- [妓](../characters/妓.md) "courtesan, female entertainer" + [女 (char)](../characters/女%20(char).md) "woman"

妓女 is the direct, unvarnished word for "prostitute." 妓, a character built from the 女 radical plus phonetic 支, originally denoted a female entertainer skilled in music and dance — a 芸妓 ("geisha," cross-linked from [[妓]]'s own Words section) — whose social and professional proximity to commercial sex work led the character's meaning to broaden over centuries from "skilled female performer" toward "sex worker" specifically, with 妓女 marking the unambiguous end of that semantic spectrum.

妓女 and [[舞妓]] thus represent the two poles of meaning that 妓 has carried throughout its history: 妓女 the blunt, modern "prostitute," and 舞妓 the elevated, art-focused "dancing girl/apprentice geisha" that preserves 妓's older, performance-centered sense. The Art page defines 舞妓 explicitly against 妓女, as a performer defined by dance and not by the sex-worker sense. The 妓 character page's own gloss, "a female entertainer, actress," records the older meaning, and that character's stand-in is [[芸妓]], not this word.

The Reproduction page lists 妓女 with the plain gloss "a prostitute," and that is the sense Dan'a'yo keeps, with no divergence: the word is the direct term, while the entertainer sense belongs to 芸妓 and 舞妓. A related word is [妓院](妓院.md) "brothel." The readings are Mandarin jìnǚ, Cantonese gei6 neoi5, Japanese gijo (ぎじょ), Korean 기녀 (in Korean the word more often means a historical court or professional entertainer, a kisaeng) and Sino-Vietnamese kỹ nữ.
