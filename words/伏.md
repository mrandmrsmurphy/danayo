## Notes
- [伏 (char)](../characters/伏%20(char).md) "crouch; prostrate; surrender"

伏 is 会意: [[人]] ("person") + [[犬]] ("dog") — a person acting like a dog, crouched low to the ground. From this literal image of crouching/lying prone come the extended senses "to hide, to lie in wait" ([[潜伏]] "to hide, to lurk"; 伏擊, fújī, "to ambush") and "to submit, to surrender" (the prostration of surrender or subordination).

Mandarin fú, Cantonese fuk6, and Vietnamese phục (the Hán Việt reading, as in phục kích 伏擊 "ambush," and đầu phục "to surrender, submit") all preserve these senses directly. Japanese has three on'yomi (ふく/ふう/ぶく, fuku/fū/buku) alongside native ふ(せる)/ふ(す) (fu(seru)/fu(su), "to lie face down, to prostrate oneself"). Korean 복 (bok) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 안다 (anda, "to hold, embrace," reflecting the crouching-over posture).
