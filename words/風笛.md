## Notes
- [風 (char)](../characters/風%20(char).md) "wind" + [笛](../characters/笛.md) "flute, whistle" — no `#cranberry`: 風's own `stand_in` is 風 itself, 笛's is [[口笛]]; neither points here.

風笛 (fēngdí) is the real, standard Mandarin word for "bagpipes" (e.g. 苏格兰风笛, "Scottish bagpipes"), and Cantonese 風笛 (fung1dek6-2) likewise. Japanese ふうてき (fūteki) is a real, attested alternate name for the instrument, though バグパイプ (the direct English loanword) is more common in everyday use.

Korean instead uses 백파이프, the direct English loanword, as the specific term for the (Scottish-style) bagpipe; the compositional Sino form 풍적 exists but denotes wind-pipe instruments more broadly (including traditional Korean pipes), rather than specifically the bagpipe. Vietnamese kèn túi ("bag horn") is a native descriptive term, with no live Sino-Vietnamese equivalent.
