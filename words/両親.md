## Notes
- [両 (char)](../characters/両%20(char).md) "both" + [親](../characters/親.md) "parent, kin" — literally "both parents."

両親 is nearly synonymous with [[父母]] ("father and mother"), but carries a slightly more impersonal, formal register — more likely to be used of someone else's parents than one's own, the same subtle register distinction English draws between "my parents" and the more formal "one's parents." Japanese りょうしん is the everyday word for this exact meaning, with no register gap the way some other on'yomi compounds in this vault diverge from native equivalents.

**Vietnamese content note**: lưỡng thân (confirmed via web search) is directly and correctly derived from these exact two characters, but Vietnamese formal usage more commonly favors the near-synonym song thân (雙親/双親, built on 雙 "pair" rather than 両 "both") for the same meaning — song thân is the term more often seen in ceremonial contexts (weddings, Vu Lan festival invitations). lưỡng thân was used here since it matches this word's own actual written characters, rather than substituting the more common but differently-written near-synonym. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 량친 diverges from Sino-Korean 양친 in the first syllable's onset — the same 두음법칙-adjacent divergence (initial ㄹ vs. dropped) already documented on [[老]] earlier in this sweep, though here it is a genuine Dan'a'yo/Korean phonological difference rather than a North/South Korean field-choice bug. No homophones (注音 ㄌ⼘ㄫㄑㄧㄋ unique among both words and characters).
