## Notes
- [黒 (char)](../characters/黒%20(char).md) "black" — stand-in for [[黒 (char)|黒]], which cannot appear independently

黒 is 象形: the oracle-bone form depicts a chimney or window with soot rising above a fire, the original sense "soot, smoke-blackened" generalizing to "black" overall — a concrete origin paralleling how many color terms across languages derive from a specific dyed/burnt substance rather than an abstract hue (compare [[青]]'s own pigment-based origin earlier in this sweep). The compound family spans the literal (黒色, 黒板 "blackboard") and figurative (暗黒 "darkness, pitch black") alongside a set of animal names that pick out a genuinely black-colored creature (黒貂 "sable," 黒猩 "chimpanzee") and the scientific 黒洞 ("black hole," a modern coinage built the same way across Chinese/Japanese/Korean independently). Within this vault's own periodic-table convention, 黒 additionally prefixes hassium via [[黒金]].

Japanese carries a single on'yomi こく (KOKU), alongside two native forms — くろ (kuro, the noun "black," as in 黒 alone naming the color) and くろい (kuroi, the adjective "to be black") — a productive native layer distinct from the Sino-Japanese one, matching the pattern seen on most concrete-object/color characters throughout this sweep. Vietnamese hắc is the character's sole stored reading, appearing mainly in Sino-Vietnamese compounds (hắc ám "dark, gloomy") rather than as the everyday word for "black," which Vietnamese names with the native đen. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 훅 (hug, from Middle Chinese 曉母 x + 德開韻 ək) diverges from Sino-Korean 흑 in the vowel. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄨㄎ unique among both words and characters).
