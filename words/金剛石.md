## Notes
- [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "metal" + [剛](../characters/剛.md) "hard, unyielding" + [石 (char)](../characters/石%20(char).md) "stone" — diamond, in the mineralogical/scientific sense (the carbon crystal), distinct from [[鑽石]] (the gemstone/jewelry sense), per the explicit note on 鑽's character page. Attested across Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, and Korean.

The blank `vietnamese` field now holds the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading of the three characters, kim cương thạch (金 kim, 剛 cương, 石 thạch), following the vault's convention for these fields; everyday Vietnamese says kim cương for diamond, which is the first two syllables of this reading. The Korean 금강석 differs from the Dan'a'yo 諺文 김강석 in the first syllable's vowel (금 versus 김; the other two syllables match), so `kwin` is false. The Metals page lists the word as "diamond — a mineral rather than a metal proper, but grouped here with the other precious materials," and Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "diamond (mineral)," the scientific term, with the gemstone sense carried by the existing word [鑽石](鑽石.md). It is a kind of [鉱石](鉱石.md) "ore, mineral," and its second element 剛 is the one that names the stone's hardness. The readings are Mandarin jīngāngshí, Cantonese gam1 gong1 sek6 and Japanese kongōseki (こんごうせき). The Vietnamese kim cương remark is from memory and has not been checked against a source.
