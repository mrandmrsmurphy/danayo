## Notes
- [不 (char)](../characters/不%20(char).md) "not" + [[可以]] "can, may" — the direct negation of [[可以]], marking that something is not permitted.

不可以 does the same double duty as its unnegated counterpart, ruling out both capacity and permission depending on context, and is one of the most common negative modal expressions in spoken Mandarin (不可以這樣做, "you can't/mustn't do it this way"). **Vietnamese bất khả dĩ** is a defensible compositional extension of 可以's own already-confirmed khả dĩ ("can be, acceptable") using the independently productive negating prefix bất- (bất khả thi "infeasible," bất khả xâm phạm "inviolable") — but unlike khả dĩ itself, bất khả dĩ did not turn up as an independently attested fixed dictionary entry in its own right during this search, so it is presented here as a compositionally sound but not separately idiomatic form, rather than claiming the same level of direct attestation as its unnegated counterpart.

**Japanese and Korean genuinely lack a corresponding word built from these characters**, exactly as already established on [[可以]]'s own page — both instead negate their native grammatical constructions for "can/may" (Japanese ～できない, Korean -을/ㄹ 수 없다) rather than using any Sino-Japanese/Sino-Korean compound — `japanese`/`korean` deliberately left blank rather than fabricated, the same honesty standard already applied to [[可以]]/[[不但]]/[[分之]] earlier in this vault's history. `kwin` is false, following the character-level AND-rule already established on 可以's own page (可 is `kwin: false`), unaffected by adding the negating 不. No homophones (注音 ㄅㄛㄊㄎㄜㄧ unique among both words and characters).
