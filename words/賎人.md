>[!caution] The senses split — "plebeian" against a vulgar Chinese insult
>賎人 is a plebeian, a person of low rank, in Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese and in archaic Chinese, and that is the Dan'a'yo sense. In modern Chinese 賤人 is also a vulgar, offensive insult ("nasty person; bitch; bastard; slut"), so calling someone 賎人 may be heard as abuse. Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).
## Notes
- [賎](../characters/賎.md) "inferior, plebeian" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person"

賎人 is a plebeian: a person of low rank, an inferior or petty person. English Wiktionary glosses Japanese せんじん as "an inferior; a petty person; a plebeian", Korean 천인 as "plebeian" (historically a person of the lowest social class, the opposite of 良人), and Vietnamese tiện nhân as "plebeian". For Chinese 賤人 (an alias; Mandarin jiànrén, Cantonese zin6 jan4) it gives the archaic "plebeian; lowly person; inferior person; petty person", but first a vulgar, offensive, derogatory sense: "nasty person; bitch; bastard; slut". The Dan'a'yo sense is the plebeian one, and the word is listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).

This word is the stand-in for [[賎]], which cannot appear independently. Another term of low social standing is [[奴隷]], slave.
