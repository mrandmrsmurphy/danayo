## Notes
- Filled blank `cantonese: gun1 jan4` (compositional) and `vietnamese: quan nhân`, a classical/literary Sino-Vietnamese term for "an official," attested in Hán-Việt dictionaries citing 逸周書. **Left `hsk_level` unset**: not a standard-list entry, and the word is archaic in modern Mandarin (see below).
- **Genuine cross-linguistic drift**: 官人 originally meant simply "an official," and Japanese かんじん preserves exactly that sense — a historical Ritsuryō-era (律令) term for a bureaucrat/civil servant, still used today only in historical contexts. Mandarin, by contrast, has narrowed the word entirely: the "official" sense is now obsolete, and the *only* meaning surviving into modern usage is an archaic form of address used by a wife toward her husband ("my husband," in period dramas/classical vernacular fiction) — the same word having passed through intermediate senses as an address between men and from servant to master before settling there.
