## Notes
- [枝](../characters/枝.md) "branch; bough" + [葉 (char)](../characters/葉%20(char).md) "leaf" — stand-in for [枝](../characters/枝.md), which cannot appear independently
- Literally "branches and leaves," the compound carries the same double life across the Sinosphere: a plain botanical description of a tree's outer growth, and by extension the parts of any matter that are secondary, peripheral, or merely decorative — the things that grow off the trunk rather than forming it. Japanese keeps both the on'yomi しよう (shiyō) and a native kun'yomi reading えだは (edaha) for the literal sense, while Mandarin zhīyè, Cantonese zi1 jip6, and Sino-Korean 지엽 (jiyeop) lean toward the figurative "nonessential details" — as in the common derived term 지엽적, "peripheral, beside the point."

The figurative sense is built into set phrases: the Chinese and Japanese idiom 枝葉末節 (Japanese しようまっせつ) means "trivial details," the twigs and leaf-tips of a matter as opposed to its trunk, and the figure of "the trunk and the branches" (幹と枝葉) is the usual way to separate essentials from detail. The Plants page lists the word with both senses, "branches and leaves, together" and "trivial, peripheral details," and Dan'a'yo keeps both: the literal botanical one and the figurative one, as a single word with a plain and a metaphorical use, in line with the shared meaning. For the tree itself see [樹木](樹木.md) "tree, trees," and for the leaf alone [葉](葉.md).
