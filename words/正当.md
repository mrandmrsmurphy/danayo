## Notes
- [正 (char)](../characters/正%20(char).md) "correct; right" + [当 (char)](../characters/当%20(char).md) "to fit; just at"

正当 is "proper; legitimate; justified," and as a noun "legitimacy, justness." According to Wiktionary, Chinese 正當 (zhèngdàng, Cantonese zing3 dong3 or zing3 dong1) is used of behavior and actions: "proper; appropriate; legitimate; reasonable; above board," and also "just when; just the time for." Japanese 正当 (せいとう) is "right, legitimate, allowable" and "legality, justness." Wiktionary has no Korean or Vietnamese section, so `korean` 정당 and `vietnamese` chính đáng are compositional readings, with the Korean form matching the ordinary word (from memory, not re-checked). The traditional form 正當 is the alias. It is used in the Dan'a'yo rendering of the 1919 Korean Declaration of Independence ([宣言書](../translation/宣言書.md)), in 正当権利 "legitimate right," where the Korean 正權 was too obscure to coin. It is related to [正義](正義.md) "justice" and [権利](権利.md) "right."
