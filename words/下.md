## Notes
- [下 (char)](../characters/下%20(char).md) "down, under"

下 is 指事 (indicative): a short stroke placed beneath a longer baseline stroke, marking "below" — the most basic word for "down, under, below" across the sphere, and one of the highest-frequency characters in Classical Chinese (21st most used). It appears in compounds spanning literal position ([[天下]] "the world," literally "under heaven"; [[地下道]] "underpass, tunnel") and figurative/honorific extension ([[陛下]] "your majesty," literally "below the palace steps" — an indirect address that historically avoided naming the emperor directly).

Mandarin xià, Cantonese haa6, and Vietnamese xuống (a native, not Sino-Vietnamese, form — the bound Sino-Vietnamese reading hạ appears instead in formal compounds like hạ cấp "subordinate") all cover the core "down/below" sense. Japanese has on'yomi カ/ゲ (ka/ge, in compounds like 下品 げひん "vulgar" and 下車 げしゃ "getting off a vehicle") alongside the native reading した (shita), the everyday word for "below, under." Korean 하 (ha) is the Sino-Korean reading used throughout compounds, with native 아래 (arae) preferred in plain everyday speech.

This word shares its Dan'a'yo reading (ha/하/ㄏㄚ) with [[何]] ("what") — an accidental collision between an extremely common preposition/noun and an equally common interrogative, disambiguated only by context.
