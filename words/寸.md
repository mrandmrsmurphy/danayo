## Notes
- [寸 (char)](../characters/寸%20(char).md) "inch; measurement" — stand-in for [[寸 (char)|寸]], which cannot appear independently

寸 is a 指事 (indicative) character: a mark placed just below 又/手 ("hand") on the wrist, indicating the pulse-point traditionally located one 寸 inward from the base of the palm — the unit was defined directly against the body rather than an external standard, the same anthropometric logic behind 尺 ("a forearm's length," roughly ten 寸) and 丈 ("an adult's span," ten 尺). The classical proportional set 寸/尺/丈 (roughly 1:10:100) survives as a coherent unit family across the whole CJKV sphere even where the absolute lengths have since been redefined against the metric system (modern Mainland China fixes 1 寸 = 1/3 decimeter ≈ 3.33 cm; Taiwan and Japan use very slightly different legal conversions), so the character still reliably denotes "a small, body-derived unit of length" even to speakers with no exposure to the historical measure itself.

Beyond its literal use as a unit, 寸 carries a strong idiomatic life as a stand-in for "a small amount, a mere fraction" — 寸陰 ("an inch of shadow," i.e. a brief moment of time, as in the classical maxim 一寸光陰一寸金, "an inch of time is worth an inch of gold") and 寸心 ("an inch of heart," i.e. one's innermost, humble feelings) both trade on this smallness sense rather than the measurement itself. Japanese keeps the character fully alive in both registers: そん in learned/measurement compounds, but the native reading すん also survives independently in fixed expressions like 寸法 (sunpō, "dimensions, measurements") and the idiom 寸暇 (sunka, "a moment's leisure"), a native-reading survival unusually robust for a character this narrowly technical.
