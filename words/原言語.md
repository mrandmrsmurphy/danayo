## Notes
- [原](../characters/原.md) "plain; original" + [言 (char)](../characters/言%20(char).md) "say; speech" + [語](../characters/語.md) "language" — "original language." It is built from the word [[言語]] "language" with 原 "original" in front, so it is not a 語-suffix derivation like [[中国語]]. No stand-in relationship applies (原's own `stand_in` is [[原始]], 言's is itself, 語's is [[言語]]).

原言語 means the language that something derives from, and the vault uses it in the sense of a speaker's source language: Dan'a'yo's phonology chapter says that "a high allophonic difference is tolerated among the dialects influenced by each 原言語," meaning that a learner's Dan'a'yo will carry the sound patterns of whichever of Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean or Vietnamese they already speak. The 原 here is the "original, source" sense of the character (compare [[原始]] "primitive; original" and [[原因]] "cause").

The same two words cover a family of related senses, and it is worth keeping them apart. Japanese uses 原言語 (げんげんご) for the source language of a translation, with 源言語 as a variant spelling, and also for "protolanguage" in the study of how language evolved, the primitive language-like stage before fully developed language; the shorter 原語 (げんご) means the original language of a text or word. In Chinese translation studies the usual terms are 来源语 for the source language and 目的语 / 译入语 for the target language (compare [[目的語]], where the Mandarin sense is "target language"). The ancestor language of a language family is a different concept, and the vault's [[母語]] page assigns that to 祖語 or 原語. A speaker's own native language is [[母語]]; on the phonology page it is that language's influence on the speaker's Dan'a'yo that the 原言語 names.

This coinage is mostly a Dan'a'yo and Japanese usage: no entry for 原言語 was found on Wiktionary, and the Mandarin, Cantonese and Korean readings are the compositional readings of the three characters, not independently confirmed attestations. The Vietnamese reading is omitted for that reason. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 원언요 differs from Sino-Korean 원언어 in the last syllable (語 reads 요, not 어). No homophones (注音 ⼔ㄋ·ㄝㄋ·⼄ is unique). The dots mark two genuine forks: ⼔ㄋ|ㄝㄋ could be re-read as ⼔|ㄋㄝㄋ, and ㄝㄋ|⼄ as ㄝ|ㄋ⼄, the latter as on [[言語]].
