## Notes
- [手 (char)](../characters/手%20(char).md) "hand" — stand-in for [[手 (char)|手]], which cannot appear independently

手 is a 象形 pictograph of an open hand, fingers and wrist visible — one of the most productive body-part characters in the corpus, anchoring both literal anatomical compounds (手指 "finger," 手足 "limbs," 手掌 "palm") and a large figurative layer built on "hand" as agency, skill, or manpower: 人手 ("manpower," literally "person's hand"), 妙手 ("an expert, a master hand"), 空手 ("empty-handed" and, via 空手道, the sport karate itself — literally "empty-hand way"), 捕手 (the baseball catcher, "the catching hand"). The gesture compounds 招手/拍手/握手 (beckon/applaud/shake hands) show the same pictograph extending naturally into verbs of hand-use, a pattern shared by most CJKV languages independently rather than being any single language's innovation.

Japanese confines the on'yomi しゅ (SHU) mostly to compounds like this bare word and technical/sport vocabulary, while ordinary spoken Japanese overwhelmingly prefers the native て (te, "hand") for everyday reference — て itself has no on'yomi-independent kanji reading of its own listed on the character page (`japanese_native: ø`), an unusual gap suggesting て's productivity lives almost entirely outside the Sino-Japanese layer this vault otherwise tracks. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 슈 (syu, from Middle Chinese 書母 ɕ + 尤韻 ɨu) diverges from Sino-Korean 수 in the onset, Dan'a'yo preserving a palatalized sibilant the Korean form has smoothed away. 手 shares its Dan'a'yo reading with two other standalone words, [[痩]] ("thin") and [[銹]] ("rust") — a three-way coincidental convergence, unrelated etymologically to either.
