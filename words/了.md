## Notes
- [了 (char)](../characters/了%20(char).md) "done; finished; over"

了 is 象形 in origin (though the modern form, built from 亅 and 乛, has lost any transparent picture), and today functions in two related but distinct roles: as a free-standing word meaning "finished, done, over" (了結, "to settle, conclude"; [[完了]], "completion"), and — far more frequently in modern Mandarin — as an unstressed grammatical aspect particle marking completed or changed-state action (吃了飯, "have eaten"; 下雨了, "it's raining [now]"), a use so pervasive it is one of the most common characters in the language despite carrying little semantic weight of its own in that role.

Japanese has on'yomi りょう (ryō), confined to compounds like 完了 (かんりょう, "completion") and 終了 (しゅうりょう, "conclusion") — Japanese has no native aspect-particle use parallel to Mandarin's grammaticalized 了. Korean 료 (ryo) parallels the Japanese compound-only usage. Vietnamese liễu is the Hán Việt reading, attested in liễu giải ("to understand," from [[了解]]) — the character page's other five stored candidates (léo, líu, lẽo, lếu, lểu) are the same kind of corpus noise already flagged when [[了解]] was perfected earlier in this sweep.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (lyau/럇/ㄌ⼘ㄨ) with another word still awaiting its own turn in this sweep, [[聊]] ("chat") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed when it comes up.
