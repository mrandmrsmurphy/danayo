## Notes
- [艇](../characters/艇%20(char).md) "dinghy, rowboat, boat" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 艇 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

Mandarin tǐng, cantonese ting5, and korean 정 (jeong) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Dan'a'yo's own deng/덩/ㄉㄝㄫ (d-initial) derives from the Middle Chinese 定 initial and doesn't need to match modern Mandarin's t-initial — a normal divergence, not a bug. Japanese has no native kun-reading (stored as ø on the character page), so the on-reading テイ (TEI) is used directly. Vietnamese đĩnh is the character's sole stored reading. Part of a genuine three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group with [[丁]] ("fourth on a list of four") and [[釘]] ("nail") — coincidental, unrelated in meaning. Checked the other ㄉㄝㄫ-reading characters (亭, 停, 庭, 廷, 挺) — none has a self-pointing `stand_in`, confirming no fourth homophone.
