## Notes
- [禁](../characters/禁.md) "prohibit, forbid" + [書](../characters/書.md) "book, writing" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (禁's is [[禁止]], 書's is [[書本]]), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`.

禁書 names a book officially banned by a government, religious authority, or other censoring power — its printing, sale, possession, or circulation prohibited, typically for political, moral, or religious reasons. The archetypal historical case in East Asia is the Qin dynasty's burning of the classics (焚書坑儒, "the burning of books and burial of scholars"), which the vault's own [[書經]] tradition long remembered as the near-destruction of the Confucian canon.

Mandarin jìnshū, Cantonese gam3 syu1, Japanese きんしょ (kinsho), and Korean 금서 (geumseo) are all standard, fully compositional readings, each the ordinary term for a banned book in its language. Vietnamese cấm thư is likewise compositional, though everyday Vietnamese more often phrases the concept analytically (sách bị cấm, "book that is forbidden").
