## Notes
- [龍 (char)](../characters/龍%20(char).md) "dragon" + [巻 (char)](../characters/巻%20(char).md) "roll" — a tornado, literally "dragon roll"; also covers waterspout, whirlwind, and dust devil more loosely.

**Content corrected**: `korean` was `폭풍` ("storm, tempest" generically) — not a match at all for the specific "tornado" sense. Corrected to `용오름` (yong-oreum, literally "dragon('s) ascent"), the term Korean meteorological and journalistic usage actually reaches for, sharing the same dragon-imagery metaphor as this word's own 龍. Korean also commonly uses the English loanword 토네이도 (tonèido) for "tornado" generically; a fully Sino-Korean compositional form, 용권(풍) (yonggwon(pung), directly paralleling 龍卷風), exists but is markedly rarer, reading as literary/technical rather than everyday vocabulary.

Mandarin lóngjuǎn and Cantonese lung4 gyun2 preserve the sense directly. Japanese たつまき (tatsumaki) is the standard, everyday Japanese word. No `vietnamese` field is given: Vietnamese has no attested Sino-Vietnamese compound for this word (a hypothetical "long quyển" is phonologically constructible but unattested in any source checked). Vietnamese instead independently coined its own dragon-metaphor term, vòi rồng ("dragon's spout/trunk") — a striking cross-linguistic parallel to 龍巻/tatsumaki's own "dragon" imagery, arrived at separately rather than borrowed; lốc xoáy is a near-synonym in modern usage, not a broader/narrower term as might be assumed.
