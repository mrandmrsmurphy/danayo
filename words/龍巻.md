## Notes
- [龍 (char)](../characters/龍%20(char).md) "dragon" + [巻 (char)](../characters/巻%20(char).md) "roll" — a tornado, literally "dragon roll"; also covers waterspout, whirlwind, and dust devil more loosely.

Mandarin lóngjuǎn (巻's minority "to roll" verb-sense reading juǎn, distinct from its stored default noun-sense juàn "volume, book" — the same reading-split pattern seen on [[拓]]/[[間]]) and Cantonese lung4 gyun2 preserve the sense directly. Japanese たつまき (tatsumaki) is the standard, everyday Japanese word.

Vietnamese has no attested Sino-Vietnamese compound for this word (a hypothetical "long quyển" is phonologically constructible but unattested in any source checked); Vietnamese instead independently coined its own dragon-metaphor term, vòi rồng ("dragon's spout/trunk") — a striking cross-linguistic parallel to 龍巻/tatsumaki's own "dragon" imagery, arrived at separately rather than borrowed — filled in as the `vietnamese` field per the real-attested-usage-over-compositional convention. lốc xoáy is a near-synonym in modern usage, not a broader/narrower term as might be assumed.
