## Notes
- [揚 (char)](../characters/揚%20(char).md) + [州 (char)](../characters/州%20(char).md) — Yang Province, the fifth of the [[九州]]

揚州 was the southeastern province, covering the region south of the Yangtze — roughly modern Jiangsu, Zhejiang, and southern Anhui. The *Tribute of Yu* places it between the Huai and the sea (淮海惟揚州), the most southerly and exotic of the nine in early texts, described as a land of rivers, lakes, metals, and strange products. During the Sui and Tang dynasties, the city of 揚州 (Yangzhou) on the Yangtze became one of the wealthiest and most cosmopolitan cities in the world, a hub of the Grand Canal trade. The Tang poet Du Mu's line 十年一覺揚州夢 ("after ten years I awake from the Yangzhou dream"), looking back on his youthful years of pleasure there, made 揚州 a byword for a luxurious, fleeting dream.

The word names the province 揚州 as listed among the 九州, and also the city of Yangzhou that kept its name.  Mandarin yángzhōu, Cantonese joeng4 zau1, Japanese ようしゅう, Korean 양주 and Vietnamese Dương Châu are the readings of that name.  The Dan'a'yo reading 양줏 differs from the Korean 양주, so `kwin` is false.
