## Notes
- [広 (char)](../characters/広%20(char).md) "wide" + [域 (char)](../characters/域%20(char).md) "domain; region" — stand-in for neither: 広's own stand-in is the bare word [広](広.md), and 域's is [域](域.md).

広域 is "a wide area," a region that spans several smaller units, and so, used before another noun, "wide-area" or "regional" in the administrative sense. It is built the same way as [広範](広範.md) "wide-ranging" (広 "wide" as the modifier), but where 広範 describes scope and is an adjective, 広域 names the stretch of ground itself, which is why it is typed as a noun, like its neighbours [区域](区域.md) "area," [地域](地域.md) "district, region" and [域内](域内.md) "within the region." Those three differ from it in what they say about the area: 区域 is a bounded or designated zone, 地域 a locality or region of any size, 域内 being inside such an area; 広域 adds that the area is large enough to cover more than one local unit, and it works as an attributive noun, modifying what follows it: 広域市 "metropolitan city," 広域圏 "wide-area zone."

Attestation was checked on 2026-10-11 through the Wiktionary parse API. Japanese 広域 (こういき) is an ordinary noun on the English and Chinese Wiktionaries and the Japanese one glosses it "a wide area," with derived terms such as 広域行政 "wide-area administration," 広域経済, 広域圏, 広域生活圏 and 広域連合. In Chinese the form is 廣域 (guǎngyù, Cantonese gwong2 wik6, simplified 广域), marked literary on the English and Chinese Wiktionaries, which gloss it "wide area" and give 廣域網 (wide-area network) and 廣域市 as derived terms; it is an uncommon word in ordinary Chinese, and 區域 or 廣泛地區 are the usual way to say it. Korean 광역 has no entry of its own on the English, Chinese or Korean Wiktionaries beyond the hanja-form stub 廣域 (a bare "hanja form of 광역"), but it is familiar from everyday Korean usage (from memory, not re-checked): 광역시 "metropolitan city" and 광역자치단체 "metropolitan/provincial-level local government." Vietnamese has no 廣域 entry on hvdic.thivien.net, so the `vietnamese` value is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese quảng + vực and not an attested word. The Korean field takes the North Korean form 광역, which is the same as the South Korean one, so no 두음법칙 shift applies.

The Korean 廣域市 is the best-known place the word turns up: 釜山廣域市 (Busan Metropolitan City), 大邱廣域市, 仁川廣域市, 光州廣域市, 大田廣域市 and 蔚山廣域市 are the six cities with that status, each administered separately from the surrounding province. The names of those cities and their place in the country are on [Korea](../lexipedia/Korea.md). Only 広域 itself is a word here: 広域市 is a compound that is not coined, since 広域 plus the existing noun for "city" already expresses it.

In Dan'a'yo 広域 keeps the shared sense, "wide area; regional."
