## Notes
- [椰](../characters/椰.md) "coconut" + [子](../characters/子.md) "child, thing" — 子 here functions as its common grammaticalized noun-suffix, not literally "child" (compare 椅子 "chair," 帽子 "hat," 桌子 "table"); stand-in for [[椰]], which cannot appear independently. 子 itself is separately legitimized by [[児子]], not this compound.

椰子 names the coconut, and by extension the coconut palm itself (the tree and its fruit share the same word, the same pattern seen throughout this vault's plant vocabulary). 椰 is the semantically loaded half of the compound; 子 is purely grammatical filler, the standard way Chinese nominalizes a bound root into a free-standing noun.

Mandarin yēzi, Cantonese je4 zi2, Japanese やし (yashi), and Korean 야자 (yaja) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 椰子 is itself the everyday word for "coconut" in all four. Vietnamese has two genuinely attested Sino-Vietnamese readings for 椰 itself — da and the less common variant gia — with gia tử being the form already stored here; everyday Vietnamese instead uses the native dừa (already the character's own documented native/vernacular gloss), the word that actually shows up in compounds like nước dừa ("coconut water") and dầu dừa ("coconut oil"). No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ⼘ㄐㄜ unique among both words and characters).
