## Notes
- [深](../characters/深.md) "deep" + [川](../characters/川%20(char).md) "river" — stand-in spelling of Shenzhen (real spelling 深圳). No stand-in relationship applies (深's is [[深刻]]; 川 is its own stand-in).

This name is a collision between a Chinese city and several Japanese places, so the Chinese sense comes first. The real Chinese spelling is 深圳, but 圳 (a Guangdong and Hakka word for a drain or ditch in the fields, found in many southern place names; from memory, not re-checked) has no page in the vault, so by the user's decision the city is written 深川 and the collision with Japanese Fukagawa is accepted. 深圳 is kept as the word's alias. The substitution is not arbitrary: 圳 takes its sound-part from 川, English Wiktionary says the Japanese reading せん of 深圳 probably comes from 川's on-reading, and Japanese Wikipedia lists 深川 as a substitute spelling for 深圳市.

The reading fields follow the precedent of [[呵爾浜]] and [[抗州]]: they hold the reading of the real name, not of the stand-in string. So Mandarin Shēnzhèn, Cantonese sam1 zan3 and Vietnamese Thâm Quyến are the real names of the city (Wiktionary Chinese, Vietnamese), where the string 深川 would be read Shēnchuān, sam1 cyun1 and Thâm Xuyên. Japanese しんせん is the Wiktionary reading of 深圳 and coincides with the Sino-Japanese reading of the string (深 SHIN + 川 SEN); Korean 심천 (Wiktionary Korean 심천) likewise coincides with the North Korean reading of the two characters. The 注音 is the plain concatenation of 深 ㄙㄧㄇ and 川 ㄑ⺢ㄋ, with no boundary dot because no second legal partition exists. For the Japanese places the reading is ふかがわ (some local places are read ふかわ, from memory, not re-checked). Korean and Vietnamese entries for the Japanese places do not exist on Wiktionary.

1. Shenzhen, a subprovincial city of Guangdong in southern China, a special economic zone since 1980 and the gateway to Hong Kong across the border (the special economic zone and the Hong Kong border are from memory, not re-checked). Wiktionary traces the name to a market town, 深圳墟, first attested in 1688; in 1979 Bao'an County was renamed and made Shenzhen City. The Mandarin is Shēnzhèn, the Japanese usual form is 深圳 しんせん or 深セン, and the Korean is 심천.
2. Fukagawa, a district of Kōtō ward in Tokyo, formerly one of the 35 wards of Tokyo City (1868–1943). In the Edo period it was an area of canals, timber yards and a pleasure quarter, and gives its name to the Fukagawa shrine festival and the rice-and-clam dish fukagawa-meshi (from memory, not re-checked). Attested on English Wiktionary (Japanese section) and Japanese Wikipedia.
3. Fukagawa, a city in central Hokkaido (Wiktionary Chinese and Japanese entries). English Wiktionary's Chinese entry calls the Chinese use of 深川 for this city an orthographic borrowing from Japanese.
4. Fukagawa, a place in the city of Nagato, Yamaguchi Prefecture, formerly a town (Japanese Wikipedia, read ふかわ there; not on Wiktionary).
5. Fukagawa, a Japanese surname (Japanese Wikipedia; not on Wiktionary).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: sense 1 is on the English, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries as 深圳 (the Vietnamese one only briefly); senses 2 and 3 are on English Wiktionary as 深川. Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 深川 page. No attested ordinary sense "deep river" exists as a word. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄇㄑ⺢ㄋ unique across both words and characters).
