## Notes
- [露 (char)](../characters/露%20(char).md) "dew" (transliteration character) + [斯](../characters/斯.md) "this" (transliteration character) + [亜](../characters/亜.md) "Asia; secondary" — no `#cranberry`: no constituent's own `stand_in` points here (露's is the word [[露]] itself, 斯's is the special "name-only character" marker 名専字, 亜's is [[亜細亜]]).

露西亜/露西亞 (Japanese ロシア, rendered in kanji) is the historical Japanese transliteration of "Russia," built purely phonetically from 露+斯/西+亜 with no semantic content, unlike the shortened [[露国]]. Korean 로서아 (roseo'a) is the corresponding older Sino-Korean transliteration borrowed from this same Japanese kanji form; modern Korean instead most commonly uses the direct loanword 러시아 (reosia), with a separate older alternative 아라사 (arasa) derived from the different transliteration base 俄羅斯 rather than from 露西亜.

Mandarin Lùxīyà and Cantonese lou6sai1aa3 give the reading if pronounced with East Asian dictionary readings, but 露西亚 is a Japanese kanji usage, not standard modern Mandarin/Cantonese vocabulary — Chinese uses 俄罗斯/俄羅斯 (Éluósī) instead, listed here as an alias. Sino-Vietnamese Lộ Tây Á is likewise a compositional rendering of the Japanese form rather than an independently common Vietnamese word, in the same "old country name" tradition as [[露国]]'s Lộ quốc.

**Historical note**: the original Japanese transliteration used 魯 ("foolish, rash") instead of 露: 魯西亜 (ロシア, *rosi'a*). In 1877 the Russian consulate objected that 魯 evoked "dull-witted, stupid," and Japan switched to the neutral 露 out of diplomatic consideration — 魯西亜 survives today only as a disused, mildly derogatory historical variant.
