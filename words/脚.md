## Notes
- [脚](../characters/脚%20(char).md) "leg" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 脚 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

脚 means the leg or foot: the limb used for standing and walking (脚踝, "ankle"; 腿脚, "legs and feet"), the base or support of an object (桌脚, "table leg"; 山脚, "foot of a mountain"), and by extension a trace or telltale sign (馬脚, lit. "horse's leg," figuratively "the cat out of the bag"). The traditional form 腳 is listed as an alias. The graph is a 形声 compound of 月 (肉) "flesh" with the phonetic 却.

Mandarin jiǎo, Cantonese goek3, Japanese きゃく (脚本/きゃくほん, "script"; 脚光/きゃっこう, "spotlight"; 三脚/さんきゃく, "tripod"), Korean 각 (각본, "script"), and Vietnamese cước are the standard Sino-xenic readings. Japanese uses the native noun 脚 (あし) for the everyday word, so on'yomi きゃく is bound to compounds; Korean has native 다리.
