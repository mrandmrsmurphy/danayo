## Notes
- [最 (char)](../characters/最%20(char).md) "most" + [高 (char)](../characters/高%20(char).md) "high" + [級](../characters/級.md) "level; rank"

最高級 is the grammatical term for the superlative degree: the "highest rank" on a scale of comparison, after the comparative. Wiktionary gives it as the Chinese grammar term (with 比較級 for the comparative), and in Japanese and Korean the same spelling also means "top grade, finest quality", as in 最高級ホテル. Japanese school grammar usually says 最上級 for the superlative, so 最高級 is the Chinese-side term; Dan'a'yo uses it because it reads naturally to all five speech communities as "highest level".

In Dan'a'yo the plain superlative is built with [[最]] "most" (最少 "least"); that is a unit of degree, not a word class. Reduplicated adjectives are the expressive extreme: the reduplicated stative means the maximum degree, stronger than [[第一]] or 最 and often humorously exaggerated (see 文法 - 05形態). 最高級 is the label for the category those forms realise. Its neighbour among the form-naming terms is [[指小詞]], the diminutive.
