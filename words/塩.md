## Notes
- [塩 (char)](../characters/塩%20(char).md) "salt" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 塩 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

Mandarin yán, Cantonese jim4, and Vietnamese diêm are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese uses しお (shio) as the everyday native word for salt itself, reserving the on'yomi えん (EN) for compounds like 塩素 (enso, "chlorine," this vault's own paired word) and 塩分 (enbun, "salt content") — the same Sino-Japanese/native split seen throughout this sweep. The shinjitai form 塩 (used here) replaces the traditional 鹽's semantic radical 鹵 ("salt") with 土 ("earth"); Dan'a'yo's own `aliases` preserve both the traditional 鹽 and simplified 盐 forms. Part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all 염/⼶ㄇ) with [[艶]] ("plump, beautiful") and [[閻]] ("village gate") — a coincidental convergence, unrelated etymologically to either.
