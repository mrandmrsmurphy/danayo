## Notes
- [食 (char)](../characters/食%20(char).md) "eat" — stand-in for [[食 (char)|食]], which cannot appear independently

食 means "to eat," the character itself 会意 of 亼 ("mouth") over [[皀]] ("a bowl of rice on a stand") — a mouth over a bowl of food. Beyond the plain verb, 食 extends productively into a large compound family: the concrete (食物 "food," 食堂 "restaurant, cafeteria," 食欲 "appetite"), the behavioral (暴食/大食 "binge eat," 断食 "fast," 摂食 "ingest"), and the figurative — 浸食 ("erode, corrode," eating away at something over time) and the astronomical 日食/月食 ("solar/lunar eclipse," literally the sun or moon being "eaten").

Mandarin shí, Cantonese sik6, and Korean 식 (sik) all preserve the Sino reading directly and productively in compounds. Japanese じき (jiki) is one of three on'yomi (alongside ショク shoku, the more common one in compounds like 食事, and シ shi in rarer terms); the everyday native verbs for "to eat" are たべる (taberu) and くう/くらう (kuu/kurau, blunter/more casual registers). Sino-Vietnamese thực survives bound in formal/literary compounds (thực phẩm "foodstuff," ẩm thực "food and drink, cuisine"), while everyday Vietnamese uses the native ăn.
