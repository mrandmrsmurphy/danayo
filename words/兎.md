## Notes
- [兎 (char)](../characters/兎%20(char).md) "rabbit; hare"

兎 (alternate form 兔) is 象形: a crouching hare, with long ears, a rounded body, and a prominent short tail. It survives today mainly in East Asian folklore and idiom — the Moon Rabbit (月兎, associated with mortar-and-pestle imagery on the moon's surface across Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese tradition alike), and the chengyu [[守株待兎]] ("guarding the stump, waiting for luck to repeat," from the fable of a farmer who, having once caught a rabbit that ran into a tree stump, foolishly waits by the same stump ever after).

Mandarin tù, Cantonese tou3, and Japanese と (to, on'yomi, rare outside compounds and names) alongside native うさぎ (usagi, the everyday word for "rabbit") all preserve the sense. Korean 토 (to) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 토끼 (tokki, the everyday native word). Vietnamese has two attested readings: thố (the Hán Việt reading, as in Ngọc Thố, "Jade Rabbit," the moon-rabbit myth) and thỏ (the everyday native word for "rabbit," con thỏ) — this word uses thố, matching the Hán Việt/mythological register.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (to/토/ㄊㄛ) with [[吐]] ("spit," still unstamped) and [[土]] ("earth," already perfected — its own callout has been added alongside this iteration).
