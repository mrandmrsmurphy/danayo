## Notes
- [沙 (char)](../characters/沙%20(char).md) "sand, gravel, pebbles" + [羅](../characters/羅.md) "Rome" + [双 (char)](../characters/双%20(char).md) "pair, double, twin, even" + [樹](../characters/樹.md) "plant" — no stand-in relationship applies (羅's own stand-in is [[羅馬]]; 樹's own is [[樹木]]).

沙羅双樹 names the sal tree (Shorea robusta), sacred in Buddhism as the species under which the Buddha died — traditionally described as a pair (双, "twin") of trees. `諺文`/`羅馬字`/`注音` are built compositionally from all four characters (sa+lo+swang+su).

**Genuine cross-linguistic divergence**: real-world Mandarin (娑羅樹, suōluóshù), Cantonese (so1 lo4 syu6), and Korean (사라수) all use a shorter three-character form, dropping 双/twin entirely — matching the aliases 娑羅樹/沙羅/娑羅 already recorded. Fixed a real truncation bug: `cantonese` had been missing its final syllable (so1 lo4 → so1 lo4 syu6, restoring the syu6/樹 reading to match the real three-character term). Fixed a kana typo in `japanese`: しやらのき,さらじゆ (using full-size や/ゆ) → しゃらのき, さらじゅ (proper small-kana しゃ/じゅ, matching real Japanese 沙羅の木/沙羅樹 forms). Vietnamese sa la is the real Buddhist loanword (via Sanskrit sāla). No homophones (注音 ㄙㄚㄌㄛㄙ⺢ㄫㄙㄨ unique across both words and characters).
