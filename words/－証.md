>[!info] Bound suffix
> －証 never appears alone: it always follows a noun naming the holder, the permitted activity or the issuing body, and returns the card or document that proves it.

## Notes
- [証](../characters/証.md) "prove; confirm" — as a suffix, "card; certificate; licence; ID"

－証 is the document suffix in Dan'a'yo — "card; certificate; licence; ID" — attached to a noun to derive *the paper or card that proves X*: 学生証 (student ID, the card proving one is a student), 免許証 (licence, the document proving one is licensed), 身分証 (identity card), 保険証 (insurance card, from memory, not re-checked). It is the document-noun counterpart of the verb 証 "to prove; to testify" that gives [[証明]] "prove; confirm", [[証人]] "witness", [[証拠]] "evidence" and [[証言]] "testify"; the suffix keeps only the sense "the proof that one holds in hand". The same character also closes older compounds such as [[認証]], [[保証]], [[物証]], [[人証]] and [[検証]], which are about proving rather than about a card, and so are not derivations with this suffix.

**Motivating example.** 俳優証 ("actor's card", the card proving one is an [[俳優]]) is the rendering of Chinese 演員證 in sentence 26 of [[lookup/HSK/China]], 我有俳優証. 俳優証 is an illustrative coinage on the pattern, not an attested Japanese or Chinese word; 演員證 itself I have not re-checked as attested. Further illustrative derivatives, none of them attested as set terms: 医者証 "doctor's card", 会員証 "membership card", 運転証 "driving permit".

**Productivity: full.** －証 meets the policy's bar of being productive in at least three of the five source languages with a transparent meaning (verified against Wiktionary, 2026-10-11): Mandarin has 學生證 "student's identity card", 駕駛證 "driving licence", with 身分證 and 領證 in the same entry; Cantonese reads the same compounds zing3 (學生證 hok6 saang1 zing3); Japanese has 学生証 "student ID card" and 免許証 "licence; driver's licence"; Korean 증 is glossed "certificate, card" on the Wiktionary 證 entry with 신분증 "identification card", 학생증 "student ID" and 면허증 "driver's license" as derived terms. Vietnamese is the weakest of the five: Sino-Vietnamese chứng is attested in bound compounds meaning "proof; to prove", but I did not verify a living "-card" suffix there (from memory, 'giấy chứng nhận' and 'chứng chỉ' mean certificate, but they are not suffixation of the same type). Any transparent *noun + 証* meaning "the card or certificate proving X" is therefore valid Dan'a'yo without its own page, and a derivative gets a file only when it is lexicalized or needs its own notes. [[通行証]] "permit; pass" is such a lexicalized word and stays its own page; it is not replaced by the suffix. The suffix is a **派生辞**: its output is a new noun and it can sit inside a longer chain, so 学生証 can itself be a stem for further derivation. The page filename uses the fullwidth hyphen `－` (U+FF0D) to mark a word that cannot stand alone but is fully productive; the ASCII form `-証` is kept as an alias.

The comparison chart: Mandarin 證 (zhèng): 學生證 (xuéshēngzhèng), 駕駛證 (jiàshǐzhèng), 身分證 (shēnfènzhèng), where modern Mandarin also has the near-synonyms 證件 (a general "document") and 卡 (kǎ, "card"). Cantonese 證 (zing3): 學生證 (hok6 saang1 zing3). Japanese 証 (しょう, shō): 学生証 (gakuseishō), 免許証 (menkyoshō), where 証 is the shinjitai of 證; the native 証 (あかし, akashi) means "proof; evidence" and is a separate free word. Korean 증 (證, jeung): 학생증, 신분증, 면허증. Vietnamese chứng (證): 証-type compounds are mostly the "prove" sense, as in 證明 (chứng minh).

Dan'a'yo 징 (jing) does not match Korean 증 (jeung), so the page records `kwin: false` and the pronunciation is taken from [証](../characters/証.md). The homophone callout above cross-references the Dan'a'yo words that read ㄐㄧㄫ, [[正]] and [[蒸]]; the grammatical position of a bound suffix separates them in practice.
