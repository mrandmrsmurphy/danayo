## Notes
- [関 (char)](../characters/関%20(char).md) "relation, connection" + [詞](../characters/詞.md) "word" — "relating word."

関詞 ("relator") was coined instead of adopting the existing CJKV term 助詞 ("helping word"), because 助詞 carries a range of meanings that differ across the daughter languages. Dan'a'yo follows the Japanese/Korean sense of 助詞 (grammatical particle), which in Japanese covers a wide family of subcategories:
1. 並立助詞 — parallel marker, 並詞
2. 係助詞 — linking marker
3. 副助詞 — adverbial marker
4. 接続助詞 — conjunctive marker, 接助
5. 格助詞 — case marker
6. 準体助詞 — nominalizing marker
7. 終助詞 — sentence-ending marker
8. 間投助詞 — interjection marker

Because 関詞 is a Dan'a'yo coinage rather than an inherited CJKV word, its `mandarin`/`cantonese`/`japanese`/`korean`/`vietnamese` fields give the literal reading of its own characters 関+詞 in each language, not the pre-existing native term for "particle" (which remains 助詞/じょし/조사/trợ từ in ordinary usage). This produces one notable coincidence worth flagging: the derived Korean reading 관사 happens to collide with an unrelated existing Korean grammar term, where 관사 normally means "article" (a/an/the), not "particle" — a reader should not assume 関詞's Korean form carries that established meaning.
