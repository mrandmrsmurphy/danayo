## Notes
- [無](../words/無.md) "without" + [名](../words/名.md) "name" — literally "without-name." Both constituents are independent Dan'a'yo words in their own right (each its own stand-in), so this compound can equally be parsed as two bound characters or two free words, with no difference in reading either way — both give ㄇㄜㄇㄧㄫ.

無名/无名 means namelessness, or to be anonymous/unnamed — describing someone or something without a name or reputation, as in an unsigned work or an unidentified person.

Mandarin wúmíng, Cantonese mou4 ming4, Korean 무명 (mumyeong), and Japanese むめい (mumei) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday word for "nameless, anonymous" in its language. Vietnamese vô danh is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching this word's own compositional pattern with 無's more common alternate reading vô rather than a literal transliteration of 無's own stand-alone reading. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 므밍 diverges from Sino-Korean 무명 in the first syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄇㄜㄇㄧㄫ unique among both words and characters).
