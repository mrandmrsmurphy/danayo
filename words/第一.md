## Notes
- [第 (char)](../characters/第%20(char).md) "-th" + [一 (char)](../characters/一%20(char).md) "one" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (both 第's and 一's own are themselves), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`.

**Fixed a real bug**: `cantonese` had di4 yi2, matching neither 第's own reading (dai6) nor 一's own (jat1) — corrected to dai6 jat1.

第一 means "first," the ordinal formed by prefixing 第 to 一, and by extension "the very best, top-ranked" (第一人者, "the foremost person in a field").

Mandarin dì-yī, Cantonese dai6 jat1, Japanese だいいち (daiichi), and Korean 제일 (jeil, also commonly used as "the most, -est," as in 제일 좋다, "best of all") are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese đệ nhất is likewise compositional and is itself a common Vietnamese term for "number one, foremost."
