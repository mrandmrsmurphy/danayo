## Notes
- [縄](../characters/縄%20(char).md) "rope, cord, string" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 縄 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

縄 means a rope, cord, string, or line: a physical binding cord (縄跳び, "jump rope"; 縄文, "cord-marked pattern"), extended figuratively to a line or rule (準縄, "standard, norm"; 縄張り, "turf, territory", a Japanese compound). The traditional form 繩 and simplified 绳 are listed as aliases.

Mandarin shéng, Cantonese sing4, Korean 승 (seung), and Vietnamese thằng are the standard Sino-xenic readings. Japanese has the on'yomi じょう (縄文/じょうもん, "Jōmon, cord-marked") and uses the native noun 縄 (なわ, "rope") for the everyday word, so on'yomi じょう is bound to compounds; Korean has native 노끈.
