## Notes
- [縄](../characters/縄%20(char).md) "rope, cord, string" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 縄 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

縄 means a rope, cord, string, or line — a physical binding cord, extended figuratively in words like 縄張り (turf, territory).

Mandarin shéng, Cantonese sing4, and Korean 승 (seung) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese uses the native kun-reading なわ (nawa, "rope") rather than the on-readings ジョウ/ビン/ヨウ (JOU/BIN/YOU) for the bare character alone. Vietnamese thằng is the standard reading. No Dan'a'yo homophones (丞/僧 share the same syllable at the character level, but neither has an independent word page).
