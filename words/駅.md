## Notes
- [駅 (char)](../characters/駅%20(char).md) "station"

駅 is the Japan-coined shinjitai simplification of 驛/驿 (a post-station where couriers and travelers changed horses), narrowed in modern Japanese to "train station" — as in 東京駅 (Tokyo Station) — and now the everyday word for a rail station throughout the Dan'a'yo sphere's Japanese-influenced usage. Mandarin does not use 驛/驿 for the modern everyday "station" (that word is 站, zhàn, an unrelated character); 驛 survives mainly in the fossilized classical compound 驛站 (yìzhàn, "post station") and historical contexts. Korean 역 (yeok) is fully productive and current, as in 지하철역 (jihacheol-yeok, "subway station"). Vietnamese ga (a French loanword from gare) is the everyday word for "station," with dịch itself surviving only in the classical/historical "post station" register, paralleling Mandarin's 驛站.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (`'yeg`/역/⼶ㄎ) with [[䋇]] ("unravel, explain") purely by coincidence of phonetic component: 驛's own phonetic is 尺, the same phonetic that gives 繹 (䋇's traditional form) its reading — the two characters are unrelated in meaning but share an Old Chinese phonetic series.
