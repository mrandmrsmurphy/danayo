## Notes
- Trimmed `korean` from `"가사, 집안일"` to the compositional reading `가사` alone — 집안일 ("housework," literally "house-inside-work") is the real, more common everyday native Korean word, not a reading of this Sino-Korean compound, so it belongs in prose rather than the field itself (same convention as [[由]]/[[之間]]/[[客人]]).
- **Genuine cross-linguistic sense-narrowing, not a bug**: Japanese 家事 (かじ) is the everyday word specifically for "housework, domestic chores" (cooking, laundry, cleaning). Mandarin 家事 (jiāshì) instead leans toward the broader "household affairs/matters" sense — the real everyday Mandarin word for "housework" specifically is 家务/家務 (jiāwù, a different second character, HSK 5). **Left `vietnamese` blank rather than guessing**: the mechanical "gia sự" isn't attested; everyday Vietnamese uses the native compound công việc nhà instead. **Left `hsk_level` unset** for the same reason — 家事 doesn't appear to be the standard-list entry (家务 is).
- **Not actually a Dan'a'yo-internal homophone**: this page previously carried an informal `>[!tip]` claiming a homophone with [[茄子]] ("eggplant"). But 茄子's own Dan'a'yo reading is 注音 ㄍㄚㄐㄜ (gajǝ/가즈) — genuinely different from this word's ㄍㄚㄐㄧ (gaji/가지). The real coincidence is narrower and cross-system: 가지 is the real, native (not Sino-Korean) Korean word for "eggplant," which happens to look identical to this word's own mechanically-derived Dan'a'yo transliteration — an accident between two unrelated systems, not a same-system homophone, so no `>[!warning] Homophones` callout applies. Corrected the header to a plain description of the word instead.
- `kwin: false` confirmed (家 alone matches Korean, 事 doesn't — AND-rule).
