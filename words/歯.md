## Notes
- [歯](../characters/歯%20(char).md) "teeth, gear" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 歯 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

歯 means teeth or gear: the hard structures in the mouth used for biting and chewing (歯痛, "toothache"; 歯科, "dentistry"; 歯磨き, "toothbrushing"), and by extension the projecting teeth of a gear, comb, or saw (歯車, "gear wheel"). It is a 象形 pictograph of teeth in an open mouth; the traditional form 齒 also carries a phonetic 止.

Mandarin chǐ, Cantonese ci2, Japanese し (歯科/しか, "dentistry"; 虫歯/むしば, "cavity"), Korean 치 (치과, "dentistry"; 치아, "tooth") and Vietnamese xỉ are the standard Sino-xenic readings. Japanese uses the native noun 歯 (は) for the everyday word, so on'yomi し is bound to compounds; Vietnamese everyday speech uses the native răng and Korean the native 이.
