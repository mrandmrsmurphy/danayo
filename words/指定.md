## Notes
- [指](../characters/指.md) "finger; point" + [定](../characters/定.md) "fix, decide" — "to point at and fix": to single something out and settle it as the one meant.

指定 is to name or pick out a particular person, thing, time, or place and fix it as the one in question: to designate, specify, or appoint. It takes both a specific object (指定席 "reserved seat", 指定場所 "the designated place") and a role or status (指定を受ける "to be designated"), and as a noun it is the act or result of designating. In the writing-system chapter ([[文法 - 03文字法]]) it appears in 名字指定, a character "designated" as a naming character, and in 指定事物, "the exact things designated", the very particular referents that naming characters may still be used for in ordinary speech.

Usage is closely parallel across the languages. Japanese してい is a noun and する-verb ("naming; designation; specification") and is especially common in official contexts: 指定都市 (a statutorily designated city), 指定席, 指定文化財 (designated cultural property). Mandarin zhǐdìng is "to appoint, assign, designate, nominate" (指定人选 "a designated candidate"), Korean 지정 (지정석, 지정하다) and Vietnamese *chỉ định* are the same; Vietnamese in addition uses *chỉ định* for a doctor's prescription or order of treatment, and the hvdic entry lists the senses "to designate, specify, select, appoint". Another sense that appears in Chinese grammatical tradition is 指定詞, a name for the copula (see [[系詞]]), which shows how the idea of "pointing out and fixing the identity of" a thing carries over into grammar.

Both halves are on bound characters whose stand-ins are [[手指]] "finger" (for 指) and [[決定]] "decide" (for 定), but 指定 is an independent word and not a stand-in for either. It contrasts with [[決定]], which is to settle a choice for oneself, and with [[規定]] "to stipulate, a regulation", which lays down a general rule where 指定 picks out a particular case; compare also [[推定]] "to presume", a conclusion reached by inference rather than by an act of naming. The Dan'a'yo sense is the shared CJKV one.
