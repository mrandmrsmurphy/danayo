## Notes
- [関 (char)](../characters/関%20(char).md) "close" + [数](../characters/数.md) "count, number"

関数 means a mathematical function. Its Dan'a'yo headword is built on 関 ("close, relation") following the Japanese orthography 関数 (かんすう, "relation-number"), but Mandarin, Cantonese, Korean, and Vietnamese all write and pronounce the real word for this concept with an entirely different first character, 函 ("case, box, letter" — a 19th-century Chinese coinage translating "function"): Mandarin 函數/函数 (hánshù), Cantonese 函數 (haam4sou3), Korean 함수 (hamsu), and Sino-Vietnamese hàm số. This is why the word's own `mandarin`/`cantonese`/`korean`/`vietnamese` fields hold real readings built on 函 (hán/haam4/함/hàm) rather than a naive concatenation of 関's own stored character-page fields (guān/gwaan1/관/quan) — Japan alone standardized on 関 for this term, making 関数 a genuine orthographic split across the CJKV sphere for one mathematical concept.
