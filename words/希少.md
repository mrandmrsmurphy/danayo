## Notes
- [希](../characters/希.md) "rare, hope" + [少 (char)](../characters/少%20(char).md) "few" — scarce; few in number and therefore rare

希少 describes something that exists in small quantity and is hard to find: 希少価値 ("scarcity value", the worth a thing has because there is little of it), 希少種 ("rare species"), 希少金属 (the "rare metals", the Japanese term for rare-earth and similar elements).  希 contributes the sense of thinness and rarity, the same 希 as in 古希 and 希有, while 少 supplies the plain count of "few", so the compound names scarcity as a matter of number.

It is a close neighbor of [[希有]], which also means rare but stresses how unusual or remarkable something is, where 希少 stresses how little of it there is, and of [[貴重]], which stresses preciousness.  The usual spelling in Chinese and Korean contexts is 稀少 (alias), with 稀 "rare" in place of 希.

Mandarin xīshǎo, Cantonese hei1 siu2, Japanese きしょう and Korean 희소 are the same compound with the same sense.  `kwin` is false because the Dan'a'yo 희솟 differs from the Korean 희소.  No Vietnamese form was found attested, so none is given.
