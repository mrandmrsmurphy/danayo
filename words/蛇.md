## Notes
- [蛇 (char)](../characters/蛇%20(char).md) "snake" — the bare character used directly as a word, its own `stand_in`.

蛇 means snake: a legless scaly reptile (毒蛇, "venomous snake"; 蛇皮, "snakeskin"; 蛇行, "slither"), and the sixth of the twelve zodiac animals (巳). Figuratively it appears in 畫蛇添足 ("draw legs on a snake", unnecessary embellishment) and 虛與委蛇 ("feign politeness"). The graph is a 象形 pictograph of a snake, later given the 虫 radical; the variants 它 and 鮀 are listed as aliases.

Mandarin tā (the common modern reading is shé), Cantonese taa1, Japanese じゃ (毒蛇/どくじゃ, "venomous snake"; 蛇口/じゃぐち, "faucet"), Korean 타, and Vietnamese xà (the native word for "snake" is rắn) are the Sino-xenic readings. Dan'a'yo takes the minority Middle Chinese reading (hence ta rather than the shé-type syllable), which also lets 蛇 absorb the character 它. Japanese uses the native noun 蛇 (へび) for the everyday word, so on'yomi じゃ is bound to compounds; Korean has native 뱀.
