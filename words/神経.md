## Notes
- [神 (char)](../characters/神%20(char).md) "god, deity, spirit" + [経 (char)](../characters/経%20(char).md) "classic; channel; warp" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (神's is [[神]] itself, 経's is [[経]] itself), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 経 is individually `kwin: false`.

**Fixed a real bug**: `諺文` had 싱겅, with the first syllable's final consonant shifted from ㄴ to ㅇ (place-assimilated before the following velar ㄱ) — but `grammar/文法 - 02音韻論.md` explicitly states no sandhi or cross-syllable assimilation is permitted in Dan'a'yo; each syllable's own spelling stays independent. Corrected to straightforward concatenation 신+겅 = 신겅 (`羅馬字`/`注音` were already correctly unassimilated: singeng/ㄙㄧㄋㄍㄝㄫ).

神経 primarily means "nerve" in the anatomical/medical sense (神経系, "nervous system"; 神経質, "nervous, high-strung"), extending 経's "channel, thread" sense to the body's own internal signaling channels. The secondary sense "Mystery Classic" refers to a class of esoteric Daoist scriptural titles built on 経 ("classic, canonical text") modified by 神 ("divine, numinous"), parallel to how 道徳経/易経/詩経/書経 each pair 経 with a modifier naming the text's subject.

Mandarin shénjīng, Cantonese san4 ging1, Japanese しんけい (shinkei), Korean 신경 (singyeong), and Vietnamese thần kinh are all standard, fully compositional readings — each an ordinary, everyday medical term for "nerve" in its language.
