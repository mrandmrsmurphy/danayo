## Notes
- [電](../characters/電.md) "electric" + [子](../characters/子.md) "child, small thing" + [郵](../characters/郵.md) "post" + [件](../characters/件.md) "matter, item" — "electronic mail item." Their own `stand_in` values: 電's is [[電気]]; 子's is [[児子]]; 郵's is [[郵便]]; 件's is [[事件]]; none points here, so 電子郵件 is an independent compound.

電子郵件 is email: mail sent as electronic messages. Mandarin diànzǐ yóujiàn and Cantonese din6 zi2 jau4 gin6-2 are the ordinary Chinese words. Japanese says Ｅメール or 電子メール and Korean 이메일 or 전자우편, so those two fields hold the readings of the characters. Dan'a'yo already has [[郵便]] (postal mail), which this word builds on in sense. Compare [[電話]] (telephone) and [[消息]] (message).
