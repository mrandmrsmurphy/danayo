## Notes
- [乾 (char)](../characters/乾%20(char).md) "heavenly"

乾 is 形声 (semantic [[乙]], "upward growth, emergence" + phonetic 倝) — the ascending, creative force. In Chinese tradition it carries two distinct readings and senses that this vault treats as one unified Dan'a'yo meaning: qián, the name of the Heaven trigram (乾卦) in the I Ching, representing pure yang, and gān, "dry" — the absence of water, the yin element. This word's own character page unifies these as a single core sense, "pure, active, absent of moisture," hence one Dan'a'yo reading for both. This is distinct from [[干]] ("shield," a wholly separate character that Simplified Chinese graphic simplification equated with 乾's "dry" sense) and [[幹]] ("trunk, core"), similarly only conflated with 乾 via 干 in Simplified Chinese.

Japanese has three on'yomi (かん/けん/げん, kan/ken/gen) alongside native かわ(く) (kawa(ku), "to dry"). Korean 건 (geon) covers both senses in Sino-Korean compounds, alongside native 하늘 (haneul, "sky/heaven," the everyday word). Vietnamese has two attested Hán Việt readings, càn (the more common in modern usage, as in càn khôn 乾坤 "heaven and earth, the universe," and Càn Long 乾隆 for the Qianlong Emperor's era name) and kiền (an alternate, less common reading, as in kiền khôn, a variant of càn khôn) — used càn here, matching this word's own "heavenly" sense; the character page's remaining five stored candidates (càng, cạn, gàn, khan, kìn) don't turn up as attested readings for this character in Hán Việt dictionaries and look like corpus noise.

This word is part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read gyen/견/ㄍ⼶ㄋ): [[見]] ("see," already perfected — its own callout has been added alongside this iteration) and [[鍵]] ("key," still awaiting its own turn).
