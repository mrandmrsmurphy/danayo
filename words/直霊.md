## Notes
- [直](../characters/直%20(char).md) "straight, upright" + [霊](../characters/霊.md) "spirit" — "upright spirit." Their own `stand_in` values: 直's is itself; 霊's is [[霊魂]]; none points here, so 直霊 is an independent compound.

直霊 is the upright spirit: the part of a spirit that sets crooked things straight. It is a neologism built from 直 "straight, upright" and 霊 "spirit." The idea comes from Shinto, where the *naohi* (直霊, 直毘) is the spirit that straightens what the *magatsuhi*, the crooked spirit, has bent; the word is written 直霊 in Japanese only, and is read なおひ there. Mandarin zhílíng, Cantonese zik6 ling4 and Korean 직령 are the compositional readings of the two characters, not attested words. Dan'a'yo's Christian vocabulary takes such terms over when they carry the right feeling, and 直 here is the opposite of the "crooked" of [[罪悪]] (*ʿāwōn*, "crookedness"). Compare [[神霊]] (a divine spirit) and [[聖霊]] (the Holy Spirit).
