## Notes
- [閙](../characters/閙.md) "noisy; disturbance" + [事 (char)](../characters/事%20(char).md) "matter"
- This word is the viable form of [閙](../characters/閙.md): the character's meaning (noisy, to cause trouble) is identical, but 閙 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 閙事 is its `stand_in` compound.

閙 is a variant form of 鬧 (simplified 闹), pictured as a market (市) in a gateway (門), a place of noise and crowding, and so means noisy, uproarious, to stir up a commotion; 事 "matter, affair" turns it into the thing done: 閙事 is to make a disturbance. In Chinese 鬧事 is the standard phrase for causing trouble in public, anywhere from a rowdy drunk making a scene to a riot or an organized protest, and it often appears in news and police reports (聚眾鬧事 "to gather a crowd and cause a disturbance"). It is milder than a full uprising, and its register is that of ordinary speech and reporting, not literary.

The readings are Mandarin nàoshì, Cantonese naau6 si6 and Korean 뇨사 (the North Korean form, keeping initial 뇨). The Japanese and Vietnamese fields are left blank. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "make a scene, cause trouble," with no divergence. The Efforts page, where it is placed, lists it among the words for exerting energy, which is a loose fit for a word whose emphasis is on disruption, not effort.
