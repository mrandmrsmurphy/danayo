## Notes
- [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" + [物 (char)](../characters/物%20(char).md) "thing; object"

人物 is "a figure, a personage": a person considered as a notable individual, as in 歷史人物 "historical figure" or 風雲人物 "man of the hour." It is the word for the people who appear on a list of famous names, such as the lists on [Japan](../lookup/Japanese/Japan.md).

The compound is shared across the Sinosphere. According to Wiktionary, Chinese 人物 (rénwù, Cantonese jan4 mat6) means "figure, personage," and also a character in a story (主要人物 "main character"), personnel, and a portrait or figure painting (人物畫); Japanese じんぶつ means a person or figure, and also someone's character; Korean 인물 and Vietnamese nhân vật are the Sino-Korean and Sino-Vietnamese forms, glossed there as "figure." Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense "figure, personage," with "a character in a story" as its second sense. The older classical senses of "demeanour" and "people and things" are not carried over.

It is related to [人](人.md) "person" and [有名](有名.md) "famous": 有名 says that a person is well known, while 人物 names the person as a figure of note, so a list of 人物 does not have to be a list of famous people.
