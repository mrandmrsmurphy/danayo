## Notes
- [暹](../characters/暹.md) "Siam" + [羅](../characters/羅.md) "net, gauze" — a transliteration of the name of the kingdom, so the characters carry no meaning of their own. 暹 is a 名専字 that appears only in this name; 羅's own stand-in is [[羅馬]], which is a different word.

暹羅 is Siam, the former name of Thailand (the modern state is [[泰国]]). English Wiktionary explains it as a short form of 暹羅斛, recorded in the *History of Ming* after the merger of two kingdoms in 1349: 暹 is from Thai สยาม, ultimately from Sanskrit श्याम (śyāma, "dark, swarthy"), and 羅斛 is the Lavo kingdom (now Lopburi), from Lavapura. The name was shortened to 暹羅 and stayed in use for the later Thai kingdoms; English Wiktionary adds that some ethnic Chinese in Thailand still use it for modern Thailand. Its attestations include Ma Huan's *Yingya Shenglan* (15th century) and Zhang Xie's *Dongxi yang kao* (1618).

Mandarin Xiānluó and Cantonese cim3 lo4 are as at English Wiktionary. Japanese シャム and Korean 시암 are the ordinary names of Siam in those languages (from memory, not re-checked), so `kwin` is false. The `vietnamese` field holds Xiêm La, the Hán-Việt reading (暹 xiêm, 羅 la; from memory, not re-checked as a compound).

The aliases 暹罗 and 暹邏 are the simplified and variant spellings. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄝㄇㄌㄛ unique across words and characters, checked 2026-10-12). The 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot.
