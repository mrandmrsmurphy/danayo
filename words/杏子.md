## Notes
- [杏](../characters/杏.md) "apricot" + [子](../characters/子.md) "thing" (nominalizer)
- 杏子 is the stand-in for [杏](../characters/杏.md), which is too bounded to stand alone.
- Japanese reading あんず is a fused form, not a concatenation of 杏 + 子.

The word is the ordinary Mandarin and Cantonese term for the apricot (xìngzi, hang6 zi2; Wiktionary), with 子 as the nominal suffix that turns the bound tree-name 杏 into the fruit's name. Dan'a'yo keeps this shared sense unchanged.

Japanese あんず is itself a borrowing of this very compound, from a dialectal Chinese form of 杏子, and survives today as the normal word for the apricot, with 杏子 as its kanji spelling; the 'n' and 'z' show a fused, assimilated shape rather than a syllable-by-syllable reading of 杏 + 子, so a Japanese learner will recognize the word with some effort. Korean has the native 살구 for the apricot, so the Korean reading here is the Sino-Xenic one.

It belongs to the 子-suffixed fruit series with [李子](李子.md) "plum" and [桃子](桃子.md) "peach".
