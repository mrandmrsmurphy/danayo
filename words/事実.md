## Notes
- [事 (char)](../characters/事%20(char).md) "matter, affair" + [実](../characters/実.md) "true, real, substantial" — the real substance of a matter, hence "fact, reality, truth."

事実 is the standard word for "fact" across the Sinosphere: Mandarin shìshí, Cantonese si6 sat6, Japanese じじつ (事実上, "in fact, de facto"; 事実は小説より奇なり, "truth is stranger than fiction"), and Korean 사실 (事実, extremely common, also used as a discourse-marking adverb "actually, in fact") are all fully compositional and among the most frequent words of their kind in each language. Vietnamese has two attested near-synonymous forms, sự thật and sự thực, both meaning "the truth/fact," with sự thật somewhat more common in everyday speech and sự thực carrying a slightly more formal/literary register — both kept as equally valid readings.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 지싣 diverges from Sino-Korean 사실, since 事's own Dan'a'yo reading doesn't match Sino-Korean, even though 実's own reading matches exactly (実 is independently `kwin: true`).
