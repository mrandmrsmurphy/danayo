## Notes
- [容 (char)](../characters/容%20(char).md) "look; appearance; form; figure" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character [容 (char)](../characters/容%20(char).md) as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

容 alone denotes outward appearance, bearing, or countenance — the face or form one presents — and is far more productive as a bound morpheme than as a free word: 内容 ("content, substance"), 容易 ("easy"), 容器 ("container"), 容量 ("volume, capacity"), 形容詞 ("adjective," literally "word that describes appearance"). The character's own graphemic root is phonetic 谷 (OC \*kloːɡ, "valley") + semantic 宀 ("roof, enclosure") — "to hold within an enclosure," which extended to both "capacity/content" and, separately, "the face one holds/presents," the two senses this single character still carries side by side.

**A genuine triple Middle Chinese homophone**: 容, [[庸]], and [[湧]] all reconstruct to the identical MC reading (以母 j + 鍾韻 ɨoŋ) despite descending from different phonetic components — 容 from 谷, 庸 from 用, 湧 from 甬 — so the collision in Dan'a'yo ⼄ㄫ/yong is a real convergence already present in Middle Chinese, not an artifact introduced by Dan'a'yo's own sound-mapping rules. Mandarin keeps the three apart only by tone (róng/yōng/yǒng); Cantonese similarly (jung4/jung4/jung2, only 容/庸 remaining fully identical); Dan'a'yo, like Korean 용, merges all three into one syllable with no tone to distinguish them.
