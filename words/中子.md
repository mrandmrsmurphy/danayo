## Notes
- [中 (char)](../characters/中%20(char).md) "middle" + [子](../characters/子.md) "son" — the second, or middle, son among a set of siblings, a classical genealogical term distinct from the eldest (長子) and youngest (季子/末子).

**A genuine and striking homograph, documented rather than resolved**: 中子 is also the standard modern word for "neutron" in Mandarin (zhōngzǐ), Vietnamese (trung tử — confirmed via web search specifically in this physics sense), and likely elsewhere across the sphere — the same two characters read identically but meaning something entirely unrelated, coined independently in the 20th century from "middle/neutral" (中, since a neutron carries no charge, "between" the proton's positive and the electron's negative) + "particle" (子, the same "small unit/entity" sense 子 carries in other scientific compounds like 原子 "atom," 電子 "electron"). This vault's own word specifically covers the classical "middle son" sense only, per its own English gloss — the neutron sense is a separate, unrelated modern coinage sharing an identical written and spoken form, not a shared etymology or definitional overlap.

**Vietnamese deliberately left blank**: while trung tử is genuinely attested in Vietnamese, that attestation is specifically for "neutron," not "middle son" — no independent Vietnamese attestation for the genealogical sense turned up in search, so rather than borrowing the neutron-sense reading and implying a match that doesn't actually exist, the field was left blank per the vault's own no-fabrication rule. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄨㄫㄐㄜ unique among both words and characters).
