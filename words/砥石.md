## Notes
- [砥](../characters/砥.md) "whetstone" + [石 (char)](../characters/石%20(char).md) "rock" — this word is the viable form of [砥](../characters/砥.md): the character's meaning (whetstone) is identical, but 砥 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 砥石 is its `stand_in` compound.

砥石 is a stone used for sharpening blades by abrasion — a fine-grained whetstone for precision edges, as distinct from the classical 礪 (a coarser, larger grindstone for rougher work). Japanese whetstone culture (といし) is especially refined, with natural and synthetic stones graded by grit for successive sharpening stages, central to traditional knife- and sword-sharpening craft.

Japanese reading といし uses native kun-yomi (と + いし); the on-reading concatenation would be しせき. Korean 지석 and Vietnamese chỉ thạch are both standard compositional Sino-readings. No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄧㄜㄙㄝㄎ unique across both words and characters).
