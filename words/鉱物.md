## Notes
- [鉱](../characters/鉱.md) "ore; mineral deposit; mining" + [物 (char)](../characters/物%20(char).md) "thing; substance; object"

鉱物 is "mineral": a naturally occurring inorganic substance with a defined chemical composition and crystalline structure. The compound is literally "ore-thing" (鉱 + 物), and it is the standard technical term in geology and mineralogy. 鉱's own `stand_in` is [[鉱石]], not this word, so 鉱物 does not legitimize 鉱; it is simply one of the compounds that uses it. The derived [[鉱物学]] "mineralogy" builds directly on it.

The traditional, simplified and kyūjitai spellings are recorded as aliases: 礦物 (traditional Chinese), 矿物 (simplified Chinese) and 鑛物 (Japanese kyūjitai). These match the aliases of the character 鉱 (礦, 矿, 鑛), and no separate word files exist under those names.

Cross-linguistically (checked against Wiktionary, 2026-10-11): the Chinese entry 礦物 gives Mandarin kuàngwù and Cantonese kwong3 mat6, with simplified 矿物, glossed "mineral", and lists Japanese 鉱物 (kōbutsu), Korean 광물 (gwangmul) and Vietnamese khoáng vật as descendants. The Japanese entry 鉱物 gives こうぶつ "minerals" and lists 鑛物 as the kyūjitai. The Vietnamese field khoáng vật is the Sino-Vietnamese reading, with 物 confirmed as vật on hvdic. The Cantonese field uses kong3 for 鉱, following the 鉱 character page (Wiktionary lists kwong3 for the compound; I take the two as variants, not re-checked further). Because 物 reads 묻 (ㄇㄨㄊ) in Dan'a'yo and the Sino-Korean is 물, the Dan'a'yo 諺文 광묻 differs from the Korean 광물 in its last letter.

In the Dan'a'yo lexipedia, 鉱物 is "mineral" in general, in contrast with [[鉱石]], which is "ore" specifically. This is the same as the shared CJKV sense; no Dan'a'yo-specific divergence is claimed.

Syllable boundary: the 注音 ㄍ⺢ㄫㄇㄨㄊ has no boundary dot because it has only one legal partition into syllables. Alternative splits fail: ㄫ is only a final in the consonant table and no syllable file begins with it (so ㄍ⺢ + ㄫㄇㄨㄊ is impossible), and ㄍ⺢ㄫㄇ is not an existing syllable (the ㄍ⺢- syllables are ㄍ⺢, ㄍ⺢ㄆ, ㄍ⺢ㄇ, ㄍ⺢ㄊ, ㄍ⺢ㄋ, ㄍ⺢ㄎ, ㄍ⺢ㄧ and ㄍ⺢ㄫ), which rules out ㄍ⺢ㄫㄇ + ㄨㄊ even though ㄨㄊ exists. Only ㄍ⺢ㄫ + ㄇㄨㄊ is legal.
