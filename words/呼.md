## Notes
- [呼 (char)](../characters/呼%20(char).md) "call; shout; exhale"

呼 is 形声: semantic [[口]] ("mouth") + phonetic [[乎]] — historically the same character as [[乎]] before the two graphically differentiated, [[乎]] narrowing to a grammatical particle while 呼 retained the concrete "to call, cry out, exhale" sense. It commonly takes both personal objects (呼人, "to call a person") and impersonal ones (呼名, "to call out a name"). Paired with 吸 ("to inhale"), it forms [[呼吸]] ("to breathe; respiration").

Mandarin hū, Cantonese fu1, and Japanese コ (ko, on'yomi, productive in compounds: 呼吸 kokyū "breathing," 呼応 koō "correspond, echo") alongside native よ(ぶ) (yo(bu), "to call," the everyday verb) all preserve the sense. Korean 호 (ho) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 부르다 (bureuda, "to call"). Vietnamese hô is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in hô hấp (呼吸, "respiration") and hoan hô (歡呼, "to cheer, hail"). The character page also stores hao/hào and há as additional candidates; these are not corpus noise — hao/hào is a genuine secondary classical reading of 呼 itself, attested via an alternate Tập Vận fanqie making it homophonous with 虓 ("a tiger's roar"), though with no surviving modern compound; há is likewise attested in standard Hán Việt dictionaries (as an exclamatory sentence-opening particle) but with a murkier Middle Chinese derivation. Both are documented here rather than added to the field, since hô remains the reading in live use.

This word completes a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read ho/호/ㄏㄛ): [[乎]] ("did; do"), already perfected and already cross-linking here, and [[虎]] ("tiger"), still unperfected and awaiting its own turn.
