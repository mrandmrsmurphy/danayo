## Etymology
- [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "metal, gold" + [柑](../characters/柑.md) "mandarin orange" — "kumquat," named for its golden-orange color.

## Notes
Japanese キンカン (kinkan) is the real, directly attested standard word for kumquat and is exactly compositional (金 KIN + 柑 KAN). **Found and fixed wrong-compound-reading bugs** on `mandarin`, `cantonese`, `korean`, and `vietnamese`: all four had been filled with readings borrowed from the differently-charactered near-synonym 金橘/柑橘 (whose second character is 橘 "tangerine," not 柑 "mandarin orange") rather than 金柑's own compositional reading — corrected to honest compositional jīngān, gam1 gam1, 금감, and kim cam respectively (each matching 金's and 柑's own stored readings exactly). **Found and removed a bad-aliases-entry**: `柑橘` is a different real word (meaning "citrus" generically, built from different characters) mistakenly listed as a spelling variant; kept `金桔`/`金橘`, genuine orthographic variants of this same word (橘/桔 being interchangeable). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 김감 diverges from Sino-Korean 금감 in the first syllable. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄧㄇㄍㄚㄇ unique among both words and characters). **Found and fixed a missing citation** on 柑.md's own Words list.
