## Notes
- [肉 (char)](../characters/肉%20(char).md) "meat" + [湯 (char)](../characters/湯%20(char).md) "hot water" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (both are stand-ins for themselves), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 肉 is individually `kwin: false`.

肉湯 means broth — literally "meat hot-water."

Mandarin ròutāng, Cantonese juk6 tong1, Japanese にくとう (nikutō), and Korean 육탕 (yuktang, the pharmacological/formal Sino-Korean term for meat broth) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 肉湯 is itself a common attested word for "broth" in all four languages. Vietnamese nhục thang is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading. **Fixed a real bug**: `korean` had 국물, the everyday native Korean word for "broth/soup" rather than 肉湯's own compositional reading — corrected to 육탕. Filled blank japanese/vietnamese. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
