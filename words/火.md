## Notes
- [火 (char)](../characters/火%20(char).md) "fire" — stand-in for [[火 (char)|火]], which cannot appear independently

火 is a 象形 pictograph of flame, compressed to the four-dot form 灬 at the bottom of a character (though the original standalone shape survives even there in 灸, "moxibustion") — the same graphemic-compression pattern as [[水]]'s 氵/氺 forms. Beyond the literal element, 火 generates both a folklore/imagery layer (鬼火, 燐火 "will-o'-the-wisp," 火鼠 "the mythological fire rat") and a technical/scientific one, including 火星 ("Mars," continuing the Five-Phases planet-naming pattern alongside 水星/[[水]] and 木星), 火曜日 ("Tuesday," the fire-day in the Five-Phases weekday scheme), and the vault-internal periodic-table abbreviation prefix in [[火紅素]] ("strontium," literally "fire-red element," describing the metal's characteristic crimson flame test color — a genuine descriptive coinage, unlike 水銀's cross-CJKV calque).

Japanese carries two on'yomi, か (KA, the default — 火山 kazan, 火曜日 kayōbi) and rarer こ (KO, confined to a small set of compounds), alongside the fully productive native 訓読み ひ (hi) — visible even inside rendaku'd compounds like 花火 (hanabi, "fireworks," lit. "flower-fire," where 火 surfaces as -bi). Vietnamese hoả stays in the learned/technical register (hoả tiễn "rocket," hoả hoạn "fire disaster") while everyday speech uses the native lửa for "fire" itself — the same Sino-technical/native-everyday split as [[日]], [[月]] and [[水]].
