## Notes
- [檳](../characters/檳.md) "betel nut" + [知](../characters/知%20(char).md) "know" — no stand-in relationship applies (檳's own is [[檳榔]], a cranberry pairing; 知's own is the single-character word [[知]]). The name is really written 檳椥, kept as an alias (with the simplified 槟椥, since 檳 has the alias 槟), but 椥 has no page in this vault, so 知 stands in for it (see below).

檳知 (Bến Tre, "Ben Tre") is a city in the Mekong Delta of southern Vietnam, the seat of the former Bến Tre province. The province existed from 1900 until it was abolished on 1 July 2025 and is now the north-eastern part of Vĩnh Long province, and the city's name survives as that of a ward (phường) of Vĩnh Long (Vietnamese Wikipedia, checked 2026-10-11, lead sentences only). The province lies between arms of the Mekong and is known for coconuts (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Wiktionary derives the name from bến "landing, quay" plus tre "bamboo", a calque of a Khmer name (Kampong Ruessei, "bamboo port"), as read 2026-10-11 and not independently verified. The Chinese characters therefore record sound and meaning together: 檳 stands for Bến and 椥 for tre "bamboo".

The real spelling is 檳椥, and the character 椥 is the awkward one. English Wiktionary's Japanese section calls it a 国字 (kokuji, "Japanese-coined character"), a Hyōgai kanji read なぎ (nagi) for the tree Nageia nagi and "used in placenames in Kyoto Prefecture"; its Chinese section says "Borrowed from Vietnamese"; and its Vietnamese section describes it as a chữ Nôm character for tre "bamboo", built from 木 plus the phonetic 知 (tri). Japanese Wiktionary files it under 日本の国字. This vault has no page for 椥 and by user decision it is not to be created, so the Dan'a'yo word is spelled with 知, the phonetic component of 椥, and 檳椥 is kept in `aliases`. English Wiktionary itself lists 檳知 as an alternative form of 檳椥. The Dan'a'yo sound ㄅㄧㄋㄐㄨㄧ comes from the character pages of 檳 and 知 (知 is ㄐㄨㄧ).

The `vietnamese` field holds the readings of the real spelling 檳椥 as far as they can be verified: tân for 檳 (the only Hán-Việt reading hvdic.thivien.net gives) and tre for 椥, which hvdic.thivien.net lists as a Nôm reading (tre, tri) and which has no Hán-Việt reading of its own. Note that 椥 is not read "chi": hvdic and Wiktionary give tre, with tri as the phonetic 知's reading. So tân tre is a hybrid, not the actual name, and it is not the everyday form: Vietnamese says Bến Tre, and English Wiktionary treats 檳椥 as the Han form of Bến Tre. Mandarin Bīnzhī and Cantonese ban1 zi1 are as in English Wiktionary (for 檳椥; 椥 itself is zhī, zi1 there). Japanese びんち is the compositional on'yomi (檳 BIN, 知 CHI), and Korean 빈지 the compositional North Korean reading; the city is normally written ベンチェ in Japanese (from memory, not re-checked).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 檳椥 as a Chinese proper noun (a former province, abolished in 2025, and a city, both in Vietnam, Mandarin Bīnzhī, Cantonese ban1 zi1) with 槟椥 simplified and 檳知 as an alternative, and a Vietnamese Han-form entry for Bến Tre; 槟椥 is a redirect there. Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 檳椥 page. No Wiktionary has a page for 檳知 itself (it appears only as an alternative form in the English entry). No homophones (注音 ㄅㄧㄋㄐㄨㄧ unique across both words and characters).
