## Notes
- [鼻 (char)](../characters/鼻%20(char).md) "nose" + [水 (char)](../characters/水%20(char).md) "water" — no `#cranberry`: both 鼻's and 水's own `stand_in` point to themselves, neither to this word. `kwin` is true (AND-rule: both constituents individually true).

Japanese はなみず (hanamizu, native 鼻+水 kun-reading compound) and Vietnamese nước mũi (literally "nose water," a native word-order-reversed compound — see [[黒洞]]/[[黒色]] for the same pattern) are both the real, everyday words for "snot" in their languages. Korean similarly uses the real native compound 콧물 (kot-mul, "nose-water") rather than the literal Sino-Korean compositional form **비수** — fixed a comma-joined contamination bug that had both jammed into one field (`"비수, 콧물"`); 비수 is additionally a real, unrelated Korean word meaning "dagger" (匕首), reinforcing that it isn't the intended sense here. Mandarin bíshuǐ and Cantonese bei6 seoi2 remain the plain compositional forms.
