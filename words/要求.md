## Notes
- [要](../characters/要.md) "important, need" + [求](../characters/求.md) "request"

要求 is the stand-in for [求](../characters/求.md), which is too bounded to appear alone in Dan'a'yo discourse. As discussed at [要請](要請.md), 要求 is the forceful counterpart to that word: 求 "to seek, pursue" gives 要求 the sense of a **demand or claim** — a personal or unilateral assertion of what one is owed or wants done — as opposed to 要請's formal, polite appeal.
要 is a 象形 character that originally pictured a person's waist (now written 腰) and was extended to "essential, important; to need," and 求, also 象形, originally depicted a fur coat (the original form of 裘) and was borrowed for its sound to mean "to request, to seek," as the two character pages record. 要求 therefore reads as "to need and seek": to ask for something one regards as necessary, which is the force behind its meaning "demand."

The word works as a verb ("to demand, to require") and as a noun ("a demand, a requirement, a claim"), and it is a common word in everyday, business and legal Chinese, Japanese and Korean for what one expects or requires of another (要求を満たす "to meet a requirement"). The Speech page sets it beside [要請](要請.md), which it calls the more formal and official register, and beside [催](催.md) "to press, urge," which is urging someone into action and not the plain demand. It is distinct from [要旨](要旨.md) "gist, essential point" and [要約](要約.md) "to summarize," which share 要 but mean the essential content, not a demand. The word is cited on both the Mind page and the Speech page, and Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "to demand, firmly request; a claim." The readings are Mandarin yāoqiú, Cantonese jiu1 kau4, Japanese yōkyū (ようきゅう), Korean 요구 and Sino-Vietnamese yêu cầu.
