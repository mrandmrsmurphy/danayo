## Notes
- [来 (char)](../characters/来%20(char).md) "come" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 来 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

来 means "to come." The character is 象形, originally depicting a stalk of wheat — the pictograph was borrowed phonetically for the unrelated sense "to come," with the original "wheat" meaning later inherited by the derived character [[麦]] once the phonetic loan took over.

Mandarin lái, Cantonese loi4, and Korean 래 (rae) all preserve the Sino reading directly and productively (将来 "future," 本来 "originally," 来年/来月/来週 "next year/month/week"). Japanese らい (RAI) survives mainly bound in these same compounds; the everyday native verb is くる (kuru, "to come"). Vietnamese lai is the Hán Việt reading, attested in compounds like tương lai (将来, "future") but rare as an independent word — everyday Vietnamese instead uses the native đến/tới. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 래 (lai) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 래. No homophones (注音 ㄌㄚㄧ unique among both words and characters).
