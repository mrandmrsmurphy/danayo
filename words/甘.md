## Notes
- [甘 (char)](../characters/甘%20(char).md) "sweet" — stand-in for [[甘 (char)|甘]], which cannot appear independently

甘 is 指事 (its own character page corrected the field from a wrongly-stored 象形 to the Wiktionary-confirmed 指事): something sweet held in an open mouth, a stroke inside the mouth-shape marking the sweetness itself rather than depicting an object directly. The character page documents this as the ancestor form of [[含]] ("to hold in the mouth"), the two sharing the same "something in the mouth" image before diverging into "sweet-tasting" and "holding" respectively. Beyond the literal taste, 甘 generalizes to "pleasant, willing, agreeable" in classical usage (甘心, "to be content, to do something willingly") — the same taste-to-attitude metaphor found across many languages, though this vault's own compound family (甘露 "nectar, amrita," 甘味 "sweetness," 甘藷 "sweet potato," 甘蕉 "banana/plantain") stays mostly within the literal-taste register rather than the figurative-willingness one.

Japanese has a single on'yomi かん (KAN, 甘味 kanmi, 甘藷 kansho), alongside the native 訓読み あま(い) (amai, "sweet"), the ordinary everyday adjective for taste in Japanese — a clean Sino-Japanese/native split matching the pattern seen throughout this sweep. Vietnamese cam appears in Sino-Vietnamese compounds (cam thảo "licorice," literally "sweet grass") but not as the everyday word for "sweet" itself, which Vietnamese instead expresses with the native ngọt — notably, cam is also the modern Vietnamese word for "orange" (the fruit), an unrelated homophone collision rather than a semantic extension of 甘 itself. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 감 (gam, from Middle Chinese 見母 k + 談韻 ɑm) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 감. 甘 shares its Dan'a'yo reading with [[紺]] ("navy blue," itself one of 甘's own phonetic derivatives per the character page's Derived Characters list) — a case where the homophone relationship and the phonetic-derivation relationship are the same fact, not a coincidence.
