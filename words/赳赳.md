## Notes
- [赳](../characters/赳.md) "valiant" + [赳](../characters/赳.md) "valiant" — reduplication for emphasis

赳赳 means strong and valiant, in a stately, martial way. English Wiktionary lists it for Chinese only, as 赳赳 (Mandarin jiūjiū, Cantonese gau2 gau2, also dau2 dau2), literary, glossed "strong and valiant". It has no Japanese or Korean entry for the doubled form, so Korean 규규 here is built from the characters, and no Japanese reading is recorded. For the single 赳 it gives Japanese "strong and brave".

The word is best known from the Classic of Poetry: each of the three stanzas of 兔罝 (the rabbit net, in the Zhounan) has the line 赳赳武夫, "valiant warriors", followed by 公侯干城, "the shield and wall of the lords"; zh.Wikisource's note on the poem glosses 赳赳 as 威武的樣子, "a martial bearing", and the single 赳 after the *Shuowen* as light, strong and talented.

This word is the viable form of [赳](../characters/赳.md): the character means strong and valiant, but is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo, so 赳赳 is its `stand_in` compound. A warrior is a [[武士]], and the martial arts are the [[武芸]].
