## Notes
- [飲 (char)](../characters/飲%20(char).md) "drink" + [食 (char)](../characters/食%20(char).md) "eat" + [物 (char)](../characters/物%20(char).md) "thing, result" — no `#cranberry`: no constituent's own `stand_in` points here (飲's/物's are themselves, 食's is 食 itself).

飲食物 (yǐnshíwù) is a real Mandarin word for "food and drink (items)," and Cantonese 飲食物 (jam2sik6mat6) likewise. Japanese いんしょくぶつ (inshokubutsu) and Korean 음식물 (eumsingmul) are both standard terms, the latter especially common in the compound 음식물 쓰레기 ("food waste").

Sino-Vietnamese ẩm thực vật is compositionally exact but not independently attested and risks ambiguity, since 植物 (thực vật, "plant") shares the same second-and-third syllables — a Vietnamese reader could easily misparse it as "plant-based cuisine" rather than "food and drink." Modern Vietnamese instead uses phrases like đồ ăn thức uống ("food and drink," native).
