## Notes
- [業](../characters/業%20(char).md) "deed" + [力](../characters/力%20(char).md) "strength" — "deed-power," the force of a deed. Their own `stand_in` values: 業's is itself; 力's is itself; none points here, so 業力 is an independent compound.

業力 is the force of a deed: in Buddhism, the power karma has to shape what follows. It is a neologism for Dan'a'yo's purposes: Wiktionary attests it for Chinese only (yèlì), so the other fields are the readings of the two characters. It takes the term over from Buddhism, in line with the vault's practice of adopting other religions' words.

**Hebrew**: it best translates sin as a *power*, "sin is crouching at the door; its desire is for you" (Genesis 4:7), and the Greek *hamartia* as a master that rules a person (Romans 6:12–14, 7:17). The power comes before the deed, which is [[罪業]], and the result comes after, which is [[業報]].
