## Notes
- [山 (char)](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountains" + [川 (char)](../characters/川%20(char).md) "rivers" — literally "mountains and rivers," used figuratively for natural scenery/landscape.

Mandarin shānchuān, Cantonese saan1 cyun1, Japanese さんせん (sansen), Korean 산천, and Vietnamese sơn xuyên are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 山's and 川's own stored citations exactly.

Beyond the shared "scenery" sense, 山川 pairs the two basic landforms of the Chinese landscape, high ground and flowing water, and so reads as "the lay of the land" in all five languages, much as [[草木]] ("grass and trees") stands for vegetation as a whole. Japanese also reads the same two characters やまかわ as a surname and place-name element, a native reading that is separate from the Sino-Japanese さんせん given here.
