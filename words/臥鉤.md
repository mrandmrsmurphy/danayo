## Notes
- [臥 (char)](../characters/臥%20(char).md) "lie down" + [鉤 (char)](../characters/鉤%20(char).md) "hook"

**臥鉤** is the *lying hook*, the flat, curved stroke with an upturned end seen in the character 心. Chinese instruction also calls it the 心鉤 ("heart hook"): the brush starts lightly and moves to the lower right in a rounded arc, growing heavier, and ends slightly upturned with the hook pointing back toward the center of the character. It is a sub-variety of the hook ([[鉤]]), the fourth of the eight principles of the 永字八法, and is listed on the [[Stroke]] page beside [[戈鉤]].

The name is descriptive: the stroke lies on its side, in contrast to the upright hooks that end vertical strokes.

The Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and North Korean forms given here are compositional. No Vietnamese form was found attested, so none is given.
