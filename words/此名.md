## Notes
- [此 (char)](../characters/此%20(char).md) "this; proximal demonstrative" + [名 (char)](../characters/名%20(char).md) "name; polite person classifier"

此名 is the proximal correlative in the 名 ("name; polite person") column of the correlative table: "this person (polite)", the deferential counterpart of [此人](此人.md) "this person", which is stylistically neutral. The character [此 (char)](../characters/此%20(char).md) (Dan'a'yo *cǝ*, ㄑㄜ) is the classical Chinese proximal demonstrative, contrasting with 其 (medial) and 彼 (distal); [名 (char)](../characters/名%20(char).md) (Dan'a'yo *ming*, ㄇㄧㄫ) is "name", and the word page [名](名.md) records its second job as a polite classifier for counting people (五名 "five persons, polite", 何名様 "how many guests, very honorific"). The 名 column thus carries the polite register of the person slot: [其名](其名.md) and [彼名](彼名.md) are the polite counterparts of [其人](其人.md) and [彼人](彼人.md), and [何名](何名.md) is the very polite "who?". The vault's note on this ladder reads "誰 is normal, 何人 is rude, 何名 is very polite" (`grammar/文法 - 97品詞.md`, line 154). Note that [名](名.md)'s own prose glosses 此名 as "this name; what this is called"; that conflicts with the glosses on the character pages, on [此人](此人.md), and on this page's `english` field ("this person (polite)"), which are the documented function, so this page follows the latter.

The cross-linguistic fields hold the spoken polite equivalents, not readings of the two characters. Mandarin 這位 (zhè wèi): Wiktionary lists it as a pronoun glossed "this person" (it also lists a Hakka/Hokkien sense "here"), and lists 位 with the sense "(often, polite) classifier for persons", with the examples 這位歌手 "this singer" and 那位先生 "that man". Japanese この方 (kono kata): Wiktionary's entry for 方 has the sense "(polite) person" (あの方 "that person"), so この方 is the native adnominal この plus the polite person noun 方; Wiktionary has no separate entry for この方 itself. Korean 이분 (i-bun): Wiktionary has an entry glossed "this gentleman, this lady, he, she, it", compounded from 이 + 분, and 분 itself is listed as an honorific noun "person". Vietnamese is left blank: from memory *vị này* would be the polite equivalent, but that was not checked on hvdic.thivien.net (which covers single characters) and is not entered. Dan'a'yo does not copy any one of these morphemes; it marks politeness with the column marker 名 across all seven determiners. Why 名 was chosen (identifying someone by their "name" or standing as a mark of deference) is a plausible rationale, not something the grammar files state. Illustrative only (invented, not from `grammar/`): 此名在 "this person (polite) is here".

Wiktionary has no entry for 此名. On [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md)'s correlative table (line 121; the same chart is in `grammar/文法 - 97品詞.md`, line 147), 此名 is the fourth cell of the 此 ("this") row, after [此事](此事.md) "this matter", [此物](此物.md) "this thing" and [此人](此人.md) "this person", and before [此処](此処.md) "this place", [此時](此時.md) "this time", [此様](此様.md) "this manner", [此多](此多.md) "this amount" and [此類](此類.md) "this kind". The table is 7 determiners (此, 其, 彼, 何, 毎, 某, 皆) × 9 noun classes (事, 物, 人, 名, 処, 時, 様, 多, 類), 63 forms, so 此名 is parsed compositionally: 此 = proximal, 名 = polite person. The 名 row is the one column whose contrast is register rather than deixis or quantification, and a learner who knows the 人 row can derive it by swapping 人 for 名. Dan'a'yo's sense is the shared CJKV polite "this person"; no further narrowing is needed.

The ㄑㄜ·ㄇㄧㄫ 注音 carries a syllable-boundary dot because the string has a second legal partition: ㄑㄜㄇ + ㄧㄫ, with ㄇ as a nasal coda (allowed in `grammar/文法 - 02音韻論.md`) and ㄧㄫ as a null-onset syllable (the syllable page ㄧㄫ exists). The dot marks the intended ㄑㄜ + ㄇㄧㄫ.
