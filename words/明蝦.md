## Notes
- [明 (char)](../characters/明%20(char).md) "bright" + [蝦](../characters/蝦.md) "shrimp" — the page's gloss is "king prawn, a large striped seawater shrimp." Attested in Mandarin and Cantonese; other languages not yet confirmed.

Wiktionary's entry for 明蝦 (simplified 明虾) is Chinese only and glosses it "shrimp; prawn," specifically the Chinese white shrimp or fleshy prawn (*Fenneropenaeus chinensis*); it does not use the term "king prawn" at all. So the vault's gloss "king prawn" is a looser, English-market rendering of a large Chinese prawn, and the species named in the Chinese sources is a different animal from the large striped tiger prawns that English usually calls king prawns. I have left the `english` field and the Food page entry as they are, and flag the mismatch here for you to decide whether "king prawn" should become "Chinese white prawn" or similar.

The Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese fields, which were blank, now hold the compositional character readings (めいか, 명하, minh hà), following the vault's convention for these fields; Wiktionary has no entry for the word in those languages. The Korean 명하 matches the Dan'a'yo 諺文 exactly, so `kwin` is true. The Food page lists the word as "a king prawn."
