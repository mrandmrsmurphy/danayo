## Notes
- [簡](../characters/簡.md) "simple, brief" + [潔](../characters/潔.md) "clean, pure" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (簡's is [[簡単]], 潔's is [[清潔]]), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 潔 is individually `kwin: false`. Brief and clean at once: concise, succinct, without clutter.

簡潔 describes language or writing specifically — a 簡潔 explanation says exactly what it needs to and no more — distinguishing it from [[簡略]] (brevity achieved by omitting detail) and [[簡単]] (ease, regardless of length). The grammar book uses it to describe a structural property of Dan'a'yo itself: its adnominal constructions are 簡潔 (concise) compared to Japanese or Korean, though also more rigid (硬直的) as a consequence — a trade-off the book draws explicit attention to.

Mandarin jiǎnjié, Cantonese gaan2 git3, Japanese かんけつ (kanketsu), and Korean 간결 (gangyeol) are all standard, fully compositional readings, each the ordinary everyday word for "concise" in its language. Vietnamese giản khiết is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form (簡's own giản, matching [[簡単]]/[[簡易]]/[[簡略]], and 潔's own khiết, matching [[清潔]]).
