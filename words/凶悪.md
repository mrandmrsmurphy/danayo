## Notes
- [凶 (char)](../characters/凶%20(char).md) "unlucky; sinister" + [悪 (char)](../characters/悪%20(char).md) "bad; evil"

凶悪 means heinous: evil in a way that is monstrous and threatening, atrocious. English Wiktionary glosses Japanese きょうあく as "heinous, atrocious" and "something heinous, an atrocity", and Korean 흉악 as "evil; wicked; heinous". For Chinese 兇惡 (an alias, with 凶恶; Mandarin xiōng'è, Cantonese hung1 ok3) it gives "savage; fierce; ferocious", which leans toward fierceness of manner rather than moral horror. It has no Vietnamese entry, so Vietnamese hung ác here is built from the characters.

The compound layers 凶, ill-omened and sinister, onto 悪, bad or evil, for something stronger than either. A related, less extreme word is [[凶暴]], and the plain word for evil is [[悪]].

As a 性詞 in Dan'a'yo, it modifies nouns directly.
