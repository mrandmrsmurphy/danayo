## Notes
- [韻](../characters/韻.md) "rhyme" + [律](../characters/律.md) "rule, regulation" — no stand-in relationship applies (韻's own stand-in is [[押韻]]; 律's is [[法律]]).

韻律 is the "rule of the rhyme": the patterned sound of speech and verse. It carries three senses that run together in the source languages, and Dan'a'yo keeps all of them. In linguistics it is **prosody**, the rhythm, stress, pitch and intonation of an utterance above the level of single sounds. In poetry it is the **meter**, the measured pattern of a line. And in Chinese verse it is the **rhyme scheme**, the rules of which sounds rhyme and where. The same word covers all three because in all three the question is how sounds are arranged in time.

On the phonology page the word heads the section on tone and prosody (声調・韻律), which says that Dan'a'yo does not use lexical tone, that pitch-accent serves only as an aid to marking where words and phrases begin and end, and that the sentence-final particles carry the force that intonation carries elsewhere. It is therefore distinct from [[声調]] ("tone"), the pitch that distinguishes words, from [[押韻]] ("to rhyme", the act of matching final sounds), and from [[音律]] ("musical temperament; metrical rhythm"), which names the tuning or beat of a tune or line where 韻律 names the whole pattern of sound.

Wiktionary records Mandarin yùnlǜ with the senses "meter (in verse)", "rules of rhyming; rhyme scheme" and "rhythmic", and Cantonese wan5 leot6; for Japanese いんりつ (inritsu) it gives "(poetry) meter" and "(linguistics) prosody". Korean 운률 is the North Korean reading of the same characters, which keeps the 률 of 律; South Korean 운율 shifts it to 율 after ㄴ, and neither was separately verified in a Korean dictionary. Wiktionary and hvdic have no Vietnamese entry, so that field is omitted. No homophones (注音 ㄨㄋㄌㄨㄊ unique across both words and characters).
