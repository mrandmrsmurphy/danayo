> [[蓋然]] "highly probable, likely, plausible"

## Notes
- [概](../characters/概%20(char).md) "approximate, general" + [然](../characters/然%20(char).md) "so" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (概's own is itself; 然's own is itself); 概然 is an independent compound. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: `characters/概%20(char).md` is `kwin: true`, `characters/然%20(char).md` is `kwin: false` → compound `false`.

概然 means generally so, roughly true, broadly speaking — a judgment held as true in general or on the whole, without claiming the strict, case-by-case certainty of [[必然]]/[[確実]]. It draws on 概's own core sense of "approximate, overall" (the same root behind [[概要]] "overview" and [[大概]] "generally, for the most part"), read here as an adjectival "so" via the 然 suffix.

**Dan'a'yo-specific sense, deliberately assigned**: real Chinese and Japanese usage genuinely confuses 蓋然(性) and 概然(性), since 蓋 and 概 are true homophones in Mandarin (gài), Japanese on'yomi (GAI), and Korean (개) — a confusion documented directly in Japanese-learner reference material asking how to tell them apart. Because the ambiguity is real rather than invented, Dan'a'yo uses it as an opportunity to cleanly split a distinction the source languages only gesture at: [[蓋然]] keeps the "probability, likelihood" sense (high confidence, short of certainty), while 概然 takes the "generally/roughly/on the whole" sense already present in 概's own root meaning — the register that contrasts with [[確実]]/[[必然]] ("certain, definite") not by expressing a *probability* but by expressing an *approximation*. Vietnamese is the only one of the five daughter languages that already keeps the pair apart on its own: 蓋 is Hán Việt *cái*, 概 is Hán Việt *khái* — so 蓋然/概然's Vietnamese fields (cái nhiên / khái nhiên) are not homophones, even though every other language's reading collapses them, Dan'a'yo's own reading included (gainyen/개년/ㄍㄚㄧㄋ⼶ㄋ, identical to [[蓋然]]'s).
