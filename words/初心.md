## Notes
- [初](../characters/初.md) "beginning; first" + [心](../characters/心%20(char).md) "heart; mind" — "the mind at the beginning".

**Meaning clash across the source languages.** 初心 is one of the words on which the source languages genuinely diverge. Dan'a'yo takes the Japanese-leaning sense: a beginner, a novice, a newcomer to something, and the open, unpracticed frame of mind that goes with it ("beginner's mind"). Mandarin chūxīn (and Cantonese co1 sam1) does not normally mean that: there it is "one's original intention, initial aspiration, founding purpose", best known from Xi Jinping-era slogans and older sayings such as 不忘初心 "do not forget your original aspiration". Chinese speakers meeting 初心 in the Dan'a'yo sense will therefore expect "original aspiration", and a Chinese-speaking reader should treat the Dan'a'yo gloss "novice" as the Japanese one.

Japanese しょしん covers both ends. Wiktionary gives (1) immaturity, naiveté, (2) original intention or objective, and (3) in Buddhism and the arts, the eager, open attitude of a beginner. Its everyday derivative 初心者 (しょしんしゃ) is the ordinary word for "beginner" (初心者マーク, the green-and-yellow sticker for new drivers), and 初心忘るべからず ("never lose the beginner's mind") is a famous maxim from the Noh master Zeami's writings: keep the humility and freshness of a beginner however skilled you become. That maxim is the bridge between the two readings, since "remaining true to one's first resolve" and "keeping a beginner's mind" are two faces of the same idea. Korean 초심 and Vietnamese *sơ tâm* correspond mostly to the Chinese side: Korean 초심 is "one's original resolve" (초심을 잃지 않다 "not to lose one's initial resolve"), and hvdic.thivien.net glosses *sơ tâm* as "the heart at the beginning", similar to 本心. Dan'a'yo's novice sense is therefore a Japan-led choice that Korean and Vietnamese speakers will recognize as a variation on their "initial mind", and only Mandarin and Cantonese speakers will find it the less obvious reading. (The Korean and Chinese-side usage descriptions here are from general knowledge and were not separately verified beyond the Wiktionary and hvdic entries.)

In Dan'a'yo it is used for learning contexts, such as 初心学習, "learning by newcomers" in the writing-system chapter ([[文法 - 03文字法]]). It sits beside [[学習]] "study, learn" and [[学習者]] "learner" in the vault: 初心 marks the learner's stage (the beginning) rather than the activity. It is not a stand-in for either character: the stand-in for 初 is [[最初]] "first, beginning", and 心 is itself the stand-in for its own character, [[心]] "heart".

Readings: Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean from Wiktionary; Vietnamese from Wiktionary and hvdic.thivien.net. 諺文 초심 equals the Korean 초심, so `kwin` is true. No homophones (注音 ㄑㄛㄙㄧㄇ is unique among words and chengyu).
