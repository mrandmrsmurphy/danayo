## Notes
- [窃](../characters/窃.md) "steal" + [盗](../characters/盗.md) "burgle" — 盗's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 窃's own is [[窃取]]; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 盗 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word (already correctly noted).

窃盗 is the general legal term for theft or larceny — taking another's property without force or confrontation, distinct from 強盗 ("robbery," which involves force or threat).

Mandarin qièdào, Cantonese sit3 dou6, Japanese せっとう (settō), and Korean 절도 (jeoldo) are all standard, fully compositional readings, each the ordinary legal/everyday term for "theft" in its language. Vietnamese thiết đạo is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form; everyday Vietnamese more often uses the native ăn trộm or ăn cắp.
