## Notes
- [三 (char)](../characters/三%20(char).md) "three" + [綱](../characters/綱.md) "guiding principle" — the Three Bonds of Confucian ethics: ruler–subject (君臣), father–son (父子), and husband–wife (夫婦), each pairing assigning the first party a dominant role the second is expected to obey and respect.

The doctrine was formally systematized by Han-dynasty scholar Dong Zhongshu in his *Chunqiu Fanlu*, who paired it with the Five Constant Virtues (仁義禮智信) into the combined formula 三綱五常, already cross-linked from this page via the vault's own chengyu entry [[三綱五常]]. Vietnamese tam cương (confirmed via web search) preserves the doctrine intact and remains the standard term used when discussing Confucian ethics in Vietnamese scholarship, almost always cited alongside ngũ thường exactly as in the source Chinese formula. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 삼강 coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 삼강. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄚㄇㄍㄚㄫ unique among both words and characters).
