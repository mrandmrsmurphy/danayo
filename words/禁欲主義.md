## Notes
- [禁](../characters/禁.md) "prohibit, forbid" + [欲](../characters/欲.md) "desire, want" + [主](../characters/主.md) "lord, master" + [義](../characters/義.md) "meaning, principle" — literally "suppress-desire" ([[禁欲]]) + "-ism" ([[主義]]). None of the four characters' own `stand_in` points here, so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 禁 alone is individually `kwin: false`.

禁欲主義 is asceticism as a formal doctrine — the systematic renunciation of bodily pleasure and desire as a path to spiritual, moral, or philosophical attainment, built compositionally from [[禁欲]] ("be ascetic, suppress desire") plus the productive doctrine-forming suffix [[主義]] ("-ism," as in 社会主義 "socialism," 資本主義 "capitalism"). Historical ascetic movements across the CJKV cultural sphere — Buddhist monasticism, Daoist self-cultivation, Christian desert monasticism, Stoic-influenced Confucian self-discipline — are all describable with this term.

**Fixed a truncated `japanese`**: きんよくしゅ was missing its final mora — corrected to きんよくしゅぎ (kin'yoku-shugi), matching 主義's own standalone reading しゅぎ. Mandarin jìnyùzhǔyì, Cantonese gam3 juk6 zyu2 ji6, and Korean 금욕주의 (geumyokjuui) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese cấm dục chủ nghĩa is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form.
