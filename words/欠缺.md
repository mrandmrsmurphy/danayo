## Notes
- [欠 (char)](../characters/欠%20(char).md) "yawn" + [缺](../characters/缺.md) "incomplete, lacking"
- This word is the stand-in for [缺](../characters/缺.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

欠 is pictured as a person with the mouth open, hence "yawn," and was extended to mean "to owe, to lack" (a gap that needs filling), while 缺 is built from 缶 "earthen jar" and was first a vessel with a chipped rim, hence "chipped, incomplete, missing." 欠缺 joins the two senses of falling short into a synonym compound: to be missing something that ought to be there, or the missing part itself. It is a formal, written word, common in legal and bureaucratic language (a defect in the formalities, a gap in the law) and in the evaluation of work, much more than in daily speech.

The Existence page pairs it with [欠乏](欠乏.md) "to lack, be deficient" as near-synonyms beside the plain negation of existence, [無](無.md); the pair differs mainly in that 欠缺 stresses a specific defect or missing element and 欠乏 a general shortage of supply. The readings are Mandarin qiànquē, Cantonese him3 kyut3, Japanese kenketsu (けんけつ), Korean 흠결 and Sino-Vietnamese khiếm khuyết (khiếm for 欠 and khuyết for 缺 on their character pages). Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "lack, be deficient in, be missing."
