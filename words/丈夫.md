## Notes
- [丈 (char)](../characters/丈%20(char).md) "elder, unit of length" + [夫 (char)](../characters/夫%20(char).md) "man"

丈夫 means husband: a married man in relation to his wife. The word is built from 丈, an old unit of length (about ten 尺) that also denotes an elder or adult man, and 夫 "man"; the compound originally meant a full-grown, manly man. Related words in the vault are [[丈人]] "father-in-law" and [[夫婦]] "married couple".

Mandarin zhàngfu, Cantonese zoeng6 fu1, and Japanese じょうぶ are all real attested readings — though Japanese じょうぶ specifically means "robust, sturdy, healthy" rather than "husband," a false-friend divergence in meaning (not just pronunciation) from the Chinese/Korean sense, kept as the real Japanese reading of this exact written form regardless. Korean uses the real native 남편 rather than a Sino-Korean compositional form (장부, which as a real Korean word instead means "a great/manly man," as in 대장부, not specifically "husband"). Vietnamese chồng is likewise the real, everyday native word.
