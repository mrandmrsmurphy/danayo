## Notes
- [丈 (char)](../characters/丈%20(char).md) "elder, unit of length" + [夫 (char)](../characters/夫%20(char).md) "man" — no `#cranberry`: both 丈's and 夫's own `stand_in` point to themselves, neither to this word.

**Fixed a real internal-consistency bug**: `羅馬字`/`諺文`/`注音` had been stored as `jangbu`/`장부`/`ㄐㄚㄫㄅㄨ` — this is actually the Sino-Korean compositional reading (丈's own Korean 장 + 夫's own Korean 부), not Dan'a'yo's own derivation (丈's own stored `창`/`cang`/`ㄑㄚㄫ` diverges from Korean precisely because `kwin` is false for 丈 itself). Corrected the Dan'a'yo headword to the proper mechanical concatenation of each constituent's own stored triple: `cangfǝ`/`창쁘`/`ㄑㄚㄫㄈㄜ`. The identical mix-up had also propagated into this word's own citations on both `characters/丈 (char).md` and `characters/夫 (char).md`'s `## Words` lists, fixed there too.

Mandarin zhàngfu, Cantonese zoeng6 fu1, and Japanese じょうぶ are all real attested readings — though Japanese じょうぶ specifically means "robust, sturdy, healthy" rather than "husband," a false-friend divergence in meaning (not just pronunciation) from the Chinese/Korean sense, kept as the real Japanese reading of this exact written form regardless. Korean uses the real native 남편 rather than a Sino-Korean compositional form (장부, which as a real Korean word instead means "a great/manly man," as in 대장부, not specifically "husband"). Vietnamese chồng is likewise the real, everyday native word. `kwin` is false (AND-rule: both 丈 and 夫 individually false).
