## Notes
- [挫](../characters/挫.md) "failure, setback, frustration" + [折](../characters/折.md) "fold, break"
- This word is the stand-in for [挫](../characters/挫.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

A compound meaning a serious check or reversal — the experience of being bent back on one's course. In Japanese, ざせつ (zasetsu) is the standard word for frustration, defeat, or loss of momentum; 挫折感 (zasetsu-kan) is the sense of having failed or been thwarted. Chinese 挫折 (cuòzhé) similarly means "setback" or "frustration," and is frequently used with negative states: 遭受挫折 "to suffer a setback." Korean 좌절 (挫折) carries the same meaning with a particular emotional weight — a common word in discussions of failure, despair, and resilience. The etymological image is of being bent and broken: 挫 "to crush, collapse inward" + 折 "to snap, fold over."

The word names a check on one's progress that comes from outside or from circumstances, and it is closest to the existing [失敗](失敗.md) "failure," but where 失敗 is the failure of an attempt, 挫折 is the blow to one's momentum and spirits that follows (a failed exam, a broken career path, a thwarted plan), so it can be said of a setback one then recovers from. The Conflict page places it for that sense of being defeated or checked. Its Vietnamese form on this page, doá chiết, mixes the Nôm reading doá of 挫 (the Hán Việt reading is toả) with chiết for 折, as the character pages record. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "setback, frustration, defeat," with no divergence.
