## Notes
- [皮](../characters/皮.md) "skin, hide" + [肉 (char)](../characters/肉%20(char).md) "flesh, meat" — literally "skin and flesh." No stand-in relationship applies (皮's own `stand_in` is [[皮革]]; 肉's own is [[肉]] itself).

皮肉 traces to the Buddhist expression 皮肉骨髄 ("skin, flesh, bones, marrow"), attributed to Bodhidharma evaluating his disciples' depth of understanding — 皮肉 ("skin and flesh") named the shallow, surface-level layer, as opposed to 骨髄 ("bone and marrow," the deep essence). From "superficial, missing the real point," the sense extended in Japanese to irony, sarcasm, and shallow or roundabout criticism — saying one thing while meaning, or landing on, another.

Japanese ひにく is the living, standard word for this sense (皮肉を言う, "to say something ironic/sarcastic"; 皮肉なことに, "ironically"). Mandarin píròu, Cantonese pei4 juk6, Korean 피육, and Vietnamese bì nhục are the compositional readings from each character's own attested value — this figurative sense is Japanese-attested specifically; the other four languages are not independently confirmed to use this exact compound figuratively, only literally ("skin and flesh"). `kwin` is false: both 皮 and 肉 are individually `kwin: false`. No homophones (注音 ㄅㄧ·ㄋㄨㄎ unique across both words and characters).
