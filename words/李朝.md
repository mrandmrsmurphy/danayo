## Notes
- [李](../characters/李.md) "plum" + [朝 (char)](../characters/朝%20(char).md) "morning" — no stand-in relationship applies (李's own is [[李子]]; 朝's own is the single-character word [[朝]]). Here 李 is the surname Lǐ (Korean Yi/Ri, Vietnamese Lý) and 朝 is "dynasty", as in [[王朝]] and [[宋朝]]: "the Li dynasty", the dynasty of a house surnamed 李.

This name is a collision between two unrelated dynasties founded by houses surnamed 李, one Korean and one Vietnamese. Neither is a Chinese dynasty, so there is no Chinese sense to put first; the order follows what the Wiktionaries list first, which is the Korean one (English Wiktionary has 李朝 only as the Korean hanja form of 이조; Japanese Wiktionary lists the Joseon abbreviation first and the Vietnamese dynasty second). Chinese Wikipedia's disambiguation page lists the Vietnamese dynasty before the Korean one, and Japanese Wikipedia adds that in Vietnamese history 李朝 usually means the Vietnamese dynasty, so the order is a lexicographic choice, not a ranking of importance.

1. The Yi dynasty, an older Chinese and Japanese short name for 李氏朝鮮, the Joseon dynasty of Korea, founded in 1392 by the general Yi Seong-gye (李成桂) and ruled by his Jeonju Yi house; Chinese Wikipedia dates it 1392–1897 and the state is usually taken to run to the annexation of 1910 (the later span is from memory, not re-checked). It succeeded [[高麗]] and is the dynasty properly named by [[朝鮮]]. The `korean` field holds 리조, the reading built from the character pages (North Korean, initial 리 kept); the South Korean spelling of the same word is 이조, which is what the Korean Wiktionary and English Wiktionary give. In present-day South Korea 이조 is considered dated and 조선 is preferred (from memory, not re-checked).
2. The Lý dynasty (Vietnamese Lý triều or nhà Lý), the first long-lived independent Vietnamese dynasty, 1009–1225 (Japanese Wiktionary gives the same dates and the Hanoi capital), founded by Lý Công Uẩn (Lý Thái Tổ), who moved the capital to Thăng Long, the later Hanoi, in 1010 (from memory, not re-checked). It took the state name Đại Việt in 1054 (from memory, not re-checked) and ended in 1225 when the child empress Lý Chiêu Hoàng gave the throne to her husband Trần Cảnh, founder of the dynasty treated at [[陳朝]] (from memory, not re-checked). The `vietnamese` field holds Hán-Việt Lý triều (lý 李, triều 朝); the Vietnamese name Lý is also the surname.

Two further homographs of this spelling are set aside here: the Former Lý dynasty (前李朝, 544–602, founded by Lý Bí) of early Vietnam, and a general of Shu Han named Li Chao (both from Chinese Wikipedia's disambiguation page, not otherwise checked).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11. English Wiktionary: Korean section only (hanja form of 이조, "Joseon dynasty"). Japanese Wiktionary: 李朝 (りちょう), proper noun, with both senses (Joseon abbreviation, Vietnamese dynasty 1009–1225). Korean Wiktionary: 李朝 as the hanja writing of 이조. Chinese and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no entry. hvdic.thivien.net has no entry for the compound; lý triều is built from its entries for 李 and 朝. Mandarin Lǐcháo and Cantonese lei5 ciu4 are compositional (not attested in a dictionary). No homophones (注音 ㄌㄧㄐㄚㄨ unique across both words and characters).
