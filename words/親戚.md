## Notes
- [親](../characters/親.md) "parent" is legitimized by this exact compound (its own stand-in). [戚](../characters/戚.md) "grieving, sorrowful" is separately legitimized by [[哀戚]]. No cranberry.

## Etymology
- [親](../characters/親.md) "parent" + [戚](../characters/戚.md) "grieving, sorrowful"

Dan'a'yo distinguishes 親戚 from [[親族]]: 親戚 is the everyday word for the relatives one actually knows (Japanese example: "annoying relatives are coming"); 親族 is the formal register, the kin group as a unit. Both are real in Mandarin (qīnqī / qīnzú), Japanese (しんせき / しんぞく), Korean (친척 / 친족) and Vietnamese (thân thích / thân tộc), and Wiktionary draws no distinction between them, so this split is a Dan'a'yo design choice made for predictability, not a claim about all source usage. The classical sense of 親戚 also covers one's immediate family.
