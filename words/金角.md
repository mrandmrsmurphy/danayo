## Notes
- [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "gold" + [角 (char)](../characters/角%20(char).md) "horn" — "gold horn"; no stand-in relationship applies (金 and 角 are their own stand-ins, the single-character words [[金]] and [[角]]).

金角 is a proper noun: Jinjiao (Golden Horn, Japanese 金角, きんかく), the elder of the two brother demon kings Golden Horn and Silver Horn (金角大王 and 銀角大王) in the Ming novel *Journey to the West* (see [[孫悟空]] and [[猪八戒]] for the other characters of that book). Chinese Wikipedia's article 金角大王 says they lived in the Lotus Cave on Flat-Top Mountain, were servants of Laozi (太上老君) who stole his treasures and came down to earth, and were finally sucked into a magic gourd by Sun Wukong (the Japanese article has the pair trapped in a gourd and a vase). Japanese Wikipedia has 金角 as a short disambiguation page whose first entry is this monster ("also called 金角大王"; its other entry is the former pen name of the writer ゲッツ板谷), and the article 金角・銀角, which adds that in Japan the pair are often pictured with horns because of the name, and that in the story they turn out to be boys guarding Laozi's gold and silver furnaces. No source I checked explains the name itself, so I give no etymology of it. The word is typed 固有名詞 because both senses are names; no common-noun sense of 金角 stands on its own (see the Go note below).

Cam Ranh is not 金角: the city is [[金蘭]] and its bay [[金蘭湾]], and an earlier version of the vault wrongly conflated the two.

Go. The expression 金角 "gold corner" is used in the Go proverb 金角銀邊銅肚皮 (in mainland China 金角銀邊草肚皮, "corner gold, edge silver, belly [centre] copper or grass"), which ranks corners, edges and centre by how cheaply territory can be enclosed there. Chinese Wikipedia has the proverb as an article. None of the Wiktionaries, and no dictionary I checked, has 金角 as a Go term by itself, only inside the proverb, so I give it no sense of its own; this is a note on usage, not a definition.

The `mandarin` Jīnjiǎo, and the Japanese きんかく and Korean 금각, are the readings of the name. Japanese きんかく is attested (Japanese Wikipedia, above). Cantonese gam1 gok3 is the compositional reading, the same as English Wiktionary gives for 金角灣; no Cantonese source for 金角 as a standalone name was found. Korean 금각 is the compositional North Korean reading (金 금, 角 각), not attested for this name. The `vietnamese` field holds the compositional Hán-Việt reading kim giác (金 kim, 角 giác; hvdic.thivien.net has no entry for the compound, only the character entries), which is the Han-form reading of the Journey to the West name. Japanese Wiktionary's stub for 金角 glosses it only as 喇叭 ("trumpet"), which I take to be a different word from the above or an error and cannot explain, and I have not used it.

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English, Chinese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 金角 page. Japanese Wiktionary has a stub, 金角 (きんかく) glossed "喇叭" (above). Chinese Wikipedia's 金角 resolves to the article 金角大王 (Journey to the West); Japanese Wikipedia has 金角 and 金角・銀角; English, Korean and Vietnamese Wikipedias have no 金角 page. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄧㄇㄍㄛㄎ unique across words, chengyu and characters, other than as the beginning of [[金角湾]]); the 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot because ㄎ cannot stand alone as a syllable.
