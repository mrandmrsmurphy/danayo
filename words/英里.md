## Notes
- [英](../characters/英.md) "hero; English" + [里 (char)](../characters/里%20(char).md) "li, traditional unit of distance" — literally "English li," the standard Chinese calque for the Western mile.

英里 names the Western statute mile (1,609.344m) specifically, keeping it distinct from the native East Asian unit [[里]] it borrows its second syllable from — the same naming logic as 英尺 (foot) and 英寸 (inch), which all prefix 英 ("English") to an existing native unit of length to mark a foreign import. Mandarin and Cantonese use 英里 as their genuine, everyday word for "mile," with no living alternative.

Japanese and Korean, however, do not actually use the compositional reading in speech: modern Japanese says マイル (mairu) and modern Korean says 마일 (mail), both direct phonetic loans from English "mile" rather than a Sino-Xenic calque. えいり and 영리 are included here as the formal, fully compositional readings of the transcription characters, not as the living word — paralleling how [[墨西哥]] and [[孟加拉]] handle the same kind of gap. Vietnamese anh lý is, by contrast, a genuine attested Sino-Vietnamese term (confirmed via hvdic.thivien.net, glossed there as "English mile, equal to 1.6093m"), used alongside the older native unit *dặm*.
