## Notes
- [純](../characters/純.md) "pure" + [粋](../characters/粋.md) "pure, unadulterated" — `#cranberry`: both characters' own `stand_in` point to this exact compound (純's and 粋's alike), so transitivity holds (A=B=AB) and both are legitimized as independent Dan'a'yo entries specifically by this word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 粋 is individually `kwin: false`.

純粋 means pure, unmixed, unadulterated — two near-synonymous characters for "pure" reinforcing each other.

Mandarin chúncuì, Cantonese seon4 seoi6, Japanese じゅんすい (junsui), Korean 순수 (sunsu), and Vietnamese thuần túy are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 純粋 is itself a common attested word for "pure" in all five languages. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
