## Notes

**Form.** 惰素 is [惰](characters/惰.md) "lazy" + [素](characters/素.md) "element," following the rule for noble-gas/nonreactive elements named with an X+素 descriptor. 羅馬字/諺文/注音 are the direct concatenation dwa+so/돠+소/ㄉ⺢+ㄙㄛ. The `mandarin`/`cantonese` fields above (yà/aa3) give the readings of the standard CJK element character 氬 (simplified 氩) — the character 惰素 deliberately avoids — and `korean`/`japanese`/`vietnamese` give the loanword names for the element itself (아르곤/アルゴン/agon), per the established convention for `periodictable` neologisms: these are not readings of 惰素 as a compound.

**Etymological chain.** The international name *argon* derives from Greek ἀργός (*argós*), "idle, inactive, lazy" — a direct reference to the element's chemical inertness as a noble gas, which resists forming compounds with almost anything. This gives a fully transparent path: argós (lazy) → 惰 ("lazy").

**Motivation.**
- 惰 ("lazy") is a direct semantic calque of the element's own Greek etymology, not an arbitrary substitution — exactly the element's own naming logic, translated rather than transliterated.
- It avoids the unrelated phonetic-loan character 氬/氩, which carries no semantic content of its own.
- 素 is used appropriately, marking a nonmetal/gaseous element rather than a metal (contrast the 金 suffix used for the transition-metal neologisms in this series).

**Type of formation.** A **direct etymological calque**, exactly parallel to [[巨金]] (titanium, from Titan "giant" → 巨): the referent (Greek "lazy, inert") is translated rather than transliterated, then suffixed with 素.

**Comparative CJKV forms.**
- Modern Chinese: 氬/氩 (phonetic, from yà)
- Japanese: アルゴン (phonetic, from "argon")
- Korean: 아르곤 (areugon, phonetic, from "argon")
- Vietnamese: agon (phonetic loan, following the same Vietnamized-scientific-spelling pattern as ôxy, nitơ, hydro)

All modern forms are phonetic transcriptions of the international name; 惰素 alone restores a meaning-based graph reflecting the name's own etymology.

**Conclusion.** 惰素 belongs to the same clean category as [[巨金]]: because the international name argon is itself a semantic reference to inertness/laziness, rendering that meaning directly as 惰 produces a calque that is simultaneously etymologically faithful and free of arbitrary phonetic substitution — one of the more transparent coinages in this neologism series.
