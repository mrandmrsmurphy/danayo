## Notes
- [相](../characters/相.md) "mutual, each other" + [等 (char)](../characters/等%20(char).md) "grade, rank" — literally "mutually of the same rank," mathematically equal (a = b), the direct counterpart to [[同等]] built on the same second character but naming strict numerical/quantitative identity rather than parity of status or standing (see [[同等]]'s own Notes, which explicitly rules itself out as "not a general word for equality").

Mandarin xiāngděng and Japanese そうとう are standard, attested terms for mathematical equality; Cantonese soeng1 dang2 and Korean 상등 are compositional, matching 相's and 等's own stored citations. Note a real Japanese-internal homophone: そうとう is also the reading of the unrelated [[相当]] ("considerable, worthy, corresponding") — the two are disambiguated by context and by the different second character (等 vs 当) in writing, the same shape of collision already documented on [[実数]]'s Korean field. Vietnamese tương đẳng is independently attested as a real Vietnamese mathematical/geometric term, though its precise sense there leans toward "congruent" (same shape and size) rather than bare numerical equality — close enough in register to serve, and formed on exactly the same pattern as [[同等]]'s own đồng đẳng, swapping 同→相.

Modern classroom usage across the region more often expresses simple mathematical equality with a native adjective (e.g. Japanese 等しい, hitoshii) rather than this Sino compound; 相等/そうとう/상등 remains standard in more formal or written mathematical registers.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 상둥 diverges from Sino-Korean 상등 in the second syllable, since 等 independently carries `kwin: false` on its own character page. No homophones within Dan'a'yo itself (注音 ㄙㄚㄫㄉㄨㄫ unique among both words and characters).
