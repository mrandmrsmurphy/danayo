## Notes
- [日 (char)](../characters/日%20(char).md) "day; sun" — stand-in for [[日 (char)|日]], which cannot appear independently

日 is a 象形 pictograph of the sun — originally a circle with a dot or short internal stroke marking a sunspot or the sun's brightness, since squared off into the modern rectangular form. From "sun" the sense split in two well-attested directions found across the whole sphere: the celestial body itself (日食 "solar eclipse," 日出 "sunrise," 日月 "sun and moon," idiomatically "life and times") and, independently, "a day" as the sun's cycle (今日 "today," 生日 "birthday," and the immense calendrical family of ordinal date compounds — 一日 through 三十一日 for days of the month, 日曜日 through 土曜日 for the days of the week). This vault's own corpus of over 70 attested 日 compounds is the largest single-character family documented so far, reflecting how load-bearing the sun/day distinction is for basic timekeeping vocabulary in every one of these languages.

Japanese carries the widest reading inventory of any language here: two on'yomi, にち (NICHI, the default in most compounds — 日本 nihon, itself an irregular reduction rather than the expected *nichihon) and じつ (JITSU, confined to a smaller set including 本日 honjitsu "today, formal register"), plus two native kun'yomi not listed on the character's own page (`japanese_native: ø` — a real data gap, flagged here rather than fixed, since correcting it is out of scope for a word-creation task): ひ (hi, "day/sun" used bare, as in 日が昇る "the sun rises") and か (ka, the counter-suffix used in ordinal date compounds like 二日 futsuka "the second day/two days"). Vietnamese nhật is the standard Sino-Vietnamese reading, well attested independently of any Dan'a'yo derivation (nhật báo "daily newspaper," Chủ nhật "Sunday," nhật ký "diary") — the character's own stored field carries three additional variants (nhạt, nhặt, nhựt) that don't correspond to this core sense and were not used here. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 닏 (nid, from Middle Chinese 日母 ȵ + 質三韻 iɪt) diverges from Sino-Korean 일, which has lost the nasal onset entirely — a well-known regular sound correspondence for 日母 initials, also seen on this vault's other 日母 characters.

Distinctively for this vault, 日 doubles as the periodic-table symbol prefix for helium via [[日素]] (nissen, "day-element") — an entirely Dan'a'yo-internal naming convention with no CJKV precedent, since none of the source languages uses 日 for helium; documented on the character's own page as an abbreviation rather than a natural semantic extension.
