## Notes
- [瞻](../characters/瞻.md) "look with reverence" + [余](../characters/余.md) "surplus" — used here purely for their sound, unrelated to either character's own meaning; 瞻余 is a phonetic substitution for the real disyllabic word 蟾蜍 (chánchú, "toad"), a 聯綿詞 whose two syllables cannot be meaningfully split — 蟾 alone does not mean "toad" independent of 蜍, nor does either half correspond cleanly to a single Dan'a'yo constituent gloss.

蟾蜍/瞻余 is the toad, a recurring figure in East Asian folklore — most famously the three-legged "money toad" (金蟾/招財蟾蜍) believed to attract wealth, and the toad said to inhabit the moon alongside the Jade Rabbit in the Chang'e legend (月中蟾蜍, echoed in the poetic epithet 蟾宮 for the moon itself).

Mandarin chánchú and Cantonese sim4 ceoi4 preserve the real disyllabic word directly. Japanese instead uses the native ひきがえる (hikigaeru, "toad") rather than a Sino-Japanese reading of 蟾蜍. Korean 섬여 (seomyeo) is the Sino-Korean reading, though native 두꺼비 is far more common in everyday speech. Vietnamese thiềm thừ is the classical Sino-Vietnamese term, invoked particularly in references to the moon-toad legend, with native cóc used for the animal in ordinary speech.
