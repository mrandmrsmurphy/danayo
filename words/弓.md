## Notes
- [弓 (char)](../characters/弓%20(char).md) "bow" — stand-in for [[弓 (char)|弓]], which cannot appear independently

弓 is a 象形 pictograph of a bow, one of the oldest weapon-derived characters in the corpus. Beyond the literal weapon, it extended early to any arc-like shape or bending motion — the semantic thread running through its whole phonetic family: 穹 ("dome, vault of the sky," an arched shape), 躬 ("one's own body/person," originally "to bend the body," as in a bow) both trace back to this same arched-bow image. In some Chinese dialects the character or its compounds additionally extend to "rainbow" (paralleling 虹's own bow-shaped etymology), reflecting the same "great arc in the sky" intuition independently.

Japanese keeps 弓 vividly alive as both a weapon term and a cultural institution: 弓道 (kyūdō, "the Way of the Bow") names the formal, ceremonial martial art of Japanese archery, distinct from the merely practical act of shooting an arrow (弓を射る). The native reading ゆみ (yumi) is the everyday word for the physical object, while the on'yomi きゅう surfaces mainly in compounds and technical/institutional terms like 弓道 itself. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 궁 (gung, from Middle Chinese 見母 k + 東三韻 ɨuŋ) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 궁. This same reading is shared with [[亙]] ("across, athwart" — a semantically unrelated 指事 character built on a spanning crescent moon rather than any archery imagery), a coincidental phonological convergence rather than any etymological connection between the two.
