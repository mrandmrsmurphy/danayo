## Notes
- [主](../characters/主.md) "master, main" + [婦 (char)](../characters/婦%20(char).md) "married woman" — literally "the main woman [of the household]," hence "housewife."

**Content bugs found and fixed**: `cantonese` was stored as `yu2 fu5`, missing the leading z- from 主's own real Jyutping zyu2 — corrected to `zyu2 fu5`. `korean` mixed the real reading with an appended native Korean gloss ("주부,아낙네" — 아낙네 is a native word for "married woman, housewife," not part of the Sino-Korean pronunciation) — trimmed to the clean reading 주부.

主婦 is the standard word for "housewife" across the sphere, most notably in Japanese where 専業主婦 (sengyō shufu, "full-time housewife") is a common and culturally loaded social category. Vietnamese chủ phụ (confirmed via web search) matches these exact characters, though everyday Vietnamese far more often uses the unrelated compound nội trợ ("household help/assistance") for the same concept — chủ phụ was used here since it's the reading directly derived from this word's own written form. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 주뷰 diverges from Sino-Korean 주부 in the second syllable's vowel. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄨㄅ⼜ unique among both words and characters).
