## Notes
- [真 (char)](../characters/真%20(char).md) "true" + [核 (char)](../characters/核%20(char).md) "nucleus" — literally "true-nucleus," Eukarya, the domain of organisms whose cells have a membrane-bound nucleus. A bound root rather than a free-standing full term in any of its source languages — the textbook name for the domain is longer (真核生物, "true-nucleus organism"), with bare 真核 functioning the way "eukaryotic" does in English: an adjective, or a compact stand-in for the full noun, rather than a strictly separate word.

Mandarin zhēnhé, Cantonese zan1 hat6, Japanese しんかく (shinkaku), and Korean 진핵 are all real, standard, compositional roots, matching 真's and 核's own stored citations — appearing productively in compounds like 真核生物/真核細胞 ("eukaryote"/"eukaryotic cell") in each language. Vietnamese breaks the pattern outright: the real term is nhân thực, built from different characters entirely (仁 "kernel, nucleus" + 實 "true, real") in the reverse order from Dan'a'yo's own 真核 — not a Sino-Vietnamese reading of 真核 at all.

`kwin` is false: 核 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page (Dan'a'yo's own 학 does not match real Sino-Korean 핵, which the real word 진핵 above is built on), which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 真 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄧㄋㄏㄚㄎ unique among both words and characters).
