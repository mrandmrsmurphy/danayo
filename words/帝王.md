## Notes
- [帝](../characters/帝.md) "emperor" + [王 (char)](../characters/王%20(char).md) "king" — this word is the stand-in for [帝](../characters/帝.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

帝王 is a near-synonym doubling naming a supreme monarch — practically synonymous with [[皇帝]]. In the classical Chinese title hierarchy, 帝 ("emperor") outranked 王 ("king"), a distinction Vietnamese preserves explicitly (đế above vương), though the compound itself collapses the two into a single general term for a sovereign ruler.

Japanese ていおう, Korean 제왕, and Vietnamese đế vương are all standard, non-drifted readings. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄝㄧ·⺢ㄫ unique across both words and characters).

**Root-cause fix (2026-08-27)**: 帝's own character page was missing its primary Hán Việt reading, đế, entirely — only the rarer variants (đê/đí/đó/đấy) were listed. Added it, which also resolved this word's own `vietnamese` field.
