## Notes
- [放](../characters/放.md) "to let go, to set free" + [出 (char)](../characters/出%20(char).md) "to go out" — no stand-in relationship applies (放's own stand-in is [[釈放]]; 出 is its own stand-in).

放出 means "to release, to let out": to set something free so that it passes out, whether that is water from a reservoir, heat from a body, a prisoner or a held-back sound. The noun sense is "release, emission". The two characters carry the two halves: 放 lets go, 出 sends it outward, so it differs from [[釈放]], which frees a person, and [[開放]], which opens something up, in that what is let go goes *out*.

In phonetics it is the release of a closure: a stop that is held and then let go has a 放出, and one that is held and not let go is 無放出, "unreleased", as with the voiceless coda stops ㄆ, ㄊ and ㄎ, which Dan'a'yo's syllable structure allows. The same word is the first element of Japanese 放出音, the ejective; Dan'a'yo has its own word 喉破音 for that sound and keeps 放出 for the plain general sense, so the two do not blur.

Mandarin fàngchū (to release, to let out, to put), Cantonese fong3 ceot1, Japanese ほうしゅつ (hōshutsu, "release, emission" as a noun; "to release, emit, discharge" as a verb), Korean 방출 and Vietnamese *phóng xuất* ("to let out, to release, to set free") all share the sense. The Korean reading is the same in North and South. No homophones (注音 ㄈㄚㄫㄑㄨㄊ unique across both words and characters).
