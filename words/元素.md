## Notes
- [元](../characters/元.md) "primary; first; source; fundamental" + [素](../characters/素.md) "plain substance; basic material; elemental ingredient" — stand-in for [元](../characters/元.md), which cannot appear independently

元素 is "element" — in chemistry, a pure substance that cannot be decomposed into simpler substances by chemical means; more broadly, any irreducible fundamental constituent. Both characters independently carry senses of primacy and basicness: 元 = the originary, the foundational (元旦, New Year's Day; 元首, head of state — always the first or fundamental thing); 素 = the unadorned, the basic ingredient (素材, raw material; 素食, plain vegetarian food). Their compound doubles down: element as the irreducibly primary substance.

Mandarin 元素 (yuánsù) is the scientific standard for chemical element, used across chemistry, physics, and philosophy — 元素周期表 (the periodic table of elements). Japanese 元素 (げんそ, genso) is the scientific standard: 元素周期表 (gensoshūkihyō, periodic table), 放射性元素 (radioactive elements). Korean 원소 (wonso) is the standard term. Vietnamese nguyên tố is the Sino-Vietnamese equivalent, with 素 → tố. Whether the compound is a 19th-century wasei-kango (和製漢語) coined in Japan for Western chemical concepts and then adopted across the Sinosphere, as an earlier version of this page stated flatly, is a claim I could not verify and is left as an open question.

The kwin: true for this entry is notable: the Dan'a'yo Hangul transcription 원소 happens to match the Sino-Korean 원소 exactly. This occurs because both Dan'a'yo and Korean inherit the phonological shapes of 元 (wen → won/원) and 素 (so → 소) from closely related Middle Chinese paths, producing convergent readings despite independent development. 元素 is one of the scientific vocabulary items where Japanese coinage, Chinese adoption, Korean transcription, and Dan'a'yo phonology all align cleanly.

The Periodic Table page lists 元素 as "element, the general noun this whole table classifies," and its 118 element words (such as the metal words built on 金) all sit under it. Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "element," the chemical element first and the irreducible constituent in general. It is distinct from the existing words [要素](要素.md) "element, factor," the stand-in for 素, which names a component of anything, and from [原子](原子.md) "atom." The `kwin: true` explanation above, that the readings agree, is correct on the data: the Dan'a'yo 諺文 원소 matches the Korean 원소 exactly. The readings are Mandarin yuánsù, Cantonese jyun4 sou3, Japanese genso (げんそ) and Sino-Vietnamese nguyên tố. The examples 元旦, 元首, 素材 and 素食 are the page's existing account and have not been re-checked here.
