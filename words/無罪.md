## Notes
- [無](../characters/無%20(char).md) "lack" + [罪](../characters/罪%20(char).md) "sin, crime" — Japanese uses 罪's on'yomi ZAI here (むざい), not its native kun-reading つみ used when 罪 stands alone as its own word.

無罪 means "innocent, not guilty" — the legal verdict, as distinct from [天真](天真.md) "innocent, naive" (a personality trait, not a legal status; see that word's own Notes). Mandarin wúzuì, Cantonese mou4 zeoi6, Japanese むざい (muzai), Korean 무죄 (mujoe), and Vietnamese vô tội all converge cleanly on the same reading order and sense.
