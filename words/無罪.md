## Notes
- [無](../characters/無%20(char).md) "lack" + [罪](../characters/罪%20(char).md) "sin, crime" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (both are bare self-standing words), so 無罪 is an independent compound. Note that Japanese uses 罪's on'yomi ZAI here (むざい), not its native kun-reading つみ used when 罪 stands alone as its own word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: `characters/無 (char).md` is `kwin: false`, `characters/罪 (char).md` is `kwin: true` → compound `false`.

無罪 means "innocent, not guilty" — the legal verdict, as distinct from [[天真]] "innocent, naive" (a personality trait, not a legal status; see that word's own Notes and [[Law]]'s "innocent" slot, which had wrongly cited 天真 before this word existed). Mandarin wúzuì, Cantonese mou4 zeoi6, Japanese むざい (muzai), Korean 무죄 (mujoe), and Vietnamese vô tội all converge cleanly on the same reading order and sense.
