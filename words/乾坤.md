## Notes
- [乾 (char)](../characters/乾%20(char).md) "heaven" + [坤](../characters/坤.md) "earth" — the first and last of the eight I Ching trigrams (八卦), representing pure yang (heaven, three unbroken lines) and pure yin (earth, three broken lines) respectively, together standing for the whole cosmos.

乾坤 extends from this cosmological pairing to a poetic byword for "the entire world, the whole universe" and, verb-like, for a total, world-altering reversal of fortune — Vietnamese Càn Khôn (well-attested in classical and philosophical Vietnamese writing) preserves this exact usage, appearing in expressions like xoay chuyển càn khôn ("to turn heaven and earth," i.e. to change the whole course of events). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 견콘 diverges from Sino-Korean 건곤 in both syllables. No homophones (注音 ㄍ⼶ㄋㄎㄛㄋ unique among both words and characters).
