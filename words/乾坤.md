## Notes
- [乾 (char)](../characters/乾%20(char).md) "heaven" + [坤](../characters/坤.md) "earth" — the first and last of the eight I Ching trigrams (八卦), representing pure yang (heaven, three unbroken lines) and pure yin (earth, three broken lines) respectively, together standing for the whole cosmos. Legitimizing note: 坤's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, so 坤 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word.

乾坤 extends from this cosmological pairing to a poetic byword for "the entire world, the whole universe" and, verb-like, for a total, world-altering reversal of fortune — Vietnamese càn khôn (well-attested in classical and philosophical Vietnamese writing, a common noun/expression rather than a proper name, hence lowercase) preserves this exact usage, appearing in expressions like xoay chuyển càn khôn ("to turn heaven and earth," i.e. to change the whole course of events). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 견콘 diverges from Sino-Korean 건곤 in both syllables. No homophones (注音 ㄍ⼶ㄋㄎㄛㄋ unique among both words and characters).
