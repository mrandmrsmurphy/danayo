## Notes
- [漆 (char)](../characters/漆%20(char).md) "varnish, lacquer" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

漆 names lacquer or varnish — originally written 桼 (a pictogram of sap oozing from a lacquer tree), with 水 ("water") added later to form the modern character. 漆 is also used as the anti-forgery/formal numeral form of [七](七.md) ("seven") in financial and banking contexts, the same role 壹/貳/... play for other digits — a direct, deliberate consequence of the two words sharing this exact Dan'a'yo reading.

Mandarin qī, Cantonese cat1, and Korean 칠 (chil) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese しち (shichi, on'yomi) is productive in compounds; the native kun'yomi うるし (urushi, already 漆's own stored `japanese_native` value) is the everyday word for lacquer itself (as in urushi-ware). Vietnamese tất is the standard Hán Việt reading.
