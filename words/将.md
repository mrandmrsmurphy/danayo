## Notes
- [将 (char)](../characters/将%20(char).md) "will; shall; prospective" — stand-in for [将 (char)](../characters/将%20(char).md) itself, whose `stand_in` field points here (the bare character is bound to this word form).

将 is the prospective (future) aspect-tense marker in Dan'a'yo — "will; shall; about to." It is a 修飾語 that **suffixes** to the verb, like the other tense-aspect suffixes [[已]] (perfective) and [[－中]] (continuous): the grammar book gives 行将 "will go, is about to go" (`grammar/文法 - 05形態.md`, §接尾辞) and the learner booklets give 我 食将 "I will eat" (e.g. `grammar/文法 - 92普通話小冊子.md`; the Korean booklet writes it with the 注音 ㄙㄧㄎㄐ⺢ㄫ). The ordering rule in the same section says that 将, when present, always sits closest to the verb, innermost of any aspect-suffix chain. The grammar book lists the sentence-level function as marking an action in the future, "whether intentional, predictive, or irrealis"; the formal category name is [[将然]]相 (prospective). Compare [[未]] ("not yet"), the negative counterpart of [[已]].

The Dan'a'yo-specific sense is purely grammatical. Although 将 and [[意志]] are both glossed "will" in English, 将 is only the future marker, never the psychological faculty or volition; the Mind lexipedia page keeps the two apart for this reason.

In classical Chinese 将 (OC *tsaŋ) was a prospective marker ("will; about to; soon") and also a verb "to take, hold, carry" and, in a separate reading (Mandarin jiàng), a noun "military commander." The commander sense survives in Dan'a'yo compounds such as [[将軍]], [[将帥]], [[将兵]] and [[将校]], while [[将来]] ("future") and [[将然]] retain the prospective sense. Wiktionary (將, checked 2026-10-11) lists "will; going to," "soon," "nearly," "just," and "to take; to hold" for the jiāng reading; the traditional form 將 is recorded as an alias here.

The comparison chart (from memory, not re-checked): Mandarin 将/将要 (jiāng/jiāngyào) and 要 (yào, volitional future) — placed before the verb; Cantonese 會/將會 (wui6/zoeng1 wui6); Japanese 〜ようとする (be about to do) and 〜だろう (inferential future); Korean 〜을/ㄹ 것이다 and 〜겠- (prospective/future modal); Vietnamese sẽ (will; preverbal future marker) and sắp (about to). The `japanese` (しょう), `korean` (장) and `vietnamese` (tương) fields hold the Sino-Xenic bound readings; the native equivalents above belong to Notes only. Vietnamese tương was re-checked on hvdic.thivien.net (將: thương, tương, tướng). Note the word-order contrast: the Mandarin, Cantonese, and Vietnamese future markers precede the verb, whereas Dan'a'yo postposes 将 directly after it.

Homophones: [[装]] (注音 ㄐ⺢ㄫ, listed above); the characters 壮 and 状 share the same sound but are not words.
