## Notes
- [哀](../characters/哀.md) "sad, mournful" + [戚](../characters/戚.md) "grieving, sorrowful" — 哀戚 is the stand-in word for [戚](../characters/戚.md); 戚 is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo discourse.

哀戚 is a near-synonym doubling of two grief-words, each independently meaning "sorrowful" — the compound intensifies rather than shifts the sense, describing a deep, visible mourning rather than mild sadness. It carries a formal, literary register: appropriate for funerary contexts, elegiac writing, and descriptions of bereavement, rather than everyday disappointment or a passing bad mood — closer to English "grief-stricken" or "in mourning" than to plain "sad."

戚 itself carries a second, unrelated sense in other compounds — "a relative, kin" (as in [[親戚]], "relatives") — a homograph rather than a related meaning; the "grieving" sense seen here in 哀戚 is a separate classical usage of the same character.

Japanese あいせき and Korean 애척 are both standard compositional Sino-readings, `kwin` true since Dan'a'yo 애척 matches Sino-Korean 애척 exactly. Vietnamese ai thích is likewise compositional and shares the same formal, literary register as the Chinese source. No specific HSK level is confirmed (some vocabulary lists place it beyond the standard 6-level range, reflecting its literary rarity). No homophones (注音 ㄚㄧㄑㄝㄎ unique across both words and characters).
