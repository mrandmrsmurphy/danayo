## Notes
- [就 (char)](../characters/就%20(char).md) "to approach; accomplish" (here used as this vault's borrowed stand-in glyph, 代用字, for the unlinked character 鷲 "eagle, vulture") + [鳥 (char)](../characters/鳥%20(char).md) "bird" — no stand-in relationship applies (both 就's and 鳥's own `stand_in` fields point to themselves).

就鳥 (鷲鳥) is a vulture or large bird of prey. 就 keeps its own single Dan'a'yo sound (cwai/쵀/ㄑ⺢ㄧ) here; 鷲 is its alias, and the real word's Mandarin jiù is only a cross-linguistic reading, the same as in [[禿就]] ("bald vulture, condor").

Japanese しちょう (from the classical idiom 鷲鳥不群, "an eagle does not flock") and Vietnamese thứu điểu (verified — 鷲's own real Sino-Vietnamese reading is thứu, as in Linh Thứu Sơn "Vulture Peak," the mountain where the Buddha preached the Lotus Sutra) are both literary/dictionary-register Sino-forms. **Korean deliberately uses the native 수리 rather than the mechanical Sino-form 취조** — 鷲's real Korean reading (취) would combine with 鳥's (조) to give 취조, which is already a very common, unrelated everyday word meaning "interrogation" (a police/legal term); 수리 avoids that collision entirely. Vietnamese everyday speech similarly prefers the native kền kền over the literary thứu điểu recorded here.
