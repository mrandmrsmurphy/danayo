## Notes
- [就 (char)](../characters/就%20(char).md) "to approach; accomplish" (here used as this vault's borrowed stand-in glyph, 借代字, for the unlinked character 鷲 "eagle, vulture") + [鳥 (char)](../characters/鳥%20(char).md) "bird" — no stand-in relationship applies (both 就's and 鳥's own `stand_in` fields point to themselves).

就鳥 (鷲鳥) is a vulture or large bird of prey. 就 here does **not** use its own regular Dan'a'yo reading (cwai/쵀/ㄑ⺢ㄧ) — [[就]]'s own Notes explicitly document that when borrowed for 鷲's sense, it deliberately switches to the alias reading ㄐㄨㄛ (matching 鷲's own real Chinese pronunciation, jiù) instead, the same substitution used in [[禿就]] ("bald vulture, condor").

Japanese しちょう (from the classical idiom 鷲鳥不群, "an eagle does not flock") and Vietnamese thứu điểu (verified — 鷲's own real Sino-Vietnamese reading is thứu, as in Linh Thứu Sơn "Vulture Peak," the mountain where the Buddha preached the Lotus Sutra) are both literary/dictionary-register Sino-forms. **Korean deliberately uses the native 수리 rather than the mechanical Sino-form 취조** — 鷲's real Korean reading (취) would combine with 鳥's (조) to give 취조, which is already a very common, unrelated everyday word meaning "interrogation" (a police/legal term); 수리 avoids that collision entirely. Vietnamese everyday speech similarly prefers the native kền kền over the literary thứu điểu recorded here. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄨㄛㄑㄛㄨ unique across both words and characters).
