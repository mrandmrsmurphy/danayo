>[!caution] The senses split — Japanese "pierce through", Chinese "link up; know thoroughly"
>貫通 is to pierce or bore through in Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. Chinese records it as "to link up, to thread together" and "to know a subject thoroughly", so a Chinese reader may hear "mastery" rather than "penetration". Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md); for plain passing through prefer [通過](通過.md).

## Notes
- [貫](../characters/貫.md) "to pierce, to string together" + [通 (char)](../characters/通%20(char).md) "to pass through"

貫通 means to pierce through: to penetrate or bore right through something, and by extension to run right through it, as a tunnel or a road does. English Wiktionary glosses Japanese かんつう as "penetration; boring through; to pierce, to penetrate". For Chinese 貫通 (Mandarin guàntōng, Cantonese gun3 tung1) it gives different senses: "to link up; to thread together" and "to know thoroughly about; to be well versed in". It has no Korean entry, so Korean 관통 here is built from the characters. The Dan'a'yo sense is the Japanese one, and the word is listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).

This word is the viable form of [貫](../characters/貫.md): the character means to pierce or thread through, but is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo, so 貫通 is its `stand_in` compound. Passing through is [[通過]], and the perforating of a hole is [[穿孔机]], a drilling machine.
