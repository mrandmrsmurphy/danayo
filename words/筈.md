## Notes
- [筈](../characters/筈%20(char).md) "nock" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 筈 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

筈 names the nock — the notched end of an arrow shaft where the bowstring rests when drawn. In Japanese, はず (hazu, the native reading) has extended figuratively to mean "expectation, likelihood, that which is supposed to be" (as in the common construction 〜はず, "should be/supposed to be"), a semantic drift from the physical archery term.

Mandarin kuò, Cantonese kut3, and Korean 괄 (gwal) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese uses the native kun-reading はず (hazu) rather than the on-reading カツ (KATSU) for the bare character alone. Vietnamese quát is the standard Sino-Vietnamese reading.
