## Notes
- [凶 (char)](../characters/凶%20(char).md) "unlucky; calamitous; sinister"

凶's origin is debated between 象形 and 指事 readings (an open question rather than settled consensus): [[凵]] ("a hole in the ground") with 㐅 either marking the hole's existence or depicting rock/mud/stone/bamboo inside it. Whatever its pictographic origin, 凶 means "unlucky, ominous, calamitous" — the opposite of 吉 ("auspicious, lucky") — and by extension "vicious, fierce, murderous" ([[凶悪]] "heinous," [[凶器]] "murder weapon," [[凶手]] "murderer," [[凶暴]] "ferocious, brutal").

Mandarin xiōng, Cantonese hung1, and Japanese きょう (kyō, on'yomi, fully productive: 凶悪 きょうあく "heinous") all preserve both senses. Korean 흉 (hyung) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 흉하다 (hyunghada, built on the same Sino-Korean root, "to be inauspicious, ominous"). Vietnamese hung is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in hung ác ("vicious, fierce") and hung thủ (凶手, "murderer, perpetrator").
