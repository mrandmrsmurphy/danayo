## Notes
- [権](../characters/権.md) "power, authority" + [利](../characters/利.md) "benefit, profit" — stand-in for [権](../characters/権.md), which cannot appear independently

権利 is the standard East Asian compound for "right(s)" or "privilege" in the legal and political sense — an entitlement recognized and enforceable by law or social convention. The pairing of 権 (power, authority) with 利 (benefit, gain) captures the dual nature of a right as both a claim against others and a benefit accruing to the holder.

The compound gained its modern sense in the nineteenth-century translation of Western law into Chinese characters (the Chinese rendering in the 1860s translation of an international-law text and the Japanese Meiji jurists' work are generally both credited), and 権利 became the canonical translation of German *Recht* and French *droit* in their subjective sense — a right held by an individual, as opposed to objective law (法律). It is one of the clearest examples of a translation compound that circulated across the entire Sinosphere.

In contemporary usage 権利 (quánlì / けんり / 권리) appears in constitutional law (fundamental rights), labour law (labour rights), and everyday speech wherever a person asserts an entitlement. It is contrasted with 義務 (obligations) in the paired formula 権利と義務 / 权利与义务.

The word is placed on the [Law](../lexipedia/Law.md) page, where its legal sense belongs, and also on [Directions](../lexipedia/Directions.md), where it appears only as the Rosenfelder "right" entry. The two senses of English "right," the legal entitlement and the direction, are different words in Dan'a'yo, so 権利 is only ever the legal entitlement. The Sino-Vietnamese reading is not recorded on this page. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "right, privilege."
