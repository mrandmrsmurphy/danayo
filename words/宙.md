## Notes
- [宙 (char)](../characters/宙%20(char).md) "eternity" — stand-in for [[characters/宙 (char)|宙]], which cannot appear independently

宙 is the classical Chinese word for infinite time, defined in contrast to 宇 (infinite space) as early as the pre-Han text 尸子 and canonized in 淮南子: "往古來今謂之宙，四方上下謂之宇" ("past, present, and future are called 宙; the four directions and up-and-down are called 宇"). Together they form 宇宙, "the universe" — literally spacetime itself, one of the earliest attested spacetime concepts in any language. In modern Mandarin and Cantonese 宙 survives almost exclusively bound inside 宇宙 and its derivatives (宇宙人, 宇宙船), rarely standing alone. Japanese ちゅう has drifted furthest from the classical "time" sense: dictionaries now list 宙 primarily as そら, "sky, midair" (宙返り, "somersault"; 宙ぶらりん, "dangling in midair") — the temporal meaning has receded behind a spatial one, the reverse of what the character originally specialized in versus 宇. Korean 주 and Vietnamese trụ remain bound to 우주/vũ trụ ("universe") much like Mandarin.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 줏 (juo) diverges from Sino-Korean 주 (ju) — 宙 reads with MC 澄母 (ɖ) + 尤韻 (ɨu), and Dan'a'yo preserves the compound-vowel structure of 尤韻 as a uo-diphthong (as also seen on [[州]], [[洲]], and [[由 (char)|由]], all sharing the same rime class) where Sino-Korean monophthongizes to a plain -u. This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo pronunciation with [[州]] and [[洲]] — a real three-way homophone cluster, cross-linked above.
