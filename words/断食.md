## Notes
- [断](../characters/断.md) "cut off" + [食 (char)](../characters/食%20(char).md) "eat"

断食 names fasting — literally "cutting off food" — the voluntary abstention from eating, whether for religious observance (Ramadan-style fasts, Buddhist ascetic practice), medical/health reasons, or protest (a hunger strike). It denotes a deliberate, often total abstention over a defined period, distinct from ordinary dieting or skipping a meal.

Cross-linguistically the compound is a stable Sino-Xenic loan: Sino-Korean 단식 and Japanese だんじき (using the alternate on'yomi JIKI rather than the more common SHOKU) both directly concatenate the component readings and carry the same sense, with 단식 in Korean also doubling as the term for a hunger strike (단식투쟁, "fasting struggle"). No Sino-Vietnamese compound reading is attested for the term.
