## Notes
- [他 (char)](../characters/他%20(char).md) "another, other" + [国](../characters/国.md) "nation" — "another country; other countries." 他 is the Dan'a'yo quantifier [[他]], a modifier meaning "other than the one already given"; 国 is a bound character whose stand-in is [[国家]]. Neither stand-in points here, so this is not a legitimizer word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both characters are individually `kwin: false`.

他国 means a country other than the one under discussion. Mandarin tāguó, Cantonese taa1 gwok3, Japanese たこく (takoku) and Korean 타국 are attested on Wiktionary as "another country; foreign country." Wiktionary has no Vietnamese entry for 他國 and hvdic has no compound entry, so the Vietnamese field is omitted rather than invented; Vietnamese would ordinarily say nước khác or ngoại quốc.

The word is relational rather than absolute, which is the exact sense Dan'a'yo uses it in. [[外国]] ("foreign country") looks at a country from outside one's own and treats "foreign" as a standing category, while 他国 simply names the country that is *not* the reference country, whichever that is: from Korea, 他国 may be Japan, and from Japan it may be Korea. This is why a phrase like 他国用字 ("characters used in other countries") is natural: the reference country is fixed by the context, and 他国 gathers every other one. It is also close to, but different from, [[他郷]] ("foreign land"), which carries the emotional register of being far from home. In the source languages 他国 is somewhat literary or formal; Japanese also has 他国者 ("outsider"), and Korean has the set phrase 타국살이 ("living abroad"), both of which lean toward the same "elsewhere, not here" feeling.

Reading and spelling are compositional: 트곡 and ㄊㄜㄍㄛㄎ are the direct concatenation of 他's and 国's stored values, and the Korean reading 타국 differs from 트곡 in the stored vowels of both characters (타 vs. 트, 국 vs. 곡), hence `kwin: false`. No clash with a different established word of the same spelling was found in any of the five languages.
