## Notes
- [喝 (char)](../characters/喝%20(char).md) "yell; shout"

**Content corrected**: `mandarin` was stored as `hē` — the reading for 喝's other, unrelated sense, "to drink" (a common, everyday verb). This word's own gloss ("yell, shout") belongs to the character's separate reading `hè` (喝彩, hècǎi, "to cheer, applaud"; 喝道, hèdào, "to clear the way by shouting," of old imperial retinues) — corrected accordingly.

Mandarin hè and Cantonese hot3 preserve the shout sense directly. Japanese カツ/アイ (KATSU/AI, on'yomi, formal/martial-arts register: 喝, katsu, a sharp shout used by Zen masters to jolt a student, or by martial artists) cover the sense, with no dedicated native kun'yomi. Korean 갈 (gal) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 꾸짖다 (kkujitda, "to scold"). Vietnamese hát is the Hán Việt reading for this sense (distinct from the more famous hát meaning "to sing" — a different word), attested directly in hát thái (喝采, "to cheer, applaud," matching Mandarin 喝彩); a secondary reading, ái, is also attested specifically for "to cry out loudly" (ái thái, an alternate rendering of the same compound). The character page's other stored candidates (hét, hết, hít, kệ, ạc, ặc) are Nôm readings, not Hán Việt — hét in particular ("to scream, yell," the everyday native word) closely resembles hát in both sound and meaning, which is likely why Nôm scribes chose 喝 to write it, but it remains a separate native word rather than a genuine doublet.
