>[!caution] The senses split — Japanese "endurance", Mandarin and Korean "arrogance"
>我慢 is endurance in Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. Mandarin wǒmàn and Korean 아만 mean only the Buddhist "arrogance", so a Chinese or Korean reader may hear the opposite of patience. Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md); for plain endurance prefer [忍耐](忍耐.md).

## Notes
- [我 (char)](../characters/我%20(char).md) "self; I" + [慢](../characters/慢.md) "arrogance"

我慢 is to endure: to bear pain, hardship or an urge without giving way, in Japanese; in Buddhism the pride of the self. Wiktionary glosses Japanese がまん as "patience, endurance, tolerance" and as the Buddhist "arrogance". Mandarin wǒmàn (Buddhism) and Korean 아만 give only "arrogant", the pride of the self. Cantonese ngo5 maan6 matches Mandarin.

The Dan'a'yo sense is the Japanese one, endurance. The word is listed on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md) as a Split. The same endurance is the [忍耐](忍耐.md) and its steadier kin is [寛容](寛容.md), tolerance.
