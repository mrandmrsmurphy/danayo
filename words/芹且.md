## Notes
- [芹](../characters/芹.md) "celery, water dropwort" + [且](../characters/且%20(char).md) "also" — no stand-in relationship applies (芹 is a 名専字; 且's own is the single-character word [[且]]). The name is really written 芹苴, kept as an alias, but 苴 has no page in this vault, so 且 stands in for it (see below).

芹且 (Cần Thơ, "Can Tho") is the largest city of the Mekong Delta in southern Vietnam and its economic centre, on the Hậu River, a branch of the Mekong, and is known for its river markets (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Wikipedia (checked 2026-10-11, lead only) calls it one of Vietnam's centrally governed cities and the economic centre of the delta, nicknamed "Tây Đô" (the western capital). After the 2025 administrative reorganization it was merged with Hậu Giang and Sóc Trăng provinces (see [[朔荘]] for Sóc Trăng), so its extent is now much larger than the old city. The name is the Vietnamese Cần Thơ; the Chinese characters record its sound and are not a translation. Vietnamese Wiktionary gives several unproven origins for the name: a corruption of "Cầm Thi" in "Cầm Thi Giang", a poetic name for a local river; a folk reading as "rau cần" plus "rau thơm" (water dropwort plus mint or herb), which suits the character 芹 "water dropwort"; or a Khmer origin, a fish name. English Wiktionary calls the first two folk etymologies and notes that the "Cần" in southern place names usually reflects a Khmer presyllable (all as read 2026-10-11, not independently verified).

The real spelling is 芹苴, with 苴 (艹 + phonetic 且). 苴 is a rare character (an archaic word for female hemp, and a surname) and has no page in this vault, and by user decision it is not to be created. So the Dan'a'yo word is spelled with 且, the phonetic component of 苴, and 芹苴 is kept in `aliases`. The Dan'a'yo sound ㄍㄧㄋㄑㄛ comes from the character pages of 芹 and 且, not from any reconstruction of 苴 (Mandarin jū against 且's qiě, Cantonese zeoi1 against 且's ce2, so the substitution is graphic, not phonetic).

The `vietnamese` field holds the Hán-Việt reading of the real hanzi 芹苴, cần thư (hvdic.thivien.net gives 芹 as cần and lists thư among the Hán-Việt readings of 苴, alongside bao, thỏ, tra, trư, trạ, tô and tư). It is not the everyday name: Vietnamese says Cần Thơ, where Thơ is the vernacular form, and Vietnamese Wiktionary writes the Hán form 芹苴 on its Cần Thơ entry. Mandarin Qínjū and Cantonese kan4 zeoi1 are as in English Wiktionary (for the real 芹苴). Japanese きんしゃ is the compositional on'yomi (芹 KIN, 且 SHA); Japanese actually writes the city カントー, and Korean writes 껀터 (as listed under Chinese Wiktionary's translation table), so the Korean field 근차 is likewise the compositional North Korean reading.

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 芹苴 as a Chinese proper noun (a city in Vietnam, Mandarin Qínjū, Cantonese kan4 zeoi1) and a Vietnamese Han-form entry for Cần Thơ. Chinese Wiktionary has a 芹苴 page (Mandarin Qínjū; city of the Mekong delta in Vietnam, with Japanese カントー, Korean 껀터 and Vietnamese Cần Thơ (芹苴) among its translations). Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 芹苴 page. The substituted spelling 芹且 itself is not attested anywhere (no page on English Wiktionary). The 注音 ㄍㄧㄋㄑㄛ is shared with one other word, 近処 (see the callout above); no character has it.
