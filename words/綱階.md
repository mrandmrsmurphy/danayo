## Notes
- [綱](../characters/綱.md) "headrope, guiding principle" + [階](../characters/階.md) "level, tier, storey" — literally "headrope-level," the taxonomic rank of class.

Mandarin gāng, Cantonese gong1, Japanese こう (kō), and Korean 강 are all the real, standard bare-character terms used in taxonomy, matching 綱's own stored citations exactly. Vietnamese again breaks the pattern: the real term is lớp, a native word (literally "layer," the same word used for a school "class" or grade) rather than 綱's own Sino-Vietnamese reading (cương/cáng) — stored here as the real attested term.

**Why append 階 at all, when none of the four source languages do**: Dan'a'yo appends 階 uniformly across all eight ranks of the taxonomic hierarchy ([[域階]], [[界階]], [[門階]], [[綱階]], [[目階]], [[科階]], [[属階]], [[種階]]) as a purely internal disambiguation device — natural languages rely on context alone to read 目 as "order" rather than "eye" inside a taxonomy chart, but a constructed language benefits from removing that ambiguity outright, especially since Dan'a'yo already independently uses bare [[目]] for "eye." In casual or space-constrained usage, the bare character (e.g. 目 alone) remains an understood abbreviation of the full 階-suffixed word, the same way scientific Latin abbreviates "Order" to "O." — but the full form is what a dictionary entry or first mention should use.

`kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo's own 강계 coincides with what a matching Sino-Korean compound of these exact characters would sound like, since both 綱 and 階 independently carry matching readings on their own character pages — though note the real Korean word for this rank is simply bare 강 with no 階/계 suffix at all (see above), so this is a phonological match, not evidence that 階-suffixing is itself attested in Korean. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄚㄫㄍ⼶ㄧ unique among both words and characters).
