## Notes
- [越 (char)](../characters/越%20(char).md) "Yue, exceed" + [語](../characters/語.md) "language" — "the Vietnamese language," the compact form of [[越南語]]. Neither character's own `stand_in` points here (越's is itself, 語's is [[言語]]).

越語 is the short name of the Vietnamese language: the ethnonym 越 (Việt) plus 語, with 南 dropped from 越南語. It is the compact sibling of the full word [[越南語]] and of [[韓語]], and appears in Dan'a'yo's phonology chapter in the grouping 漢語・韓語・越語 ("Chinese, Korean and Vietnamese").

The shorthand is a Sinitic usage and an uneasy one. Wiktionary records Mandarin yuèyǔ (chiefly in Taiwan and Vietnam) and Cantonese jyut6 jyu5, with the note that it is rarely used in speech because it sounds exactly like 粵語 (Cantonese), the same collision that holds in Dan'a'yo, where 越語 and [[粤語]] share the same reading (⼔ㄊ⼄). Japanese えつご (etsugo) exists but is a rarity: the ordinary word is ベトナム語 (see [[越南語]], which documents the ベトナム/베트남 transliteration pattern). In Vietnamese, 越語 corresponds to Việt ngữ, which Wiktionary labels dated or formal, used mainly by overseas Vietnamese; everyday Vietnamese simply says tiếng Việt. Because Việt ngữ is also the Vietnamese reading of 粵語 ("Yue language," see that page), the same written sound can mean either language. Korean 월어 is the compositional Sino-Korean reading; Korean usually says 베트남어, as on [[越南語]].

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 웓요 diverges from Sino-Korean 월어 in both syllables (越 reads 웓, 語 reads 요). No dot is needed in 注音: ⼔ㄊ⼄ has no second legal partition, because ㄊ may not take a y on-glide, so ㄊ⼄ is not a syllable.
