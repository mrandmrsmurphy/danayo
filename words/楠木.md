## Notes
- [楠](../characters/楠.md) "camphor tree" + [木 (char)](../characters/木%20(char).md) "tree"
- This word is the viable form of [楠](../characters/楠.md): the character's meaning (camphor/nanmu tree) is identical, but 楠 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 楠木 is its `stand_in` compound.

In Chinese 楠木 (nánmù) is nanmu, a group of large evergreen trees of the laurel family (such as *Phoebe zhennan*), whose fragrant, rot-resistant timber was prized for palace beams, temple pillars and fine furniture, and was a tribute wood of imperial construction. The name is the 形声 character 楠 plus 木 "tree/wood," which lets the compound mean both the tree and its wood.

Here the sphere's usage diverges. In Japanese the character 楠 is read kusunoki and names the camphor tree (*Cinnamomum camphora*), a different tree that Chinese writes 樟, so the English glosses "camphor wood" and "nanmu" cover two real referents depending on the language. This page's Japanese field is blank for that reason, and Dan'a'yo takes the Chinese sense, nanmu, with "camphor wood" kept only as a loose gloss. Readings: Mandarin nánmù, Cantonese naam4 muk6, Korean 남목. The word is placed under [Elements](../lexipedia/Elements.md) for its wood material.
