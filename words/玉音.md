## Notes
- [玉 (char)](../characters/玉%20(char).md) "jade; precious; honorific" + [音](../characters/音.md) "sound; voice"

玉音 is the honorific "voice of the emperor," literally "jade sound." The element 玉 is the honorific prefix for what belongs to the sovereign (compare [玉璽](玉璽.md) "royal seal"), and 音 is the sound or voice. According to Wiktionary, Chinese 玉音 (yùyīn) is literary, meaning "beautiful sound, wonderful voice," and also an honorific for a received message ("your beautiful words"), and in historical use the speech of an emperor; Japanese ぎょくおん is the honorific "voice of the emperor," and its standard example is 玉音放送, the 1945 broadcast of the Emperor's surrender speech. Dan'a'yo keeps the imperial sense, "voice of the emperor," which is the one the Japan page needs.

Wiktionary has no Korean or Vietnamese section for 玉音, so `korean` 옥음 and `vietnamese` ngọc âm are the compositional readings of the two characters, not attested usage. The page is related to the broadcast word [放送](放送.md), with which it forms 玉音放送.
