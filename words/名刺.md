## Etymology
- [名 (char)](../characters/名%20(char).md) "name" + [刺 (char)](../characters/刺%20(char).md) "stab, prick, sting"

## Notes
- "Business card, name card." The Japanese めいし preserves the original Chinese compound 名刺 directly (name + "stab/prick," from the historical practice of inscribing one's name on a bamboo/wooden slip). Vietnamese, however, did not lexicalize this exact compound — the standard Vietnamese word for "business card" is **danh thiếp** (名帖), using 帖 ("note, card") rather than 刺; a compositional "danh thích" from 名+刺 is not real Vietnamese usage (domain knowledge, this session's search quota exhausted). `korean: 명함` (名銜, "name" + "title/rank") likewise uses a different second character than 名刺 — this is reflected in the `aliases: 名銜` entry, a genuine cross-linguistic compound-formation divergence rather than an error. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 밍칙 (from 名刺) diverges from Sino-Korean 명함 (from 名銜) entirely, since Korean lexicalized a different compound for this concept. No homophones (注音 ㄇㄧㄫㄑㄧㄎ unique among both words and characters).
