## Notes
- [閑](../characters/閑.md) "free time" + [暇](../characters/暇.md) "spare time" — genuine `#cranberry`: both 閑's and 暇's own stand-ins point to this exact compound (rare full transitivity).

閑暇 means leisure or free time, the state of being unoccupied by work or duty. Mandarin 閑暇 (xiánxiá) and Cantonese 閑暇 (haan4haa6) are both everyday nouns for free time, and Sino-Vietnamese nhàn hạ is likewise a common, everyday word (though it leans slightly more adjectival — "leisurely, at ease" — than the Chinese noun usage).

Japanese 閑暇 (かんか) and Korean 한가 (hanga) both skew more formal/literary than their Chinese and Vietnamese cognates; everyday speech in both languages favors native or verb-derived alternatives instead (暇 hima alone in Japanese; 한가하다 hangahada as an adjective, "to be leisurely/idle," in Korean) — 閑暇 itself appears mainly in writing rather than casual conversation.
