## Notes
- [新](../characters/新%20(char).md) "new" + [婦](../characters/婦%20(char).md) "lady, woman" — literally "new woman," i.e. bride.

新婦 names a bride, most naturally at or around the wedding itself — Japanese 新郎新婦 (shinrō shinpu), "the bride and groom," is standard wedding vocabulary. Japanese しんぷ (shinpu) and Korean 신부 (sinbu) are both the real, everyday words for "bride" in their languages. Mandarin xīnfù and Cantonese san1 fu5 are real, attested (Wiktionary-documented) compositional readings, though modern colloquial Mandarin more often reaches for 新娘 (xīnniáng) instead — noted honestly rather than presented as the dominant everyday term there. Vietnamese tân phụ is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
