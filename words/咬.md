## Notes
- [咬 (char)](../characters/咬%20(char).md) "bite; gnaw; chew"

咬 combines [[口]] ("mouth") with phonetic [[交]]. It means "to bite, gnaw, chew" (咬人, "to bite someone"; 咬牙切齒, "to grit one's teeth in anger/hatred," literally "gnash teeth, grind molars").

Mandarin yǎo and Cantonese ngaau5 preserve the sense directly. Japanese コウ/ゴウ/ヨウ (KOU/GOU/YOU, on'yomi, rare, largely confined to loanword transliteration use — 咬 sometimes appears in katakana-adjacent phonetic borrowings) alongside native かむ (kamu, "to bite, chew," the everyday verb, more commonly written 噛む) cover the sense. Korean 교 (gyo) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 물다 (mulda, "to bite") and 씹다 (ssipda, "to chew"). Vietnamese giảo is the Hán Việt reading, attested with the core "bite, gnaw" sense (e.g. in the Vietnamese translation of Journey to the West, describing gritted teeth in anger); the character page's other stored candidate, rao, is a Nôm reading of the same glyph reused for an unrelated native Vietnamese word meaning "to announce publicly, hawk goods" (rao hàng, "to advertise goods"), with no semantic connection to biting — excluded here as a different etymological layer, not a variant Hán Việt pronunciation.
