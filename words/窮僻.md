## Notes
- [窮](../characters/窮.md) "poverty" + [僻](../characters/僻.md) "remote"
- This word is the viable form of [僻](../characters/僻.md): the character's meaning (remote, out-of-the-way) is identical, but 僻 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 窮僻 is its `stand_in` compound.

窮僻 means remote and out of the way: 窮 is "to reach the end, extreme" (and so also "poor") and 僻 "remote, secluded, off the beaten track", so the compound pictures a place at the end of the road and far from the centre, usually also backward and poor. Mandarin qióngpì, Cantonese kung4 pik1 and Korean 궁벽 (gungbyeok; 궁벽하다) are the same literary word, familiar in the set phrase 窮郷僻壌 ("a remote, impoverished backwater"). The Japanese and Vietnamese readings are not in the page's data.

Dan'a'yo-specific sense: the shared one. It is housed in Locatives for the sense of distance from the centre, beside [[周辺]] (the surroundings, periphery). 周辺 names the area around a core; 窮僻 says a place is far from it, and carries the added sense of poverty and isolation.
