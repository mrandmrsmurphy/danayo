## Notes
- [韓](../characters/韓.md) "Han (Korean)" + [服](../characters/服.md) "clothes, wear, dress" — no `#cranberry`: neither constituent's own `stand_in` points here (韓's is [[韓国]], 服's is [[服事]]).

韓服 (hánfú) is the standard Mandarin term for the Korean traditional dress, and Cantonese 韓服 (hon4fuk6) likewise. Korean 한복 (hanbok) is the country's own standard, everyday name for it. Japanese, while かんふく (kanfuku) is the compositionally regular on'yomi, in practice usually borrows the Korean pronunciation directly as ハンボク (hanboku) rather than using the Sino-Japanese reading, alongside the older, female-garment-specific loanword チマチョゴリ (chima jeogori). Sino-Vietnamese Hàn phục is the formal term (paralleling áo dài, sườn xám, and kimono as national-dress terms in their own languages), though the direct Korean loanword Hanbok is what is most commonly used in everyday Vietnamese.
