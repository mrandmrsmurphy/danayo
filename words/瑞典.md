## Notes
- Purely phonetic: [[瑞]] "auspicious" + [[典]] "dictionary, classic" — neither character's own literal meaning applies here; both are chosen for their sound value alone, per the standard Chinese phonetic-transliteration practice for foreign place names.

瑞典 is the standard Chinese name for Sweden. 瑞 does double duty across two unrelated European country transliterations in this vault — it also supplies the first syllable of [[瑞士]] ("Switzerland") — a real, frequently-noted source of confusion for learners of Chinese, since the two countries' names share no etymological connection in Swedish/German/English and the overlap is purely a coincidence of which syllable Chinese happened to phonetically match to "Swe-"/"Schwei-".

Mandarin Ruìdiǎn and Cantonese seoi6 din2 are both the real, standard names, matching 瑞's and 典's own stored citations. Japanese スウェーデン (Suwēdon) and Korean 서전 (actually the older Sino-Korean transliteration; modern Korean instead almost always uses 스웨덴, seuweden) both diverge from the kanji/hanja-reading approach — Japanese transliterates directly from the English/Swedish name rather than reading 瑞典 with on'yomi, while Korean's Sino-Korean 서전 has become rare and literary, largely displaced by the newer transliteration. Vietnamese Thuỵ Điển is the real, standard Sino-Vietnamese name, still in active everyday use (unlike Korean's shift away from its Sino-Korean equivalent), matching 瑞's thụy and one of 典's three stored readings, điển. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄙ⼔ㄉㄝㄋ unique among both words and characters).
