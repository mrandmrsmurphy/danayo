## Notes
- [破](../characters/破%20(char).md) "break, rend" + [壊](../characters/壊.md) "demolish, ruin"
- This word is the stand-in for [壊](../characters/壊.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

A compound denoting forceful destruction or demolition. In Japanese, はかい (hakai) is the standard term covering destruction by natural disaster and deliberate sabotage alike; compounds include 環境破壊 "environmental destruction" and 破壊工作 "sabotage." In Chinese, 破坏 (pòhuài) skews toward deliberate damage or undermining — total annihilation uses 摧毀. The Korean 파괴 (破壞) and Vietnamese phá hoại inherit the same Sino-Vietnamese roots, with phá hoại carrying a particular connotation of wartime sabotage or subversion.

破 is to break, split or smash, and 壊 (traditional 壞) is to ruin or collapse, so 破壊 is to break something so that it can no longer work or stand: the everyday general-purpose word for "destroy, demolish," as the Existence page puts it. It sits at the base of a graded family on that page: [毀](毀.md) is the more literary and formal "to destroy"; 滅 is total eradication, stronger than physical demolition; and 潰 is collapse from within or under pressure, where 破壊 is damage applied from outside. It is also distinct from [破裂](破裂.md) "to burst, rupture," which is breaking apart from internal pressure.

The Vietnamese form phá hoại is the usual word for "to sabotage, to wreck," and the same pair of characters in Vietnamese and Korean often carries the figurative sense of ruining something intangible (a relationship, the environment, an atmosphere). Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "destruction, demolition," as the general word.
