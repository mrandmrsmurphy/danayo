## Notes
- [闖](../characters/闖.md) "intrude" + [入 (char)](../characters/入%20(char).md) "enter"
- 闖入 is the stand-in word for [闖](../characters/闖.md): the character's meaning (to intrude; to barge in) is identical, but 闖 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 闖入 is its `stand_in` compound.

闖入 means to break in, intrude, or barge in uninvited — the image behind 闖 itself (a horse rushing through a gate) gives the compound a forceful, sudden connotation distinct from neutral "entering." Mandarin 闖入/闯入 (chuǎngrù), Cantonese 闖入 (cong2jap6), and Japanese 闖入 (ちんにゅう) are all real, if somewhat literary/formal, words for this concept — Japanese in particular favors it in news and legal registers over the more casual 押し入る (oshiiru). Korean 틈입 (teumip) is comparatively rare and dated in everyday use, with 침입 (侵入, "invasion/trespass") now the more common near-synonym.
