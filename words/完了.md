## Notes
- [完](../characters/完.md) "finish, whole" + [了 (char)](../characters/了%20(char).md) "complete, finish" — two near-synonymous "finish" roots stacked together for emphasis: thoroughly done. **Left `vietnamese` blank rather than guessing**: the mechanical Hán-Việt reading "hoàn liễu" turned up no attestation as a real fixed compound in Vietnamese dictionaries — 完/了's individual Sino-Vietnamese readings don't appear to combine into a standing word for this grammatical sense. **Left `hsk_level` unset**: this is a grammar/linguistics term rather than an entry that appears in its own right on standard HSK vocabulary lists (only its constituent characters do).

Beyond its everyday sense ("it's finished"), 完了 is the standard grammatical term, across Japanese and Korean alike, for the perfective aspect — the verbal category marking an action as completed rather than ongoing. The grammar book uses exactly this technical sense for Dan'a'yo's own suffix –已, glossed as "完了相 (perfective)" within its broader [[時相]] system, and explicitly notes that this suffix may co-occur with the durative –中 (in the fixed order 中已, "ongoing, then completed") and with the prospective [[将然]] suffix –将, which always sits closest to the verb. Attested across Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, and Korean.
