## Notes
- [駝](../characters/駝.md) "hunchback" + [背 (char)](../characters/背%20(char).md) "back" — stand-in for [[駝]], which cannot appear independently.

駝背 means "hunchbacked, humpbacked." 駝 itself, best known in 駱駝 ("camel," lit. "camel"), carries the extended sense "hunchbacked, curved-backed" from the camel's hump.

Mandarin tuóbèi and Cantonese to4 bui3 preserve the sense directly. Japanese せむし (semushi) is the everyday native word for "hunchback," matching [[駝]]'s own native reading directly. Korean 타배 (tabae) is the direct compositional Sino-xenic reading. Vietnamese đà bối is directly attested (Hán Nôm dictionary sources gloss it "lưng gù, gù lưng," "a hunched, bent back") but functions as a learned/literary compound rather than the everyday word; ordinary spoken Vietnamese uses the native gù/lưng gù instead. 背's other stored candidates: bội is a legitimate secondary Hán Việt reading restricted to 背's separate "betray, break faith" sense (背叛, bội bạn); bồi and bổi are unrelated — bồi belongs to different characters entirely (賠, 陪, 培) and also has a separate colonial-era loan origin from English "boy," while bổi is a native/dialectal word for "kindling, chaff."
