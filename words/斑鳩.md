## Notes
- [斑](../characters/斑.md) "spot, speck(led)" + [鳩](../characters/鳩.md) "pigeon, dove" — literally "speckled dove," describing the turtledove's mottled neck/wing plumage.

斑鳩 names the turtledove in Chinese, but the same two-character combination is read differently and points to a different bird in Japanese: いかる/いかるが (ikaru/ikaruga), the Japanese grosbeak (*Eophona personata*, family Fringillidae, unrelated to doves). This mismatch has a documented history — one theory holds that 斑鳩 was originally applied in Japanese to a genuinely dove-like bird (the collared dove, whose neck pattern resembles a string of prayer beads) and only later came to be associated with the unrelated grosbeak by a historical misreading, a drift preserved today in the place name 斑鳩 (Ikaruga), the Nara town famous for Hōryū-ji temple.

Mandarin bānjiū, Cantonese baan1 kau1, and Korean 반구 (bangu, compositional) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese ban cưu is likewise compositional, from 斑's ban and 鳩's own cưu. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 반큐 diverges from Sino-Korean 반구 in the second syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄅㄚㄋㄎ⼜ unique among both words and characters).
