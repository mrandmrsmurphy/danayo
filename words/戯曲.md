## Notes
- [戯](../characters/戯.md) "play; drama" + [曲](../characters/曲.md) "tune; song" — literally "drama-tune," reflecting the sung/musical roots of Chinese opera traditions. This word is the stand-in for [戯](../characters/戯.md), which cannot appear independently: its meaning (play, dramatic performance) is identical, but 戯 alone is too bound to stand as a Dan'a'yo word.

戯曲/戲曲 names Chinese opera broadly (a sung dramatic tradition combining music, dance, and stylized performance, e.g. Peking opera) and, by extension, a play or dramatic script more generally — distinct from [劇](劇.md) (drama/theatre as an art form) and [劇詩](劇詩.md) (dramatic poetry specifically).

Mandarin xìqǔ, Cantonese hei3 kuk1, Korean 희곡 (huigok), and Japanese ぎきょく (gikyoku) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday term for "opera/play" in its language. Vietnamese hí khúc is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 戯's hí and 曲's own khúc.
