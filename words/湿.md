## Notes
- [湿](../characters/湿%20(char).md) "wet, damp" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 湿 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

湿 means wet or damp: moistened by water or holding moisture (潮湿, "damp"; 湿度, "humidity"; 湿潤, "moist"; 除湿, "dehumidify"), and in traditional Chinese medicine the pathogenic "dampness" (濕氣). The traditional form 濕 is listed as an alias. The graph is a 形声 compound of 氵 "water" with the component 显.

Mandarin shī, Cantonese sap1, Japanese しつ (湿度/しつど, "humidity"; 湿気/しっけ, "dampness"), Korean 습 (습도, "humidity"; 습기, "moisture") and Vietnamese thấp (thấp is also the ordinary word for "low") are the standard Sino-xenic readings. Japanese uses the native adjective 湿っぽい (しめっぽい) and the verb 湿る (しめる) for the everyday word, so on'yomi しつ is bound to compounds, and Korean has native 축축하다.
