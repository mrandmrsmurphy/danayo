## Notes
- [雍](../characters/雍.md) "harmonious" + [雍](../characters/雍.md) "harmonious" — genuine `#cranberry`: 雍's own stand-in points to this exact reduplicated compound (both halves being the same character, transitivity trivially holds).

雍雍 is a reduplicated Classical Chinese expressive term meaning harmonious, calm, and gentle — famously describing the harmonious call of wild geese in the Classic of Poetry (詩經): 雍雍鳴雁, "the geese cry out in harmony" (邶風·匏有苦葉). Mandarin 雍雍/雝雝 (yōngyōng), Cantonese 雍雍 (jung1jung1), Japanese 雍雍 (ようよう, "calm and composed"), Korean 옹옹 (ongong), and Sino-Vietnamese ung ung all preserve this literary sense of gentle harmony, surviving mainly in classical/literary registers rather than everyday speech in any of the five languages.

**Found and fixed two real bugs**: `諺文` had drifted to 오옹, inconsistent with both halves of 雍's own correctly-stored 옹 (注音 ㄛㄫㄛㄫ was already correct) — corrected to 옹옹. `japanese` held ヨンヨン, an unrelated katakana string — corrected to ようよう (confirmed via web search).
