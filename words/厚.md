## Notes
- [厚 (char)](../characters/厚%20(char).md) "thick"

厚 is 会意 of 厂 ("a cliff, a cave on a cliff") and 𣆪 ("a jug," per Shuowen an inverted 亯) — the semantic logic bridging the two components to "thick, substantial" isn't fully spelled out in the source. It means "thick" both physically and figuratively: [[濃厚]] ("concentrated, dense, strong [flavor]"), [[敦厚]] ("honest, sincere, warm-hearted"), and [[厚顔]] ("brazen-faced, shameless," literally "thick-faced" — the same "thick skin = shameless" metaphor found across many languages, culminating in the chengyu [[厚顔無恥]]).

Mandarin hòu, Cantonese hau5, and Japanese こう (kō, on'yomi, largely confined to formal/literary compounds) alongside native あつ(い) (atsu(i), "thick," the everyday adjective) all preserve the sense. Korean 후 (hu) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 두텁다 (duteopda, "to be thick, deep [of relationships]"). Vietnamese hậu is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in hậu đãi ("to treat generously") and đôn hậu ("honest, warm-hearted," matching 敦厚 directly).

This word is part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read hou/홋/ㄏㄛㄨ): [[侯]] ("marquis," already perfected — its own callout already cross-links here from earlier in this sweep) and [[吼]] ("roar," still awaiting its own turn).
