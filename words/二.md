## Notes
- [二 (char)](../characters/二%20(char).md) "two"

二 is 指事: two parallel horizontal strokes, a straightforward duplication of [[一]] ("one," one stroke), continued by [[三]] ("three," three strokes). Like the other low numerals, 二 has a dedicated anti-forgery/financial variant sharing its exact reading, [[貳]], used on financial documents for the same reason as [[一]]/[[壱]], [[七]]/[[漆]], and [[九]]/[[玖]] — many more strokes, much harder to alter.

Mandarin èr, Cantonese ji6, and Japanese に (ni, on'yomi, fully productive in compounds like 二月 "February," 二人 "two people") alongside native ふた(つ) (futa(tsu), preferred for casual object-counting) round out the reading. Korean similarly pairs Sino-Korean 이 (i) with the native numeral 두 (du), used mainly in a handful of fixed native-numeral contexts (두 사람, "two people"). Vietnamese distinguishes three related forms: nhị is the direct Hán Việt reading used in compounds (nhị thập, "twenty"; đệ nhị, "the second"), nhì is a native ordinal specifically for "second" (thứ nhì, "second place"), and hai is the everyday native cardinal "two" — this field uses nhị, matching the character page's own value per this vault's single-character-inherits-directly convention.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (niǝ/늬/ㄋㄧㄜ) with another word still awaiting its own turn in this sweep, [[貳]] ("disloyal") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed when it comes up.
