## Notes
- [冬 (char)](../characters/冬%20(char).md) "winter"

冬's oracle-bone form depicts a cord with both ends knotted off — the original pictograph for "end," later differentiated as [[終]]. Two dots were added at the bottom (the "ice" radical 仌) to specify "the end of the year, winter." It heads the vault's seasonal-month vocabulary: [[冬至]] ("winter solstice"), [[孟冬]]/[[仲冬]]/[[季冬]] ("early/mid/late winter," the 10th/11th/12th lunar months).

Mandarin dōng, Cantonese dung1, and Japanese とう (tō, on'yomi, largely confined to compounds like 冬至 とうじ "winter solstice") alongside native ふゆ (fuyu, the everyday word for "winter") all preserve the sense. Korean 동 (dong) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 겨울 (gyeoul, the everyday native word). Vietnamese đông is the Hán Việt reading, familiar throughout the calendar vocabulary (mùa đông, "winter season").

This word is part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read tong/통/ㄊㄛㄫ): [[桶]] ("pail") and [[通]] ("pass through"), both still awaiting their own turn in this sweep — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed on each when it comes up.
