## Notes
- [矮 (char)](../characters/矮%20(char).md) "short, dwarf" + [行 (char)](../characters/行%20(char).md) "go, travel" + [星 (char)](../characters/星%20(char).md) "star" — a planet-like body that has not cleared its orbital neighbourhood.

矮行星 is the standard astronomical term for a dwarf planet — a body large enough to be rounded by its own gravity but too small to gravitationally clear its orbital neighborhood of other debris, distinguishing it from a full planet under the International Astronomical Union's 2006 definition. Pluto is the best-known example, reclassified from planet to dwarf planet under this criterion, alongside Ceres, Eris, Haumea, and Makemake.

Mandarin ǎixíngxīng, Cantonese ai2 hang4 sing1, Japanese わいこうせい (waikōsei), Korean 왜행성 (waehaengseong), and Vietnamese ải hành tinh are all fully compositional and are the standard scientific terms across the sphere.
