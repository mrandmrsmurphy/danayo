## Notes
- [損](../characters/損.md) "diminish, harm" + [失](../characters/失.md) "lose"

損失 is a near-synonym compound: 損, whose graphemic component 員 (originally a phonetic, now reanalyzed) sits beside the hand radical 扌, denotes damage or reduction — to make something less than it was — while 失, a 象形 character (the 失 character page reads it as the footprint of someone who has fallen, ringed by drops of blood; the Shuowen alternative is 手 + 乙, something slipping from a hand), denotes the act of losing outright. Together they cover the full range of "loss," from gradual erosion of value to sudden, total deprivation.

The word functions as both verb ("to suffer a loss, to lose") and noun ("a loss, damages"), and is the standard term for financial and material loss in all of Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, and Korean — covering everything from a company's quarterly losses to casualties in war (損失人員). It contrasts with the related compound 喪失 (sōshitsu/상실), which leans more toward the loss of abstract qualities — confidence, rights, memory — than of concrete property.

Both constituent characters are independently productive in Dan'a'yo: 損 also appears in compounds like 破損 (damage, breakage), and 失 in 失業 (unemployment) and 失礼 (rudeness), already cross-linked from [[失]]'s own Words section.

The Conflict page sets out five words for "to lose" and says none is interchangeable: [喪失](喪失.md) is the loss of abstract qualities, 損失 the standard concrete financial or material loss, [滅失](滅失.md) the specifically legal total loss or destruction of property, [自失](自失.md) a momentary loss of composure, and [遺](遺.md) the plainest general "to lose, leave behind." Dan'a'yo keeps 損失 for the concrete, material sense, with the readings Mandarin sǔnshī, Cantonese syun2 sat1, Japanese sonshitsu (そんしつ) and Korean 손실.
