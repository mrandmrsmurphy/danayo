## Notes
- [噸 (char)](characters/噸%20(char).md) "ton"

噸 is a purely phonetic coinage: [[口]] ("mouth," marking a loanword/sound character) + phonetic [[頓]], with the reading deliberately altered to take advantage of the vacant Dan'a'yo syllable "ton," matching Western orthography for the unit of mass.

Mandarin dūn and Cantonese deon1 preserve the transliteration directly. Japanese トン (ton) is likewise a direct phonetic loan and the standard everyday word for the unit. Korean 톤 (ton) parallels this, also the standard everyday term. Vietnamese đốn is attested directly in Hán Nôm dictionary sources, glossed explicitly as a phonetic transliteration of English "ton" (a 1000 kg weight unit); it is a literary/character reading rather than the word Vietnamese speakers actually use, however — everyday Vietnamese says tấn (a separate loanword from French tonne) for the unit, and đốn in living usage instead means "to fell, chop down (a tree)" or, as an adjective, "shoddy, vile" — unrelated senses that happen to share the same syllable.
