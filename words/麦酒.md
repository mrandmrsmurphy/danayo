## Notes
- [麦](../characters/麦.md) "wheat, barley, oats" + [酒](../characters/酒.md) "alcohol, liquor" — no `#cranberry`: 麦's own `stand_in` is [[大麦]], 酒's is [[酒精]]; neither points here.

麦酒 ("barley liquor") was the original Japanese-coined word for beer, later displaced by the phonetic loanword ビール (bīru) in everyday use — the word survives mainly in formal/legal contexts (e.g. 麦酒税, "beer tax") and on some traditional labels. `mandarin`/`cantonese` (píjiǔ/be1zau2) here give the real modern standard word 啤酒 (a phonetic transliteration of "beer," listed as this word's alias) rather than the compositional màijiǔ/mak6zau2 — the same real-name-over-compositional-reading convention seen elsewhere this sweep. Korean 맥주 happens to be both the compositional Sino-Korean reading and the genuine standard word.
