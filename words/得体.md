## Notes
- [得](../characters/得.md) "to obtain, achieve" + [体](../characters/体.md) "body, form, essence" — literally "having achieved the [proper] form." No stand-in relationship applies (得's own `stand_in` is [[獲得]]; 体's own is [[体系]]).

得体 means fitting to the thing's own nature — appropriate, proper, decent — successfully matching one's words, actions, or appearance to what a situation, one's station, or a thing's own essence actually calls for. Mandarin détǐ and Korean 득체 both keep this straightforward "appropriate, fitting" sense.

**Japanese diverges sharply**: えたい survives almost exclusively in the set phrase 得体の知れない (etai no shirenai), "of unknown/mysterious nature" — describing something whose true form or identity *can't* be determined, nearly the mirror image of the Chinese "correctly matching one's true form" sense. Both readings are honestly kept rather than picking one to stand for the whole CJKV sphere.

Cantonese dak1 tai2 and Vietnamese đắc thể are the compositional readings from each character's own attested value. `kwin` is false: both 得 and 体 are individually `kwin: false`. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄜㄎㄊㄝㄧ unique across both words and characters).
