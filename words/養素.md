## Notes
- [養](../characters/養.md) "nourish" (phonetically near-homophonous with real Mandarin 氧, "oxygen") + [素](../characters/素.md) "element" — inspired by the word "nutrient" [[栄養素]].
- The theory behind Japanese/Korean 酸素/산소 ("acid-element," reflecting the old belief that oxygen was the essential component of all acids) has been debunked, so Dan'a'yo does not follow it — 養素 instead reflects oxygen's role in respiration/nourishment.

`mandarin` yǎng and `cantonese` joeng5 are the real, standard bare words for oxygen (氧), matching 養's own stored citations exactly — a deliberate phonetic near-coincidence exploited for this coinage, the same strategy used for 弗素 (弗/氟) and 天金. `japanese` さんそ and `korean` 산소 are the real, standard external words for oxygen in each language — genuinely built on 酸 ("acid"), not 養 — stored here despite the structural mismatch with Dan'a'yo's own coinage, per the periodic-table convention of recording each language's actual attested word. Vietnamese dưỡng khí ("nourishing gas") is a real, older Sino-Vietnamese calque, genuinely built on 養's own "dưỡng" reading — direct historical support for Dan'a'yo's own naming rationale.

`kwin` is false: there is no real Sino-Korean word "양소" — the real Korean word 산소 is built on an entirely different first character (酸, not 養), so Dan'a'yo's own 양소 has no genuine Sino-Korean counterpart to match against. No homophones (注音 ⼘ㄫㄙㄛ unique among both words and characters).
