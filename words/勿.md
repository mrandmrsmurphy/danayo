## Notes
- [勿 (char)](../characters/勿%20(char).md) "do not ever"

勿's own glyph originally depicted blood on a knife — the original character for [[刎]] ("to cut [the throat]," OC \*mɯnʔ) — later borrowed by 假借 (phonetic loan) for the unrelated negative imperative "do not," a repurposing already in place by the oracle-bone period. 勿 functions as a more emphatic prohibition than [[別]] — a formal, classical "never do X" rather than the more colloquial modern negator.

Mandarin wù, Cantonese mat6, and Japanese ぶつ/ぼつ/もち (butsu/botsu/mochi, on'yomi, rare in modern use) alongside native なかれ (nakare, the classical Japanese negative-imperative particle, "let there not be...") all preserve the prohibitive sense. Korean 물 (mul) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 말다 (malda, the native negative-imperative auxiliary, "don't"). Vietnamese vật is the Hán Việt reading, attested in the classical phrase vật vong (勿忘, "do not forget"); the character page's other two stored candidates (vặt, vất) are unrelated native words and were not used here.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (mud/묻/ㄇㄨㄊ) with [[物]] ("thing," already perfected — its own callout has been added alongside this iteration).
