## Notes
- [下](../characters/下%20(char).md) "down, below" + [龍](../characters/龍%20(char).md) "dragon" — no stand-in relationship applies (下's own is the single-character word [[下]]; 龍's own is the single-character word [[龍]]). The simplified 下龙 and the alternate 下竜 are kept as aliases, because 竜 and 龙 are aliases of the parent form 龍 on its character page.

下龍 (Hạ Long, "Ha Long") is a city on the coast of Quảng Ninh province in northeastern Vietnam (廣寧 in Chinese characters), and the gateway to Ha Long Bay, a UNESCO World Heritage Site of thousands of limestone islets (from memory, not re-checked). The name means "descending dragon", from the legend that a dragon family descended to the sea to help the Vietnamese repel invaders, its spitting of jewels and jade forming the islets (from memory, not re-checked). The bay itself is 下龍灣 (traditional) or 下龍湾, "Ha Long Bay"; that compound has no page of its own yet and is only mentioned here.

The `japanese` field holds the compositional on'yomi かりゅう; Japanese actually writes ハロン (ハロン湾 for the bay), and Korean 하롱 (하롱베이), both from memory, not re-checked. Korean 하룡 is the compositional North Korean reading (龍 gives 룡 on its character page). Mandarin Xiàlóng and Cantonese haa6 lung4 are as in Wiktionary; Vietnamese Hạ Long is the real city name and a perfect compositional match (hạ 下, long 龍).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 下龍 as a Chinese proper noun (a city in Vietnam, Mandarin Xiàlóng, Cantonese haa6 lung4) and a Vietnamese Han-form entry for Hạ Long; Chinese Wiktionary has a 下龍 entry that places it in Quảng Ninh (廣寧). Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no entry. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄚㄌ⼄ㄫ unique across both words and characters).
