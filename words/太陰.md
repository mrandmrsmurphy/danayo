## Notes
- [太](../characters/太%20(char).md) "great" + [陰](../characters/陰%20(char).md) "yin, shade" — no stand-in relationship applies (太's own stand-in is itself; 陰's own is itself).

太陰 means the moon, and by extension the yin principle it represents in classical cosmology — literally "great yin," the counterpart to [[太陽]] ("great yang," the sun).

Mandarin tàiyīn, Cantonese taai3 jam1, Korean 태음, and Japanese たいいん are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese Mặt Trăng is the standard native term for the Moon (rather than the more literary/classical thái âm). Classical Chinese has several other poetic names for the moon, including 月球, 月亮, and 玄兔 ("Dark Hare," from the lunar-hare motif) — none of these are alternate spellings of 太陰 itself, so they are recorded here as related synonyms rather than `aliases`; the simplified form 太阴 is the only genuine orthographic alias. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄚㄧ·ㄧㄇ unique across both words and characters).
