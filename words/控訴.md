## Notes
- [控](../characters/控.md) "accuse, charge" + [訴](../characters/訴.md) "sue" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character [控](../characters/控.md) as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

控訴 is a legal appeal: to bring a case to a higher court after a first judgment. The senses differ across the sphere. Wiktionary gives Mandarin kòngsù as "to make a formal complaint against someone; to accuse; to file charges", and gives Japanese こうそ as "the first appeal to a higher court". Dan'a'yo takes the Japanese sense, so 控訴 is the appeal, while accusing someone is [告訴](告訴.md) and bringing a charge is 起訴.

Mandarin kòngsù, Cantonese hung3 sou3, Japanese こうそ, Korean 공소, and Vietnamese khống tố (a genuine standard legal term) are all standard, fully compositional readings.
