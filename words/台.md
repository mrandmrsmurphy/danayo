## Notes
- [台 (char)](../characters/台%20(char).md) "platform; machines (classifier)" — this word is also the `stand_in` of its own character page (台 is its own stand-in; 臺, 颱, 檯, and 坮 are listed there as aliases, variant spellings never used independently, and none has a word file of its own)

台 is the Dan'a'yo classifier (量詞) for machines, following the rule NP + Numeral + Classifier from the [grammar](../grammar/文法%20-%2004句法.md). In the grammar's twelve-classifier table 台 is glossed "machines" and exemplified by computers, cars, blenders, and nanobots; the [品詞 chapter](../grammar/文法%20-%2097品詞.md) lists it the same way. A noun phrase with a classifier is always definite. It sits beside [匹](匹.md) (animals), [条](条.md) (long thin things), [冊](冊.md) (books), and [杯](杯.md) (cupfuls), and the general classifier [個](個.md) can stand in for it informally. The grammar gives no full sentence with 台, so any example is an illustrative construction, not an attested sentence: 電腦三台 "the three computers" (illustrative).

**Which 台 this is.** The character 台 is a merger of several originally distinct graphs. As the character page notes, it absorbs 臺 "platform; stage", 颱 "typhoon", and (from memory, not re-checked) 檯 "desk; counter"; the archaic first-person 台 (yí) and the place name use (Taizhou, tāi) are older graphs sharing the same form. The classifier for machines is the 臺/檯 line of descent: the sense "a raised base or stand" extended to "a thing set on a stand", then to machines and performances. The word 台 here carries only the classifier sense, plus the vault's gloss "platform" from the character; the grammar itself attests only "machines". The other words using the character are separate words: [台風](台風.md) "typhoon" (the 颱 strand), [台湾](台湾.md) "Taiwan" and [台北](台北.md) "Taipei" (the 臺 strand, as a place name), [台球](台球.md) "billiards" (台 as "stand; table"), [台悦](台悦.md) "happy" (台 used only as the phonetic of 怡), and [断頭台](断頭台.md) "guillotine" (the 臺 "platform" strand).

**A Dan'a'yo-specific scope.** The gloss "machines" is a deliberate simplification of several source usages. Mandarin 台 (tái) is the measure word for machines and appliances (televisions, computers) and also for performances and stage shows; the stage use does not carry over to Dan'a'yo. Japanese 台 (だい/dai) counts vehicles, machines, and some large furniture (from memory, not re-checked). Korean's everyday counter for machines and vehicles is the native 대, which happens to be the same syllable as the Sino-Korean reading; unlike in Mandarin, it is not used for performances. Cantonese uses 部 for many of the same machines, from memory, not re-checked, so Cantonese speakers will find 台 less automatic. Vietnamese uses chiếc or cái for vehicles and machines (from memory, not re-checked); its đài means a tower or broadcasting station, a different branch of the same character. Dan'a'yo takes the common core, machines, and uses it uniformly for computers, cars, blenders, and nanobots alike. The vault's [Measurement](../lexipedia/Measurement.md) page describes it as the classifier for platforms and machines, which is consistent.

The cross-linguistic fields hold the Sino-Xenic readings of the character: Mandarin tái, Cantonese toi4, Japanese ダイ (だい), Korean 대 (North Korean reading), Vietnamese đài. They are readings of 台, not the native counters discussed above.
