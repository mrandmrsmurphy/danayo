## Notes
- [獅](../characters/獅.md) "lion" + [子](../characters/子.md) "thing" — 子 here is the standard grammaticalized noun-suffix (compare [椰子](椰子.md), [帽子](帽子.md)), not literally "child"; stand-in for [獅](../characters/獅.md), which cannot appear independently.

獅子 is the everyday word for "lion," but also carries a secondary "bodhisattva"-adjacent sense in this vault, since the lion is a recurring Buddhist iconographic symbol — most directly in 獅子吼 ("lion's roar," a set metaphor for the Buddha's authoritative teaching, already documented on [吼](吼.md)'s own page) and in the lion as the mount of Mañjuśrī. This word also shares its alias 僧伽羅 with [獅子国](獅子国.md) ("lion country," the historical name for Sri Lanka) — a real etymological chain, since "Sinhala" itself derives from Sanskrit siṃhala, "lion," via the same root as 獅子 itself.

Mandarin shīzi, Cantonese si1 zi2, Japanese しし (shishi, also 獅's own stored kun'yomi/native reading, since 獅 was borrowed whole rather than given a separate on'yomi-based compound form), Korean 사자 (saja), and Vietnamese sư tử are all standard, fully compositional readings and also the real, everyday words for "lion" in each language.
