## Notes
- [紅 (char)](../characters/紅%20(char).md) "crimson, red" + [鶴 (char)](../characters/鶴%20(char).md) "crane" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (紅's is [[紅]] itself, 鶴's is [[鶴]] itself), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: true` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: true`.

紅鶴 names the flamingo — literally "crimson crane," describing its distinctive pink-red plumage.

Mandarin hónghè, Cantonese hung4 hok6-2 (with the tone-2 change common to this kind of compound noun), Korean 홍학 (honghak), and Vietnamese hồng hạc are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 紅鶴 is itself a common attested word for "flamingo" in all four languages. Japanese べにづる (beni-zuru) is a compositional native-reading compound (紅's native べに "crimson" + 鶴's native つる "crane," with rendaku), distinct from the more common loanword フラミンゴ (furamingo). No Dan'a'yo homophones.
