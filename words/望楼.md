## Notes
- [望](../characters/望.md) "hope; gaze" + [楼](../characters/楼.md) "watchtower"
- This word is the viable form of [楼](../characters/楼.md): the character's meaning (watchtower) is identical, but 楼 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 望楼 is its `stand_in` compound.

望 is to look into the distance, and 楼 is a multi-storey building or tower, so 望楼 is a tower built for looking out: a watchtower, as on a city wall, a frontier post, a harbor or a fire-watch station. The form is functional and military or civil-defense in character, and the word turns up in descriptions of walls and fortifications and in modern fire-lookout and coastal-lookout contexts.

The lexipedia lists it under "tower" on [Buildings](../lexipedia/Buildings.md), but 望楼 is narrower than English "tower": it names a tower defined by its job of watching, not any tall structure (a bell tower, a high-rise, or a decorative tower are different things). The shared readings are Mandarin wànglóu, Cantonese mong6 lau4 and Korean 망루. Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "watchtower, lookout tower," and a general word for a tower is still to be supplied.
