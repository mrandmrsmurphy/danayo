## Notes
- [電](../characters/電.md) "electricity" + [気 (char)](../characters/気%20(char).md) "feeling" — 電気 is 電's own legitimizing compound (電's `stand_in` points here), but since 気's own `stand_in` remains 気 itself, transitivity fails and no `#cranberry` tag applies.

電気 is the ordinary, everyday word for "electricity" in Japanese (でんき, denki), covering both the physical phenomenon and household/utility current (電気をつける, "turn on the light/power"). Mandarin 電氣/电气 (diànqì) and Cantonese 電氣 (din6hei3) are real, compositionally identical words, but they are not the primary modern term for "electricity" in either language — that role belongs to 電 alone or to compounds like 電力 (diànlì, "electric power"); 電氣/电气 instead survives mainly in older or more formal/technical contexts and in compounds like 電氣工程 ("electrical engineering," now more often 電機工程). Korean 전기 (電氣, jeongi) is, by contrast, the standard everyday word for electricity, directly paralleling Japanese usage.

Sino-Vietnamese điện khí is likewise attested and compositionally exact (điện "electric" + khí "vapor, energy, matter"), but its register has drifted: historically it denoted electric current in general, while in contemporary technical Vietnamese — especially energy-policy contexts — điện khí has narrowed toward "gas-fired electricity" specifically (as in điện khí LNG, "LNG-fired power"), with điện năng ("electrical energy") now the standard plain word for electricity in general. The Dan'a'yo compound follows the older, broader sense shared with Mandarin/Cantonese/Korean rather than the newer Vietnamese narrowing.
