## Notes
- [歩](../characters/歩.md) "step, walk" + [兵](../characters/兵.md) "soldier" — "walking soldier." Neither character's own `stand_in` points here (歩's is [[散歩]]; 兵's is [[兵士]]), so 歩兵 is an independent compound.

歩兵 is infantry: soldiers who fight on foot, as opposed to [[騎兵]] on horseback. It is attested with the same characters in Chinese (Mandarin bùbīng, Cantonese bou6 bing1), Japanese (ほへい), Korean (보병) and Vietnamese (bộ binh). In Hong Kong Cantonese 步兵 has also become slang for a food courier who delivers on foot. Dan'a'yo uses it for both the individual soldier and the arm of service; the plain soldier is [[兵士]] or [[兵卒]].
