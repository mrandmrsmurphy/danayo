## Notes
- [篆](../characters/篆.md) "seal script" + [書](../characters/書.md) "book, writing" — this word is the viable form of [篆](../characters/篆.md): the character's meaning (seal script) is identical, but 篆 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 篆書 is its `stand_in` compound.

篆書 is the oldest of the five major East Asian calligraphic script styles still in regular use, characterized by rounded, uniform-width strokes and pictographic-leaning forms closer to the original bronze and oracle-bone inscriptions than any later script. Historically standardized under the Qin dynasty (小篆, "small seal script," as opposed to the earlier, more varied 大篆, "large seal script"), it survives today mainly in ceremonial contexts and — true to its name — the carving of name seals/chops (印章), where its dense, symmetrical forms suit small, legible-at-a-glance engraving.

Japanese てんしょ and Korean 전서 are both standard compositional Sino-readings. No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄐ⼔ㄋㄙ⼄ unique across both words and characters).
