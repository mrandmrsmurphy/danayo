## Notes
- [祖](../characters/祖.md) "ancestor; founder" + [先](../characters/先.md) "before; prior; predecessor" — stand-in for 祖, which cannot appear independently

祖先 pairs 祖 (ancestor) with 先 (before, prior) to form the ordinary word for ancestors and forebears: those of an earlier generation from whom a person or group descends. 祖's character page classifies it graphemically under 且, and the common account reads 且 as an ancestral tablet and 示 as an altar, which grounds the idea of ancestry in ritual observance (that etymology is from memory, not re-checked here). 先 contributes the temporal sense of "coming before." The vault's `stand_in` for 祖 is this word, so 祖 reaches discourse as 祖先, while 祖 itself still appears inside kinship compounds such as [祖父](祖父.md), [祖母](祖母.md) and [始祖](始祖.md).

Wiktionary lists 祖先 for Chinese (Mandarin zǔxiān, Cantonese zou2 sin1), Japanese (そせん, sosen) and Korean (조선), and gives the Vietnamese form as tổ tiên, all with the sense "ancestor." It gives Chinese synonyms 先祖, 祖宗, 祖上 and 先人, and in Japanese the antonym 子孫 (shison, "descendant"). The vault's descendant-side counterparts are [子孫](子孫.md) and [後裔](後裔.md). Ancestor veneration (祭祖, ancestral memorial rites, and Korean 제사) is widespread across the CJKV sphere, but no divergence in the meaning of the word itself is established here. Other register or usage claims about the individual languages are from memory and not re-checked.

Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense "ancestor, forebear" in general, with no narrowing or shifting. On the Kinship page the contrast is with [始祖](始祖.md), the specific founding ancestor, which is narrower than this general word.

A Korean homonym exists: 조선 is the Sino-Korean reading of 祖先 and also of 朝鮮, the name of the Joseon dynasty (1392-1897, followed by the Korean Empire). The two words share no characters. The Dan'a'yo form of 祖先 is ㄐㄜㄙㄝㄋ, and no other word or character in the vault shares it, so no homophone callout applies. The 注音 takes no syllable-boundary dot, since the only other split, ㄐㄜㄙ + ㄝㄋ, fails because ㄐㄜㄙ is not a legal syllable (only ㄐㄜ, ㄐㄜㄆ, ㄐㄜㄊ, ㄐㄜㄎ and ㄐㄜㄫ exist).
