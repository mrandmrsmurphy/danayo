## Notes
- [呈 (char)](../characters/呈%20(char).md) "display; show"

呈 means "to display, present, submit" — often upward, to a superior or authority (呈交, "to submit"; 呈报, "to report to a superior"; 呈現, "to appear, present itself").

Mandarin chéng and Cantonese cing4 preserve the sense directly. Japanese テイ (TEI, on'yomi, formal/literary register: 呈する, "to present, offer") covers the sense without a dedicated native kun'yomi. Korean 정 (jeong) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 드리다 (deurida, "to give, offer respectfully"). Vietnamese trình is the genuine Hán Việt reading, well attested in trình bày ("to present, explain"), trình diện ("to report/present oneself"), and thuyết trình ("to give a presentation"). The character page's other stored candidates (chiềng, chường, rềnh, triềng, trành, xình) are legitimately attested but as colloquial/reduplicative-word variant readings of 呈 (chiềng làng "to announce to the village," chán chường "weary," tròng trành "unsteady," etc.) rather than separate literary Hán Việt vocabulary, so they were excluded from the field; chiệng does not appear in either dictionary source consulted and looks like a tone-mark corruption of chiềng, so it was dropped as noise rather than kept.

呈 shares its reading (ding/딩/ㄉㄧㄫ) with [[鼎]] ("tripod cauldron") — a coincidental phonological convergence, now cross-linked reciprocally.
