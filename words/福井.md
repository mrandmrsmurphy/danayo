## Notes
- [福](../characters/福%20(char).md) "blessing" + [井](../characters/井.md) "well"

福井 is Fukui, written "fortune well". The name was made when the castle town was renamed in the early Edo period from the older Kitanoshō, supposedly after a well in the grounds (from memory, not re-checked). The reading ふくい is the on-reading of 福 (ふく) and the native 井 (い).

Fukui faces the Sea of Japan, with its capital Fukui. It is known for dinosaur fossils, the Zen temple Eihei-ji, the cliffs of Tōjinbō and Echizen crab, and it hosts a concentration of nuclear power stations.

In Dan'a'yo 福井 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 후쿠이 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
