## Notes
- [簡](../characters/簡.md) "simple, concise" + [体](../characters/体.md) "form, body" + [字 (char)](../characters/字%20(char).md) "character" — literally "simple-form characters."

簡体字/简体字/簡體字 names Simplified Chinese characters, the standard character set of mainland China (and Singapore), contrasting directly with [[繁体字]] ("Traditional Chinese characters," used in Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau). This vault's own frontmatter `aliases` list both real-world spelling variants of this term — 简体字 (fully simplified) and 簡體字 (traditional 體 with simplified 简) — reflecting how the term itself gets written differently depending on which character set is doing the writing.

Mandarin jiǎntǐzì, Cantonese gaan2 tai2 zi6, Korean 간체자 (gancheja), and Japanese かんたいじ (kantaiji) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real term used to describe this character set in its language. Vietnamese giản thể tự is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 簡's giản (one of eleven candidates stored on its own page, most of which look like corpus noise) and 体's own thể. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 간테지 diverges from Sino-Korean 간체자 in the second and third syllables. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄍㄚㄋㄊㄝㄧㄐㄧ unique among both words and characters).
