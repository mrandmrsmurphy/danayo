## Notes
- [寿](../characters/寿.md) "long life" + [着](../characters/着%20(char).md) "wear" — the real-world spelling 躊躇 adds 足 ("foot") to both graphemes, but this is a 聯綿詞 (disyllabic binome): the whole word is indivisible, neither syllable carries independent meaning, and 足 functions only as a category marker (pacing/hesitation) shared across the pair, not as live semantic content. Since 躊 and 躇 never occur outside this one word, Dan'a'yo does not mint separate characters for them — it spells the word directly with the bare phonetic hosts 壽/著 (aliased onto [[寿]] and [[着 (char)|着]] respectively), and records 躊躇/踌躇 as the real-world alias.
English Wiktionary (at 躊躇) explains the word as a reduplication of an original form *du da, cognate with 躑躅, 踟躕 and 彳亍, all meaning "walking slowly or with difficulty", and so "hesitant". It gives the Chinese verb two senses, "to walk back and forth" and "to hesitate; to pause" (the example has a teacher hesitating a moment before giving his opinion), and a literary adjective "smug; complacent; self-satisfied". Its synonyms are 猶豫 for hesitating and 走來走去 for pacing; its derived terms include 躊躇不決 and 躊躇滿志. Japanese ちゅうちょ is a noun ("hesitation; indecision; wavering") and a suru-verb ("to hesitate; to waver"), with 躊躇い, 遅疑, 逡巡 and 猶予 as synonyms. Korean 주저 and Vietnamese trù trừ are listed as descendants.

Mandarin chóuchú and Cantonese cau4 cyu4 are as at English Wiktionary. The Mind page lists the word with the Dan'a'yo sense "to hesitate, waver, be undecided", the second Chinese sense.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄙ⼜ㄐ⺢ㄎ (checked 2026-10-12).
