## Notes
- [章 (char)](../characters/章%20(char).md) "chapter" + [魚 (char)](../characters/魚%20(char).md) "fish" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (both 章's and 魚's own are themselves), so `#cranberry` does not apply.

**Fixed a real typo**: `mandarin` had hāngyú instead of the correct zhāngyú. Also cleaned up `korean`, which had comma-joined 장어 with 문어 — 장어 is the mechanical Dan'a'yo-style compositional reading (章's own 장 + 魚's own 어), but it happens to coincidentally collide with the real, unrelated Korean word for "eel." The actual standard Korean word for octopus is 문어, written with a different first character (文, "culture," not 章) — already correctly preserved here as the alias 文魚.

章魚 names the octopus — 章 here is used somewhat loosely/by loan rather than for its literal "chapter" sense, likely referencing the animal's patterned or segmented appearance.

Cantonese zoeng1 jyu4 preserves the compositional reading directly and is the everyday Cantonese word for octopus. Japanese たこ (tako, native) is the standard word, unrelated to either character's on-reading. Vietnamese chương ngư is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form; everyday Vietnamese uses the native bạch tuộc.
