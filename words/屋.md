## Notes
- [屋 (char)](../characters/屋%20(char).md) "roof" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character [屋 (char)](../characters/屋%20(char).md) as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

屋 names a roof, and by extension a house or building — a place one arrives at to rest, per the character's own 会意 etymology (尸 "body, recline" + 至 "arrive"). It is extremely productive as a compound suffix in Japanese especially, where -や (its native kun'yomi) names shops and tradespeople by their wares (本屋 "bookshop," パン屋 "bakery").

Japanese おく, Korean 옥, and Vietnamese ốc are all standard readings — Vietnamese ốc in particular survives mainly in set phrases and idioms (愛屋及烏 "ái ốc cập ô," loving the house extends to the crow on its roof) rather than everyday standalone use, which prefers native nhà. No homophones among other words (注音 ㄛㄎ; the unrelated characters [[渥]] and [[沃]] share the same reading, but neither's own stand-in word collides with 屋's).
