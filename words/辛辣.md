## Notes
- [辛](../characters/辛.md) "spicy" + [辣 (char)](../characters/辣%20(char).md) "spicy, hot" — 辛辣 is the stand-in for [辛](../characters/辛.md), which is too bounded to stand alone.

辛辣 is a near-synonym doubling: both characters independently mean "spicy, pungent," and the compound intensifies rather than shifts the sense. Beyond taste, it's just as commonly used figuratively for sharp, biting speech or writing — 辛辣的批評 ("scathing criticism"), 辛辣的諷刺 ("biting satire") — the same taste-to-tone metaphor English makes with "biting" or "cutting."

Japanese しんらつ is the direct on'yomi compositional reading (辛 SHIN + 辣 RATSU), matching both characters' own citations, and carries the same figurative "caustic, biting" extension in Japanese usage. Korean 신랄 is likewise standard compositional Sino-Korean. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 신랃 diverges from Sino-Korean 신랄 in 辣's syllable (랃 vs 랄). **`vietnamese` intentionally left blank**: both constituent characters carry several competing historical Sino-Vietnamese layers (辛: tân/tăn/tơn; 辣: lát/lướt/lượt/lạt/nhạt/nhợt), and no single compositional reading for this exact compound could be confirmed as attested — rather than guess among the candidates, this is left for a future dedicated check. No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄋㄌㄚㄊ unique across both words and characters).
