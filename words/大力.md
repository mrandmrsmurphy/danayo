## Notes
- [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) "big" + [力 (char)](../characters/力%20(char).md) "power; strength" — no stand-in relationship applies (大's own is the single-character word [[大]]; 力's own is the single-character word [[力]]).

This spelling is an intentional homograph. It serves two unrelated senses, an ordinary Chinese word and the name of a Vietnamese city, so the ordinary sense comes first.

1. Great strength, mighty force; used as an adverb, vigorously, with great effort (大力發展 "to strive to develop"). The characters are literally "big strength". English Wiktionary lists three Chinese senses: an adverb ("strongly; energetically; vigorously; striving to"), a noun ("great efforts") and, in Cantonese, an adjective ("physically strong", daai6 lik6, with the antonym 細力 sai3 lik6). The vault types the word as a noun, like [[力]] and [[威力]]; the adverbial use is a matter of syntax, not a second part of speech. Mandarin dàlì and Cantonese daai6 lik6 are as given at English Wiktionary. Japanese Wiktionary has 大力 (だいりき) as a noun, "a very strong force; also its possessor", so the `japanese` field holds だいりき; Chinese Wiktionary writes the same Japanese word たいりき, which is a different, older kana spelling of the same word and not a second word. Korean 대력 and Vietnamese đại lực (hvdic.thivien.net confirms 大力 as đại lực) are the compositional Sino-Xenic readings, and are not attested for this word as ordinary vocabulary in Korean or Vietnamese. For the effort and strength senses see [[努力]] and [[実力]].
2. Đà Lạt, the highland city of the Central Highlands (Tây Nguyên) and capital of Lâm Đồng province, Vietnam. In Chinese it is written 大叻 (English Wiktionary: Mandarin Dàlè, Cantonese daai6 lik1 or daai6 lik6; "provincial capital of Lâm Đồng"). The Vietnamese name is Đà Lạt; English Wiktionary derives it from Koho "daa" ("water") and "Laac" (the indigenous people of Da Lat), and I take the Lat people as the source of the name on that basis. Chinese Wiktionary also has a page for 大叻. It was founded in the 1890s as a French hill station after exploration by Alexandre Yersin (from memory, not re-checked).

The substitution. The rare character 叻 is not given a page in the vault, so the city is spelled 大力, with 力 (the phonetic of 叻) standing in for 叻. The Cantonese reading lik of 叻 matches 力 lik6, so the spelling works as a sound-for-sound rendering of the Cantonese reading. Mainland Mandarin reads the real spelling 大叻 as Dàlè, which is different from dàlì, and the Cantonese city reading lik1 differs in tone from lik6. The `mandarin` and `cantonese` fields hold the readings of the ordinary word (sense 1). 大叻 is kept as an alias, and the homograph with the ordinary word is accepted by decision. In Vietnamese the real spelling would be read đại lặc: hvdic.thivien.net gives 叻 as lặc, a character used for Singapore's old name 石叻 and so a southern-Chinese rendering, not a native Vietnamese writing of Đà Lạt; Vietnamese itself does not write the name with Chinese characters, and the Hán-Việt reading is a reconstruction.

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11, and honestly. Sense 1: English Wiktionary has 大力 (Chinese adverb, noun, adjective, with Mandarin, Cantonese, Hakka and Min readings); Japanese Wiktionary has 大力 (だいりき) as a Japanese noun and a Chinese section (dàlì, adverb "forcefully", noun "great force"); Chinese Wiktionary has a 大力 page with a Japanese section (たいりき); Korean Wiktionary has 大力 only as a Chinese word (강력하게, 힘껏, 대대적으로), not as Korean; Vietnamese Wiktionary has no 大力 entry. Sense 2: English Wiktionary has 大叻 as a Chinese proper noun, and Vietnamese Đà Lạt as a Vietnamese proper noun, in both of which it glosses the city "provincial capital of Lâm Đồng"; Chinese Wiktionary has a 大叻 page (its section text could not be read through the extract tool); Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 大叻 page. The 注音 ㄉㄚㄧㄌㄧㄎ is shared with no other word or character.
