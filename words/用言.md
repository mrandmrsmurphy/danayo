## Notes
- [用](characters/用.md) "use" + [言](characters/言%20(char).md) "word" — a term from Japanese school-grammar (国文法) tradition: a "declinable word," i.e. a verb or adjective that conjugates, as opposed to [[体言]] (taigen, "indeclinable word," covering nouns and the like).

Japanese ようげん (yōgen) is the real, standard term in Japanese grammatical tradition. Mandarin yòngyán, Cantonese jung6 jin4, and Korean 용언 (yong'eon) are all direct compositional Sino-xenic readings used when discussing Japanese grammar in those languages, rather than native grammatical terminology in their own right — Chinese and Cantonese linguistic description doesn't organize word classes this way natively. Korean is a partial exception: 용언 is actually native Korean grammatical terminology too (Korean traditional grammar also splits 용언/체언, paralleling the Japanese distinction), so it functions as a real, independently-used term there, not just a loan-gloss for discussing Japanese.

Vietnamese dụng ngôn combines dụng (用, the standard Hán Việt reading, sử dụng "to use") with ngôn (言, the standard Hán Việt reading, ngôn ngữ "language"); it is a transparent compositional gloss following the pattern of the other three languages, but does not appear to be an established, independently attested Vietnamese linguistic term — Vietnamese-language material on Japanese grammar more often keeps the Japanese term directly (yōgen) or describes the concept periphrastically. 用's other stored candidate, dùng, is a genuine doublet (an earlier, vernacular-layer borrowing of the same root, now the everyday native verb "to use," alongside pairs like 房 phòng/buồng); the remaining candidates on both characters are unrelated words sharing only a rhyme-family resemblance, not doublets.
