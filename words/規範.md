## Notes
- [規](../characters/規.md) "rules; compass and square" + [範](../characters/範.md) "pattern, model" — "the compass-and-square and the mould": a norm. Neither character stands alone in Dan'a'yo (規 is carried by [[規律]], 範 by [[模範]]); 規範 is a third compound built from the same two stand-in families.

規 is the carpenter's compass and square (see [[規定]]), and 範 originally names the mould or form into which a casting is poured. Both are instruments that make a thing conform, so 規範 is "that to which conduct or form must conform": a **norm**, in the sense of a prescription about how things *ought* to be done, held up as a pattern. It is the standard word in Chinese (规范), Japanese (きはん) and Korean (규범) for social, ethical, legal, linguistic and technical norms alike: 道徳規範 "moral norms", 行動規範 "code of conduct", 言語規範 "linguistic norm", and in Chinese also the verb "to standardise, to regulate" (规范化) and the adjective "standard, proper" (不规范 "non-standard, improper"). Vietnamese *quy phạm* is equally at home in law and ethics (*quy phạm pháp luật*, "legal norm"). The same word is used in all four languages in this normative-linguistic sense, which makes it a reliable shared term for "orthographic norm".

How 規範 differs from its neighbours in the vault: [[規定]] is a *provision*, a specific clause fixed in a written text, with an act of stipulating behind it ("as stipulated"); a 規範 is the broader standard that such provisions express, and need not be written anywhere. [[標準]] is a *benchmark* or measure against which things are rated, a yardstick that is not necessarily binding. [[規律]] is a *regularity* or the discipline that results (also "law" in the scientific sense, as in 自然規律), a pattern that is observed as much as imposed. [[規則]] is a rule of a game or procedure. 規範 sits above the specific rule and carries the force of "should": it is what a 規定 or 規則 spells out and what a 標準 measures.

In Dan'a'yo this is the word of the chapter 文字規範 ("orthographic norms") in [[文法 - 03文字法]], where four maxims of the writing system are set out under four-character mottoes, such as 【[[一字一音]]】 (every character has one and only one pronunciation) and 【[[毎字明意]]】. Those are norms in exactly the sense above: general principles governing the shape of the script, not clauses of a statute. This is the same sense as the shared CJKV one; no Dan'a'yo-specific narrowing is intended.

Mandarin guīfàn, Cantonese kwai1 faan6, Japanese きはん, Korean 규범 and Vietnamese quy phạm are all standard readings (Wiktionary; Vietnamese confirmed on hvdic.thivien.net). Cantonese is given as kwai1 faan6 to match [[規定]] and [[模範]]'s conventions. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄨㄧㄈㄚㄇ unique across words, chengyu and characters).
