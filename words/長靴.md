## Notes
- [長 (char)](../characters/長%20(char).md) "long" + [靴](../characters/靴.md) "boot" — this word is the viable form of [靴](../characters/靴.md): the character's meaning (boot) is identical, but 靴 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 長靴 is its `stand_in` compound.

長靴 names a tall boot reaching partway up the calf or higher — rain boots, riding boots, waders — as distinct from a low shoe or ankle boot, the "long" of the name describing shaft height rather than the boot's length front-to-back.

Japanese reading ながぐつ uses the native kun-yomi (なが + ぐつ), not the on-reading concatenation ちょうか. Korean 장화 is a standard compositional Sino-Korean reading, and `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 장화 matches Sino-Korean 장화 exactly. Vietnamese trường ngoa is compositionally derived from each character's own confirmed reading, though everyday Vietnamese more commonly says ủng or giày cao cổ ("high-calf shoe"). No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄚㄫㄏ⺢ unique across both words and characters).
