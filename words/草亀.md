## Notes
- [草 (char)](../characters/草%20(char).md) "grass" + [亀 (char)](../characters/亀%20(char).md) "turtle"

The name 草亀 ("grass turtle") reflects the species' preferred habitat — the grassy margins of rivers, ponds, and rice paddies across temperate East Asia. The animal in question is *Mauremys reevesii*, the Chinese pond turtle (also called Reeves' turtle after the British naturalist John Reeves), one of the most familiar freshwater chelonians in the region. It is native to China, Korea and Taiwan, and is also found in Japan (where it is widespread but may have been introduced), and is one of the commonest freshwater turtles of the region.

In Japanese the compound takes the special reading くさがめ: 草 contributes its native reading くさ rather than its on-yomi そう, and the following かめ ("turtle") undergoes rendaku — sequential voicing — to がめ. The species is also known colloquially as 臭亀 (read identically, くさがめ, "stinky turtle") for the musky odour it releases when threatened.

The species is currently listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List, principally due to overcollection for the food and traditional-medicine trades across the region.

In Dan'a'yo 草亀 names this one freshwater species, as distinct from the sea turtle [[海亀]] and the land tortoise [[陸亀]]; the plain word for a turtle is [[亀]]. It keeps the shared CJKV sense.
