## Notes
- [援](../characters/援.md) "assist" + [手 (char)](../characters/手%20(char).md) "hand" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character [援](../characters/援.md) as an independent Dan'a'yo entry, which is too bounded to stand alone.

援手 is literally "the hand that helps": an offered hand, and by extension aid or rescue extended to someone in difficulty. It is the concrete, personal counterpart to the more abstract [[援助]] ("aid, assistance"), and is typically found in the phrase "to extend a helping hand" (伸出援手).

Mandarin yuánshǒu, Cantonese wun4 sau2, Japanese えんしゅ, Korean 원수, and Vietnamese viện thủ are all compositional readings. Mandarin and Japanese use the word in a literary or formal register; it is most at home in appeals for help and news reports of disaster relief. Korean 원수 is an unrelated homograph of 怨讐 ("enemy") and 元帥 ("marshal"), distinguished only by the Hanja.
