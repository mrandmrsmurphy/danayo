## Notes
- [別 (char)](../characters/別%20(char).md) "other, distinct" + [字 (char)](../characters/字%20(char).md) "character, glyph"

別字 names a "different/wrong character" — a typo, a misspelling, or a character mistakenly substituted for the intended one (often because the two are homophones or look similar). 別, a 会意 character built from 𠮠 and 刂 ("knife"), means "other, distinct, separate" — already prolific in [[別荘]] ("villa"), [[区別]] ("to differentiate"), [[離別]] ("to part ways"), and [[個別]] ("individual, separate"), all cross-linked from [[別 (char)]]'s Words section.

字 is the basic word for a written character or glyph, anchoring an enormous family of script-related vocabulary in [[字 (char)]]'s Words section — [[文字]] ("script"), [[字典]] ("dictionary"), [[正字]] ("correct character," the conceptual opposite of 別字), and [[識字]] ("literacy"). 別字 sits naturally alongside [[正字]]: where 正字 is the standard, correct form of a character, 別字 is "a different character [from the one that should be there]" — the everyday term a teacher might use marking a student's homework, or a proofreader catching a substitution error.

In Chinese the word is the standard name for a wrongly written character (the full set phrase is 錯別字, "wrong and mistaken characters"), and the gloss on the Grammar page, "typo, misspelt or variant character," keeps both the error and the variant sense. It is not the same as a deliberate substitution of a common character for a rare one, which this vault calls a 代用字: a 別字 is an error, while a 代用字 is a convention. The readings are Mandarin biézì, Cantonese bit6 zi6, Japanese betsuji (べつじ), Korean 별자 and Sino-Vietnamese biệt tự. Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "a wrong or variant character," placed under [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md) with the writing-system words.
