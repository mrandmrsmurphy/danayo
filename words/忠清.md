## Notes
- [忠](../characters/忠.md) "loyalty" + [清](../characters/清.md) "pure" — the name of the Chungcheong region of Korea.

忠清 (Chungcheong) is the historical Joseon province in the central west of the Korean peninsula, south of the Seoul region. The name is conventionally explained as combining the first characters of its two chief cities, 忠州 (Chungju) and 清州 (Cheongju), a pattern shared with 全羅, 慶尚 and 江原. The province was divided in 1896 into 忠清北道 (North Chungcheong) and 忠清南道 (South Chungcheong), which are the two provinces of South Korea today; the bare name now survives as a regional term, the Chungcheong region. The variant 忠淸 uses the older form 淸 of 清, which is recorded on the character page as an alias.

Mandarin zhōngqīng, Cantonese zung1 cing1, Japanese ちゅうせい, Korean 충청 and Vietnamese trung thanh are the compositional Sino-Xenic readings, and are from the character pages. Chinese Wiktionary has 忠清 and 忠淸 entries (Zhōng qīng, taken from CC-CEDICT); English, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionary have no entry. Japanese ordinarily writes the name in katakana after the Korean reading (チュンチョン, from memory, not re-checked), with the on'yomi ちゅうせい being a bookish alternative.
