## Notes
- [羸](../characters/羸.md) "weakness" + [弱 (char)](../characters/弱%20(char).md) "weak"
- This word is the viable form of [羸](../characters/羸.md): the character's meaning (frail, thin) is identical, but 羸 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 羸弱 is its `stand_in` compound.

羸 is thin, emaciated and worn down, and 弱 is weak, so 羸弱 is bodily frailty: the weakness of the underfed, the sickly, the aged. It is the literary word for a weak constitution, as in the historical and medical descriptions of a person of 羸弱 body, and is only secondarily extended to a weak state or organization. This sets it apart from [薄弱](薄弱.md) "weak, feeble," listed beside it on the Efforts page, which is used chiefly of abstract things (will, basis, evidence), and from the general [衰弱](衰弱.md) "to weaken, decline," which names the process of becoming weak.

The readings are Mandarin léiruò, Cantonese leoi4 joek6, Japanese ruijaku (るいじゃく) and Korean 리약 (the North Korean form, keeping initial 리; the South Korean form would be 이약). Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense "frail, weak, feeble" with no divergence. It is placed under [Efforts](../lexipedia/Efforts.md).
