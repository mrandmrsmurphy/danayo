## Notes
- [林 (char)](../characters/林%20(char).md) "grove" — here a phonetic substitute for 霖 ("prolonged rain," an alias of 林, sharing its exact Mandarin/Cantonese/Dan'a'yo reading, and already documented among 林's own stored `aliases`) + [雨 (char)](../characters/雨%20(char).md) "rain" — literally "grove-rain," standing in for 霖雨.

林雨/霖雨 names a long, sustained spell of rain — the kind that soaks in over days rather than a passing shower (contrast [[俄雨]] "rain shower" or [[驟雨]] "sudden downpour"). Classically it also carries a metaphorical extension, "favor bestowed by the monarch" (rain as the sovereign's life-giving grace upon the land), the same figurative move found in the set phrase 久旱逢甘霖 ("a timely rain after long drought," i.e. welcome relief).

Mandarin línyǔ, Cantonese lam4 jyu5, and Korean 림우 (rimu) are all standard, fully compositional readings, with 림 following this vault's North Korean/문화어 convention (dropping the South Korean 두음법칙 shift to 임). Japanese りんう (rin'u) is likewise compositional. Vietnamese lâm vũ is directly attested as a real compound (from 霖's own Hán Việt reading lâm, identical to 林's), used in the same "prolonged rain" sense. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 림우 matches Sino-Korean 림우 exactly. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄌㄧㄇ·ㄨ unique among both words and characters).
