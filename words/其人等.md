## Notes
- [其 (char)](../characters/其%20(char).md) "that, those" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" + [等 (char)](../characters/等%20(char).md) "etc., rank"

其人等 is "they, them" in the formal/classical register, extending the X-等 collective-plural pattern seen in [[人等]] ("persons") and [[我等]] ("we"): the demonstrative 其 ("that, those") stands in for the third person, literally "those persons." The shorter 其等 (without 人) is the more common classical form of this pronoun; 其人等 spells the "person" element out explicitly, parallel to 人等 and 我等.

Mandarin 其等 (qíděng) survives only in classical or legal register, alongside 彼等 (bǐděng, "they," with a stronger connotation of an external or opposing party); everyday Mandarin uses 他們. Spoken Cantonese "they" is 佢哋 (keoi5dei6), built from the unrelated character 佢 and not a reading of 其, so it is not a cognate of this word. (From memory, not re-checked against a dictionary.)

Japanese "they" is 彼等/彼ら (karera, from 彼), Korean is the native 그들 (geudeul) and Vietnamese is the native họ; none of them uses this three-character formation as a standalone word. The frontmatter readings (きじんとう, 기인등, kỳ nhân đẳng) are the compositional Sino-Japanese, Sino-Korean and Sino-Vietnamese readings of the three characters, given as a formal cross-reference and not as everyday usage.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄍㄜ·ㄋㄧㄋㄉㄨㄫ (checked 2026-10-13).
