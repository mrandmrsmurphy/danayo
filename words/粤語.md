## Notes
- [粤](../characters/粤.md) "Yue" + [語](../characters/語.md) "language" — 粤's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 語's own is [[言語]]; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 粤 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 語 is individually `kwin: false`. **Fixed a real bug found in passing**: `characters/粤.md`'s own `羅馬字` had stored man'wet — an extraneous prefix and wrong final consonant — corrected to 'wed, matching `syllables/⼔ㄊ.md` (this word's own 羅馬字/諺文/注音 were already correctly derived from the right value).

粤語 names the Yue language, most commonly known in English as Cantonese. In most everyday contexts it is synonymous with 広東語/廣東語/廣東話 ("Guangdong speech"), though that term is technically narrower, referring specifically to the Guangzhou-Hong Kong prestige variety rather than the Yue language family as a whole.

Mandarin yuèyǔ, Cantonese jyut6 jyu5, Japanese えつご (etsugo), and Korean 월어 (woreo) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese việt ngữ is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form — note that 粤's own reading việt is identical in spelling to the unrelated 越 ("Viet"), so this compound can misleadingly read as "Vietnamese language" to a Vietnamese speaker despite meaning "Yue/Cantonese."
