## Notes
- [侠](../characters/侠.md) "knight-errant, chivalrous hero" + [客](../characters/客.md) "guest, traveller, wanderer" — the figure of the wandering chivalrous swordsman, a stock character across East Asian literary traditions. 侠 (hiệp in Vietnamese) encodes the ethic of heroic loyalty and righteous violence; 客 frames the hero as a roaming independent, beholden to no lord. In Chinese wuxia (武侠) fiction, 侠客 is the standard term for such a figure — from the Tang dynasty tales of 俠客行 (Xíkè xíng) to the novels of Jin Yong. In Japanese, きょうかく (kyōkaku) appears in historical dramas and refers to a swordsman or champion of the weak outside formal social structures. Korean 협객 similarly appears in period dramas for heroic wandering figures. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 헙칵 diverges from Sino-Korean 협객 in both syllables. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄝㄆㄎㄚㄎ unique among both words and characters).

Legitimizing note: 侠's own `stand_in` is this exact word (侠客), so 侠 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by 侠客.
