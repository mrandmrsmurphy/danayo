## Notes
- [有 (char)](../characters/有%20(char).md) "to have; there is" + [利](../characters/利.md) "advantage; profit; sharpness" — literally "having advantage," hence "advantageous, favorable." Neither character's `stand_in` points here (有's is itself, 利's is [[利潤]]), so no legitimizer relationship applies. `kwin` is false because Dan'a'yo 유릐 differs from Sino-Korean 유리, since 利 keeps its stored Dan'a'yo reading 릐.

有利 is a plain have-X adjective, built the same way as many other 有 compounds, and it describes a position, condition, or argument that improves someone's chances. It is not a possession like a "benefit" in hand. That separates it from [[利益]] "benefit, advantage," the noun for the gain itself. In Mandarin the normal frame is 有利於 + noun, "to be favorable to X," and 有利條件 "favorable conditions" is a stock collocation. Japanese ゆうり is an na-adjective, used constantly in contests, negotiations, and law: 有利な立場 "an advantageous position," 有利に進める "to advance with the advantage." Its opposite is 不利. Korean 유리 is used the same way. It is also the reading of 琉璃, so in Korean 유리 is the everyday word for "glass," and context separates the two. Japanese ゆうり has its own homophones too, including 遊離 "separation." Vietnamese hữu lợi is the compositional form (hữu for 有, lợi for 利) and is confirmed by hvdic.thivien.net as "có lợi, thuận lợi."

Dan'a'yo keeps the shared CJKV sense, "advantageous to someone or something," with no narrowing or broadening. The Dan'a'yo grammar uses it in the phrase 東亜話者有利, "advantageous to East Asian speakers," where the beneficiary is simply put in front of it. In the Valuation domain it sits with the other judgments of fitness and quality as the adjective for "to one's advantage."
