## Notes
- [日](../characters/日%20(char).md) "day, sun" + [耳](../characters/耳%20(char).md) "ear" + [曼](../characters/曼.md) "long, beautiful" — a phonetic transliteration of "Germanic"; no stand-in relationship applies (日/耳's own stand-ins are themselves; 曼's own is name-only). 曼 is used for its sound only; its meaning plays no part.

日耳曼 means Germanic — the ethno-linguistic group and adjective.

Mandarin Rì'ěrmàn, Cantonese jat6 ji5 maan6, and Vietnamese Nhật Nhĩ Man are the Sinitic phonetic transliteration used compositionally here. Japanese ゲルマン and Korean 게르만 are direct international loanwords (from "German[ic]"), the standard, expected forms in those languages. No homophones (注音 ㄋㄧㄊㄋㄧㄇㄚㄋ unique across both words and characters).

Dan'a'yo keeps the shared CJKV sense, an ethno-linguistic term, kept apart from the state: [[徳国]] is modern Germany, while 日耳曼 names the Germanic peoples and languages (and, as an adjective, anything Germanic). The three characters are a sound-match on "Germanic" and carry no meaning of their own; Japanese and Korean use the direct loan ゲルマン / 게르만 instead. I did not trace when the Sinitic form first appeared.
