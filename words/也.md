## Notes
- [也 (char)](../characters/也%20(char).md) "as for; EMPHASIS"

也's pictographic origin is famously disputed — proposals include a crying child with open mouth, female genitalia, a simplified snake glyph, a wash basin, and a flat-legged scorpion, with no scholarly consensus. Whatever its original picture, 也 grammaticalized very early (possibly of Sino-Tibetan origin, compared to Tibetan ལ la) into a sentence-final assertive/topic particle — marking a statement as a definitional or emphatic assertion ("X 也," "it is indeed X") — and later, in the topic-comment construction "A 也, B" ("as for A, B"), a topic marker. It is the third most frequently used character in the entire Classical Chinese corpus.

Japanese has on'yomi や (ya) alongside the native reading なり (nari, itself a classical Japanese copula echoing 也's own assertive function — a striking functional parallel rather than a borrowed reading). Korean 야 (ya) is likewise confined to classical/literary use. Vietnamese dã is the Hán Việt reading, attested in classical Vietnamese texts written under heavy Chinese literary influence; the character page's other four stored candidates (dạ, giã, giãi, rã) belong to unrelated words and were not used here.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading ('ya/야/⼘) with another word still awaiting its own turn in this sweep, [[夜]] ("night") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed when it comes up.
