## Notes
- [平](../characters/平.md) "even; level; peaceful" + [安](../characters/安.md) "peaceful" — this word is the stand-in for [安](../characters/安.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

平安 is a near-synonym doubling, both halves independently meaning "peaceful" — safety and calm together, used both as a wish (一路平安, "safe travels") and a plain descriptor of a peaceful state of affairs. Japanese 平安 (Heian) additionally names the Heian period (794–1185), one of the classical eras of Japanese history and literature, taken directly from this same word.

Japanese へいあん, Korean 평안, and Vietnamese bình an are all standard, non-drifted readings. No homophones (注音 ㄅ⼶ㄫ·ㄚㄋ unique across both words and characters).

As a proper noun, 平安 (Pyongan) is also the bare name of a historical province of Korea in the northwest of the peninsula, which takes 道 "province" to make 平安道. The name joins the first characters of its two chief towns, [[平壌]] (Pyongyang) and 安州 (Anju) (from memory, not re-checked). The province was divided in 1896 into 平安北道 (North Pyongan, whose capital is [[新義州]] Sinuiju) and 平安南道 (South Pyongan, whose capital is Pyongsong), and both halves are now in North Korea (from memory, not re-checked). The province sense is the same string with a place-name use, not a second part of speech, so the word keeps its single `pos`. The Korean reading 평안 is the same for the adjective and the province name. English Wiktionary lists Chinese place names written 平安 (a district in Qinghai among them), but not the Korean province, so the province sense is from memory, not re-checked on a dictionary page.

1. peaceful, safe.
2. Pyongan, the historical province of Korea (now North and South Pyongan, both in North Korea).
