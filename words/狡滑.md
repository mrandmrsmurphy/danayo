## Notes
- [狡](../characters/狡.md) "cunning, sly" + [滑 (char)](../characters/滑%20(char).md) "slippery" — this word is the stand-in for [狡](../characters/狡.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

狡滑 is a synonym-doubling compound: both halves independently mean "crafty, sly, cunning," reinforcing rather than combining two distinct senses — the same shape as many two-character adjective compounds in this register. Applied to people or animals (classically, especially the fox and the hare, both proverbial tricksters across the CJKV sphere), it carries a squarely negative charge: shrewdness turned toward deception rather than mere cleverness.

**A genuine graphemic curiosity sits underneath this word.** The everyday real-world spelling of this word — 猾, "crafty" — has no separate character page in this vault; it is recorded as an alias of [滑](../characters/滑%20(char).md), "slippery," which does. 猾 and 滑 both derive from the same phonetic component 骨 and share the exact same Middle Chinese reading (户八切, 匣母鎋韻合口), making them true homophones with completely unrelated meanings — structurally the same relationship as this vault's own [[且]]/[[処]] pair, just resolved differently: 且/処 got two separate character entries sharing one reading, while 猾/滑 were folded into a single entry. Per this vault's own convention, when a character is registered as an alias, the parent form is the real entry and the alias itself is never used independently anywhere in the vault — not in a word's `characters:` field, and not in a word's own title either — so this word is titled and spelled 狡滑, with 狡猾 recorded only as a spelling-variant `alias`.

**Correction (2026-09-12, user-directed):** this page was originally titled `狡猾.md`, on the theory that a word could still be conventionally spelled with an alias glyph even while its `characters:` frontmatter cited the parent form. The user identified this as wrong and it's now corrected — see the equivalent fix on [[辺疆]] and the corrected [[feedback_alias_parent_form]] memory.

Mandarin (jiǎohuá), Cantonese (gaau2 waat6), Japanese (kōkatsu), Korean (gyohwal), and Vietnamese (giảo hoạt) all preserve the compound transparently and regularly, with every syllable a clean concatenation of the two characters' own independent readings — no fused or irregular reading, unlike some doubled-adjective compounds elsewhere in this vault.
