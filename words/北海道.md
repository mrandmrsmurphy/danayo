## Notes
- [北](../characters/北.md) "north" + [海](../characters/海.md) "sea" + [道](../characters/道%20(char).md) "way"

北海道 is a Meiji-era coinage: in 1869 the new government replaced the old name Ezo (蝦夷地) with 北海道, "northern sea circuit", after the ancient Chinese-style circuits (道) into which the country had once been divided, so the final 道 is an administrative term and not the word for "road" in the ordinary sense. The Japanese reading is ほっかいどう with a doubled consonant. The name is generally credited to the explorer Matsuura Takeshirō (from memory, not re-checked).

Hokkaido is the second largest of the four main islands, separated from Honshu by the Tsugaru Strait, and the traditional homeland of the Ainu. Its capital is Sapporo, and it is known for a cold, snowy climate, dairy farming, seafood and the Sapporo Snow Festival. It is the only prefecture-level unit called a 道 (dō, "circuit"), beside the 都 of Tokyo, the 府 of Kyoto and Osaka, and the 43 ordinary 県.

In Dan'a'yo 北海道 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [道](道.md), a circuit. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 홋카이도 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
