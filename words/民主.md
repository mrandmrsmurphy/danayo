## Notes
- [民](../characters/民.md) "citizen" + [主](../characters/主.md) "lord, master" — no stand-in relationship applies (民's own stand-in is [[人民]]; 主's own is [[主人]]).

In Classical Chinese, 民主 meant "sovereign," as in 民之主, "ruler of the people." This was repurposed in the mid-1860s to mean "democracy, democratic" during the translation of the book Elements of International Law.

Mandarin mínzhǔ, Cantonese man4 zyu2, Japanese みんしゅ, Korean 민주, and Vietnamese dân chủ are all standard, fully compositional readings (all match 民's and 主's own stored citations exactly). `kwin: true` because 諺文 민주 matches Korean 민주 exactly. Removed the redundant `品詞` field (duplicate of `pos`). No homophones (注音 ㄇㄧㄋㄐㄨ unique across both words and characters).
