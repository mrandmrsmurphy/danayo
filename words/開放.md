## Notes
- [開](../characters/開.md) "open" + [放](../characters/放.md) "release" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes 開 as a bound character (放's own stand-in is [[釈放]]; transitivity fails, so no `#cranberry`).

開放 means to be open, accessible, or unrestricted — of a place open to the public, a policy of liberalization, or a mind open to new ideas. Mandarin 開放 (kāifàng), Cantonese 開放 (hoi1fong3), Japanese 開放 (かいほう), and Korean 개방 (gaebang) all share this general sense across physical, political, and economic registers alike (e.g. 改革開放 gǎigé kāifàng, "Reform and Opening-up," the watershed Chinese economic policy).

Sino-Vietnamese khai phóng carries the same core "open up, liberate" sense but has taken on a distinctive intellectual coloring in modern Vietnamese: beyond the literal or economic-policy sense, it names an "open, liberated" spirit of thought — often glossed as a fusion of khai minh ("enlightenment") and giải phóng ("liberation") — used especially in educational and philosophical contexts to describe free, undogmatic inquiry, a more idea-oriented register than its Chinese/Japanese/Korean cognates typically carry.
