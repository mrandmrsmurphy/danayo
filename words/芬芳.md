## Notes
- [芬](../characters/芬.md) "fragrant" + [芳](../characters/芳.md) "fragrant"
- This word is the viable form of [芬](../characters/芬.md): the character's meaning (fragrant, aromatic) is identical, but 芬 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 芬芳 is its `stand_in` compound.

芬芳 means fragrant, sweet-smelling: 芬 and 芳 both mean "fragrance", so the compound doubles the idea, as many Sinitic descriptive words do. It is used of flowers and perfume and, in literary writing, of what is figuratively sweet-scented, such as a good name or a fine text. Mandarin fēnfāng, Cantonese fan1 fong1, Korean 분방 (bunbang) and Vietnamese phân phương are the same compound; in Japanese it is a rare literary word (ふんぽう). 芳 also appears alone as an honorific ("your" in 芳名, "your esteemed name").

In Dan'a'yo it is the pleasant-smell word in Sensation, set against [[臭]] ("to stink"), the unpleasant one.
