## Notes
- [指](../characters/指.md) "finger; point" + [揮](../characters/揮.md) "command; conduct" — this word is the viable form of [揮](../characters/揮.md): the character's meaning (to conduct, command) is identical, but 揮 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 指 ("to point; direct") supplies the first syllable, evoking the gesture of the conductor's baton.

指揮 covers both musical conducting (leading an orchestra) and military/organizational command, the same dual sense reflected in its counterpart in every source language.

Mandarin zhǐhuī, Cantonese zi2 fai1, Korean 지휘 (jihwi), and Japanese しき (shiki) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each covers both senses in its language. Vietnamese chỉ huy is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 指's chỉ and 揮's own huy, and is itself the real, everyday Vietnamese word for "command" (chỉ huy trưởng, "commander"). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 즤휘 diverges from Sino-Korean 지휘 in the first syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄐㄧㄜㄏㄨㄧ unique among both words and characters).
