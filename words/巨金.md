## Notes

**Form.** 巨金 is [巨](characters/巨.md) "giant, huge" + [金](characters/金%20(char).md) "metal," following the rule for transition/structural elements named with a X+金 descriptor. 羅馬字/諺文/注音 are the direct concatenation gyo+gim/교+김/ㄍ⼄+ㄍㄧㄇ. The `mandarin`/`cantonese` fields above give the readings of the standard CJK element character 钛 (tài/taai3) — the character 巨金 deliberately avoids — and `korean`/`japanese`/`vietnamese` give the loanword names for the element itself (티타늄/チタン/titan), per the established convention for `periodictable` neologisms: these are not readings of 巨金 as a compound.

**Etymological chain.** The international name *titanium* was itself coined (by Martin Heinrich Klaproth) after the Titans of Greek mythology — a race of primordial giants. This gives a direct, semantically faithful two-step path: Titan (mythological giant) → giant/huge → 巨.

**Motivation.**
- 巨 ("giant, huge") is not a phonetic transcription but a semantic calque of the element's own etymology — titanium is *already* named for giants, so this is a case where meaning-based naming and the source etymology align exactly, unlike toponymic or property-based coinages elsewhere in this series.
- It avoids the unrelated phonetic-loan character 钛, which carries no semantic content of its own beyond "metal + tài."
- 金 is used appropriately for a structural transition metal.

**Type of formation.** A **direct etymological calque**: the referent (Titans, giants) is translated rather than transliterated, then suffixed with 金.

**Comparative CJKV forms.**
- Modern Chinese: 钛 (phonetic, from tài)
- Japanese: チタン (phonetic, from "titan")
- Korean: 티타늄 (phonetic, from "titanium")
- Vietnamese: titan (phonetic loan)

All modern forms are phonetic transcriptions of the international name; 巨金 alone restores a meaning-based graph reflecting the name's own mythological origin.

**Conclusion.** 巨金 is one of the more transparent coinages in this neologism series: because the international name titanium is itself a semantic reference to the Titans, rendering that meaning directly as 巨 ("giant") produces a calque that is simultaneously etymologically faithful and free of arbitrary phonetic substitution.
