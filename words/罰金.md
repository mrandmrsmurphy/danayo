## Notes
- [罰](../characters/罰.md) "penalty" + [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "metal, gold; money" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (罰's own is [[懲罰]]; 金's own is itself), so 罰金 is an independent compound. `kwin: true` per the AND-rule: `characters/罰.md` and `characters/金 (char).md` are both individually `kwin: true`.

罰金 means "fine, monetary penalty" — distinct from [[懲罰]]/[[処分]] "to punish" in general, this names specifically the sum of money an offender must pay. Mandarin fájīn, Cantonese fat6 gam1, Japanese ばっきん (bakkin), and Korean 벌금 (beolgeum) are all standard, fully compositional readings, keeping 罰's "penalty" first and 金's "money" second.

**Vietnamese reverses the morpheme order.** Living Vietnamese doesn't calque 罰金's own Sino-Vietnamese order (phạt "penalty" + kim "money," which is not independently attested as a real word); instead it says tiền phạt — literally "money-penalty," with the native word tiền ("money") placed first and Hán Việt phạt second. This is the one language of the five where the word isn't just a different lexical choice but a genuine word-order flip.
