## Notes
- [清](../characters/清.md) "pure, clear" + [馨](../characters/馨.md) "fragrant" — this word is the viable form of [馨](../characters/馨.md): the character's meaning (fragrant; aromatic) is identical, but 馨 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 清馨 is its `stand_in` compound.

清馨 is a literary compound describing a delicate, pure, understated fragrance — the light scent of tea, flowers, or fine wine — rather than a strong or heavy perfume. 馨 itself carries a figurative extension beyond literal smell: 德馨 ("the fragrance of virtue") uses the same image for a good reputation or moral character that "spreads far," as in the classical phrase 惟吾德馨 ("it is my virtue that gives fragrance," from Liu Yuxi's 《陋室銘》).

Japanese せいけい and Korean 청형 are both standard compositional Sino-readings. Vietnamese thanh hinh is compositionally derived from each character's own confirmed reading, though not independently attested as a fixed dictionary compound — consistent with this word's own literary, relatively rare register. No specific HSK level applies to a term this literary. No homophones (注音 ㄑㄧㄫㄏㄝㄫ unique across both words and characters).
