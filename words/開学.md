## Notes
- [開](../characters/開.md) "open" + [学](../characters/学.md) "learn"

開学 denotes the start of a school term or the founding of an educational institution. Mandarin 開學 (kāixué) and Cantonese 開學 (hoi1hok6) are both everyday, general-purpose terms for the start of a school year at any level, and Sino-Vietnamese khai học parallels this closely, used interchangeably with the native-feeling khai giảng ("opening lesson") for the same event.

Japanese 開学 (かいがく) has narrowed sharply: it refers specifically to the founding of a university (establishing the institution itself, its charter and mission), not the everyday start of a term — that everyday sense is instead carried by 始業 or 新学期. This contrasts with the related 開校 (kaikō), which covers the founding of a school at any level. Korean 개학 (gaehak) instead keeps the broad Chinese sense, referring to the start of a new semester after a break at any level of school — the same everyday meaning as the Mandarin/Cantonese/Vietnamese cognates, making Japanese the outlier here.
