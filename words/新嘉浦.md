## Notes
- [新](../characters/新%20(char).md) "new" + [嘉](../characters/嘉.md) "good" + [浦](../characters/浦%20(char).md) "shore" — a phonetic transliteration of "Singapore," built for sound rather than sense.
- A novel combination for sound, for Dan'a'yo — Dan'a'yo's own characters (新嘉浦) differ slightly from the standard real-world Chinese name 新加坡 (using 加 not 嘉, 坡 not 浦), chosen from among near-homophones already available in Dan'a'yo's own character set; see `aliases` for the standard and variant Chinese spellings.
- Nicknames: [[獅城]] "lion city" or [[星洲]] "star island"

Mandarin Xīnjiāpō and Cantonese san1 gaa3 bo1 are the real, standard names, matching 新's and 嘉's own stored citations closely (浦's own pǔ/pou2 stand in for 坡's pō/po1, a near-homophone substitution). Japanese シンガポール and Korean 싱가포르 are both real, standard direct phonetic loans from English "Singapore," not Sino-Xenic compositional readings — Singapore has no traditional Sino-Japanese/Sino-Korean exonym in modern use, unlike China or Japan's own names. Vietnamese Tân Gia Ba is the real, older Sino-Vietnamese exonym, still used historically alongside the modern "Singapore."

`kwin` is false: Korean 싱가포르, a phonetic loan, does not match the compositional Sino-Korean stacking of 新's own 신 + 嘉's own 가 + 浦's own 포 (신가포) — despite all three characters individually being kwin-true, there is no real Sino-Korean compound in modern use to match against. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄧㄋㄍㄚㄆㄛ unique among both words and characters).
