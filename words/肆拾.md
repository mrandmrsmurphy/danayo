## Notes
- [肆](../characters/肆.md) "four (anti-forgery form)" + [拾](../characters/拾.md) "ten (anti-forgery form)" — the formal, fraud-resistant way of writing the numeral [[四十]] ("forty"), exactly as [[参拾]] is to [[三十]].

肆 stands in for 四 in the same "大寫數字" (formal/anti-forgery numeral) system as 參/参 for 三 — characters chosen because their complexity makes after-the-fact alteration of a written amount difficult or impossible. **肆拾** therefore carries the same meaning as [[四十]]; the two differ in which register of numeral character is used, with 肆拾 reserved for checks, contracts, ledgers, and other documents where a number's integrity matters.

[[四十]]'s own Notes observe that it is "an anagram of [[十四]]" (14) — the same is naturally true of 肆拾 relative to 拾肆 (a hypothetical formal writing of 14, which has no page here), though the formal numerals are far less often used for teen-numbers, where ambiguity and forgery risk are lower.

The Dan'a'yo reading of 肆拾 is ㄙㄧㄜㄙㄜㄆ (諺文 싀습), built from 肆's own reading ㄙㄧㄜ and 拾's own reading ㄙㄜㄆ, where the ordinary [四十](四十.md) is ㄙㄧㄜㄙㄧㄆ (싀십); the two therefore differ in the vowel of the last syllable. The Korean field, 사십, is the ordinary Sino-Korean reading of the numeral forty, which is why `kwin` is false, and the Japanese よんじゅう is likewise the everyday reading of 四十. The Numbers page pairs it with [参拾](参拾.md) as the formal register "alongside the everyday 三十/四十." Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "forty, in the anti-forgery numeral form." The Vietnamese form is tứ thập.
