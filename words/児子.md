## Notes
- [児 (char)](../characters/児%20(char).md) "baby" + [子](../characters/子.md) "son" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes [子](../characters/子.md) as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

児子 is "son, child" — the Swadesh basic-vocabulary term for a child, built by reinforcing 子 ("son, child") with 児 ("baby, infant"). Its own aliases 兒子/儿子 (traditional/simplified Mandarin orthography) and 息子 (the real Japanese word, むすこ) make explicit that this compound is genuinely attested as an everyday word in Mandarin and Cantonese (儿子/児子, érzi/ji4 zi2) but functions as a compositional cross-reference rather than a natural standalone word in Japanese, Korean, or Vietnamese.

Mandarin 兒子/儿子 (érzi) and Cantonese 兒子 (ji4 zi2) are the ordinary, everyday words for "son." Japanese uses 息子 (むすこ, musuko) as the real native word, with 児子 itself only a compositional on'yomi reference (じし); Korean uses native 아들 (adeul, "son") in everyday speech, with 아자 given here as the compositional Sino-Korean cross-reference instead; Vietnamese likewise reaches for the native con trai ("son," literally "child-male") rather than the compositional Sino-Vietnamese nhi tử stored here.

For the intimate/diminutive doubled form used to refer to one's own child, see [[子子]], built by reduplicating 子 alone (児子 itself, already a two-character compound, does not reduplicate further).
