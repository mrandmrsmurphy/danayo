## Notes
- [飽 (char)](../characters/飽%20(char).md) "sated, saturated, full, satisfied" + [足 (char)](../characters/足%20(char).md) "foot" — no `#cranberry`: both 飽's and 足's own `stand_in` are themselves; neither points here. Here 足 carries its extended sense "sufficient, adequate" rather than the literal "foot."

飽足 (bǎozú) is a real Mandarin word for "fully satisfied," and Cantonese 飽足 (baau2zuk1) likewise. Japanese ほうそく (hōsoku, also read あきだり in an older form) and Korean 포족 (pojok, in 포족하다, "to be full and satisfied") are both real, attested terms.

Sino-Vietnamese bão túc is compositionally straightforward but not independently attested — modern Vietnamese uses the native no đủ or thỏa mãn for the same sense.
