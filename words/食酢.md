## Notes
- [食 (char)](../characters/食%20(char).md) "eat" + [酢](../characters/酢.md) "vinegar" — 食酢 is 酢's own legitimizing compound (酢's `stand_in` points here), but since 食's own `stand_in` is 食 itself instead, transitivity fails and no `#cranberry` tag applies.

酢 carries a genuine reading split between senses: its classical sense ("to toast, to reciprocate a host's wine," Mandarin zuò, matching this character's own stored Dan'a'yo derivation) versus its modern "vinegar" sense, which has effectively merged with 醋's own reading in both Mandarin (cù) and Cantonese (cou3, not zok6) — this word takes the vinegar-sense readings throughout: Mandarin shícù, Cantonese sik6cou3. Japanese しょくす (shokusu, also read しょくず) is the standard official term (designated by the Japan Agricultural Standards in 1979), and Korean 식초 (sikcho) is likewise the standard, everyday word for vinegar — both using 酢's own real reading directly, not the vault's Dan'a'yo derivation.

Sino-Vietnamese thực thố is compositionally straightforward but not independently attested — Vietnamese instead uses the native giấm ("vinegar") or giấm ăn ("cooking vinegar").
