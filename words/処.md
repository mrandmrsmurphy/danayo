## Notes
- [処 (char)](../characters/処%20(char).md) "place" — this word is the `stand_in` that legitimizes its own character 処.

処 is the Dan'a'yo noun "place; location", and, more importantly, the "place" noun-class of the correlative table. Combined with the seven determiners it generates the whole spatial series: [此処](此処.md) "here" (lit. this place), [其処](其処.md) "there (medial)", [彼処](彼処.md) "yonder", [何処](何処.md) "where?", [某処](某処.md) "somewhere; a certain place", [毎処](毎処.md) "at each place" and [皆処](皆処.md) "everywhere". The table is 7 determiners × 9 noun classes (事, 物, 人, 名, 処, 時, 様, 多, 類), and the 処 row is parallel to the temporal 時 row, so 此処/此時, 其処/其時 and so on pair spatial and temporal deixis; the chart is in `grammar/文法 - 97品詞.md` and on [Grammar](../lexipedia/Grammar.md). As in 此処於 在 ("is here", `grammar/文法 - 05形態.md`, relative-clause example 1), place words take the postposition 於 after them, not before.

On orthography, the character page lists 處 (traditional) and 处 (simplified) as aliases of 処, and they are aliases here too; no separate `words/處.md` or `words/处.md` exists. The character page follows Shuowen in treating 処 (夂 "foot" + 几 "table, stool", "to arrive at a table and stop", hence "to dwell") as the older form, with 處 (虍 added) the later variant. That is the traditional account; Wiktionary's own glyph analysis is of 處 itself, as 虍 + 夂 + 几, and it lists 処 as a variant of 處 and the Japanese shinjitai. Whichever is the older form, the dwelling/stopping sense is the root of the "place" sense. The claim that 処 depicts a person resting in a shelter is not supported and is not made here.

Wiktionary's 處 entry (checked 2026-10-11) separates two Mandarin readings. chǔ (the reading on the character page) covers the verbal senses: to reside, to dwell, to be situated in, to manage or deal with, to punish, to get along with; the compounds 處理 "deal with", 處分 and 處決 belong here. chù covers the noun senses: "place; location; spot", "part; aspect", "department; office", with Cantonese cyu3 (also syu3). This word carries the noun sense "place", so the `mandarin` field gives chù and the `cantonese` field cyu3, whereas the character page gives the primary reading chǔ. The fields `japanese` しょ (on'yomi, Wiktionary: place, locale, department), `korean` 처 (the North Korean reading; Wiktionary Hanja: place, locate, stop) and `vietnamese` xứ (hvdic lists xứ, xử, xớ, xở for 處; the Wiktionary Sino-Xenic descendants line gives xứ for the noun reading) are the Sino-Xenic bound readings of the character, not the everyday words. The native equivalents are Japanese ところ (tokoro) and its deictic series ここ, そこ, あそこ, どこ; Korean 곳 (got); and Vietnamese chỗ and the deictics đây, đó, kia, đâu (the Vietnamese equivalents from memory, not re-checked).

Dan'a'yo 処 is therefore the noun class, and in the vault the verbal sense "to deal with" lives in separate compounds: [処決](処決.md) "handle, resolve, execute" and [処分](処分.md) "punish, discipline", plus [処女](処女.md), [処格](処格.md) "locative case", [一処](一処.md) "in one place, together" and [近処](近処.md) "vicinity". There is no word 処理 in the vault. On [Locatives](../lexipedia/Locatives.md) it is one of four "place" words alongside [位置](位置.md), [所在](所在.md) and the verb [置](置.md); there it is described as demonstrative in flavor ("this place") rather than a general abstract noun, which is the Dan'a'yo-specific nuance and follows from its role as the correlative stem. Words such as 場所, 場 and 地 are not treated here.

処 is a Dan'a'yo homophone of [[且]] "also; additionally" (ㄑㄛ).
