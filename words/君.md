## Notes
- [君 (char)](../characters/君%20(char).md) "lord, ruler; (literary, honorific) you" — this word is the character's own `stand_in`: 君 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry by itself.

君 is the second-person singular pronoun in Dan'a'yo, "you" or "thou." The grammar lists it among the 代詞 as the counterpart of [[我]] ([[文法 - 04句法]]), and the personal-pronoun table in [[文法 - 97品詞]] glosses it "Thou." The same table sets 君 apart from [[汝]], the intimate "you" (see also [[人称]]), and from the royal [[朕]] and the humble [[俺]], so 君 is the default form of address and 汝 is the marked intimate one. The grammar uses it as an ordinary free-standing pronoun: 我 君如 高 ("I am as tall as you"), 我 君比 更好 ("I am better than you"), 我之描 君之犬比 少大 ("my cat is less big than your dog"), all in [[文法 - 05形態]]. It also appears in the grammar's Korean booklet, 君 父親于 贈与 書本 (glossed there as "You gave a book to father"). The plural is formed with the collective suffix [[等]]: [[君等]] "you all" ([[文法 - 05形態]]); the archaic plural [[爾等]] "ye" also exists. [[文法 - 98違法字]] tells writers to use 君 in place of the forbidden 你 for the second person.

君 is the Swadesh 2 word ("thou") on [[Swadesh]], next to [[我]] (Swadesh 1) and [[君等]] (Swadesh 5, "you").

This is an extension of the classical word, not its ordinary Chinese meaning. In Mandarin 君 (jūn) means "sovereign, monarch, lord, ruler"; per Wiktionary it also has a literary honorific sense "you; your (referring to a male)", a polite form of address between spouses, and an internet-slang suffix like Japanese -kun. The everyday Mandarin "you" is 你 (nǐ), or 您 (nín) when respectful. Cantonese everyday "you" is likewise 你 (nei5; from memory, not re-checked). Japanese has the same character in the same range of senses. 君 (きみ, kimi) is a pronoun for "you" (Wiktionary: informal, chiefly men's speech), as well as the noun "ruler, lord". 君 is also the suffix くん (-kun), "indicates respect or familiarity," used for boys' names and for equals or juniors, and ぎみ (-gimi) is a respectful suffix as in 父君 "your father". Korean 군 (gun) is the Sino-Korean reading with hanja glosses "sovereign" and "young man; young sir" (native gloss 임금); in Korean the ordinary "you" is 너 or 당신. Vietnamese quân (also vua, "king") is "sovereign, monarch"; the ordinary "you" is bạn, anh, chị or em depending on relationship. So in Dan'a'yo the classical and Japanese-style honorific "you" use of 君 is made the standard pronoun, while the sphere's everyday pronouns (你, あなた, 너, bạn and so on) are not part of the language. The `japanese`, `korean` and `vietnamese` fields hold the Sino-Xenic readings of the character, not these native "you" words.

The compounds [[君主]] "monarch", [[君子]] "gentleman", [[君臣]] "ruler and subject", [[明君]] "enlightened ruler", [[弒君]] "regicide" and [[君士坦丁堡]] "Constantinople" keep 君 in its older "lord" sense. The vault does not record why 君 was chosen as the pronoun beyond the grammar's listing, so the choice is stated here as a fact of the language, not explained. The honorific [[公]] ("sir") is a separate word, and its role relative to 君 is not documented in the grammar pages read for this entry.
