## Notes
- [徽](../characters/徽.md) "badge; insignia" + [章 (char)](../characters/章%20(char).md) "chapter; emblem" — this word is the viable form of [徽](../characters/徽.md): the character's meaning (badge, insignia) is identical, but 徽 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. The compound pairs it with 章 ("emblem; mark"), reinforcing the meaning.
- Japanese reads きしょう (KI+SHOU), the same reading as 記章 "decoration; medal."

徽章 names a badge, insignia, or emblem — a near-synonym of [[符号]] ("symbol, mark," a broader category also covering badges and insignia), with 徽章 leaning more specifically toward decorative or ceremonial emblems (school badges, medals) than 符号's broader "any symbol" range.

Mandarin huīzhāng, Cantonese fai1 zoeng1, and Korean 휘장 (hwijang) are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 徽's and 章's own stored citations. Vietnamese huy chương is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 徽's huy and 章's own chương, and is itself the real, everyday Vietnamese word for "medal" (huy chương vàng, "gold medal"). **Real `kwin` bug found and fixed**: was stored as `false`, but Dan'a'yo 휘장 is byte-identical to Sino-Korean 휘장 — corrected to `kwin: true`. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄏㄨㄧㄐㄚㄫ unique among both words and characters).
