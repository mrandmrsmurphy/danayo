## Notes
- [首](../characters/首.md) "head, prime" + [尾 (char)](../characters/尾%20(char).md) "tail" — no `#cranberry`: 首's own `stand_in` is [[首長]], 尾's is 尾 itself; neither points here.

首尾 (shǒuwěi) is the standard Mandarin word for "beginning and end, head and tail," and Cantonese 首尾 (sau2mei5) likewise. Japanese しゅび (shubi) keeps the literal sense but is also extended figuratively to "outcome, result" (e.g. 首尾よく, "successfully"). Korean 수미 (sumi) matches the literal sense, notably in the compositional term 수미상관 ("head-tail correspondence," a literary device). Sino-Vietnamese thủ vĩ is a real, if more literary/technical, term (as in thủ vĩ tương ứng, "head and tail in mutual correspondence") — a clean case across all five languages.
