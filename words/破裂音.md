## Notes
- [破 (char)](../characters/破%20(char).md) "break, burst" + [裂](../characters/裂.md) "split, tear" + [音](../characters/音.md) "sound" — no stand-in relationship applies (破 is its own stand-in; 裂's is [[破裂]]; 音's is [[音楽]]).

破裂音 is the "bursting sound": the plosive, a consonant made by closing the airway and releasing it in a sudden burst. In Dan'a'yo it is the plain, ordinary member of a three-way system of bursts, named by where the air goes: 破裂音 is the **outward burst**, driven by air from the lungs (pulmonic egressive); [[入破音]] is the **inward burst**, with air drawn in by a lowered larynx (glottalic ingressive, the implosive); and [[喉破音]] is the **throat burst**, with air pushed out by a raised larynx (glottalic egressive, the ejective). The three share 破 "burst": the plain plosive is the unmarked default, and the other two add 入 "in" or 喉 "throat" to name where the air comes from.

Mandarin pòlièyīn (Cantonese po3 lit6 jam1) lists 破裂音 as an alternative name for 塞音, the stop, and Japanese はれつおん is the ordinary term for a plosive; Korean uses 破裂音 as well, and the `korean` field holds the North Korean 파렬음 (South Korean 파열음 drops the initial ㄹ under the 두음법칙). Dan'a'yo keeps [[塞音]] as the word for the class of stops and uses 破裂音 for the plain pulmonic plosive when contrasting it with the other two, as in the lenition of a voiced velar plosive to a fricative described on the phonology page. Wiktionary has no Vietnamese entry, so that field is omitted. No homophones (注音 ㄆㄜㄌㄝㄊ·ㄨㄇ unique across both words and characters).
