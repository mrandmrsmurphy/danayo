## Notes
- [踊](../characters/踊.md) "jump" + [躍](../characters/躍.md) "leap"

踊躍 literally stacks two near-synonymous "jump/leap" roots, and the resulting compound reads figuratively rather than physically: it describes the eagerness of leaping rather than an actual jump. In Modern Written Mandarin yǒngyuè survives as a formal register word for enthusiastic participation — 踊躍發言 ("to speak up eagerly"), 踊躍參加/踊躍捐款 ("participate/donate enthusiastically") are standard in announcements and official writing, though colloquial speech reaches for 積極 or 熱烈 instead. The same compound also carries a distinct Buddhist devotional sense inherited from sutra translation: 踊躍歡喜 ("to leap for joy") is a stock phrase describing a listener's rapture on hearing the Buddha's teaching, and this reading is shared across the Chinese, Japanese, and Korean Buddhist canons rather than being a Mandarin-only coinage. Korean 용약 (踊躍) preserves this literary/devotional register directly, essentially unused in ordinary speech. Japanese ようやく is likewise a bookish, literary word — not to be confused with the unrelated native adverb ようやく ("finally, at last"), an accidental homophone with a completely different etymology. Vietnamese dũng dược is the honest compositional reading (踊's own dũng + 躍's own dược); no independently attested Vietnamese loanword for this compound was found. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 용약 matches Sino-Korean 용약 exactly. No homophones (注音 ⼄ㄫ⼘ㄎ unique among both words and characters).

踊's own `stand_in` is this exact word (踊躍), so 踊 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by 踊躍; 躍's own `stand_in` is instead 跳躍, so this word does not legitimize 躍.
