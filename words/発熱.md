## Notes
- [発 (char)](../characters/発%20(char).md) "issue forth, discharge" + [熱 (char)](../characters/熱%20(char).md) "heat up, be hot" — literally "to issue forth heat." 発's own stand-in is [[発]] itself and 熱's is [[熱]] itself, so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply; neither constituent is legitimized by this word.

発熱 covers both the literal physics sense — a device or reaction generating heat — and the medical sense of running a fever, the latter being the word's most common everyday use. Mandarin fārè, Cantonese faat3 jit6, Japanese はつねつ (hatsunetsu), Korean 발열 (baryeol), and Vietnamese phát nhiệt are all fully compositional and share both senses consistently.

In colloquial Mandarin, 發燒/发烧 (fāshāo, literally "to catch fire/burn") is actually the more common everyday way to say "have a fever," with 発熱/发熱 reserved for more formal or medical registers — hence its inclusion among this page's aliases alongside the straightforward traditional/simplified orthographic pair 發熱/发热.
