## Notes
- [苦 (char)](../characters/苦%20(char).md) "bitter" + [土 (char)](../characters/土%20(char).md) "earth" — literally "bitter earth."

苦土 names magnesia (magnesium oxide, MgO) — and the literal "bitter earth" compositional meaning is not a coincidence or a loose calque, but a direct semantic match for the substance's own history. Magnesium salts (most famously magnesium sulfate, Epsom salt) are distinctively bitter-tasting, and early mineralogists across both the Chinese and European traditions independently named related "earths" (mineral/oxide substances) after that bitterness — the English word "magnesia" itself, though Greek in origin (from Μαγνησία, a place name in Thessaly), converges on the same "earth" framing that 苦土 makes explicit.

Mandarin kǔtǔ, Cantonese fu2 tou2, and Japanese くど (kudo) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 苦土 is itself the real, historically attested term for magnesia in all three — still current in Chinese as a name for magnesium oxide used medicinally (as a stomach antacid). No Vietnamese field: modern Vietnamese has no Sino-Vietnamese compound for this substance, using the French/English loan magie oxit instead — omitted rather than guessed.

苦土 is also the direct etymological source of this vault's own Dan'a'yo name for the element magnesium itself, [[苦土素]] ("bitter-earth element") — magnesium was historically isolated from magnesia, the same relationship reflected in [[Periodic Table]]'s own cross-reference between the two entries.
