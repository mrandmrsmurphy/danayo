## Notes
- [門](../characters/門.md) "gate" + [戸](../characters/戸.md) "door" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes 戸 as a bound character (門's own stand-in is [[大門]]; transitivity fails, so no `#cranberry`).

門戸 denotes a door or gateway, and by extension the point of entry or access to something. Mandarin 門戶 (ménhù) and Cantonese 門戶 (mun4wu6) both carry this literal sense as well as two figurative extensions: "faction, school of thought" (as in 門戶之見 ménhù zhī jiàn, "sectarian bias") and household social standing, the same standing invoked in the marriage-matching idiom 門當戶對 (méndāng-hùduì, "matched in family status").

Japanese 門戸 (もんこ) is common in the fixed phrase 門戸開放 (monko kaihō, "open-door policy," used of trade and diplomacy) and in 門戸を開く/広げる ("to open/widen the gates," i.e. broaden access or opportunity) — a metaphorical use built on the same "gateway = access" logic as the Chinese figurative senses. Korean 문호 (門戶) parallels this closely, appearing in 문호개방 (munho gaebang, "open-door policy") with the same diplomatic/trade sense. Vietnamese retains only the literal "door" meaning as môn hộ, a classical Sino-Vietnamese term now largely supplanted in everyday speech by native cửa nẻo; it survives mainly in compounds and set phrases rather than as a live standalone word.
