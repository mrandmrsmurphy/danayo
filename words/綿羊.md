## Notes
- [綿](../characters/綿.md) "cotton" + [羊](../characters/羊.md) "sheep, goat" — 羊's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, but 綿's own is [[綿花]], so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply; 綿羊 legitimizes 羊 alone. `kwin: true` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: true`.

綿羊 names the (domestic) sheep — literally "cotton/fleece goat," referring to its woolly coat.

Mandarin miányáng, Cantonese min4 joeng4-2 (with the tone-2 change common to this kind of compound noun), and Korean 면양 (myeonyang) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and 綿羊 is itself a common attested word for "sheep" in all three. Japanese ひつじ (hitsuji) is 羊's own native kun-reading, used bare rather than in a compositional compound. Vietnamese cừu is the standard, native/loan word for "sheep," not a Sino-Vietnamese compound. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
