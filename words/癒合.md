## Notes
- [癒](../characters/癒.md) "get well, recover" + [合 (char)](../characters/合%20(char).md) "join, fit together" — literally "recovery-joining." 癒's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 合's own is [[合]] itself; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 癒 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word.

癒合 is the standard clinical term for wound healing in the specific sense of tissue closure — the biological process by which separated skin, flesh, or bone margins fuse back together, rather than "recovery" in the broader sense of an illness resolving. It appears constantly in surgical and wound-care contexts: 傷口癒合 ("wound healing"), 骨癒合 ("bone union/fracture healing").

The term is remarkably stable across the Sinosphere precisely because it is technical, medical vocabulary rather than colloquial speech: Mandarin yùhé, Cantonese jyu6 hap6, Japanese ゆごう (yugō), and Korean 유합 (yuhap) are all directly compositional, fully-transparent readings with no semantic drift or native-coinage substitution — a contrast to more everyday compounds where each language tends to diverge.
