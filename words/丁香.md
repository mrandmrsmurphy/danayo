## Notes
- [丁 (char)](../characters/丁%20(char).md) "nail" + [香](../characters/香.md) "fragrance" — clove, named for its nail-shaped dried flower bud, exactly the same visual logic behind the English word "clove" itself (from Latin *clavus*, "nail").

丁香's own two characters are shared across the whole CJKV sphere for this spice, an unusually clean case of a shared botanical name with no semantic drift between languages. **Content note**: Japanese most commonly writes this word with a different second character, 丁子/丁字 (read ちょうじ, chōji, the everyday word for clove), rather than 丁香; when the compound is specifically written 丁香 as it is here, the plain on'yomi concatenation ちょうこう is the applicable reading — documented as such rather than substituting the more common but differently-written ちょうじ form. Vietnamese đinh hương (confirmed via web search) is a direct, transparent Hán Việt borrowing with no drift from the source meaning, still the standard everyday Vietnamese word for the spice. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 덩향 diverges from Sino-Korean 정향 in the first syllable's vowel. No homophones (注音 ㄉㄝㄫㄏ⼘ㄫ unique).
