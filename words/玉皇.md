## Notes
- [玉 (char)](../characters/玉%20(char).md) "jade" + [皇](../characters/皇.md) "emperor" — the Jade Emperor.

玉皇 is the Jade Emperor, the ruler of heaven in Daoist myth and Chinese folk religion, and in practice the supreme god of Daoism. His formal titles are 玉皇上帝, 玉皇大天尊 and 玄穹高上帝; the short forms are 玉皇 and 玉帝, and the popular names 玉皇大帝, 天公伯, [天公](天公.md) and 天帝. He heads the Four Sovereigns (四御, or six), and the belief grew out of the ancient worship of the high god of heaven. Overseas Chinese communities in Southeast Asia still treat him as the supreme god. Wiktionary records the Mandarin yùhuáng, the Japanese ぎょっこう and the Vietnamese ngọc hoàng, and notes the archaic synonym 太一; the Cantonese and Korean fields are compositional, and Korean follows the North Korean readings of the characters.

The full name 玉皇大帝 is compositional, 玉皇 plus [大帝](大帝.md), and needs no entry of its own. His title 玉皇大天尊 contains [天尊](天尊.md). In the vault the Christian terms for God are [天主](天主.md) and [上帝](上帝.md); 玉皇 is the Daoist counterpart and stays a proper name.
