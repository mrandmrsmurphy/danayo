## Notes
- [草 (char)](../characters/草%20(char).md) "grass" + [花](../characters/花.md) "flower" — stand-in for [[花]], which is never used on its own

草花 (grass-flower) names flowering plants collectively, with a herbaceous/humble connotation next to showier cultivated blooms — a genuine semantic contribution from 草, not just a legitimizing filler syllable for bounded 花.

Mandarin cǎohuā is glossed on English Wiktionary as "showy; ornamental flowers" and, in a regional aside, is also the Beijing-area name for the clubs (♣) suit. Japanese has two real readings, the native くさばな (kusabana, "grass-flower") and the Sino-Japanese そうか (sōka); the `japanese` field holds そうか, following the vault's Sino-Xenic convention. Korean 초화 (chohwa) is the Sino-Korean reading, next to the everyday native 꽃 (kkot, "flower"). Vietnamese thảo hoa is attested, also in reversed order (花草, hoa thảo), next to a native calque cỏ hoa. The Korean and Vietnamese remarks are from memory, not re-checked.

花朵 ("a flower, a blossom") is a different word, 花 plus the measure word 朵. It is not an alias of this word and has no entry of its own in this vault.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄑㄚㄨㄏ⺢ (checked 2026-10-13).
