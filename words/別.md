## Notes
- [別 (char)](../characters/別%20(char).md) "don't; distinct; other"

別 is 会意: 冎/𠮠 ("bone") + 刂 ("knife") — to cut bone and flesh apart, hence "to separate, divide, distinguish." From "separate" comes both the adjectival sense "distinct, other, another" ([[個別]] "individual, separate"; [[特別]] "special, particular") and, as an imperative negator in Mandarin, "don't" (別動, "don't move"; 別説, "don't say [that]").

Mandarin bié, Cantonese bit6, and Japanese べつ/べち (betsu/bechi, on'yomi, fully productive: 特別 とくべつ "special") alongside native わか(れる) (waka(reru), "to part, separate," the everyday verb) all preserve the "separate/distinct" sense (the imperative "don't" use is specific to modern Mandarin, without a parallel in the other daughter languages). Korean 별 (byeol) parallels the Sino-xenic reading. Vietnamese biệt is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in biệt danh ("nickname," 別名) and phân biệt ("to distinguish," 分別); the character page's other two stored candidates, bết ("sticky, matted") and bịt ("to cover, plug"), are unrelated native words and were not used here.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (bed/벋/ㄅㄝㄊ) with another word still awaiting its own turn in this sweep, [[鼈]] ("Pelodiscus sinensis, a soft-shell turtle") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed when it comes up.
