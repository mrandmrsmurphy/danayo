## Notes
- [受](../characters/受.md) "to receive, undergo" + [難](../characters/難.md) "difficulty, disaster"

受難 means "to undergo hardship, to suffer disaster" — and, in specifically Christian usage (borrowed via Japanese and Chinese Bible translation), names "the Passion of Christ" (受難週, "Holy Week"; 受難曲, "a Passion," as in Bach's St. Matthew Passion). 難, a 形声 character (semantic 隹 "bird" with phonetic 漢, originally a kind of bird and later borrowed for the sound of "difficult," as the 難 character page records), means "difficult, disaster, calamity" — already developed in [[殉難]] ("martyrdom"), [[苦難]] ("hardship, tribulation"), and [[艱難]] ("arduous trials," all cross-linked from [[難]]'s Words section).

受難 belongs to the family of 受-compounds, and pairs naturally with [[受賞]] as a kind of dark mirror: where 受賞 is "to receive something good" (an award), 受難 is "to receive something terrible" (suffering, disaster) — together illustrating how 受's neutral core sense of "to receive, to undergo" takes its emotional valence entirely from what follows it. The Christian theological sense of 受難 — Christ's suffering accepted willingly for the sake of others — adds yet another layer: not merely passive misfortune, but suffering *undergone* in a morally significant, even redemptive sense.

The Emotions page lists it with both senses together, "to suffer hardship; the Passion (Christianity)," and Dan'a'yo keeps both, the general sense and the Christian one, as one word whose Christian use is a specialization of the general "to undergo hardship." The readings are Mandarin shòunàn, Cantonese sau6 naan6, Japanese junan (じゅなん), Korean 수난 and Sino-Vietnamese thụ nạn.
