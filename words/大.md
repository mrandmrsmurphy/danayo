## Notes
- [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) "big; great"

大 is one of the oldest and most basic adjectives in the sinographic sphere, a pictograph of a person (人) with arms outstretched to indicate largeness. It functions as a fully standalone adjective in Chinese — 大 "big," 很大 "very big" — and Dan'a'yo preserves this independent usage, which is why the character also stands alone as a word in this vault.

Across the CJKV sphere the standalone/compound split varies sharply. Mandarin and Cantonese use the Sinitic morpheme directly as the everyday adjective (dà, daai6). Japanese and Korean instead use native adjectives for the standalone sense — おおきい (大きい) and 크다 — reserving the Sino-readings for bound compounds: 大学 (だいがく, university), 大小 (だいしょう, size), 대학 (大學, university), 대통령 (大統領, president). The `japanese` field therefore holds the character's on'yomi (kan-on たい, go-on だい), not a standalone word, per this vault's convention for such morphemes. Vietnamese likewise uses đại chiefly in compounds (đại học "university," vĩ đại "great, grand"), the native adjective being lớn.

The Dan'a'yo reading dai (ㄉㄚㄧ, 대) descends from Middle Chinese 定母 (voiced d) + 泰韻 (ɑiH) — the departing-tone reading that also gives Mandarin dà and Cantonese daai6. `kwin` is true: the Dan'a'yo and Sino-Korean forms are identical (대), both mapping MC 定母 to a plain stop and the -ai final to ㅐ. The character has a second, rarer reading (tài, as in 大王 "great king," archaic) that Dan'a'yo does not preserve for this word.
