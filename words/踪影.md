## Notes
- [踪](../characters/踪.md) "footprints; tracks" + [影](../characters/影.md) "shadow; image" — 踪影 is the stand-in for [踪](../characters/踪.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo (影's own stand-in is instead [[陰影]]).

踪影 literally combines "footprint/trace" with "shadow/image" to mean a trace, sign, or vestige of someone or something's presence — almost always used in a negative or interrogative frame in practice (毫無踪影 "not a trace to be found," 不見踪影 "nowhere to be seen," 找不到踪影 "unable to find any trace"), describing an absence as vividly as a presence would be described.

Japanese そうえい is the direct on'yomi compositional reading (踪 SOU + 影 EI), matching 踪's and 影's own character-page citations exactly, though attested mainly in dictionaries rather than everyday speech (Chinese in origin, and comparatively rare in real Japanese usage). Korean 종영 is likewise a standard compositional Sino-Korean reading, and `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 종영 matches Sino-Korean 종영 exactly. **`vietnamese` intentionally left blank**: Vietnamese doesn't calque this exact compound — the equivalent concept is expressed instead by 踪迹/蹤跡 ("tung tích," lit. "trace-footprint," using a different second character), and no attested "tung ảnh" exists for this word (that string is an unrelated native Vietnamese phrase meaning "to post a photo online"). No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄛㄫ⼶ㄫ unique across both words and characters).
