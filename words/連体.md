>[!caution] The senses split — Japanese grammar term against Chinese "conjoined"
>連体 is the grammatical "attributive, adnominal" in Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. In Chinese 連體 means physically joined in bodies, as conjoined twins, or one-piece, so a Chinese reader will not hear a grammar term. Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).

## Notes
- [連 (char)](../characters/連%20(char).md) "mutual" + [体](../characters/体.md) "body; form"

連体 has two unrelated groups of senses. English Wiktionary glosses Japanese れんたい as "attribution", the grammatical term for a quality attached to a noun, dated from 1834, and as short for 連体形, "attributive form", dated from 1844. It lists 連用 as its counterpart. For Chinese 連體 (an alias, with 连体; Mandarin liántǐ, Cantonese lin4 tai2) it gives, chiefly attributive, "to be joined in bodies; to be conjoined" and, of clothing and the like, "to be one-piece"; the related words 連體嬰 and 連體嬰兒 mean "conjoined twins". It has no Korean entry, so Korean 련체 here is built from the characters. The word is listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md); the senses below carry the Japanese grammatical sense first.

1. (Linguistics) **adnominal; attributive**: the modification of a noun by a preceding element; the domain of adnominal words and forms
2. (Linguistics) **連体形**: the attributive inflectional form of adjectives and verbs, used when the modified noun follows directly
3. (Linguistics) **連体詞**: the class of adnominal words, modifiers that precede nouns but cannot serve as predicates
4. (Chinese) **conjoined; joined in bodies**: physically connected, as in conjoined twins (連体嬰兒)
5. (Chinese) **one-piece**: forming a single continuous garment or structure

The first element is [[連]], and the body is the [[体]]. A noun-class term from the same tradition is [[体言]].
