## Notes
- [肛](characters/肛.md) "anus" + [門](characters/門.md) "gate" — literally "anus-gate."

Mandarin gāngmén and Cantonese gong1 mun4 preserve the sense directly. Japanese こうもん (kōmon) and Korean 항문 (hangmun) are both the standard, everyday medical/anatomical terms for "anus" in their respective languages — note that 肛's own Sino-Korean reading is 항 (hang), diverging from the mechanical Middle-Chinese-derived Dan'a'yo value (강, gang), an irregular but well-attested real reading.

Vietnamese soang môn is the reading directly attested in Hán Nôm dictionary sources for this exact compound (glossed "lỗ đít," "anus"). 肛 itself carries several competing Hán Việt readings (cương, giang, khang, soang, xoang), of which giang, soang, and xoang all independently gloss to "anus" — soang was used here since it's the one the dictionary's own 肛門 compound entry prefers, though giang môn may also see occasional classical/TCM use. The character page's other candidate, dom, is a genuine native Vietnamese doublet for the same body part (lòi dom, "hemorrhoid prolapse") — historically sometimes written with 肛's own glyph in Nôm, but etymologically separate from the Sino-Vietnamese layer. None of this, though, is the term a Vietnamese speaker actually reaches for: the standard everyday word is hậu môn ("back gate"), which corresponds to an entirely different Chinese compound, 後門, not 肛門.
