## Notes
- [梁 (char)](../characters/梁%20(char).md) + [州 (char)](../characters/州%20(char).md) — Liang Province, the eighth of the [[九州]]

梁州 was the southwestern province, which the *Tribute of Yu* bounds as the land south of Mount Hua and to the Black River (華陽黑水惟梁州), covering the rugged terrain of the Sichuan basin and surrounding mountains — roughly modern Sichuan and parts of Shaanxi south of the Qinling range. Its isolation, defined by the great mountain barriers of the Qinling and the gorges of the upper Yangtze, gave it a distinctive character in Chinese geography: fertile within, nearly impenetrable from without. This geography made the Sichuan basin a natural refuge for states and dynasties under pressure, and the kingdom of Shu (蜀) — Liu Bei's state in the Three Kingdoms period — found its base here.

The word names the province 梁州 as listed among the 九州.  Mandarin liángzhōu, Cantonese loeng4 zau1, Japanese りょうしゅう and Vietnamese Lương Châu are the readings of that name, and the Korean field gives the North Korean form 량주 (the South Korean form is 양주).  The Dan'a'yo reading 량줏 differs from it, so `kwin` is false.
