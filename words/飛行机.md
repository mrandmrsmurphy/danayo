## Notes
- [飛](../characters/飛.md) "fly" + [行 (char)](../characters/行%20(char).md) "attend, go to" + [机](../characters/机.md) "machine" — no `#cranberry`: no constituent's own `stand_in` points here. The long form of [[飛机]], using 飛's other reading branch (ㄆㄧ) rather than the primary ㄈㄝㄧ branch — confirmed correct against all three constituents' own citations of this exact word.

飛行機/飞行机 (fēixíngjī) is a real, fuller-form Mandarin word for "airplane," and Cantonese 飛行機 (fei1hang4gei1) likewise, though both languages more commonly use the short form 飛机/飞机 in everyday speech. Japanese ひこうき (hikōki) and Korean 비행기 (bihaenggi) are, by contrast, the standard everyday words in their languages — built on this fuller form rather than the short one.

Sino-Vietnamese phi hành cơ is compositionally straightforward (phi hành, "aviation," as in phi hành gia "astronaut," + cơ, "machine") but is not independently attested as a fixed compound — Vietnamese instead uses the shorter phi cơ or the native máy bay.
