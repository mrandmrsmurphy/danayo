## Notes
- [蟹 (char)](../characters/蟹%20(char).md) "crab" + [爪 (char)](../characters/爪%20(char).md) "claw"

**蟹爪** is an ordinary word for the claw of a crab. As a calligraphy term it is the figurative name of the fourth of the eight principles of the 永字八法, the hook ([[鉤]], classically 趯): the small pincer-like flick at the end of a stroke is imagined as a crab's claw.

Li Puguang (李溥光), writing in the Yuan dynasty in his 永字八法解, gave each of the eight strokes of the 永字八法 a figurative two-character name; 蟹爪 is the hook's name. See [[怪石]] for the full set, and the [[Stroke]] page.

The Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and North Korean forms are the ordinary compound readings of the word. No Vietnamese form is given.
