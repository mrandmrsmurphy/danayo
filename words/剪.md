## Notes
- [剪 (char)](../characters/剪%20(char).md) "scissors"

剪 derives from [[前]] — 前's own character page explains that the 刀 ("knife") component originally added to that character was later reassigned to form this separate character, 剪, while 前 itself kept only the "front, before" sense. 剪 means "scissors" as a noun and "to cut, trim" as a verb: 剪刀/剪子 ("scissors"), 剪髮 ("to cut hair"), 剪紙 ("to cut paper," paper-cutting as a folk art), 剪草 ("to mow grass").

Mandarin jiǎn, Cantonese zin2, and Japanese せん (sen, on'yomi, rare outside compounds) alongside native はさみ (hasami, the everyday word for "scissors") all preserve the sense. Korean 전 (jeon) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 가위 (gawi, the everyday word for "scissors"). Vietnamese tiễn is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in tiễn đao/tiễn tử (剪刀/剪子, "scissors") and tiễn thảo (剪草, "to mow grass"); the character page's other stored candidate, tiện, is unrelated (likely contamination from 便, a different character) and was not used here.

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (jen/전/ㄐㄝㄋ) with [[前]] ("front; before," already perfected — its own callout already cross-links here).
