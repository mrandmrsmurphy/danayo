## Notes
- [三 (char)](../characters/三%20(char).md) "three" + [焦 (char)](../characters/焦%20(char).md) "burned, scorched" — no stand-in relationship applies.

三焦 (the "triple burner" or "triple energizer") is a concept unique to Traditional Chinese Medicine: one of the six fu organs, a functional rather than anatomical entity dividing the torso into upper, middle, and lower zones governing different aspects of metabolism and fluid regulation. Mandarin sānjiāo, Cantonese saam1 ziu1, Korean 삼초, and Sino-Vietnamese tam tiêu are all the standard TCM terms across the sphere. **Fixed a real bug**: `japanese` was written in obsolete historical kana spelling (さんせう) instead of modern さんしょう (焦's own on'yomi SHOU), the same bug pattern found on [[龍王]]. Filled entirely missing `vietnamese: tam tiêu`. Fixed the duplicate `品詞` field.

**Real genuine Dan'a'yo homophone found**: this word shares its exact reading (samjou/삼좃/ㄙㄚㄇㄐㄛㄨ) with [[参照]] ("refer to, cross-reference") purely by phonetic coincidence — added the reciprocal callout on both pages.
