## Notes
- [銑](../characters/銑.md) "mill, pig iron" + [鉄 (char)](../characters/鉄%20(char).md) "iron" — 銑鉄 is the stand-in word for [銑](../characters/銑.md); 銑 is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo discourse.

銑鉄 (pig iron, cast iron) is the crude, high-carbon iron product of a blast furnace — too brittle for most direct structural use, but the essential intermediate refined further into [[鋼鉄]] (steel) or wrought iron. The name traditionally comes from the pig-shaped ingot molds historically used to cast it as it cooled.

Japanese せんてつ and Korean 선철 are both standard compositional Sino-readings. **`vietnamese` intentionally left blank**: no attested Sino-Vietnamese compound for this specific metallurgical term could be confirmed; Vietnamese uses the native gang ("cast iron, pig iron") instead.
