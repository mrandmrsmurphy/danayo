## Notes
- [逃](../characters/逃.md) "escape" + [遁](../characters/遁.md) "evade"
- This word is the viable form of [遁](../characters/遁.md): the character's meaning (to evade) is identical, but 遁 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 逃遁 is its `stand_in` compound.

逃 is to flee, to run from danger, and 遁 is to slip away, withdraw, vanish from sight, as in 遁走 "to slip off" and 隠遁 "to withdraw into seclusion." Together they form a synonym compound, 逃遁, "to escape and evade": getting away from something and not being found.

The Movement page flags this word as an undocumented near-duplicate of [逃亡](逃亡.md) "to flee, be on the run," and the shared core meaning is real. The difference that can be stated honestly is one of emphasis: 逃亡 stresses the state of being a fugitive (running from the law, an enemy or a catastrophe, often for a long stretch), while 逃遁 stresses the getaway itself and the concealment inherent in 遁, the slipping away unseen. It is also close to [逃避](逃避.md) "to avoid, shirk," which is mainly figurative (evading responsibility, reality), whereas 逃遁 is more literal. In practice the two are largely interchangeable, and Dan'a'yo does not force a hard distinction.

The readings are Mandarin táodùn, Cantonese tou4 deon6 and Korean 도둔. The Japanese form is not recorded in this page's frontmatter. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "flee, escape, evade," placed under [Movement](../lexipedia/Movement.md).
