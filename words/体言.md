## Etymology
- [体](../characters/体.md) "group, body, form" + [言 (char)](../characters/言%20(char).md) "say"

## Notes
A traditional Japanese grammatical term (originating from the 体・用 "substance/function" philosophical distinction, used since the Edo-period kokugaku tradition) for "indeclinable word" — nouns and pronouns, as opposed to conjugating 用言 (verbs/adjectives). Confirmed via web search that Korean linguistics borrowed this term directly as 체언 (real, still in active use, alongside native alternatives). Chinese has no native grammatical tradition using this Japanese-originated category, so `mandarin`/`cantonese` are compositional (used, if at all, only when discussing Japanese grammar itself); likewise `vietnamese: thể ngôn` is compositional with no independent Vietnamese linguistic-terminology attestation found. Filled the blank `pos: 名詞` and `cantonese: tai2 jin4`. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 테언 diverges from Sino-Korean 체언 in the first syllable's vowel. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄝㄧ·ㄝㄋ unique among both words and characters).
