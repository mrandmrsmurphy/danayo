## Notes
- [鎬](../characters/鎬.md) "stove" + [京](../characters/京.md) "capital"

鎬京 (Haojing) is the former capital of the Western Zhou dynasty. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 鎬京 (alias 镐京; Mandarin Hàojīng, Cantonese hou6 ging1) as a historical place, a former capital of China under the Zhou dynasty, now within Xi'an, Shaanxi, and, literary, "capital city (of a country)". English Wikipedia says Hao, or Haojing, also called Zongzhou, was one of the two settlements comprising the capital of the Western Zhou (c. 1046 – c. 771 BCE). The other was Feng, or Fengjing (豐京), and the two stood on opposite banks of the Feng River, Feng on the west bank and Hao on the east, together known as Fenghao. King Wu moved his court across the river to Haojing after defeating the Shang. The Japanese こうきょう, Korean 호경 and Vietnamese Cảo Kinh fields are built from the readings of the two characters.

This word is the stand-in that legitimizes the character [鎬](../characters/鎬.md), which is too bounded to stand alone.
