## Notes
- [鎬](../characters/鎬.md) "stove" + [京](../characters/京.md) "capital" — this word is the viable form of [鎬](../characters/鎬.md): the character's primary meaning in Dan'a'yo is carried by its compound. 鎬京 is its `stand_in` compound.

鎬京 (Haojing) was the capital of the Western Zhou dynasty (c. 1046–771 BCE), founded by King Wu of Zhou after his conquest of the Shang, and also known as 宗周 (Zongzhou). It stood on the eastern bank of the Feng River near modern Xi'an, Shaanxi, paired across the river with the earlier settlement 豐京/灃京 (Fengjing) — together the twin-city complex 豐鎬. Its fall, when King You of Zhou was overthrown, marked the end of the Western Zhou period and the start of the Eastern Zhou.

Japanese こうきょう and Korean 호경 are both standard compositional Sino-readings. Vietnamese Cảo Kinh is the real, directly-attested name used in Vietnamese historiography for this same city. No specific HSK level applies to a proper noun. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄚㄨㄍ⼶ㄫ unique across both words and characters).
