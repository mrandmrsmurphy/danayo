## Notes
- [養](../characters/養.md) "nourish; oxygen" + [化](../characters/化.md) "-ification, change" — oxidation.

養化 is oxidation: a chemical reaction in which a substance combines with oxygen ([[養素]]) or loses electrons, as when [[鉄]] rusts. The word is built on 養素, Dan'a'yo's name for oxygen, and its opposite is [[還元]] (reduction). Mandarin's word for the same thing is 氧化 (yǎnghuà), written with 氧, the Chinese element character for oxygen; Dan'a'yo avoids that character, as it does for every element, and writes 養, so Mandarin's reading carries over exactly. Japanese and Korean instead write 酸化 (さんか, 산화), from 酸素 "acid element," their name for oxygen; but in Mandarin 酸化 means "to acidify," so Dan'a'yo keeps the two apart and does not use 酸化 for oxidation. Cantonese joeng5 faa3 is the reading of 氧化.
