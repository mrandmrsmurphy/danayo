## Notes
- [目 (char)](../characters/目%20(char).md) "eye" + [宿](../characters/宿.md) "lodging" — used here purely for their sound, not their meaning; 目宿 is a Han-dynasty transliteration variant of the specialized botanical spelling 苜蓿 (using dedicated grass-radical characters), both representing a foreign borrowing rather than a native Chinese coinage.

Alfalfa is not native to China; it was reportedly introduced during the Han dynasty by the diplomat Zhang Qian, who brought it back from Dayuan (Ferghana, in Central Asia) around 126 BC — originally as horse fodder for the prized "heavenly horses" of the region. The name is generally traced to a Persian or Central Asian source, transliterated with whatever characters approximated the sound at the time; 目宿 is an early/variant form later standardized to 苜蓿 once dedicated botanical characters were coined.

Mandarin mùxu (also read mùsù in more classical/literary contexts), Cantonese muk6 suk1, Japanese もくしゅく (mokushuku, alongside the modern loanword アルファルファ), Korean 목숙 (moksuk), and Vietnamese mục túc are all descended from the same transliteration, still legible as a unit even though 目 and 宿 individually mean "eye" and "lodging."
