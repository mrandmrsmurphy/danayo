## Notes
- [性](../characters/性.md) "nature; quality; gender" — a bound character; －性 is its fully productive suffix form.

－性 means "the nature or quality of being X", the abstract noun built on a property: English "-ness", "-ity", "-ic character". Attached to a stative or noun, it names the inherent quality: 可能性 "possibility" (the nature of being possible), 重要性 "importance", 必要性 "necessity", 酸性 "acidity". It is the regular way to turn a quality into a thing that can be topic or comment, and it is read compositionally, so any transparent *X + 性* is valid Dan'a'yo without its own page. A derivative gets a page only when its meaning is lexicalized.

Several already-enumerated words carry 性 and stay valid. [[性質]] "nature, character, disposition" is the closest to the bare noun, and [[磁性]] "magnetism" and [[知性]] "intelligence" are plain －性 derivations. The gender cluster is lexicalized rather than productive: [[男性]], [[女性]], [[陰性]], [[陽性]], [[雌性]], with [[性別]] "sex, gender" as the stand-in word that legitimizes the bare character, and [[跨性別]] and [[同性愛]] built on it. [[率性]] "following one's nature" preserves the classical "innate nature" sense (the *Zhongyong* opens with 天命之謂性, "what Heaven confers is called the nature"). Dan'a'yo's own part-of-speech term [[性詞]] "stative" uses 性 in this same sense of "quality", not as the suffix.

The suffix is productive across the written languages. Mandarin 性 (xìng), Cantonese sing3, Japanese せい (SEI; ショウ in older words like 本性 honshō), and Korean 성 (seong) all form abstract nouns this way (可能性: kěnéngxìng, kanōsei, ganeungseong). Vietnamese tính (Hán-Việt for 性) is also fully productive but is placed *before* its base as a nominalizer (tính chính xác, "accuracy"; tính cần thiết, "necessity"), not after it. With all five sources attesting the pattern, －性 clears the productivity bar.

－性 is semantically adjacent to semi-productive 的, the adjectival suffix. 的 is held back by its overlap with Dan'a'yo's genitive particle 之 (ti), but －性 has no such conflict and so stays fully productive: 可能性 is "possibility", while a hypothetical 可能的 would be an adjectival form competing with grammar already doing that work.

Dan'a'yo 싱 (sing) and Korean 성 (seong) differ, so `kwin` is false; the pronunciation is the same as [[成]] "become" (ㄙㄧㄫ), cross-referenced in the callout above. As with other homophones, position keeps them apart: －性 is a bound suffix and never stands alone.

The page filename uses the fullwidth hyphen `－` (U+FF0D) to mark a word that cannot stand alone but is fully productive; the ASCII form `-性` is kept as an alias.
