## Notes
- [九 (char)](../characters/九%20(char).md) "nine" + [州 (char)](../characters/州%20(char).md) "province, region" — a genuine two-referent word, not a single concept with two names.

The primary classical sense is the ancient Chinese division of the realm into nine legendary provinces, attributed to the flood-taming labors of Yu the Great — 九州 became a poetic byword for "the whole civilized world, all under heaven" (still alive in Vietnamese Cửu Châu in exactly this classical/literary sense). Separately, and by direct analogy to the same "nine provinces" concept, Japan's own historical division into nine ancient provinces gave the southern island its name, 九州/Kyūshū, one of Japan's four main islands — `korean` reflects the general compositional reading 구주 (matching Mandarin/Cantonese's own compositional pattern), while 규슈 is the distinct Korean phonetic transliteration specifically used when referring to the Japanese island by its Japanese name, worth flagging even though not stored as the primary field value here. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 큐줏 diverges from Sino-Korean 구주 in both syllables. No homophones (注音 ㄎ⼜ㄐㄨㄛ unique among both words and characters).
