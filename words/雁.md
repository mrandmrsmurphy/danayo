## Notes
- [雁 (char)](../characters/雁%20(char).md) "wild goose" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; standalone form, 雁 is a 名専字 in Dan'a'yo, used chiefly in proper names and classical poetry.

雁 names the wild goose, a recurring image in classical Chinese poetry (associated with autumn migration, distant travel, and letters — wild geese were traditionally believed to carry messages) and in the chengyu [[沈魚落雁]] ("so beautiful that fish sink and geese fall from the sky," a stock phrase for great beauty). 雁 itself also directly supplies the phonetic component of [[贋]] ("counterfeit"), one of this word's own two homophones — a genuine etymological link, not just a coincidental shared Dan'a'yo reading.

雁 shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (an/안/ㄚㄋ) with two other words, [[岸]] ("bank, shore") and [[贋]] ("counterfeit, fake") — a three-way homophone group cross-referenced on all three pages.

Mandarin yàn, Cantonese ngaan6, and Korean 안 (an) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese かり (kari, already 雁's own stored `japanese_native` value) is the everyday native reading. Vietnamese nhạn is the standard Hán Việt reading, one of two candidates stored on 雁's own page (alongside nhẹn), and the same reading independently verified as correct for its homophone [[贋]]. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 안 matches Sino-Korean 안 exactly.
