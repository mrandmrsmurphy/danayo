## Notes
- [広](../characters/広%20(char).md) "wide" + [南](../characters/南.md) "south" — no stand-in relationship applies (広's own is the single-character word [[広]]; 南's own is [[南方]]). The traditional form 廣南 is kept as an alias because 広 is the parent form on its character page.

This name is a collision between a Chinese place and a Vietnamese one, so the Chinese senses come first.

1. Guangnan, a county of the Wenshan Zhuang and Miao Autonomous Prefecture in southeastern Yunnan, China. English Wiktionary also records a historical sense: a short-lived early Song circuit on the territory of the former Southern Han, split into Guangnan East and Guangnan West circuits in 997.
2. Quảng Nam, a coastal province of central Vietnam, the seat of the old trading port [[会安]] and of the Mỹ Sơn ruins. The province was abolished in the 2025 reorganization and merged into Đà Nẵng (the abolition is stated at English Wiktionary; the merger is from memory, not re-checked). Historically 廣南 was also the name of the southern realm ruled by the Nguyễn lords, called Đàng Trong in Vietnamese ("Cochinchina" in European sources) (from memory, not re-checked).

The characters are "wide south": 廣南 is "the broad southern territory", an expansion name given by Vietnamese rulers as their power spread south (from memory, not re-checked). The `vietnamese` field holds the Hán-Việt reading of the Vietnamese sense, Quảng Nam (quảng 廣, nam 南), which English Wiktionary's Vietnamese section lists next to the variant Quảng Nôm. Japanese こうなん and Korean 광남 are compositional readings; Japanese does not normally use 広南 for either place, and Korean prefers 꽝남 for the Vietnamese province (from memory, not re-checked).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 廣南 as a Chinese proper noun with the four senses above (Guangnan County; Quảng Nam province; Đàng Trong; the Song circuit) and a Vietnamese Han-form entry for Quảng Nam; Chinese Wiktionary has a 廣南 page that was not read in full. Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no entry. No homophones (注音 ㄍ⺢ㄫㄋㄚㄇ unique across both words and characters).
