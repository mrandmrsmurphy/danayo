## Notes
- [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "gold" + [門](../characters/門.md) "gate" — "golden gate"; no stand-in relationship applies (金 is its own stand-in; 門's is [[大門]]).

金門 is Kinmen, which English Wiktionary lists as a proper noun with two senses: the county (Kinmen County, administered by Taiwan) and its largest island. The spelling gives the English names Kinmen, Chinmen, Quemoy (the last from the Hokkien reading Kim-mn̂g, borrowed through Spanish and English). English Wiktionary has no etymology for the name. The islands lie just off the coast of Fujian, and Japanese Wikipedia notes that Kinmen is administratively part of Fujian Province in the constitution of the Republic of China, not a part of Taiwan island; Korean Wikipedia gives the Sino-Korean reading 금문현 beside the standard 진먼현. The islet 檳榔屿 (Binlang Islet) belongs to Kinmen County, in Lieyu Township.

Mandarin Jīnmén and Cantonese gam1 mun4 are as at English Wiktionary. The Japanese reading きんもん is the standard one, but I did not verify it on a dictionary page. Korean 진먼 is the standard Korean spelling of the Mandarin name, as at Korean Wikipedia, so `kwin` is false. Vietnamese Kim Môn is compositional (金 kim, 門 môn, each as its page gives it) and is the Hán-Việt reading.

Dan'a'yo keeps the shared CJKV sense: a place name, placed on the Geography page under 台湾 and listed on China for the Fujian coast. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄧㄇㄇㄛㄋ unique as a whole word across words and characters; 今晩安 begins with the same syllables, but its 注音 ㄍㄧㄇㄇㄛㄋ·ㄚㄋ continues, so it is a different word). The 注音 needs no syllable-boundary dot.
