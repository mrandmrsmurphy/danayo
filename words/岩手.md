## Notes
- [岩](../characters/岩.md) "rocks" + [手](../characters/手%20(char).md) "hand"

岩手 is Iwate, written "rock hand". A well-known folk etymology ties it to a legend in Morioka in which a demon (oni) was subdued and pressed his hand into a rock, leaving the "rock hand" that gave the place its name (from memory, not re-checked). The Japanese reading いわて is the native reading of 岩 (いわ) and 手 (て).

Iwate is the second largest prefecture in Japan after Hokkaido, in the northeast of Honshu on the Pacific coast, with its capital at Morioka. It is known for the temples of Hiraizumi, wanko soba, the writer Miyazawa Kenji, and the ria coast hit by the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami.

In Dan'a'yo 岩手 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 이와테 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
