## Notes
- [原](../characters/原.md) "original, source" + [理](../characters/理.md) "reason, logic" — literally "original logic," the underlying rule or mechanism behind how something works.

原理 names a principle — specifically the fundamental, underlying rule or mechanism by which something operates (as in a scientific or mathematical principle), distinct from the near-homograph-in-English [[原則]] ("principle" in the policy/rule-of-conduct sense, e.g. sovereignty as a principle of statehood).

Mandarin yuánlǐ, Cantonese jyun4 lei5, Korean 원리 (wolli), and Japanese げんり (genri) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday word for "principle" in its language. Vietnamese nguyên lí (also spelled nguyên lý, both valid orthographic variants) is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 原's nguyên and 理's own lí/lý. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 원리 matches Sino-Korean 원리 exactly. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ⼔ㄋㄌㄧ unique among both words and characters).
