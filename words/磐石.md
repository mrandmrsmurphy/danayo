## Notes
- [磐](../characters/磐.md) "boulder" + [石 (char)](../characters/石%20(char).md) "rock"
- This word is the viable form of [磐](../characters/磐.md): the character's meaning (a great boulder; bedrock) is identical, but 磐 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 磐石 is its `stand_in` compound.
- Japanese reading ばんじゃく uses JAKU, the less common on-yomi of 石 (vs. the expected ばんせき).
- Figuratively: something immovably firm; a solid foundation — the real Mandarin idiom 堅如磐石 ("as solid as a rock") reflects this figurative sense, though it isn't yet its own entry in this vault.

Wiktionary gives Mandarin pánshí as "huge rock", written also 盤石 or 蟠石, and quotes it in the Chinese Union Version of Psalm 18:2 ("my strong rock"). Its derived terms include 安如磐石, 堅如磐石 and 泰山磐石, all expressing firmness. It also records Pánshí as a county-level city in Jilin. Cantonese pun4 sek6 matches. The Japanese entry gives ばんじゃく for both the "huge rock" sense and the figurative "firm thing", as in 磐石の守備 "a firm defense"; Korean 반석 and Vietnamese `bàn thạch` are the matching forms.

In Dan'a'yo the word is placed on the [Nature](../lexipedia/Nature.md) page for the literal boulder or bedrock, with the figurative sense of something immovably firm, as in Japanese and Chinese.
