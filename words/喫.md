## Notes
- [喫 (char)](../characters/喫%20(char).md) "devour; ingest"

喫 is an older, more formal variant of 吃 (its modern replacement in simplified Mandarin usage), meaning "to eat, devour, ingest," and by extension "to endure, suffer" (喫驚, "to be startled, taken aback," literally "to eat a shock"; 喫虧, "to suffer a loss").

Mandarin chī and Cantonese hek3 preserve the sense directly. Japanese キツ/ケキ (KITSU/KEKI, on'yomi, formal/literary register: 喫煙, kitsuen, "smoking") alongside native の(む) (no(mu), "to drink, swallow," here in an extended sense) cover the sense. Korean 끽 (kkik) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 먹다 (meokda, "to eat"). Vietnamese khiết is the Hán Việt reading, glossed "to eat/drink" with a secondary "to accept, endure" sense (khiết khuy 喫虧, "to suffer a loss") — phonologically regular from Middle Chinese kʰ-...-ek, parallel to 潔/khiết, 結/kết. The character page's other stored candidates — khế, khịa, khịt — are Nôm phonetic-loan readings for unrelated native words, not Sino-Vietnamese doublets; ngật belongs etymologically to the separate character 吃 ("to stutter," as in khẩu ngật, "to stammer"), conflated here because 吃 later absorbed 喫's "eat" sense in modern Chinese usage; khè is unattested in any dictionary source checked and looks like noise or a transcription error. None were used in the field.
