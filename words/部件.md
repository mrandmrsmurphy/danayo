## Notes
- [部 (char)](../characters/部%20(char).md) "part" + [件](../characters/件.md) "item, matter" — no stand-in relationship applies (部's own stand-in is the bare word [[部]]; 件's is [[事件]]).

部件 is "component": a part that a larger whole is assembled from. In Dan'a'yo it is the one word for a **component of a character**, the sub-shape that a 会意 or 形声 character is built out of ([[会意]], [[形声]]): the writing-system chapter uses it in 部件一律 "component-wise consistency", 部件同処 "component parity", and "the components of the originals". The literal sense is "part + item": 部 "part" ([[部分]]) and 件 "item, piece".

**Chinese.** Mandarin bùjiàn (Cantonese bou6 gin6) is a general noun for a part or component of a machine or an assembly (synonyms 零件 and 配件), and also the standard term in Chinese character studies and in the national standard for character structure, where 部件 is the stroke-group that a character is analysed into, smaller than a whole character and not necessarily a radical. Dan'a'yo takes this second, character-studies sense as primary.

**Japanese and Korean — the term is not in ordinary use.** Japanese does not normally say 部件: it uses 部品 (*buhin*) for a mechanical part, and for characters it says 部首 "radical" ([[部首]]), 偏 and 旁 for the left and right parts, or 構成要素 "constituent element". The Japanese reading ぶけん (*buken*) is recorded in ja Wiktionary only as a Chinese loan entry, so the `japanese` field holds the compositional on'yomi of 部 + 件, not a living Japanese word. Korean likewise says 부품 for a machine part and 부수 for a radical, and 구성 요소 for a constituent; 부건 is the compositional Sino-Korean reading and I found no real Korean use of the string. (A Korean Wiktionary entry glosses the Chinese word with 부품, but that is the Korean word for the mechanical sense, not a reading of 部件.) Vietnamese uses *bộ phận* for a part and *bộ thủ* for a radical, so no Vietnamese reading is given. Dan'a'yo is therefore borrowing a Chinese specialist term here, and adopting it as the shared word fills a gap that Japanese and Korean cover only by circumlocution.

**Neighbours.** [[部首]] "radical" is one specific kind of component (the one a dictionary indexes by); 部件 is the general class, which also includes phonetic components and parts that are no radical at all. [[字形]] is the shape of the whole character. No homophone exists (注音 ㄅㄛㄨㄍ⼶ㄋ unique among words, chengyu and characters).
