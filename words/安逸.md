## Notes
- [安](../characters/安.md) "peaceful" + [逸 (char)](../characters/逸%20(char).md) "flee, escape" — ease, comfort; can carry a negative shade of complacency (안일하다, "to be complacent"). Attested across Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, and Korean.

安逸 pairs 安 "peaceful, at ease" with 逸, whose sense here is "ease, leisure" (the character also means "to escape" and "to excel"), so the compound names a life of comfort without effort; it is the word behind the warnings against 貪圖安逸, "to crave a comfortable life," and it often carries a faint reproach of softness or complacency, as the Korean 안일하다 does. The Conflict page lists it as "ease, comfort, complacent," and Dan'a'yo keeps that sense, with the complacent shade understood as part of the word. It is related to the existing words [安楽](安楽.md) "comfort, ease," [安心](安心.md) "peace of mind" and [安静](安静.md) "quiet, rest," which lack that note of reproach. The Vietnamese field now reads an dật, from the two characters' own Sino-Vietnamese readings (an for 安, dật for 逸), which was blank before. The readings are Mandarin ānyì, Cantonese on1 jat6, Japanese anitsu (あんいつ) and Korean 안일, and the Dan'a'yo 諺文 안읻 differs from the Korean 안일 only in the final consonant (ㄷ against ㄹ), which the vault's standing rule treats as a match, so `kwin` is true. The 貪圖安逸 example and the remark about the shade of reproach are from memory and have not been checked against a source.
