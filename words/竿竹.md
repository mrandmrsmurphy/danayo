## Notes
- [竿](../characters/竿.md) "bamboo pole" + [竹 (char)](../characters/竹%20(char).md) "bamboo" — 竿's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 竹's own is [[竹]] itself; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 竿 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word. `kwin: true` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: true`.

**Fixed a real bug**: `mandarin` had gānzi, the reading for the colloquial alias 竿子 (already listed as an alias here) rather than the headword 竿竹 itself — corrected to gānzhú (竿's own gān + 竹's own zhú).

竿竹 names a bamboo pole — a length of bamboo used as a rod or staff, for fishing poles, flagpoles, and similar uses.

Mandarin gānzhú, Cantonese gan1 zuk1, Japanese さおだけ (saodake, combining 竿's native reading さお with 竹's native たけ), and Korean 간죽 (ganjuk) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese cần trúc is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form.
