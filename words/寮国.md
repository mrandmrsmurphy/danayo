## Notes
- [寮](../characters/寮.md) "dormitory" + [国](../characters/国.md) "nation" — no stand-in relationship applies (寮's own `stand_in` is [[学寮]], 国's is [[国家]]).

寮国 is Laos, using 寮 purely for its sound rather than its modern "dormitory" sense — Chinese-sphere transcriptions of Laos are built on the endonym *Lao*, and 寮/老 are simply two of the characters historically chosen to render that syllable, with 国/國 "nation" appended the same way as every other country name in this family ([[泰国]], [[美国]], [[英国]]). The alternate Mandarin name 老撾 (Lǎowō, listed here as an alias) uses an entirely different pair of transliterating characters for the same endonym, underscoring that neither is semantically motivated.

**None of the three non-Chinese daughter languages use a compositional Sino-form reading here** — a real and instructive divergence from the rest of this vault's country-name compounds, most of which do carry through as regular Sino-Japanese/Sino-Korean/Sino-Vietnamese readings. Japanese ラオス and Korean 라오스 are both direct phonetic loans of the English/international name "Laos," not りょうこく/료국; Vietnamese nước Lào ("country of Lào") uses the native word *nước* ("country") plus the endonym directly, rather than a Sino-Vietnamese liêu quốc. This is likely a genuine 20th-century-and-later naming layer — Laos as a modern nation-state postdates the older, character-mediated CJK naming conventions that still govern most of this vault's other country words — so the Sino-character compound stayed a Chinese-internal convention while Japan, Korea, and Vietnam each independently adopted the international endonym instead. No homophones (注音 ㄌ⼘ㄨㄍㄛㄎ unique across both words and characters).
