## Notes
- [大](../characters/大%20(char).md) "big" + [坂](../characters/坂.md) "slope"

大坂 is Osaka, written "large slope". The name is first found in the late fifteenth century and was written 大坂 until the early Meiji period, when 大阪 became the official spelling, said to be because the right-hand part of 坂 could be taken as 土 + 反 ("returning to earth"), an ill omen; 大阪 is kept as an alias, because 阪 is itself an alias of the parent character 坂. The reading おおさか is native: 大 (おお) plus 坂 (さか, "slope").

Osaka is the commercial centre of western Japan. Toyotomi Hideyoshi began Osaka Castle in 1583, and in the Edo period the city was the "kitchen of the realm" (the rice-trading capital). The area is known for the Dōtonbori district, for takoyaki and okonomiyaki, and for hosting Expo '70 and Expo 2025. Its prefecture is, with Kyoto, one of the two 府.

In Dan'a'yo 大坂 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a 府, an urban prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 오사카 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
