## Notes
- [腰](../characters/腰.md) "waist" + [帯 (char)](../characters/帯%20(char).md) "sash; belt" — neither character is legitimized by this compound (腰's own stand-in is [[腰部]]; 帯's own stand-in is [[帯]] itself), so no `#cranberry` applies.

腰帯 names the modern Western-style clothing belt specifically — the strap worn around the waist to hold up trousers — distinct from bare [[帯]], whose real cross-linguistic center of gravity is the broader "sash/band/zone" sense (the kimono obi, 熱帯 "the tropics," 地帯 "zone," 携帯 "to carry"). Real Japanese dictionaries record two distinct readings of 腰帯: ようたい, specifically glossed as a leather belt with a metal buckle/fittings (the Western-style belt, matching this word's own sense exactly), and こしおび, a cord used to bind a kimono at the waist — Dan'a'yo's own reading is built from the ようたい sense.

Mandarin yāodài (腰带) and Cantonese jiu1 daai3 (腰帶) are both the ordinary, everyday words for "belt" in those languages — fully compositional, matching 腰's and 帯's own stored citations exactly. Japanese ようたい is likewise compositional and independently attested for the specific Western-belt sense. Korean 요대 is real but now rare in colloquial use — modern spoken Korean overwhelmingly prefers the English loanword 벨트, with native 허리띠 as the more common non-loanword alternative; 요대 still exists in dictionaries as the Sino-Korean compositional form. Vietnamese yêu đái is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading (built from 腰's own yêu and 帯's own đái) rather than an independently attested everyday word — Vietnamese typically uses the native compound thắt lưng for a clothing belt.

This word resolves a real gap on [[Clothing]]: Japanese and Korean both lean on a transliterated English loanword (ベルト/벨트) for the everyday sense of "belt," rather than a native Sino-Xenic compound — so rather than transliterate, or overload the already-drifted [[帯]], Dan'a'yo coins from this real, dictionary-attested compound, the same "ancient forms" dispute-resolution method used for [[妓院]] and [[坊巷]].
