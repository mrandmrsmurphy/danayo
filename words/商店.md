## Notes
- [商](../characters/商.md) "commerce; trade" + [店](../characters/店.md) "shop; establishment" — stand-in for [店](../characters/店.md), which cannot appear independently

商店 is "shop; store" — 商 (commerce; trade; merchant) + 店 (shop; place of business). 商 is traditionally said to derive from the name of the Shang dynasty (商朝), whose merchants were so associated with trade that the dynastic name generalised into the word for commerce itself (a traditional account, not independently checked here); 商人 (merchant) and 商業 (commerce) both carry this origin. 店 (MC t + em, 端母 + 添韻) denotes a shop or stall — a physical place where goods are kept and sold. Together, 商店 names any retail shop or store. The compound is transparent. (An earlier version of this page listed 書商店, 薬商店 and 花商店 as productive examples; those are not real words, since the real forms drop 商 and use 店 as a suffix: 書店, 藥店, 花店.)

Mandarin 商店 (shāngdiàn) is the standard written term; colloquially 店 appears freely as a suffix: 書店 (bookstore), 藥店 (pharmacy), 飯店 (hotel/restaurant). Cantonese 商店 (soeng1 dim3) is the formal term; everyday Cantonese often uses 舖頭 (pou3 tau4). Japanese 商店 (しょうてん, shōten) is the formal term — 商店街 (しょうてんがい, shōtengai, shopping street/arcade) is a fixture of Japanese urban life, naming the covered pedestrian shopping arcades that run through the centres of most Japanese towns and cities. Korean 상점 (商店, sangjeom) is the Sino-Korean term; the native word 가게 (gage) is more common in everyday speech. Vietnamese thương tiệm (Sino-Vietnamese: 商=thương, 店=tiệm) is the formal Sino-Vietnamese form; the everyday term is cửa hàng (literally "goods door").

kwin is false: Dan'a'yo 샹덤 diverges from Korean 상점 in both syllables. 商 reads 샹 (syang/ㄙ⼘ㄫ) in Dan'a'yo but 상 (sang) in Korean — Dan'a'yo preserves a medial element from the MC 陽韻 (ɨɐŋ) final, reflected in the -y- of the ROmaji, while Korean drops it. 店 reads 덤 (dem/ㄉㄝㄇ) in Dan'a'yo but 점 (jeom) in Korean — MC 端 initial (t) before the 添韻 (em, a front vowel final) palatalizes to ㅈ (j) in Sino-Korean, while Dan'a'yo preserves the unpalatalized dental ㄉ (d).

The Buildings page lists 商店 as the plain entry "shop," and the Trade page as "a store, shop," so Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "shop, store," a retail place of business. It is related to the existing word [商業](商業.md) "commerce," the stand-in for the character 商. The `kwin: false` follows the characters, since both 商 and 店 carry `kwin: false` on their character pages. The readings are Mandarin shāngdiàn, Cantonese soeng1 dim3, Japanese shōten (しょうてん) and Sino-Vietnamese thương tiệm. The 商店街 description and the colloquial equivalents are the page's existing account and have not been re-checked here.
