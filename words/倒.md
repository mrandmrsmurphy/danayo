## Notes
- [倒 (char)](../characters/倒%20(char).md) "turn over; take turns"

倒 is 形声: semantic 亻 ("person") + phonetic [[到]]. Its core sense is "to fall over, to overturn, to invert" ([[絆倒]], "to trip and fall"), extending to figurative reversal and, as a grammatical/adverbial marker, "on the contrary, actually" (marking an unexpected or reversed state of affairs in Mandarin). This word's own "take turns" sense derives from the idea of alternation — flipping from one state or position to another and back, as in 倒班 (dàobān, "to work shifts, to rotate shifts").

Mandarin dào, Cantonese dou2, and Japanese とう (tō, on'yomi, largely confined to literary compounds) alongside native たお(れる) (tao(reru), "to fall over, collapse," the everyday verb) all preserve the core "fall/overturn" sense. Korean 도 (do) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 넘어지다 (neomeojida, "to fall over"). Vietnamese đảo is the Hán Việt reading, attested in đảo lộn ("turned upside down, reversed") and đả đảo ("down with...!," a protest slogan literally "strike and overturn").

This word is part of a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read tau/탓/ㄊㄚㄨ): [[島]] ("island") and [[超]] ("transcend"), both still awaiting their own turn in this sweep — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed on each when it comes up.
