## Notes
- [八 (char)](../characters/八%20(char).md) "eight(h)" + [月 (char)](../characters/月%20(char).md) "month" — literally "eighth month."

八月 is the standard Gregorian-calendar name for August, formed the same transparent "number + 月" way as every other month in this vault's calendar vocabulary (compare [[七月]] "July," [[九月]] "September"). It's distinct from [[酉月]] ("rooster month"), the corresponding month of the older lunar/zodiacal calendar, which does not align neatly with August on the solar calendar.

Mandarin bāyuè, Cantonese baat3jut6, and Korean 팔월 (parwol) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese はちがつ (hachigatsu) uses the native kun'yomi count-word はち for "eight" rather than the Sino-Japanese on'yomi, the ordinary pattern for Japanese month names (compare 一月 いちがつ vs. the on'yomi-based numeral itself). Vietnamese tháng tám is a native construction ("month eight"), not a Sino-Vietnamese compound — the everyday way all Vietnamese month names are formed, alongside the character-based readings stored on 八/月 themselves. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 받웓 diverges from Sino-Korean 팔월 in both syllables. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄅㄚㄊ·⼔ㄊ unique among both words and characters).
