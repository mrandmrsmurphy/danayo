## Notes
- [棍](../characters/棍.md) "stick" + [棒](../characters/棒.md) "stick, rod" — both 棍 and 棒's own stand-ins point to this compound (transitivity holds, confirming the `#cranberry` tag: A = B = AB).

棍棒 is "stick, club, bludgeon" — a coordinate compound pairing two near-synonymous single-character words for a wooden implement used as a weapon or tool, neither of which is independently productive enough in Dan'a'yo to stand alone (hence the `#cranberry` tag: both 棍's and 棒's own `stand_in` fields point back to this exact compound, and transitivity holds — A = B = AB). 棍 by itself covers implements like nunchucks (双節棍) in compounds, while 棒 covers rods and bars more generally; together they form the general word for "a stick used to strike."

Mandarin gùnbàng, Japanese こんぼう, and Korean 곤봉 are all standard, fully compositional readings — 곤봉 in particular is the standard Korean term for a police or military baton. Cantonese gwan3 paang5 and Vietnamese côn búng are likewise compositional, matching 棍's and 棒's own stored citations exactly. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄛㄋㄅㄚㄫ unique across both words and characters).
