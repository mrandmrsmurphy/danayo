## Notes
- [型](../characters/型.md) "model, pattern" + [式](../characters/式.md) "style" — this word is the viable form of [型](../characters/型.md): the character's meaning (model; type) is identical, but 型 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 型式 is its `stand_in` compound.

型式 names classification by kind or model rather than by individual identity — the "type" a manufactured item belongs to, most concretely a product's model designation (型式番号, "model number"), but extending to any formal pattern or specification a thing conforms to. It sits closer to industrial/technical register than to the more abstract, everyday-common near-homophone 形式 (also "xíngshì" in Mandarin, using 形 "shape" rather than 型 "mold, model") — a real Mandarin homophone pair between two distinct Dan'a'yo words, since 形's own Dan'a'yo syllable differs from 型's.

Japanese reading かたしき is a mixed kun+on reading (型=かた, 式=しき); the pure on-reading would be けいしき. Korean 형식 and Vietnamese hình thức are both standard compositional Sino-readings — hình thức happens to coincide with the Vietnamese reading of 形式 too, since 形 and 型 share the same Sino-Vietnamese reading (hình) despite being distinct characters. No specific HSK level is confirmed. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 헝식 diverges from Sino-Korean 형식 in 型's syllable (헝 vs 형).
