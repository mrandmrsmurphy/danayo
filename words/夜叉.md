## Notes
- [夜 (char)](../characters/夜%20(char).md) "night" + [叉 (char)](../characters/叉%20(char).md) "fork; prong"

夜叉 is a yaksha: a phonetic transcription of Sanskrit यक्ष (yakṣa). The characters, 夜 "night" and 叉 "fork", are chosen for their sound only. English Wiktionary lists the Chinese word (Mandarin yèchā, Cantonese je6 caa1) as, in Buddhism, "yaksha (a malevolent spirit)", by extension "a ferocious and hideous looking person", and, figurative, humorous and dated, "wife"; its synonym is 藥叉. For Japanese やしゃ it gives "yaksha, a broad class of nature spirits or minor deities who appear in Hindu, Buddhist and Jain mythology, male or female, benevolent or malevolent", in Buddhism followers of Vaiśravaṇa (毘沙門天) and guardians of the north, and figuratively "a demon". For Korean 야차 it gives "yaksha; or the indigenous Korean dokkaebi" (synonym 도깨비), and Vietnamese dạ-xoa is "yaksha", a two-syllable phonetic loan.

English Wikipedia describes the yakshas as a broad class of nature spirits in Hindu mythology, associated with water, trees, forests, wilderness, treasure and fertility, usually benevolent and sometimes mischievous, appearing in Hindu, Jain and Buddhist texts and as guardian figures on temples. It gives them a dual character: an inoffensive spirit of woods and mountains, or a darker kind that haunts the wilderness and devours travellers. Kubera is their overlord and the owner of the treasures of the world. The Chinese sense of a ferocious spirit is therefore one side of a figure that is more mixed in its Indian sources.
