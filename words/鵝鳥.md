## Notes
- [鵝](../characters/鵝.md) "goose" + [鳥 (char)](../characters/鳥%20(char).md) "bird" — legitimizing note: 鵝's own `stand_in` is [[鵝鳥]] itself, already annotated in its own citation; 鳥's own `stand_in` is 鳥 itself, so transitivity fails — no `#cranberry`.

Mandarin éniǎo and Cantonese ngo4niu5 keep the compositional Sino reading (though bare 鵝/鹅 alone is also common in everyday speech). Japanese がちょう and Korean 거위 are both native/everyday words for "goose" rather than Sino readings. Sino-Vietnamese nga điểu is the compositional form; everyday Vietnamese instead uses the native word ngỗng.
