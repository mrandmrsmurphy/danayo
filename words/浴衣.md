## Notes
- [浴](../characters/浴.md) "bathe" + [衣](../characters/衣.md) "clothes" — yukata, a light unlined cotton kimono originally worn as a bathrobe. Mandarin (yùyī) and Cantonese (juk6 ji1) both read these characters in their own pronunciation to refer to the garment, the same naturalization pattern as 寿司/抹茶. Korean and Vietnamese instead phonetically transliterate the Japanese word itself (유카타, "áo yukata") rather than reading the hanja, so those fields are omitted from the frontmatter, as the word checklist requires for properties with no value.

The `japanese` field now holds the Sino-Japanese reading よくい (浴 YOKU + 衣 I), following the vault's convention for these fields; the everyday Japanese word for the garment is the native-reading ゆかた (a jukujikun spelling of 浴衣), which is the form the Notes above mean. With no Korean field to compare, `kwin` is false. The Clothing page lists the word as "a yukata, light cotton kimono," and Dan'a'yo keeps that sense. It is related to the existing words [浴室](浴室.md) "bathroom" and [浴槽](浴槽.md) "bathtub," which share 浴 "bathe," and to the general [衣服](衣服.md) "clothes." The 寿司 and 抹茶 comparison in the Notes is the page's own and has not been re-checked here.
