## Notes
- [卿 (char)](characters/卿%20(char).md) "noble; high officer; sir; m'lord"

卿 is 会意 (once graphically indistinguishable from 鄉/乡, "village"): a semantic element depicting two people facing each other, plus 皀 ("a food vessel on a stand") — people engaged in ritual feasting together. From this shared-ceremony image, 卿 came to denote a high-ranking court official or noble ([[九卿]], "the Nine Ministers," a set of top imperial offices), and by extension a respectful or affectionate term of address ("sir, my lord," historically also used by a ruler addressing a favored subject, or between spouses as an endearment).

Mandarin qīng, Cantonese hing1, and Japanese きょう/けい (kyō/kei, on'yomi, largely confined to historical/formal contexts) alongside native きみ (kimi, "you, lord" — an archaic/poetic term of address) all preserve the sense. Korean 경 (gyeong) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 벼슬 (byeoseul, "an official post, rank"). Vietnamese khanh is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in ái khanh (a monarch's term of endearment for a favored subject or consort) and khanh tướng ("high ministers and generals").

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (kyeng/켱/ㄎ⼶ㄫ) with another word still awaiting its own turn in this sweep, [[慶]] ("congratulate") — the reciprocal half of this callout will be completed when it comes up.
