## Notes
- [瀑](characters/瀑.md) "waterfall" + [布](characters/布.md) "linen" — stand-in for [[瀑]], which cannot appear independently. The image is poetic: water falling like a length of draped cloth.

**Content corrected**: `mandarin` was stored as the malformed string `"pùbù, bàobù"`. 瀑 is polyphonic (pù, in 瀑布; bào, only in the separate "heavy rain" sense and the place name 瀑河), but 瀑布 itself is read exclusively pùbù in every dictionary source checked — corrected by dropping `bàobù` entirely rather than keeping it as a spurious alternate.

Mandarin pùbù and Cantonese buk6 bou3 preserve the sense directly. Japanese ばくふ (bakufu) is standard vocabulary, though the everyday native word たき (taki, "waterfall") is far more common in ordinary speech. Korean 폭포 (pokpo) parallels the Sino-xenic reading and is the standard, everyday Korean word for "waterfall." Vietnamese bộc bố is directly attested in Hán Nôm dictionary sources ("dòng nước trên cao đổ xuống, như tấm vải vắt," "water pouring down from a height, like a length of draped cloth" — matching the same poetic image as the Chinese characters themselves), most famously encountered via Lý Bạch's poem title Vọng Lư Sơn Bộc Bố (望廬山瀑布, "Viewing the Waterfall at Mount Lu"). It is a classical/literary term, however; everyday Vietnamese uses the native thác or thác nước for "waterfall."
