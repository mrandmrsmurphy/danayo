## Notes
- [秋](../characters/秋%20(char).md) "autumn, fall" — this word is the stand-in for its own character; 秋 stands alone as a word in Dan'a'yo.

秋 names autumn, the harvest season — its 会意 etymology combines 禾 ("grain") and 火 ("fire"), picturing grain parched by fire at harvest time, or the burning of stubble after reaping. It is highly productive: 秋分 ("autumn equinox"), 中秋節 ("Mid-Autumn Festival"), 春秋 ("spring and autumn," also the name of the Spring and Autumn historical period and chronicle).

Mandarin qiū, Cantonese cau1, and Korean 추 (chu) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese uses the native kun-reading あき (aki) rather than the on-reading しゅう (shū) for the bare character alone — the on-reading instead surfaces in compounds like 秋分 (しゅうぶん). Vietnamese thu is the standard Sino-Vietnamese reading, extremely common (mùa thu, "autumn season," using the native word mùa for "season").
