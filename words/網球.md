## Notes
- [網 (char)](../characters/網%20(char).md) "netting, net, network" + [球 (char)](../characters/球%20(char).md) "sphere, ball" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (網's is [[網]] itself, 球's is [[球]] itself), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 球 is individually `kwin: false`.

網球 names tennis — literally "net ball." Mandarin wǎngqiú and Cantonese mong5 kau4 are standard, fully compositional readings, and 網球 is itself the common attested word for "tennis" in both. Japanese and Korean instead virtually always use the English loanword — テニス (tenisu) and 테니스 (tenniseu) respectively — rather than a Sino-Japanese/Sino-Korean compound reading, a genuine real-world pattern (unlike most compounds in this vault, tennis entered the Sinosphere too recently and too thoroughly via the loanword route for a native compositional reading to have taken hold in Japanese or Korean). Vietnamese quần vợt is likewise a native calque ("trousers" + "racket," reflecting the sport's clothing and equipment) rather than a Sino-Vietnamese compound. No Dan'a'yo homophones.
