## Notes
- [脂](../characters/脂.md) "fat" + [肪](../characters/肪%20(char).md) "fat, obese"

脂肪 is a near-synonym doubling: both components already mean "fat" on their own, combined into the standard clinical/biological compound for animal fat or adipose tissue. 脂 (OC \*kji, phonetic [[旨]] under the flesh radical 月) denotes fat in its more concrete, substance sense — grease, lard, the fatty layer itself (脂膏 "grease; fat"). 肪, by contrast, is a rare, largely bound character (`stand_in: 肪` — it does have a standalone word page, but with very narrow use) built the same way, phonetic [[方 (char)|方]] under 肉/月, whose core sense is "fat, obese" as a bodily condition; 脂肪 is by far its most common appearance in the vocabulary.

Mandarin zhīfáng, Cantonese zi1 fong1, Japanese しぼう (shibou), and Korean 지방 (jibang) are all the ordinary, everyday clinical/general term for "fat" (dietary or bodily) in their respective languages. Sino-Vietnamese chi phương is dictionary-attested (hvdic: "lớp mỡ ở dưới lớp da động vật," the layer of fat beneath an animal's skin) but genuinely rare in everyday use — ordinary Vietnamese instead reaches for the native word mỡ (as in mỡ động vật, "animal fat") in almost all registers, medical included.
