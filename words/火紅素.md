## Notes
- [火 (char)](../characters/火%20(char).md) "fire" + [紅 (char)](../characters/紅%20(char).md) "red" + [素](../characters/素.md) "element" — a descriptive coinage for strontium, replacing the real Chinese chemistry character 锶 (sī) with a name built from strontium's most famous visible property rather than a transliteration.

Strontium is most readily identified in a laboratory or a fireworks display by its flame test, which burns a brilliant, saturated crimson red — the exact hue used in emergency flares and firework formulations. 火紅 ("fire-red") names this property directly, deliberately contrasted with [[茜素]] (rubidium), whose own name draws on a biological/botanical red rather than a flame color: 火紅素 is specifically "the red of the flame," a visual descriptor immediately intuitive to anyone who has seen strontium's signature pyrotechnic use, rather than the more common practice elsewhere in this vault's periodic-table series of transliterating a place name or scientist's surname. The real English name, by contrast, is purely geographic — Strontian, the Scottish village where the element was first isolated from a lead mine — making 火紅素 a genuine etymological departure from the source name: phenomenological (what the element visibly does) rather than toponymic (where it was found), in keeping with this vault's broader naming philosophy that favors guessable, descriptive coinages when a transliteration would carry no intuitive content for a Dan'a'yo speaker.

As a periodic-table symbol, 火 alone (rather than the full three-character compound) marks strontium in formulae, continuing this vault's convention of using a single constituent character as the compact chemical symbol — the same role [[石]]/[[月]]/[[日]] play for lithium/selenium/helium elsewhere in this series. Mandarin/Cantonese here are the plain compositional concatenation of the three constituent characters' own readings (huǒhóngsù/fo2 hung4 sou3), not the real element name. Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese instead all borrow the international scientific name directly (ストロンチウム, 스트론튬, Stronti) — the same pattern as every other entry in this series, regardless of whether the Dan'a'yo name itself is a transliteration or, as here, a descriptive coinage. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 화홍소 and Sino-Korean 스트론튬 are unrelated strings by construction, since this word's Dan'a'yo reading is a compositional concatenation while its Korean name is an independent loanword.
