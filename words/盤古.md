## Notes
- [盤 (char)](../characters/盤%20(char).md) "tray" + [古](../characters/古.md) "ancient"

盤古 is the primordial giant of Chinese creation mythology, first recorded in the 3rd-century *Sanwu Liji*. Born from a cosmic egg containing the chaotic, undifferentiated universe, Pangu split the egg upon waking: the light, clear yang rose to become the sky, and the heavy, murky yin sank to become the earth. Pangu stood between them for 18,000 years, growing taller each day and pushing the two apart to keep them from reuniting, until the sky and earth were fixed and stable. Upon Pangu's death, the body transformed into the features of the natural world — breath became wind and clouds, the voice became thunder, the left eye became the sun and the right eye the moon, limbs became mountains, blood became rivers, and body hair became forests and grasses, among other correspondences.

Mandarin Pángǔ, Cantonese pun4 gu2, Japanese ばんこ (Banko), Korean 반고 (Bango), and Vietnamese Bàn Cổ are all fully compositional renderings of the name across the Sinosphere, where the myth is broadly shared.
