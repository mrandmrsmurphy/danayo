## Notes
- [鶏 (char)](../characters/鶏%20(char).md) "chicken, rooster" + [卵](../characters/卵.md) "egg, ovum" — no `#cranberry`: 鶏's own `stand_in` is 鶏 itself, 卵's is [[卵子]]; neither points here.

**Fixed a real bug**: `cantonese` was "ji1 dan4," which matches neither constituent's own reading — corrected to gai1 leon2, matching straightforward concatenation (dan4/蛋 is the real Cantonese word for "egg," a different lexeme entirely). `vietnamese` had an odd embedded parenthetical, "trứng (thức ăn)" — replaced with the cleaner, real everyday term trứng gà, which reverses the Sino word order (native noun-then-modifier, "egg-chicken"), joining the vault's documented set of Vietnamese word-order-reversal cases.

Mandarin jīdàn, Japanese けいらん, and Korean 계란 are all standard, fully compositional readings.
