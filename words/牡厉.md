## Notes
- [牡](../characters/牡.md) "male" + [厉 (char)](../characters/厉%20(char).md) "whetstone" — here purely a sound-loan for 蛎/蠣 ("oyster"), the other of the two unrelated 借代字 senses documented on 厉's own page (the other being "leprosy," in [[厉病]]) — literally "male-oyster," standing in for 牡蛎/牡蠣.

牡厉/牡蠣 names the oyster. Per hvdic.thivien.net, 蠣 itself is glossed as "also called 牡蠣" — i.e. the two-character compound is in effect the standard citation form for this creature in classical sources, not merely "male" + a free-standing "oyster" root; the shell is separately termed 蠣房 and the meat 蠣黃.

Mandarin mǔlì, Cantonese maau5 lai6, and Korean 모려 (moryeo) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese keeps two genuinely distinct attested readings: かき/カキ (kaki) is the everyday kun'yomi, used in ordinary speech and menus, while ぼれい (borei) is the more formal on'yomi, seen especially in traditional-medicine contexts (roasted oyster shell as a TCM ingredient) — kept as a real two-item list rather than collapsed to one. **Corrected 2026-09-22**: the `vietnamese` field previously stored hàu (also spelled hào), the native/regional Vietnamese word for oyster (per hvdic, used "in southern regions") — corrected to mẫu lệ, the actual attested Sino-Vietnamese compound. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 못러 diverges from Sino-Korean 모려 in both syllables. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄇㄛㄨㄌㄝ unique among both words and characters).
