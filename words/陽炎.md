## Notes
- [陽 (char)](../characters/陽%20(char).md) "shine, Yang" + [炎](../characters/炎.md) "flame"

陽炎 means the shimmering optical distortion caused by rising heat over sun-warmed ground — a heat haze or mirage. Japanese 陽炎 has three attested readings: かぎろい, an archaic/classical form found in the Man'yōshū (notably Kakinomoto no Hitomaro's poetry); the everyday modern reading かげろう; and the compositional on'yomi ようえん. Korean 양염 (yangyeom) and Sino-Vietnamese dương viêm are both real Sino-forms, though everyday speech in each language favors a native word instead (아지랑이 in Korean). In Chinese Buddhist philosophy the same image (written 陽焰, "mirage") is a stock metaphor for illusion and the emptiness of phenomena.
