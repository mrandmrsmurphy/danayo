## Notes
- [穹](../characters/穹.md) "high; sky" + [隆 (char)](../characters/隆%20(char).md) "prosperous; abundant", standing in as a 代用字 for 窿 (an alias of 隆), with its narrowed sense "an arched cavity, a vault"
- 穹隆 is the stand-in word for [穹](../characters/穹.md) ("high; sky"), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

穹隆 (written 穹窿 in the source languages, kept as an alias) names a vaulted, arching shape — a dome, a curved ceiling, the bowl of the sky itself. The compound doubles down on a single image: 穹 already names the high vault of heaven, and 窿/隆 (an arch, a rise, a swelling prominence) reinforces it from the architectural side. The result describes both literal domed structures (穹窿 天井, vaulted ceilings; the fornix in neuroanatomy) and, by extension, the heavens conceived as a great dome arching overhead — a usage shared with its near-synonym 穹蒼. Korean preserves the same image directly: 궁륭 (gungnyung) names an arched ceiling or roof, "shaped like a drawn bow or a rainbow, high in the middle and curving down at the ends." Japanese きゅうりゅう (kyūryū) keeps the celestial sense alongside the architectural one — the vast sky, an arch-like shape, a domed ceiling — three readings of the same underlying curve.

The sky sense is carried by its near-synonym [穹蒼](穹蒼.md) "the vault of heaven," and the architectural and general-shape sense is the one the Shape page records, "a dome." That is the sense Dan'a'yo keeps: 穹隆 as a vaulted, domed form, with the celestial dome as the shared figurative use, and no divergence from the shared sense. The Korean form 궁륭 is the North Korean spelling (the 륭 keeps its initial), and the alias 穹窿 is the usual Chinese spelling, with 窿 as the proper "vaulted" character that 隆 stands in for here.
