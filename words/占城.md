## Notes
- [占 (char)](../characters/占%20(char).md) "occupy" + [城](../characters/城.md) "castle; wall" — no stand-in relationship applies (占's own is the single-character word [[占]]; 城's own is [[城郭]]). The characters are literally "occupied city" or "divined city", but they transliterate a foreign name and are not read for meaning.

Champa, the kingdom or kingdoms of the Cham people on the coast of what is now central and southern Vietnam (English Wiktionary: "a former kingdom in southern Vietnam", historical). The name 占城 is the Chinese rendering of Sanskrit Campapura and Campanagara, "the city of Campā", which appear in inscriptions from the 7th century (Japanese Wikipedia). Chinese sources used older names, 林邑 (Linyi) and then 環王 (Huanwang), until about the late 9th century; Chinese Wikipedia says the name 占城 was used for the sixth to fifteenth dynasties of Champa and was taken from a Tang record of the year 877, "Champa sent three tame elephants". English Wiktionary lists 環王 and the other transliteration 占婆 (Zhānpó) as related terms; the Cham call their homeland Nagar Cam. Champa was conquered in stages by Đại Việt: Vijaya fell in 1471 (from memory, not re-checked), and what remained was reorganized as a Nguyễn commandery, ending in 1832 (Japanese Wikipedia says Vietnamese texts call the remnant the Thuận Thành garrison until its direct annexation in 1832). The `vietnamese` field holds Hán-Việt Chiêm Thành (chiêm 占, thành 城), as at hvdic.thivien.net ("the name of an ancient country, whose former land was central Vietnam, later swallowed by Vietnam").

Reading of 占. The character page for 占 gives Mandarin zhàn ("occupy"), and the character also has the reading zhān ("divine"). English Wiktionary gives the name as Zhānchéng, with Zhànchéng as a variant, so the `mandarin` field holds Zhānchéng. The Dan'a'yo sound is set by the character page, which does not distinguish the two Mandarin readings, so the word is ㄐㄝㄇㄙㄧㄫ either way. Cantonese zim1 sing4 is as given at English Wiktionary; Japanese せんじょう is as given at Japanese Wikipedia; the Korean value 점성 is built from the character pages (the usual Korean name of the kingdom today is 참파, from memory, not re-checked).

Derived term (not created in this vault): 占城稻, Champa rice, the early-ripening, drought-resistant rice brought to China from Champa under the Song (the origin is from memory, not re-checked).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11. English Wiktionary: 占城 as a Chinese proper noun (historical, Champa) and as a Vietnamese Han form of Chiêm Thành. Chinese Wiktionary: "古國名" with the synonyms 占婆 and 瞻波. Chinese Wikipedia and Japanese Wikipedia both have a page on Champa under this name (Chiêm Thành). Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese Wiktionaries have no 占城 entry. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄝㄇㄙㄧㄫ unique across both words and characters).
