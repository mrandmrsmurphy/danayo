## Notes
- [雑 (char)](../characters/雑%20(char).md) "miscellaneous; mixed; various" + [誌](../characters/誌.md) "journal; written record; to record"

雑誌 is "magazine; periodical" — 雑 (miscellaneous, mixed) + 誌 (a written record, as in 日誌 "diary"). Literally "a record of miscellaneous things": a periodical that gathers varied articles and topics in one publication. Neither constituent's `stand_in` is this word (雑 is its own stand-in; 誌's is [標誌](標誌.md)), so it legitimizes neither character.

Per Wiktionary, the Japanese term is a late-Edo coinage (1867), credited to Yanagawa Shunsan (柳川春三) for his 西洋雑誌 (*Seiyō Zasshi*, 1867) as a rendering of English "magazine"; Wiktionary's Chinese entry separately dates the Chinese sense to 1862, so the direction of borrowing is not settled here. All four languages share the word with the same meaning, and the readings below are each directly attested on Wiktionary (and, for Vietnamese, hvdic):
- Mandarin 雜誌 / 杂志 zázhì (classifiers 本, 份, 期)
- Cantonese 雜誌 zaap6 zi3
- Japanese 雑誌 ざっし (kyūjitai 雜誌); on'yomi for both characters
- Korean 잡지 (japji)
- Vietnamese tạp chí

The alias 雜誌 is the traditional spelling of 雑 (雜), and no separate `words/雜誌.md` exists. Mandarin simplified 杂志 and variant 雜志 are deliberately not aliased: they use 志, which is a separate character with its own word in Dan'a'yo, and the [誌](../characters/誌.md) page rejects treating 志 as an equivalent of 誌.

The 注音 ㄐㄚㄆㄐㄧ is the plain concatenation of 雑 ㄐㄚㄆ and 誌 ㄐㄧ (羅馬字 jab + ji, 諺文 잡 + 지). No syllable-boundary dot is needed: the only other split, ㄐㄚ + ㄆㄐㄧ, is illegal because ㄐ is a stop and cannot be a glide after the onset ㄆ (there are no ㄆㄐ- syllables under `syllables/`), so ㄐㄚㄆ + ㄐㄧ is the sole legal parse. No other word shares this 注音.

Within the vault's media/writing field (see [Art](../lexipedia/Art.md)) it sits beside [日報](日報.md) "newspaper", [画報](画報.md) "picture magazine" and [週刊](週刊.md) "weekly periodical". Dan'a'yo uses the same shared CJKV sense; no divergence is claimed. (Whether 雑誌 also covers a scholarly journal, as in Japanese, was not re-checked against vault sibling words.)
