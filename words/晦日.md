## Notes
- [晦](../characters/晦.md) "dark; last day of lunar month" + [日 (char)](../characters/日%20(char).md) "day"

晦日 names the last day of a lunar month — the night the moon is fully dark, immediately before the new moon marks the next month's [[朔日]] (first day). Together with [[望日]] (the 15th, full moon), these three terms mark the lunar month's structural anchor points: new moon, full moon, dark moon. 大晦日 (ōmisoka), "great 晦日," is the specific and still very much alive Japanese term for New Year's Eve — the last day of the last lunar month of the year, a usage that has outlived the Gregorian calendar switch itself.

Japanese つごもり (tsugomori) is a native reading rather than an on'yomi — from 月籠り (tsuki-gomori, "the moon hiding/withdrawing"), describing the moon's invisibility on this date. Korean 회일 is the standard compositional Sino-Korean reading. Vietnamese hối nhật is likewise standard and attested, part of the same three-term set as sóc nhật (朔日) and vọng nhật (望日). No specific HSK level applies to a term this specialized. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄛㄧㄋㄧㄊ unique across both words and characters).
