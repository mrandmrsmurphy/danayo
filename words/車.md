## Notes
- [車 (char)](../characters/車%20(char).md) "cart, car" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

車 depicts a carriage seen from above, with wheels on both sides and a central shaft — one of the vault's clearer 象形 pictographs. It's highly productive across a wide range of vehicle compounds, from the traditional ([[火車]], "hwacha," a wheeled multiple-rocket-launcher cart) to the fully modern ([[自動車]] "automobile," [[自転車]] "bicycle," [[電車]] "trolley/tram").

Mandarin chē, Cantonese ce1, and Korean 차 (cha) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese しゃ (sha, on'yomi) is productive in compounds, while the native kun'yomi くるま (kuruma, already 車's own stored `japanese_native` value) is the everyday standalone word for "car." Vietnamese has three genuinely distinct attested readings — xa (literary/classical, as in the chess piece "xe" derives from, and formal compounds), xe (the everyday native word for "vehicle"), and xế (a colloquial/informal variant) — kept as three separate list items rather than the single comma-joined string previously stored. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 촤 diverges from Sino-Korean 차 (adding a glide). No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄑ⺢ unique among both words and characters).
