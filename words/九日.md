## Notes
- [九 (char)](../characters/九%20(char).md) "nine" + [日 (char)](../characters/日%20(char).md) "day" — a genuine multi-sense word, not a single concept with several names.

1. **Ninth day of the month.** Japanese ここのか (kokonoka) is the standard, idiomatic native reading — Japanese calendrical day-counting words (ついたち "1st," ふつか "2nd," みっか "3rd," etc., through とおか "10th") use a dedicated native numbering system rather than compositional on'yomi, and ここのか for the 9th is the regular member of that set. Vietnamese mùng chín (colloquial "the 9th") is likewise the standard everyday way to name a date in this range, distinct from the bare cardinal number chín ("nine").
2. **Nine days** — a duration rather than a calendar date, e.g. 九日間 (kokonokakan, "a period of nine days").
3. **(Chinese mythology) the nine suns** shot down by the archer Hou Yi (后羿), leaving only the one sun that scorches the earth today — the same myth referenced on [[中秋節]]'s own page.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 큐닏 diverges from Sino-Korean 구일, since both 九 and 日 independently carry `kwin: false` on their own character pages. No homophones (注音 ㄎ⼜ㄋㄧㄊ unique among both words and characters).
