## Notes
- [陰 (char)](../characters/陰%20(char).md) "Yin, shade" + [茎 (char)](../characters/茎%20(char).md) "stem, stalk"

陰茎 is the standard anatomical term for the penis. Mandarin 陰莖/阴茎 (yīnjīng), Japanese 陰茎 (いんけい), and Korean 음경 (eumgyeong) are all everyday medical terms. Cantonese 陰莖 (jam1ging3) takes a minority reading of 茎 (ging3) distinct from the character's own stored default (hang1) — the same kind of sense-conditioned reading split documented elsewhere in the sweep (cf. [[拓]], [[間或]]).

Sino-Vietnamese âm hành is real but rare, confined mostly to traditional-medicine or academic texts; everyday and modern-medical Vietnamese instead uses the native compound dương vật — literally "yang object," built on 陽 ("yang"), the opposite polarity from this word's own 陰 ("yin"). This makes Vietnamese medical vocabulary genuinely inconsistent with the yin/yang logic that Mandarin, Japanese, and Korean all preserve for this pair of terms (cf. 陰核/âm vật for "clitoris," which does keep 陰).
