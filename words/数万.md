## Notes
- [数](../characters/数.md) "several, a number of" + [万](../characters/万.md) "myriad, ten-thousand" — literally "several ten-thousands."

数万/數萬 is a productive grammatical pattern rather than a fixed lexical item: 数 functions here as a bound "several/a number of" quantifier prefix, the same role it plays elsewhere (数十 "several tens/dozens," 数百 "several hundred," etc. — neither yet its own page), attached to the myriad unit 万 — Chinese, Japanese, and Korean all count large numbers in units of 10,000 (万/萬) rather than 1,000, so 数万 names an imprecise "tens of thousands," bigger than a mere handful but not precisely counted.

Mandarin shù wàn, Cantonese sou3 maan6, Korean 수만 (suman), and Japanese すうまん (sūman) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and this same 数+万 pattern is productive and everyday in all four. **No Vietnamese field**: modern Vietnamese does not use 萬/vạn (ten-thousand) as its base counting unit the way Chinese/Japanese/Korean do — it counts in thousands instead — so 数+万 has no fixed Sino-Vietnamese compound equivalent; the natural Vietnamese phrasing is instead a native quantifier construction like vài vạn ("a few myriads") or hàng vạn ("in the tens of thousands"), not a single lexical item — omitted rather than guessed, per this vault's standing practice for gaps like this (compare [[苦土]]).

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 수몬 diverges from Sino-Korean 수만 in the second syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄙㄨㄇㄛㄋ unique among both words and characters).
