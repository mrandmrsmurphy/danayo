## Notes
- [夫 (char)](../characters/夫%20(char).md) "right?" — stand-in for [[夫 (char)|夫]], which cannot appear independently

夫 as a standalone Dan'a'yo word is the Classical Chinese sentence-final particle (read fú, not the noun-reading fū): an exclamatory/rhetorical confirmation marker best glossed as "right?" or "indeed!" Its locus classicus is the Analects — 子在川上曰:逝者如斯夫,不舍晝夜 ("The Master, standing by the river, said: it passes on like this, never ceasing day or night!") — where 夫 closes the exclamation. A homophonous sentence-*initial* particle 夫 (also fú) instead introduces a topic or a reasoned judgment, as in 夫戰,勇氣也 ("Now, warfare is a matter of courage," Zuo Zhuan); Dan'a'yo retains only the final, confirmatory use.

The two uses of 夫 go back to two distinct Middle Chinese readings: the noun "man; husband; laborer" (非母 pju → Mandarin fū, Cantonese fu1, Vietnamese phu) and the particle (奉母 bju → fú, fu4, phù). Dan'a'yo assigns the standalone word the reading fǝ/ㄈㄜ per the character page's own stored fields; the noun sense lives on in compounds — [[夫人]] "lady; wife," [[丈夫]] "man; husband," [[夫婦]] "married couple," [[夫子]] "master; teacher," [[漁夫]] "fisherman" — several of which preserve the older voiced-initial reading (b-), a fossil of the 奉母 layer.

No living CJKV language uses the particle productively; it survives only in classical/literary quotation. Japanese reads it ふ (fu) in kanbun contexts, Korean 부 (bu) in hanmun recitation, and Vietnamese phù in Hán Văn texts — all strictly literary. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 쁘 (fǝ) differs from Korean 부 in both initial and vowel.
