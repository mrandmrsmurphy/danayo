## Notes
- [秋 (char)](../characters/秋%20(char).md) "autumn" + [分 (char)](../characters/分%20(char).md) "divide" — literally "autumn's division," the point at which day and night split evenly.

秋分 is one of the 二十四節氣 (twenty-four solar terms), falling around September 22-24. It's the autumn counterpart to [[春分]] (spring equinox), both using 分 ("division," an even day/night split) in contrast to the solstices [[冬至]]/[[夏至]], which use 至 ("extreme") instead.

Mandarin qiūfēn, Cantonese cau1 fan1, Korean 추분 (chubun), and Japanese しゅうぶん (shūbun) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday term for the autumn equinox in its language. Vietnamese thu phân is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 秋's thu and 分's own phân. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 춧분 diverges from Sino-Korean 추분 in the first syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄑㄨㄛㄅㄨㄋ unique among both words and characters).
