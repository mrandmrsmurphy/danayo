## Notes
- [主](../characters/主.md) "master; host" + [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" — this word is the viable form of [主](../characters/主.md): 主 alone is too bound to stand as a Dan'a'yo word, and 主人 is its `stand_in` compound — legitimizing note: 主's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, so 主 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word.

主人 covers "master, owner, host, proprietor" across the Sinosphere. Mandarin zhǔrén names both the host of an occasion and the owner of a thing or business; 主人公 (protagonist, main character) and 女主人/男主人 (hostess/host) are common collocations. Japanese しゅじん traditionally also serves as a wife's term for her own husband, alongside "master (of a household/shop)" and "host." Korean 주인 parallels Mandarin closely — 주인공 (protagonist), 주인의식 (sense of ownership). Cantonese zyu2 jan4 follows the same pattern. Vietnamese chủ nhân is the standard modern term for "owner, possessor" (chủ nhân của = "the owner of").

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 주닌 differs from Korean 주인 in the second syllable — 人 reads 닌 (nin/ㄋㄧㄋ) in Dan'a'yo but 인 (in) in Sino-Korean, the same MC 日-initial n-/∅ split seen in [[中国人]] and [[中耳]].
