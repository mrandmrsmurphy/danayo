## Notes
- [音](../characters/音.md) "sound" + [楽](../characters/楽.md) "music" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 音 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

Mandarin yīnyuè, Japanese おんがく (ongaku), Korean 음악, and Vietnamese âm nhạc all draw on the **"music" reading** of 楽/樂 — a genuine heteronym, distinct from the character's other well-attested reading meaning "joy, pleasure" (Mandarin lè, Korean 락, Japanese らく/raku, the sense anchored by [[快楽]], 楽's own `stand_in`). Cantonese jam1 ngok6 shows the same split. This is a famous, independently attested pattern across the whole Sinosphere, not a Dan'a'yo-internal quirk: the same graph 樂 carries "yuè" (music), "lè" (joy), and a rarer classical "yào" (to be fond of), each with its own regular Sino-Xenic reflex in every daughter language. `kwin` is false for two independent reasons: 音 alone already diverges from Sino-Korean (Dan'a'yo 움 vs Korean 음, a vowel divergence explained on 音's own character page), and separately, comparing 楽's portion isn't even a clean like-for-like check — Dan'a'yo's own 楽-derived syllable (락) is anchored to the character's "joy" reading, while Korean's real word-specific reading here (악) belongs to the "music" heteronym instead.

As one of the <ruby>[六芸](../words/六芸.md)<rt>ㄌㄨㄎ·ㄝ</rt></ruby> (Six Arts), ancient music had clear ceremonial divisions, the Six Dances:
1. **雲門大卷 (_Yúnmén_)**: from the era of the Yellow Emperor.
2. **大咸 (_Dàxián_)**: from the era of Emperor Yao.
3. **大韶 (_Dàsháo_)**: from the era of Emperor Shun (famously praised by Confucius).
4. **大夏 (_Dàxià_)**: from the era of Yu the Great.
5. **大濩 (_Dàhuò_)**: from the era of Tang of Shang.
6. **大武 (_Dàwǔ_)**: celebrating the founding of the Zhou Dynasty by King Wu.
