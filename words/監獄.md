## Notes
- [監](../characters/監.md) "oversee" + [獄](../characters/獄.md) "prison" — 獄's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 監's own is [[監督]]; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 獄 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word.

監獄 is the standard word for prison or jail across most of the Sinosphere. Mandarin jiānyù, Cantonese gaam1 juk6, Japanese かんごく (kangoku), and Korean 감옥 (gamok) are all fully compositional and used in everyday speech for the institution itself.

Vietnamese giám ngục diverges semantically: the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form has drifted to mean "prison warden" (giám, "to oversee" + ngục, "prison" — i.e. one who oversees the prison) rather than the prison itself, for which everyday Vietnamese prefers the native nhà tù.
