## Notes
- [中 (char)](../characters/中%20(char).md) "middle" + [和](../characters/和.md) "harmony, peace" — no stand-in relationship applies (中 is its own stand-in; 和's is [[和平]]).

中和 is the verb "to neutralize": to bring two opposed things to the middle, so that each cancels the other. It is the chemist's word for an acid and a base meeting in a neutral solution, and it extends to any case where opposing forces are balanced out: finding a happy medium, a compromise. In phonetics it is the neutralization of a contrast, when two sounds that differ in some positions become the same in another; the phonology page uses it of the Vietnamese tendency to collapse the contrasts between syllable-final consonants that Dan'a'yo keeps apart (音節末対立).

The words are the two halves of the classical idea: 中 "the middle, the mean" and 和 "harmony". 中和 is the state the *Doctrine of the Mean* (中庸) tells us to reach: 致中和, "to attain 中和, and heaven and earth will be in their places and all things will flourish", the passage quoted on the page for 喜怒哀楽. In that sense it names equilibrium and harmony, which is also the older literary adjective "just, impartial", and 中庸 is the word for the principle where 中和 is the state it produces.

Wiktionary records Mandarin zhōnghé (Cantonese zung1 wo4) as a verb "to neutralize" (chemistry) and "to find a happy medium; compromise", and a literary adjective "just, impartial"; Japanese ちゅうわ (chūwa) is "neutralization" as a noun and "to neutralize" as a verb; and Vietnamese *trung hòa* is "to neutralize" and "neutral". The phonological sense is the same word used of sounds; Wiktionary does not list it separately. Korean 중화 is the compositional reading of the same characters, and it matches Dan'a'yo's own 중화, so `kwin` is true; it was not separately verified in a Korean dictionary.

The syllable jung-hwa (ㄐㄨㄫㄏ⺢) is shared with [[中華]] "Chinese culture", because 和 and 華 are both hwa; the homophone callout cross-references them, and 中華 is a proper noun, so they are not confused in use.
