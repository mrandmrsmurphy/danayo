## Notes
- [乾 (char)](../characters/乾%20(char).md) + [達 (char)](../characters/達%20(char).md) + [婆](../characters/婆.md) — a phonetic transliteration of Sanskrit गन्धर्व (gandharva), not a compositional Chinese coinage; the three characters were chosen purely for their sound, not their individual meanings ("heavenly," "arrive," "old woman").

乾達婆 (Gandharva) is a class of celestial musician-spirits in Buddhist and Hindu cosmology, one of the eight legions of heavenly beings (天龍八部) who guard the Buddhist dharma; they are traditionally described as living on fragrance alone and serving as the divine musicians in Indra's court. The term entered Chinese Buddhist scripture via Sanskrit transliteration during the Buddhist canon's translation into Chinese, then spread with the rest of the Buddhist textual tradition into Japanese (けんだつば), Korean (건달바), and Vietnamese (Càn Thát Bà, capitalized as the proper name of this class of celestial being). Mandarin qiántàpó and Cantonese kin4 taat3 po4 preserve the same transliteration. The alternate transliteration 乾闥婆 (kept here as an alias) uses a different final character for the same Sanskrit sound.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 견탇바 diverges from Sino-Korean 건달바, since all three of 乾, 達, and 婆 independently carry `kwin: false` on their own character pages.
