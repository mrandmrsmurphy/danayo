## Notes
- [家](../characters/家.md) "house, family" + [蝿](../characters/蝿.md) "fly" — 家蝿 is the stand-in word for [蝿](../characters/蝿.md); 蝿 is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo discourse.

The housefly (*Musca domestica*) is the common fly species most closely associated with human dwellings and food waste worldwide, a synanthropic species that has spread alongside human settlement to every inhabited continent. As a byword it carries mild connotations of filth, pestering, and nuisance across all of Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese culture — a fly landing on food remains a universal small disgust.

Japanese reads いえばえ (iebae), a native compound rather than the Sino-Japanese on-yomi — 家 read as its kun'yomi いえ ("house") rather than け/か, paired with ばえ (the rendaku form of はえ, "fly"). Korean 가승 is the standard compositional Sino-Korean reading. **`vietnamese` intentionally left blank**: Vietnamese doesn't calque this compound at all, using instead the native ruồi nhà ("house-fly," built from native ruồi "fly" + nhà "house" — the same "house + fly" logic as the Chinese/Japanese term, just built from native rather than Sino-Vietnamese morphemes, the same pattern already seen on [[天竺鼠]]). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 가잉 diverges from Sino-Korean 가승 in 蝿's syllable (잉 vs 승). No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄍㄚㄧㄫ unique across both words and characters).
