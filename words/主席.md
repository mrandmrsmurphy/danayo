## Notes
- [主](../characters/主.md) "master, main" + [席](../characters/席.md) "seat" — literally "the main seat," hence "chairman, chairperson," the one who occupies the presiding seat.

主席 names the presiding officer of an assembly, committee, or party, and — in the specific case of 国家主席 (guójiā zhǔxí) — the head of state of the People's Republic of China. Japanese しゅせき and Korean 주석 both carry this same range, from an ordinary meeting's chairperson up to a party or state's top office (북한의 국무위원장/조선로동당 총비서 aside, 주석 was historically North Korea's own head-of-state title as well, held by Kim Il-sung). Vietnamese chủ tịch is likewise both the everyday "chairperson" and the formal head-of-state title (Chủ tịch nước). `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 주석 coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 주석.

`注音 ㄐㄨㄙㄝㄎ`/`주석` is a real, reciprocally documented homophone with [[朱錫]] ("tin") — coincidental, unrelated in meaning.
