## Notes
- [広](../characters/広%20(char).md) "wide" + [州](../characters/州%20(char).md) "state" — "wide prefecture". No stand-in relationship applies (both characters are their own stand-ins).

広州 (Guangzhou) is the capital of Guangdong province in southern China, the city also known in English as Canton (see [広東](広東.md)). Per Wiktionary the name was originally that of a prefecture first established in 226 CE and re-established in 264 CE, covering parts of present-day Guangdong, with its seat originally at Panyu; it has also named later prefectures and various ancient divisions of Guangdong and Guangxi (historical). 廣 is an alias of 広, so the filename uses 広; 廣州 and 广州 are kept as aliases.

English Wiktionary's Chinese entry also lists the South Korean city of Gwangju in Gyeonggi province, and its Korean entry gives 광주 as the hanja form of both Guangzhou and that Gyeonggi city. This is a different place from [光州](光州.md) (Gwangju in the south-west of South Korea), which has its own word and a different Dan'a'yo sound (ㄎ⺢ㄫㄐㄨㄛ, against ㄍ⺢ㄫㄐㄨㄛ here), so the two are not homophones in Dan'a'yo. Japanese こうしゅう is the Sino-Japanese reading (Wiktionary Japanese 広州); the usual Japanese name is also given there as コワンチョウ and カントン (the latter for 広東). Korean 광주 is the North Korean reading of the characters; the usual South Korean name for the Chinese city is 광저우 (listed on Wiktionary). Vietnamese Quảng Châu is the Sino-Vietnamese name. Attestation: the Guangzhou sense is on the English Wiktionary Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese entries. No homophones (注音 ㄍ⺢ㄫㄐㄨㄛ unique across both words and characters).

1. Guangzhou, the city.
2. Gwangju in Gyeonggi province, South Korea (Chinese and Korean Wiktionary).
