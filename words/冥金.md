## Notes
- [[冥王]] "Pluto" (abbreviated to its first character 冥 here) + [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "metal" — literally "Pluto-metal." No cranberry: neither constituent's own `stand_in` points here.
- Avoids the Chinese invented character 鈈/钚, coining a transparent compound instead — the same naming strategy used throughout this vault's periodic table (compare [[苦土素]] for magnesium, [[石灰素]] for calcium).

冥金 names plutonium (Pu, atomic number 94), following the same naming logic as its neighbor on the periodic table, [[海金]] (neptunium, "sea-metal," after Neptune) — both elements are named, in English and in this vault's Dan'a'yo alike, after planets beyond Uranium's own namesake Uranus: Neptune and Pluto respectively. 冥 alone carries the "dark, gloomy, underworld" sense (as in [[冥王]] itself, "King of the Underworld," used for both the Western god Pluto/Hades and, by extension, Yama).

Mandarin bù and Cantonese bou1 are the real, standard readings for plutonium in those languages (of 鈈/钚, the character this vault's compound deliberately avoids in writing while still matching its pronunciation, the same convention already established on [[苦土素]] and [[石灰素]]). Japanese プルトニウム (purutoniumu), Korean 플루토늄 (peulrutonyum), and Vietnamese plutoni are all the real, standard loanword terms (from English "plutonium"), not kanji/hanja calques — Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese chemical nomenclature all draw on Western loanwords for elements discovered in the modern era, unlike Chinese's continued use of coined single characters. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄇㄝㄫㄍㄧㄇ unique among both words and characters).
