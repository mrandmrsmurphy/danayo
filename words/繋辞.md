## Notes
- [繋](../characters/繋.md) "tie, bind, connect" + [辞](../characters/辞.md) "word, expression" — 繋's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, but 辞's own is [[辞職]], so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply; 繋辞 legitimizes 繋 alone. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`. The word that "ties" a subject to its predicate: a copula.

Japanese 繋辞 (keiji) and Korean 계사 (whose hanja is 繫辭, written here with 辞, the alias under which the vault records 辭/繫) name the same grammatical category in the same two characters, even though modern Mandarin linguistics instead favors 系词 (xìcí, built on the unrelated-but-similar-looking root 系 "lineage, system" rather than 繋 "to tie") — mandarin, cantonese, and vietnamese are left blank rather than guessed, since no attested reading of this specific compound exists in those languages. The grammar book uses 繋辞 for the small closed class of Dan'a'yo words that can also function as verbal prefixes; it notes specifically that copulas are the only verbs that supplete for [[否定]] (negation), rather than taking the ordinary negating prefix 不–. Attested across Japanese and Korean.
