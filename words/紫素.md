## Notes
- A novel word coined for the [Periodic Table](lexipedia/Periodic%20Table.md), copying the etymology:
- [紫](../characters/紫.md) "purple, violet" + [素](../characters/素.md) "element"

紫素 is a Dan'a'yo-internal coinage for indium, echoing the element's 1863 discovery by Ferdinand Reich and Hieronymus Theodor Richter via spectroscopy — its signature emission line appeared as a brilliant indigo-blue, giving the element its English name (from "indigo") and motivating this vault's choice of 紫 ("purple, violet") over the real Chinese name 銦/铟 (yīn), which is instead a phono-semantic coinage (metal radical + phonetic 因).

Since 銦/铟 (and by extension this coinage) is a modern chemical-elements neologism rather than a word with deep independent roots in each language, the other CJKV daughters largely converge on international, European-derived borrowings instead: Korean 인듐 (indyum) and Japanese インジウム (injiumu) both transliterate "indium" directly, and Vietnamese indi likewise derives from the European name rather than any Sino-Vietnamese compound. Mandarin yīn and Cantonese jan1 remain the real element's own established readings, since Dan'a'yo borrows the *character* 紫 here purely for its Dan'a'yo-internal sense, not as a claim about how the real element name is pronounced.
