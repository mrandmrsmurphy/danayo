## Notes
- [鬼](../characters/鬼.md) "devil, demon" + [神 (char)](../characters/神%20(char).md) "spirit, god" — legitimizing note: 鬼's own `stand_in` is [[鬼神]] itself, already annotated in its own citation; 神's own `stand_in` is 神 itself, so transitivity fails — no `#cranberry`.

Mandarin guǐshén, Cantonese gwai2san4, Korean 귀신 (also the everyday Korean word for "ghost"), and Sino-Vietnamese quỷ thần ("ghosts and spirits," as in the interjection trời đất quỷ thần ơi) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese has two attested readings: きじん (on'yomi, more formal/technical) and おにがみ (native kun'yomi, more folkloric) — きじん was chosen as the representative form.
