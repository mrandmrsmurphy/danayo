## Notes
- [句 (char)](../characters/句%20(char).md) "phrase, sentence, stanza, paragraph, clause, pericope" + [法 (char)](../characters/法%20(char).md) "rule, law"

句法 names syntax specifically — the rules governing how words and phrases combine into well-formed sentences — distinct from the broader concept of grammar as a whole. Dan'a'yo keeps this narrower term separate from its two existing words for "grammar," [[文法]] and [[語法]], since syntax proper (word order, phrase structure, clause combination) is only one component of a grammar, alongside morphology, phonology, and usage.

Real-world linguistic terminology for "syntax" diverges across the daughter languages in exactly the way that complicates a simple compound-reading approach: Japanese and Korean academic linguistics both standardly use 統語論 (Japanese tōgoron, Korean 통어론), not a direct reading of 句法's own characters, while Mandarin and Vietnamese linguistics do use 句法/cú pháp directly. Two further near-synonyms circulate without their own dedicated word pages here: 構文 (kōbun, "sentence construction/structure") and 統語 (tōgo, "syntax" as a bound form, seen in 統語論 itself) — both refer to closely related aspects of sentence structure without being exact synonyms of 句法.
