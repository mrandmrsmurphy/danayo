## Notes
- [勃](../characters/勃.md) "rise suddenly" + [海](../characters/海.md) "sea, ocean" — 勃 is the Dan'a'yo stand-in glyph for the rarer 渤 (勃 with the water radical added), which is why 渤海 is listed as an alias; the two characters were already read alike in Middle Chinese. No stand-in relationship applies (勃's own stand-in is [[勃興]]; 海's is [[海洋]]).

勃海 names two things.

1. the Bohai Sea, the shallow inner gulf of the [[黄海]] (Yellow Sea) in northeastern China, almost enclosed by the Liaodong and Shandong peninsulas.
2. Balhae, the medieval kingdom of Manchuria, northern Korea and the Russian Far East (698–926, from memory, not re-checked), founded by the former Goguryeo general 大祚榮 (Dae Jo-yeong). It took its name from the title "Prince of Bohai Commandery" granted by the Tang court, and it fell to the Khitan Liao.

The kingdom is claimed differently by the neighbouring traditions. Korean historiography treats Balhae as a successor of [[高句麗]] and calls the era with Unified Silla the "North and South States period". Chinese historiography counts it as a Tang vassal state ruled over a largely Mohe population, and Japanese records show it as a regular diplomatic partner (the Bokkai embassies). Mandarin Bóhǎi, Cantonese but6 hoi2, Japanese ぼっかい, Korean 발해 and Sino-Vietnamese Bột Hải are all standard readings of 渤海 on English Wiktionary, which lists both the sea and the kingdom.
