## Notes
- [吼 (char)](../characters/吼%20(char).md) "roar; howl"

吼 combines [[口]] ("mouth") with phonetic [[孔]]. It means "to roar, bellow, howl" — of fierce beasts (獅子吼, "a lion's roar," a common Buddhist metaphor for the Buddha's teaching, majestic and fear-inspiring like a lion's roar) and, by extension, of an angry person shouting (一聲怒吼, "to roar out in anger").

Mandarin hǒu, Cantonese hau3, and Japanese こう/く (kō/ku, on'yomi, rare in modern use) alongside native ほ(える) (ho(eru), "to roar, howl," the everyday verb, used of animals) all preserve the sense. Korean 후 (hu) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 울부짖다 (ulbujitda, "to wail, howl"). Vietnamese hống is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in sư tử hống (獅子吼, "a lion's roar," the well-known Buddhist metaphor); rống is also listed as an attested reading in the same dictionary sources, and is additionally the everyday native word for "to bellow, roar" — a rare case where the native and classical readings converge; the character page's remaining candidates (hổng, khỏng, khống) look like corpus noise.

This word completes a three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (all read hou/홋/ㄏㄛㄨ): [[侯]] ("marquis") and [[厚]] ("thick"), both already perfected and already cross-linking here from earlier in this sweep.
