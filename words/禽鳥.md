## Notes
- [禽](../characters/禽.md) "fowl" + [鳥 (char)](../characters/鳥%20(char).md) "bird"
- This word is the stand-in for [禽](../characters/禽.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

禽 began as a general word for a captured creature (it is the same word as 擒 "to seize") and came to mean birds and fowl in particular, in the traditional pairing of 飛禽走獸 "the birds that fly and the beasts that run" (and 禽獸 "birds and beasts," also the insult "brute"). 鳥 "bird" in 禽鳥 adds the plain word for the animal and makes the bound 禽 into a free collective noun: birds as a group, fowl.

The word is collective and slightly formal, the sort used in natural-history writing and in compounds such as 家禽 "poultry" and 禽流感 "avian flu," in contrast with the plain [鳥](鳥.md) "a bird" and with [鳥類](鳥類.md) "the class of birds," the zoological term. The readings are Mandarin qínniǎo (alias 禽鸟), Cantonese kam4 niu5, Japanese kinchō (きんちょう) and Korean 금조. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "birds, fowl (collective)," placed under [Animals](../lexipedia/Animals.md).
