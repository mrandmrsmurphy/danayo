## Notes
- [閨](../characters/閨.md) "women's bedroom" + [房 (char)](../characters/房%20(char).md) "room" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes 閨 as a bound character (房's own stand-in is [[房]] itself; transitivity fails, so no `#cranberry`).

閨房 means a woman's bedroom or boudoir, and by extension (especially in Chinese literary usage) a married couple's private bedroom, with connotations of domestic intimacy. Mandarin 閨房/闺房 (guīfáng), Korean 규방 (gyubang), and Sino-Vietnamese khuê phòng are all literary/classical-register words, evoking traditional women's private quarters — 閨房 in particular carries strong associations with 閨秀 ("a lady of good family") and classical poetry about women confined to the inner chambers.

Japanese 閨房 (けいぼう) is likewise a classical, somewhat archaic term rather than everyday vocabulary, glossed as a bedroom (especially a couple's) with an extended sense of marital intimacy — it survives mainly in historical or literary contexts, such as period dramas and classical literature, rather than in casual modern speech.
