## Notes
- [兪 (char)](../characters/兪%20(char).md) "agree to"

兪 (alternate form 俞) is 會意, and functions classically as an interjection of assent — "yes, agreed" — most familiar from its use in the Book of Documents (書經) as the emperor's formal word of approval, preserved in [[兪允]] ("to consent to, to permit," specifically of the emperor). By vault policy, 兪 and its phonetic descendants (愈, 愉, 揄, 諭, 逾, 喩, 蝓, and others) are deliberately given Dan'a'yo readings ending in -m, a departure from strict Middle Chinese derivation made specifically to reduce homophony among this large phonetic family — noted explicitly on the character page itself ("by fiat... to reduce homophony").

Mandarin yú, Cantonese jyu6, and Japanese ゆ (yu, on'yomi, rare outside classical/literary contexts) alongside native しかり (shikari, "indeed, that's right," an archaic affirmative) all preserve the classical assent sense. Korean 유 (yu) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 대답하다 (daedaphada, "to answer, respond"). Vietnamese dũ is the Hán Việt reading.
