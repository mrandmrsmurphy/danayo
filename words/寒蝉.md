## Notes
- [寒](../characters/寒.md) "cold" + [蝉 (char)](../characters/蝉%20(char).md) "cicada" — a cicada chirping faintly in late autumn cold; figuratively, someone who dares not speak out, as in the idiom 噤若寒蟬 ("silent as a cicada in cold weather"). Attested in Mandarin; other languages not yet confirmed.

Wiktionary confirms the Mandarin and Cantonese word, defines it as "a cicada in winter" (and, in the literary register, a small cicada), and gives the figurative sense "one who stays silent," with a Later Han example; it lists 噤若寒蟬 only as a derived term and has no entry for the word in Japanese, Korean or Vietnamese. The Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese fields, which were blank, now hold the compositional Sino-Japanese, Sino-Korean and Sino-Vietnamese readings of the two characters (かんせん, 한선, hàn thiền), following the vault's convention that these fields record the character readings even where the word itself is not an everyday word in that language. Because the Korean 한선 matches the Dan'a'yo 諺文 exactly, `kwin` is true.

The Animals page lists the word as "winter cicada — also used figuratively for someone who dares not speak," and Dan'a'yo keeps both senses. The figurative sense is the more common one in practice. The vault has no 噤若寒蟬 chengyu page. The wording of the idiom's gloss above is the page's existing account.
