## Notes
- [掠](../characters/掠.md) "rob" + [奪](../characters/奪.md) "seize" — stand-in for [掠](../characters/掠.md), which cannot appear independently

Both components already mean taking by force: 掠 (手 "hand" radical) is to rob, raid, sweep through and carry off, and 奪 is to snatch away, wrest from someone. The compound therefore denotes seizing goods or people by violence, typically by an armed force in war or conquest, and is more collective and organized than a single theft. It is a standard term for the looting of cities, the plunder of resources by an occupier, and in modern usage for exploitation of land or resources ("resource plunder").

The reading is shared: Mandarin lüèduó, Cantonese loek6 dyut6, Japanese ryakudatsu (りゃくだつ), Korean 략탈 (the North Korean form, keeping initial 략), Sino-Vietnamese lược đoạt. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense "plunder, pillage, loot" with no divergence. It is placed under [War](../lexipedia/War.md).
