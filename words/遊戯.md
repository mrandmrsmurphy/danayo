## Notes
- [遊](../characters/遊.md) "play; roam; travel" + [戯](../characters/戯.md) "play; trick" — stand-in for [[遊]], which cannot appear independently

遊戯 means play or a game: recreational activity pursued for amusement (電子遊戲, "electronic games"; 遊戲規則, "rules of the game"; 遊戯室, "playroom"; 兒童遊戯, "children's games"). It is also used in a figurative sense, as an activity done lightly or not seriously. The variants 游戏, 游戲 and 遊戲 are listed as aliases.

Mandarin yóuxì, Cantonese jau4 hei3, Japanese ゆうぎ (yūgi), Korean 유희 (yuhui), and Vietnamese du hí are all real, standard words for "play, game, amusement" across the sphere — though each carries a slightly more formal or specialized flavor than the plainest everyday word for "to play" in its own language (Mandarin's own everyday "play" is often just 玩, wán; Japanese leans on native 遊ぶ, asobu, for the verb, reserving ゆうぎ more for structured games/activities, e.g. childhood 遊戯 as a category of organized play).
