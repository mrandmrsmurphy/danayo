## Notes
- [苛](../characters/苛.md) "harsh, exacting" + [酷](../characters/酷.md) "cruel"
- This word is the stand-in for [酷](../characters/酷.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

苛 is harsh in the sense of exacting and merciless to the point of petty cruelty (as in 苛政 "tyrannical government," the classical example of rulers who tax and punish without measure), and 酷 is extreme, severe, cruel. 苛酷 doubles the idea: treatment, conditions or demands that are harsh and unsparing beyond what is bearable, as in 苛酷な労働 "grueling labor," 苛酷な運命 "a cruel fate," or 苛酷な刑罰 "a harsh penalty." It describes the severity of conditions or treatment and is not about a person's feelings, so it is an evaluative adjective for what is done to someone.

It is close to [苛刻](苛刻.md) "harsh, demanding," which in Chinese is the more common word and leans toward overly strict terms and conditions, whereas 苛酷 stresses severity and suffering. In Japanese the spelling 過酷 (かこく) is also in common use for the same word. The Emotions page lists it among the words for harshness and cruelty. The readings are Mandarin kēkù, Cantonese ho1 huk6, Japanese kakoku (かこく) and Korean 가혹. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "cruel, harsh, severe."
