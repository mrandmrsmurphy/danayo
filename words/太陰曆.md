## Notes
- [太](../characters/太%20(char).md) "great" + [陰](../characters/陰%20(char).md) "yin, moon" + [暦](../characters/暦%20(char).md) "calendar" — no stand-in relationship applies (太/陰 are both their own stand-ins; 暦's own is itself).

太陰曆 means lunar calendar — a calendar based on the phases of the moon. This page is the traditional-orthography duplicate of [[太陰暦]] (using 曆, an alias of 暦, in the title); the two pages describe the identical Dan'a'yo word and share the same 注音/羅馬字/諺文.

Mandarin tàiyīnlì, Cantonese taai3 jam1 lik6, Korean 태음력, and Japanese たいいんれき are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese âm lịch is the standard real-world term (dropping 太, a common real-world contraction also seen in colloquial 陰曆/阴历 — these are shorter synonyms of the concept, not orthographic aliases of this specific three-character word). **Real bugs found and fixed**: `mandarin`/`cantonese` had both dropped the 太 syllable entirely (yīnlì/jam1 lik6) — corrected to tàiyīnlì/taai3 jam1 lik6. Filled blank `pos: 名詞`. Fixed `characters` to cite the real character page 暦 (char) rather than its alias glyph 曆 (which has no page of its own). Removed the malformed `aliases` list (its two entries were both the unrelated shorter synonym 陰曆/阴历, not alternate spellings of 太陰曆 itself).
