## Notes
- [同](../characters/同.md) "same" + [音](../characters/音.md) "sound" — no stand-in relationship applies (同's own stand-in is [[同一]]; 音's is [[音楽]]).

同音 is "same sound": two or more words or characters that are pronounced alike, "homophones", and as a modifier "homophonous". It is what the writing-system chapter relies on in 同音字, "homophones" among characters, the situation where an obscure character shares its Dan'a'yo sound with a common one and may be moved to another syllable page. The build is the simple compound of 同 "same" (compare [[同化]], [[同一]]) and 音 "sound" (compare [[音源]], [[異音]] "allophony", [[発音]]).

All four languages share the word in the linguistic sense. Mandarin tóngyīn (Cantonese tung4 jam1) is "homophonous", and 同音字 "characters with the same pronunciation" is the everyday label in Chinese, where homophone density is very high because of the small syllable inventory and the loss of the old finals and tones. Japanese どうおん (*dōon*; ja Wiktionary also notes どういん) means "same pronunciation", and 同音異義語 (*dōon igigo*) "words with the same sound and a different meaning" is the usual compound for a homophone pair. Korean 동음 (*dong-eum*, North and South alike) and Vietnamese *đồng âm* (as in *từ đồng âm*, "homonym") are the matching terms.

**Other senses of the same string.** In older Chinese 同音 can also mean "in harmony" (of musical notes, a literary use) and, figuratively, "in one voice"; Japanese carries the musical sense too, "unison", and a Noh term for the choral part. These are not the Dan'a'yo sense: here the word means only "same sound" in the sense of homophony, and there is no clash with another word in the vault. The Dan'a'yo-side reading 동움 differs from the Sino-Korean 동음, since 音 is stored as 움, so `kwin` is false. A homophone in Dan'a'yo is a pair with the same 注音; see the `Homophones` callouts on word pages for the vault's own records of the phenomenon.
