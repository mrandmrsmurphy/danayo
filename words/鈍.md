## Notes
- [鈍 (char)](../characters/鈍%20(char).md) "dull, not sharp" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 鈍 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

鈍 means dull, blunt, not sharp — extended figuratively to "dull-witted, slow" (鈍感, "insensitive"; 遲鈍, "slow, obtuse"; 愚鈍, "stupid"). It functions as a genuine standalone predicative adjective in the source languages (Mandarin 这刀很钝, "this knife is dull"; Cantonese 呢把刀好鈍) rather than appearing only bound inside a compound, which is why it stands alone as a Dan'a'yo word. The simplified form 钝 is listed as an alias.

Mandarin dùn, Cantonese deon6, and Korean 둔 are standard readings. Vietnamese độn (as in ngu độn, "stupid, dull-witted") is the primary reading among the character's stored candidates (nhụt, độn); nhụt is the rarer alternate. Japanese carries two on'yomi, どん (DON, the default — 鈍感 donkan "insensitive," 鈍痛 dontsū "dull pain") and rarer とん (TON), alongside the native 訓読み にぶい (nibui), the everyday standalone adjective for "dull, blunt". Korean has native 둔하다.
