## Notes
- [鈍 (char)](../characters/鈍%20(char).md) "dull, not sharp" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 鈍 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

**User decision, 2026-09-16**: 鈍 was previously stamped `stand_in: 名専字` (name-restricted), but this was a discrepancy — 鈍 is a common, productive adjective across every daughter language, not a name. The user confirmed 鈍 should stand alone as a Dan'a'yo word in its own right, since it functions as a genuine standalone predicative adjective in the source languages (e.g. Mandarin 这刀很钝, "this knife is dull"; Cantonese 呢把刀好鈍) rather than only appearing bound inside a compound. The syllable ㄉㄛㄋ was confirmed unused by any other vault word before repointing.

鈍 means dull, blunt, not sharp — extended figuratively to "dull-witted, slow." Mandarin dùn, Cantonese deon6, and Korean 둔 are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching the character's own stored citations exactly. Vietnamese độn (as in ngu độn, "stupid, dull-witted") is the primary compositional reading among the character's own stored candidates (nhụt, độn) — nhụt is the rarer alternate. Japanese carries two on'yomi, どん (DON, the default — 鈍感 donkan "insensitive," 鈍痛 dontsū "dull pain") and rarer とん (TON), alongside the native 訓読み にぶい (nibui), the everyday standalone adjective for "dull, blunt" — the same Sino-Japanese/native split seen throughout the Swadesh sweep.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 돈 (don, from Middle Chinese 定母 d + 魂韻 uən) diverges from Sino-Korean 둔 in the vowel. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄉㄛㄋ was confirmed unused by any other word; the character-level syllable-mates 沌/敦/惇/遁 remain bound inside their own compounds, per the syllable page's own listing).
