## Notes
- [臼 (char)](../characters/臼%20(char).md) "mortar; millstone; socket" — stand-in for [[臼 (char)|臼]], which cannot appear independently

臼 is 象形 of a mortar, the hollow bowl-shape carved to receive a pestle's pounding strokes. **Note**: `characters/臼 (char).md` remains genuinely unperfected (`## Notes` holding only two dangling CC-initial/final wikilinks, no real graphemic write-up, no `date-last-perfect`) — a fifth character page found in this state this sweep, after [[玄]]/[[皿]]/[[矢]]/[[禾]] — flagged for the character-perfection sweep rather than rebuilt here, though its scalar fields remained trustworthy ground truth.

Beyond the literal grinding vessel, the character extends anatomically to any mortar-shaped hollow — the "socket" sense in this word's own gloss covers joint sockets (as in a ball-and-socket anatomical description), the same concrete-shape-to-anatomical-metaphor move already seen elsewhere in this sweep (e.g. [[斗]]'s dipper-to-constellation extension). Japanese carries a single on'yomi きゅう (KYUU), rare in ordinary vocabulary, alongside the fully productive native 訓読み うす (usu), the everyday word for a mortar or millstone (usu no mochi, pounded rice cakes, being the most culturally salient use of the physical object itself). Vietnamese cối is likewise the ordinary living word (cối xay "millstone/grinding mill," cối giã "mortar"), picked over the character's other stored reading cữu, which belongs to an unrelated classical sense. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 귯 (gyuo, from Middle Chinese 群母 g + 尤韻 ɨu) diverges from Sino-Korean 구 in the coda, Dan'a'yo retaining a final consonant Korean lacks.

**Real homophone group found**: `注音 ㄍ⼜ㄛ`/`귯` is also carried by two other existing standalone words, [[旧]] ("paleo-, former, old") and [[糾]] ("twist, investigate").
