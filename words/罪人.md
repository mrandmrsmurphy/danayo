## Notes
- [罪](../characters/罪%20(char).md) "sin" + [人](../characters/人%20(char).md) "person" — "sin person," a sinner. Their own `stand_in` values: 罪's is itself; 人's is itself; none points here, so 罪人 is an independent compound.

罪人 is a sinner: a person who bears sin. Mandarin zuìrén, Cantonese zeoi6 jan4, Japanese ざいにん, Korean 죄인 and Vietnamese tội nhân all use it, so it is a word in its own right and not 罪 plus the suffix －者.

**Hebrew**: it best translates *ḥaṭṭāʾ* (חַטָּא) and *ḥôṭēʾ* (חוֹטֵא), "sinner," as in "the way of sinners" (Psalm 1:1). The "wicked," *rāshāʿ* (רָשָׁע), is the settled evildoer and is better said with [[邪悪]]. Compare [[犯人]] (a criminal, a court's word) and [[罪]] (sin).
