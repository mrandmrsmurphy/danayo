## Notes
- [恩](characters/恩.md) "kindness" + [人](characters/人%20(char).md) "person"

恩人 means "benefactor, patron" — literally "a kindness-person," someone to whom one owes a debt of gratitude for a significant favor or rescue, often carrying a sense of lasting moral obligation (恩人節, "Benefactor's Day," a Taiwanese observance honoring such figures).

Mandarin ēnrén and Cantonese jan1 jan4 preserve the sense directly. Japanese おんじん (onjin) is a standard, everyday word carrying the same weight of obligation found in the broader 恩 concept (cf. 恩返し, "to repay a kindness"). Korean 은인 (eunin) parallels the Sino-xenic reading and is likewise standard vocabulary — 생명의 은인 (saengmyeong-ui eunin, "one's savior/lifesaver," literally "benefactor of life"). Vietnamese ân nhân is the standard Hán Việt reading, equally common in everyday speech.
