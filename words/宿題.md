## Notes
- [宿](../characters/宿.md) "lodge; long-standing; overnight" + [題](../characters/題.md) "topic; question; problem"

The compound encodes its meaning in the characters: 宿 carries the sense of something carried forward, held overnight, left from one session to the next — and 題 is the question or problem to be resolved. Homework is literally the unresolved topic you take with you when you leave.

宿題 is the standard everyday word for homework in Japanese (しゅくだい), and Korean 숙제 uses it with identical meaning and frequency. Both also use it for a pending problem, an issue left unresolved ("a task still to be worked out"). In Chinese the word exists and is understood, especially in Taiwan and among speakers familiar with Japanese, but mainland Mandarin more typically uses [[作業]] (*zuòyè*) for homework; its homework sense in Chinese is largely a borrowing from Japanese.

In Dan'a'yo 宿題 is homework, a task set at school to be done afterwards; the neighbouring [[作業]] is general work or an assignment, and [[問題]] is a problem or question in general.
