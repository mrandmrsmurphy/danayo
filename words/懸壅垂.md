## Notes
- [懸](../characters/懸%20(char).md) "hang, suspend, hoist" + [壅](../characters/壅%20(char).md) "obstruct" + [垂](../characters/垂.md) "hang, dangle" — no stand-in relationship applies (懸/壅 are both their own stand-ins; 垂's own is [[垂掛]]).

懸壅垂 means uvula — the small conical piece of tissue hanging from the soft palate.

Mandarin xuányōngchuí, Cantonese jyun4 jung2 seoi4, and Japanese けんようすい (ken'yōsui) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and Korean 현옹수 is the compositional Sino-Korean reading (not separately verified in a Korean dictionary). Korean 목젖 and Vietnamese lưỡi gà are each language's own native term for the uvula, more common in everyday speech than a reading of these characters. Wiktionary records 懸壅垂 with 壅 in Chinese and Japanese and lists 懸雍垂 (with 雍) as a variant, which is kept among the aliases along with the simplified forms.

In phonetics, 懸壅垂 is the stem of "uvular", written as two words in Dan'a'yo in the same way as [[硬口蓋]] and [[軟口蓋]]: 懸壅垂 + [[鼻音]], "uvular nasal", on the phonology page, the realization of the velar nasal that the grammar attributes to the old Japanese layer. Dan'a'yo uses this form rather than the Japanese and Korean 口蓋垂 (こうがいすい, 구개수), which is a Japanese and Korean term only: 懸壅垂 is the form Chinese and Japanese share, so it reaches more of the source languages. No homophones (注音 ㄏ⼔ㄋ·ㄛㄫㄐㄨㄧ unique across both words and characters).
