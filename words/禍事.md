## Notes
- [禍](../characters/禍.md) "calamity, disaster" + [事](../characters/事%20(char).md) "thing, matter" — 禍's own stand-in is this exact compound, but 事's own is [[事]] itself; transitivity fails, so `#cranberry` does not apply, though 禍 is legitimized as an independent Dan'a'yo entry specifically by this word. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 事 is individually `kwin: false`.

禍事 names a disaster or calamity — an ill-omened misfortune, often with a sense of fated or supernatural cause rather than mere accident. Japanese まがごと (magagoto) preserves an old native reading of 禍 as まが ("evil, ill-omened," from the same root as 禍々しい, "ominous"), distinct from the more common native reading わざわい used for the bare character.

Mandarin huòshì, Cantonese wo6 si6, and Korean 화사 (hwasa) are standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese hoạ sự is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form.
