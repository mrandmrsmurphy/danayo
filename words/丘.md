## Notes
- [丘 (char)](../characters/丘%20(char).md) "hill"

丘 depicts a raised mound of earth flanked by two peaks — a natural hill, distinct from 山 (a full mountain range, a pictograph of jagged peaks). Confucius's personal name was famously 丘 (Kǒng Qiū), which led to a long-standing naming taboo: Chinese texts often substitute the graphically similar 邱 to avoid writing the sage's given name directly. Vietnamese shows the same reflex — the regular Sino-Vietnamese reading khâu is often replaced by the taboo-avoidance variant khưu specifically when transcribing Confucius's name (Khổng Khưu) and in some surnames descended from it.

Mandarin qiū, Cantonese jau1, Japanese きゅう (kyū, on'yomi) alongside native おか (oka, the everyday word for "hill"), and Korean 구 (gu) alongside native 언덕 (eondeok, the everyday word for "hill") round out the sphere — the Sino-xenic readings are comparatively rare in ordinary speech, most active in place names and in Confucius's own name.

This word shares its Dan'a'yo reading (kyu/큐/ㄎ⼜) with [[九]] ("nine") — a coincidental homophone with no semantic connection.
