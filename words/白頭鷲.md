## Notes
- [白 (char)](../characters/白%20(char).md) "white" + [頭 (char)](../characters/頭%20(char).md) "head" + [就 (char)](../characters/就%20(char).md), standing in for its alias [[鷲]] "eagle, vulture" — like [[就鳥]] and [[禿就]], this word borrows 就 as a 借代字 for the unlinked character 鷲, using 就's alias reading ㄐㄨㄛ (juo) rather than its own regular cwai/쵀/ㄑ⺢ㄧ; 就 and 鷲 happen to be true homophones in both Mandarin and Cantonese (jiù/zau6), which is presumably why 就 was chosen as the substitute glyph in the first place.

白頭鷲 names the bald eagle (Haliaeetus leucocephalus), the North American national bird, whose adults have a distinctive white-feathered head against an otherwise dark brown body. Mandarin báitóujiù and Cantonese baak6 tau4 zau6 are compositional, using 鷲's real reading. Japanese and Korean instead use their own established species names built from native or hybrid vocabulary rather than the vault-internal 就-substitution system: Japanese ハクトウワシ (Hakutou Washi, a Sino+native hybrid — はくとう 白頭 + わし "eagle") and Korean 대머리독수리 (daemeori-doksuri, literally "bald-head eagle") are both the real, standard zoological names for the species. Vietnamese đại bàng đầu trắng ("white-headed eagle") likewise uses the native descriptive pattern rather than a Sino-Vietnamese compound.
