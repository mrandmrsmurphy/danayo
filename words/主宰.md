## Notes
- [主](../characters/主.md) "master" + [宰](../characters/宰.md) "to govern, to preside over" (originally "butcher, official in charge of slaughter/sacrifice," per 宰's own ancient sense, generalized to any high administrative authority) — to superintend, rule, or dominate.

主宰 carries a real philosophical/religious weight beyond ordinary administration — used of a cosmic or ultimate ruling force (as in describing the heart as 主宰 of the Way in Neo-Confucian thought, or of yin-yang's dominion over the universe) as often as of a mundane official superintending affairs. Vietnamese chủ tể (confirmed via web search) carries this exact dual weight, spanning mastership and sovereignty from the mundane to the cosmic. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 주재 coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 주재. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄨㄐㄚㄧ unique among both words and characters).
