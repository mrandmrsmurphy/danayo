## Notes
- [剣 (char)](../characters/剣%20(char).md) "sword"

剣 (alternate forms 劍, 剑, 劔, 劒) is 形声: semantic [[刀]] ("knife, blade") + phonetic 㑒 — a double-edged blade, "sword," distinct from 刀 itself (a single-edged blade). It heads Japanese martial-arts vocabulary in particular: [[剣道]] ("kendo, the Way of the Sword") and [[剣術]] ("fencing, swordsmanship"), and appears in the chengyu [[刻舟求剣]] ("stubbornly cling to tradition, fail to adapt" — from the fable of a man who marked the side of his boat where his sword fell overboard, then searched there after the boat had moved on).

Mandarin jiàn, Cantonese gim3, and Japanese けん (ken, on'yomi, fully productive: 剣道 けんどう "kendo") alongside native つるぎ (tsurugi, a literary/archaic native word for "sword") all preserve the sense. Korean 검 (geom) parallels the Sino-xenic reading, alongside native 칼 (kal, the everyday word for "knife, blade, sword"). Vietnamese kiếm is the Hán Việt reading, well attested in kiếm sĩ ("swordsman") and đấu kiếm ("fencing").
