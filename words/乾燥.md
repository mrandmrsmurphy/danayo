## Notes
- [乾 (char)](../characters/乾%20(char).md) "dry" + [燥](../characters/燥.md) "parched, dry, arid" — stand-in for [[燥]], which cannot appear independently

乾燥 means dry or arid: free of moisture (乾燥機/건조기, "clothes dryer"; 空気乾燥, "dry air"), and by extension dull or lacking interest (枯燥, "dull"). 乾 here is the "dry" character, with its unified sense of absence of moisture; it is written 干燥 in Simplified Chinese (and 干 is the simplification this vault records), but 乾 is the real character here, not 干 ("shield"). The variant 干燥 is listed as an alias.

Mandarin gānzào, Cantonese gon1 cou3, Japanese かんそう (kansō), and Korean 건조 (geonjo) are all the standard, everyday word for "dry, arid". Vietnamese has two attested Sino-Vietnamese readings: can táo, using 乾's "gān"-parallel modern reading, and the more classical kiền táo, tied to 乾's older "qián" (Trigram/Heaven) reading — kept can táo as the field value since it matches the everyday adjective sense directly.
