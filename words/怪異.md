## Notes
- [怪](../characters/怪.md) "strange" + [異](../characters/異.md) "different"
- This word is the viable form of [怪](../characters/怪.md): the character's meaning (strange, suspicious) is identical, but 怪 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 怪異 is its `stand_in` compound.

怪 is "strange, uncanny, a monster," and 異 is "different, unusual," so 怪異 is "strange to the point of being out of the ordinary." It is the register that tilts toward the eerie and unsettling rather than the merely curious, which sets it apart from [奇妙](奇妙.md) "strange, wonderful" (admiring or amused) and from its near-synonym [奇怪](奇怪.md). Mandarin guàiyì and Korean 괴이 are used mostly as a literary adjective ("weird, uncanny"), as in 괴이하다.

Japanese kaii (かいい) works as a noun as well: a mysterious or supernatural occurrence, and the stock term of ghost-story and yōkai literature (怪異譚 "tales of the uncanny"). Cantonese is gwaai3 ji6. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared adjectival sense "strange, bizarre, uncanny," and the noun use of the Japanese is a context-dependent extension rather than a separate Dan'a'yo sense. It is placed under [Valuation](../lexipedia/Valuation.md).
