## Notes
- [寂](../characters/寂.md) "silent, quiet, still" + [寞](../characters/寞.md) "lonely, desolate" — this word is the stand-in for [寞](../characters/寞.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo. 寂 also appears here, but only lends its ordinary "quiet" sense; its own precise Dan'a'yo definition now lives in [[寂滅]] (the Buddhist "nirvana, cessation" register), leaving 寂寞 as plain, everyday "lonely, desolate."

寂寞 names the felt experience of loneliness and desolation, distinct from [[寂静]]/[[静寂]] (physical quiet or stillness, with no emotional charge) even though all three compounds share 寂's basic "still, silent" root. 寞 itself is rare outside this one compound — Wiktionary confirms it survives almost exclusively as 寂寞's second syllable, with essentially no independent life even in Classical registers, which is why it is the one bound by this word rather than the far more productive 寂.

Japanese せきばく, Korean 적막, and Vietnamese tịch mịch (all standard, non-drifted readings) preserve the same core sense of a quiet, empty desolation. Korean also builds the four-character idiom 적막강산 (寂寞江山, literally "a desolate river and mountains") — voted South Korea's "idiom of the year" for 2020 — which extends the landscape imagery figuratively to describe a bleak, uncertain state of affairs one cannot see one's way out of, the same physical-desolation-to-emotional-state extension already present in 寂寞 itself. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄝㄎㄇㄚㄎ unique across both words and characters).
