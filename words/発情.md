## Notes
- [発 (char)](../characters/発%20(char).md) "issue forth, discharge" + [情](../characters/情.md) "feelings, emotion" — literally "emotion issuing forth." 発's own stand-in is [[発]] itself and 情's is [[感情]], so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply; neither constituent is legitimized by this word.

発情 describes the onset of sexual arousal or, in its most common technical sense, an animal entering estrus/heat — the biological trigger for mating behavior. It is the standard veterinary and zoological term across the Sinosphere: 発情期 ("estrus/breeding season") is a fixed collocation in all of these languages.

Mandarin fāqíng, Cantonese faat3 cing4, Japanese はつじょう (hatsujō), and Korean 발정 (baljeong) are all fully compositional and carry the same primarily zoological register, occasionally extended figuratively to human arousal in more clinical or blunt speech. Vietnamese phát tình diverges slightly in flavor: rather than the animal-husbandry sense, it is used more colloquially of a sudden romantic or sexual attraction toward a person — "falling for someone" in a rush — making it the one language here where the word has drifted toward the interpersonal rather than the biological.
