## Notes
- [外](../characters/外.md) "outside" + [来 (char)](../characters/来%20(char).md) "come" + [語](../characters/語.md) "language, word" — "word that came from outside." Built on the adjective [[外来]] "foreign, extrinsic." No stand-in relationship applies (外's `stand_in` is [[外部]], 来's is itself, 語's is [[言語]]).

外来語 is the standard linguistic term for a loanword, a word borrowed from another language. Dan'a'yo's phonology chapter uses it in the phrase 外来語転写, the transcription of foreign words, which must follow Dan'a'yo's own phoneme-combination rules (breaking up consonant clusters as needed) and can be marked as more or less foreign by writing above an empty ◌ or vertically. Here 語 means "word" rather than "language," as in [[単語]] or [[造語]]; for "foreign language" the vault has [[外語]] and [[外国語]].

The term has a different scope in each language. It is originally Japanese: Wiktionary records that 外来語 (gairaigo) was first used by Ueda Kazutoshi in 1895, probably as a calque of German Fremdwort, and defines it as a word borrowed since Japan's modernization in the Meiji era, especially from a European language. Words of Chinese origin, although technically also borrowed, are not counted: Japanese has the separate category [[漢語]] (kango), alongside native 和語 (wago), so a 外来語 is in practice a katakana word like ググる or コンピューター. The Chinese term 外來語/外来语 (wàiláiyǔ) means "loanword; borrowing" in linguistics, but mainland Chinese scholars more often write 外来词, with 外来语 favored by Japanese linguists and some Taiwanese scholars, and Chinese distinguishes sound borrowings (借词) from translated words (译词). Korean 외래어 and Vietnamese từ ngoại lai are the matching terms: Korean sets such words apart from Sino-Korean 한자어, and Vietnamese likewise separates từ Hán Việt from từ ngoại lai, which refers to borrowed words that still keep the stamp of the foreign language and are not fully Vietnamized.

Dan'a'yo takes the shared sense, a word borrowed from another language, with no narrowing. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 왜래요 differs from Sino-Korean 외래어 in the first and last syllables. No homophones (注音 ⺢ㄧㄌㄚㄧ⼄ is unique), and no dot is needed before 語, as on [[外語]] and [[飛語]].
