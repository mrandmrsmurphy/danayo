## Notes
- [書](../characters/書.md) "book; to write" + [記](../characters/記.md) "to record; remember"

書記 names a secretary or clerk — literally "one who writes and records" — the office responsible for keeping the written minutes, correspondence, and records of an organization. Historically the term also carried a more elevated political sense, notably as the title of a Communist Party official (e.g. 總書記, "General Secretary"), a usage that survives prominently in Mandarin political vocabulary today.

Cross-linguistically the compound is a stable Sino-Xenic loan: Sino-Korean 서기 and Japanese しょき both directly concatenate the component readings and carry the same clerical/secretarial sense, and Vietnamese thư kí (also spelled thư ký) is likewise an ordinary, freely used word for "secretary" or "clerk," not merely a bound literary form.
