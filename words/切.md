## Notes
- [切 (char)](../characters/切%20(char).md) "cut; mince; carve"

切 combines phonetic [[七]] with the "knife" radical 刀. It means "to cut, sever, slice" (切斷, "to cut off"), and also appears in the fixed set phrase 一切 ("everything, all") — where Korean takes an alternate reading 체 (che, "entirety") distinct from the ordinary "cut" reading 절 (jeol): the two readings distinguish 일체 ("everything," the 体-sense) from 일절 ("not at all, absolutely not," the 切-sense), a well-known homograph trap in Korean.

Mandarin qiè, Cantonese cit3, and Japanese せつ/さい (setsu/sai, on'yomi, as in 親切 しんせつ "kind, considerate" — literally "cutting close") alongside native き(る) (ki(ru), "to cut," the everyday verb) all preserve the sense. Korean 절 (jeol) is the reading proper to this "cut" sense (this word's own reading), distinct from the alternate 체 (che) used specifically in 一切/일체 (the character page's own stored `korean` field uses 체, the other reading — left as-is, out of scope on an unperfected character page). Vietnamese thiết is the Hán Việt reading, attested in thân thiết (親切, "close, intimate," the same figurative extension as Japanese しんせつ); the character page's other three stored candidates (siết, thiếc, thướt) are unrelated and look like corpus noise.
