## Notes
- [油 (char)](../characters/油%20(char).md) "oil" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

油 is the generic word for "oil" — any oil, culinary or otherwise — with more specific oils formed by prefixing a source or type: [石油](石油.md) ("petroleum," literally "rock oil"), 汽油 (gasoline), 醤油 (soy sauce, literally "sauce oil"). The character is 形声: semantic [[Radical 085|水]] ("water," marking it as a liquid) + phonetic [[由 (char)|由]] (yóu), a common and highly productive phonetic across the whole 由-series (油/柚/軸/抽/袖 all share it).

Mandarin yóu, Cantonese jau4, and Korean 유 (yu, in Sino-Korean compounds like 석유 "petroleum") are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese keeps the same register split found throughout the vault's oil/water-adjacent vocabulary: the Sino-Japanese on'yomi ゆ (yu) is productive in compounds (醤油 shōyu, 石油 sekiyu), while the native kun'yomi あぶら (abura) is the everyday standalone word for "oil" itself — the same relationship 水's own すい/みず split shows for "water." Vietnamese du is the Sino-Vietnamese reading, used in compounds like du hỏa (石油, petroleum lamp fuel); everyday Vietnamese uses the native dầu for oil generally.
