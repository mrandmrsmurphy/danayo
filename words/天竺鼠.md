## Notes
- [天 (char)](../characters/天%20(char).md) "sky; heaven" + [竺](../characters/竺.md) "India; bamboo" + [鼠](../characters/鼠.md) "rat; mouse"

天竺鼠 is the guinea pig, *Cavia porcellus*, a domesticated South American rodent. The name means "India rat": 天竺 is, in English Wiktionary, the archaic Chinese name for India, and in Japanese also "foreign or exotic", so the name marks the animal as an exotic import even though guinea pigs come from the Americas. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 天竺鼠 (Mandarin tiānzhúshǔ, Cantonese tin1 zuk1 syu2) and Japanese てんじくねずみ both as "guinea pig"; for Japanese it lists the synonyms モルモット and ギニーピッグ. It has no Korean or Vietnamese entry, so Korean 천축서 here is built from the characters.

English Wikipedia says guinea pigs are still often used to diagnose tuberculosis, since they are easily infected by the human bacteria, and were among the animals of the germ-theory experiments of Pasteur, Roux and Koch. In the Andes the animal is eaten, under the name cuy, in dishes such as cuy chactado. Chinese uses 小白鼠, "white lab mouse", for the figurative sense of a test subject, which Wiktionary glosses "lab rat; guinea pig". A related Japanese name is 鬼天竺鼠 (おにてんじくねずみ), the capybara.
