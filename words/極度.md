## Notes
- [極 (char)](../characters/極%20(char).md) "extreme, utmost" + [度](../characters/度.md) "degree, manner" — "the utmost degree".

極度 is "extreme degree": the utmost point on a scale, and, used before a quality, "extremely". English Wiktionary gives the Chinese word as an adverb ("extremely; exceedingly; to the utmost"; for example 極度危險, "in an extremely perilous state") and as a noun ("extreme point; limit"), with 極點 as the noun synonym (not a word in the vault). Japanese 極度 (きょくど, kyokudo) is both a na-adjective ("extreme") and a noun ("limit; maximum; utmost"). Korean 극도 and Vietnamese cực độ are listed as descendants of the Chinese word.

The Valuation page lists 極度 as a near-synonym of [[極端]], with the bound root [[極]] behind both. No stricter division between the two has been fixed for Dan'a'yo.

Mandarin jídù and Cantonese gik6 dou6 are as at English Wiktionary.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄍㄧㄎㄉㄛ (checked 2026-10-12).
