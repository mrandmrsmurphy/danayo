## Notes
- [闊 (char)](../characters/闊%20(char).md) "broad, wide" + [葉 (char)](../characters/葉%20(char).md) "leaf"

闊葉 means broad-leaved, describing plants with wide, flat leaves as opposed to needle-leaved conifers. Mandarin 闊葉/阔叶 (kuòyè), Cantonese 闊葉 (fut3jip6), Korean 활엽 (hwaryeop), and Sino-Vietnamese khoát diệp are all the standard compositional botanical terms for this concept, most familiar in the compound "broadleaf tree" (闊葉樹/활엽수/khoát diệp thụ).

Japanese 闊葉 (かつよう) is likewise real and attested, but 闊葉樹 (katsuyōju, "broadleaf tree") is now considered the older term, superseded in modern Japanese by 広葉樹 (kōyōju) — built on 広 ("wide") rather than 闊 — which is the standard word in contemporary botany and everyday speech.
