>[!caution] The senses split — Chinese and Japanese "underwear", Mainland Chinese "shirt"
>親衣 is underwear in Chinese and Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. In Mainland China 襯衣 is also a shirt, so a Chinese reader may hear a shirt. Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md).


## Notes
- [親](../characters/親.md) "close" + [衣](../characters/衣.md) "clothes"

親衣 is underwear, an undershirt. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 襯衣 (alias here; Mandarin chènyī, Cantonese can3 ji1) as "underwear" and, chiefly in Mainland China, "shirt", and Japanese 襯衣 (しんい) as "underwear". The Dan'a'yo sense is the underwear shared by Chinese and Japanese, and the word is listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md). The Korean 친의 field is built from the readings of the two characters.

The spelling is a phonetic substitution. 襯, whose phonetic is 親, and its simplified form 衬 are aliases of [親](../characters/親.md), which reads ㄑㄧㄋ as 襯 does; the characters carry no meaning of their own here. The general word for clothes is [衣服](衣服.md), and the garment worn on the upper body is the [上衣](上衣.md).
