## Notes
- [将 (char)](../characters/将%20(char).md) "general; commander" + [帥](../characters/帥.md) "commander-in-chief; marshal" — this word is the stand-in for [帥](../characters/帥.md), which cannot appear independently in Dan'a'yo.

将帥 is a near-synonym doubling naming a supreme military commander — both halves independently mean "commander/general," 帥 being the more elevated of the two (a marshal ranks above a general in classical Chinese military hierarchy, hence 帥's own gloss "commander-in-chief").

Japanese しょうすい, Korean 장수, and Vietnamese tướng soái are all standard, non-drifted readings (all verified). **`cantonese` follows the real attested reading (zoeng3 seoi3) rather than 将's own single-stored citation-form cantonese (zoeng1)** — Cantonese lexicalizes tone differently across different 将-compounds in ways not fully predictable from the character's own default reading (將軍 "general" itself keeps zoeng1, while 將帥 shifts to zoeng3), the same class of divergence already documented for [[対連]]. No HSK level is given for the compound. No homophones (注音 ㄐ⺢ㄫㄙㄨㄧ unique across both words and characters).

**Correction (2026-08-27)**: `japanese` had been badly contaminated — stored チャンギ ("changgi," the Japanese name for *Korean* chess, an entirely unrelated word) instead of the real reading しょうすい.
