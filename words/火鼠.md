## Notes
- [火 (char)](../characters/火%20(char).md) "fire" + [鼠](../characters/鼠.md) "rat; mouse"

火鼠 is the fire rat, a mythological creature said to live in fire. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 火鼠 (Mandarin huǒshǔ, Cantonese fo2 syu2) as "fire rat or mouse (a mythological creature)", and Japanese as an imaginary creature of ancient China resembling a white rat, thought to live in the volcanoes of the South China Sea and to have fireproof fur. Korean 화서 and Vietnamese hỏa thử are built from the readings of the two characters.

Japanese Wikipedia cites the 《神異經》, a geography book dated after the Jin dynasty, as the source: in a burning southern volcano lives a white rat whose fur, woven into cloth, is cleaned by putting it in the fire. That cloth, 火浣布, is identified with asbestos. The Japanese readings are かそ, ひねずみ and ひのねずみ.

In *The Tale of the Bamboo Cutter* (竹取物語), Kaguya-hime sets her five suitors impossible tasks, one of which is a robe of fire rat skins. The Minister of the Right, Abe no Miushi, is the suitor sent for it. The tale was already called "the ancestor of all tales" in *The Tale of Genji*.
