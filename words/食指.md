## Notes
- [食 (char)](../characters/食%20(char).md) "eat" + [指](../characters/指.md) "finger, toe" — no `#cranberry`: 食's own `stand_in` is 食 itself, 指's is [[手指]]; neither points here. Named for the finger's traditional role in touching/tasting food (see also Japanese 食指が動く, "one's index finger twitches," idiomatic for a stirring appetite or interest).

食指 (shízhǐ) is the standard Mandarin word for "index finger," and Cantonese 食指 (sik6zi2) likewise. Japanese しょくし (shokushi) is a real, attested formal alternative to the more common native 人差し指, chiefly familiar from the idiom 食指が動く. Korean 식지 (sikji) is likewise a real, formal Sino-Korean term, alongside the more common native 검지. Sino-Vietnamese thực chỉ is likewise attested, also used to mean "the number of people eating" in some contexts.
