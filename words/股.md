## Notes
- [股 (char)](../characters/股%20(char).md) "crotch, groin, fork" — stand-in for [[股 (char)|股]], which cannot appear independently.

股 is 會意, originally written 夃 (a pictograph of a thigh, no character page exists for it in this vault) with [[肉 (char)|肉]] ("flesh") added later as the 夃 component graphically corrupted into a form resembling [[殳]]. It names the crotch/groin/fork of the body, and by extension any forked or bifurcated shape — hence its use in [[溝股]] ("Pythagorean Theorem," from the 勾股 pair of terms for the two legs of a right triangle).

Japanese carries the on'yomi こ (KO); the native 訓読み また (mata) is a narrower term for the fork/crotch shape specifically rather than the body part. Vietnamese cổ is one of two stored candidates (cỗ also attested). `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 고 (go, from Middle Chinese 見母 k + 模韻 uo) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 고.

This word is part of a real three-way Dan'a'yo homophone group (`ㄍㄛ`/`고`) with [[鼓]] ("drum") and [[錮]] ("confine, imprison") — already fully cross-linked from 鼓's own perfecting pass, which also confirmed no fourth homophone exists among the other `ㄍㄛ`-reading characters (孤, 顧, 故, 固, 姑, 古).
