## Notes
- [元](../characters/元.md) "element; origin; first" + [凶 (char)](../characters/凶%20(char).md) "unlucky; sinister; evil"

元凶 means the chief culprit: the ringleader of a crime or of wrongdoing, the first and primary source of the harm; and, by extension, the root cause of a problem. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 元凶 (an alias, 元兇; Mandarin yuánxiōng, Cantonese jyun4 hung1) as "prime culprit; arch-criminal" only. For Japanese げんきょう it gives both senses: "root, principal cause (of a problem)", as in 悪の元凶, "the root cause of evil", and "prime culprit, instigator, leader of a criminal gang". Korean 원흉 is listed under the hanja form with no gloss given, and there is no Vietnamese entry, so Vietnamese nguyên hung here is built from the characters.

The word joins 元, origin or first, with 凶, evil, so "the first evil, the one at the root". The prefix 元 is the same one that appears in [[元号]], "era name". Wiktionary does not record the root-cause sense for Chinese, so a Chinese reader may hear only the human culprit.
