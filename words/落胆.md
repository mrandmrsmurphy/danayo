>[!caution] The senses split — Japanese "disappointment", Mandarin "overwhelmed by fear"
>落胆 is disappointment in Japanese, and the Dan'a'yo sense. Mandarin 落膽 and Cantonese lok6 daam2 are recorded only as an obsolete word for being overcome by fear, so a Chinese reader will not hear "disappointed". Listed as a Split on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md); for plain disappointment prefer [失望](失望.md).

## Notes
- [落](../characters/落.md) "fall" + [胆](../characters/胆.md) "gallbladder; courage"

落胆 is to lose heart: to be disappointed and discouraged when hope fails, or the disappointment itself. Wiktionary glosses Japanese らくたん as "disappointment; to be disappointed". Korean 락담 is the reading of the characters. The Mandarin and Cantonese readings (luòdǎn, lok6 daam2) are those of 落膽, which Wiktionary gives as an obsolete word for being overwhelmed by fear; the original sense was the heart's courage "falling", and the languages took it in opposite directions.

This word is listed on [文法 - 91疑義語](../grammar/文法%20-%2091疑義語.md) as a Split. It is the weaker partner of [失望](失望.md), the loss of hope, and the quieter relative of [悲哀](悲哀.md), sorrow. The courage that falls is the gallbladder, 胆.
