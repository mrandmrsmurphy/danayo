## Notes
- [爵](../characters/爵.md) "peerage; ceremonial wine vessel" + [位](../characters/位.md) "position, rank"

爵位 names a title of nobility — duke, marquis, count, viscount, baron, and their East Asian equivalents — and the rank it confers. 爵 is a 会意 character whose original meaning was a three-legged bronze vessel for warming and pouring ceremonial wine, used in Zhou-dynasty investiture rituals; the vessel itself became metonymic for the rank it was awarded alongside, much as English "to be knighted" derives a verb from the ceremonial sword-tap. 爵's only other cross-linked compound, [[男爵]] ("baron," from [[爵]]'s Words section), names one specific rank within the system that 爵位 names as a whole.

爵位 sits one tier below [[王位]] and [[帝位]] in the hierarchy of the three: 帝位 (emperor) > 王位 (king) > 爵位 (the various ranks of nobility serving under a king or emperor). The five ranks (公侯伯子男, duke/marquis/count/viscount/baron) used in modern Japanese and Korean translations of Western nobility are themselves borrowed from an ancient Zhou-dynasty ranking system, attesting to how deep this vocabulary's roots run. The highest of these, the duke, is [公爵](公爵.md).

The Government page lists the word with the glosses "a peerage, title of nobility," and it is the sense Dan'a'yo keeps: the rank or title, as distinct from the throne (王位) and from the act of taking it. The readings are Mandarin juéwèi, Cantonese zoek3 wai6, Japanese shakui (しゃくい), Korean 작위 and Sino-Vietnamese tước vị.
