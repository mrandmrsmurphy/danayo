## Notes
- [抄](../characters/抄.md) "copy; abridge; excerpt" + [録](../characters/録.md) "record; write down" — stand-in for [抄](../characters/抄.md), which cannot appear independently
- 抄録 names the practice — and the product — of picking out the essential parts of a longer text and setting them down on their own: an excerpt, a digest, an abstract. In modern academic registers across the Sinosphere this is precisely the word for the abstract that opens a research paper — Sino-Korean 초록 (chorok) is the standard term students learn for "abstract," and Japanese しょうろく (shōroku) carries the same sense of "summary; abridged extract." The Korean reading produces an unrelated but charming homophone: 초록 is also 草綠, "grass-green," the color — so that in spoken Korean alone, "please write the 초록" could momentarily suggest either an academic summary or a shade of green, disambiguated only by the hanja that the ear cannot hear.

The characters tell the same story: 抄 is to copy out or take a selection from a text, and 録 (traditional 錄) is to record, write down, as in [録音](録音.md) "sound recording," so 抄録 is a record made by copying out the part that matters. The word is typically a noun, the written abstract or digest itself, and the verb "to excerpt" is formed with a light verb. The Mind page records that sense, "an excerpt, abstract, summary," and notes that it is the noun and not the adjective "abstract" (the opposite of concrete), for which Dan'a'yo has no dedicated word. Dan'a'yo keeps this noun sense, in line with the shared use. The alias 抄錄 is the traditional spelling, and the Mandarin and Cantonese readings are chāolù and caau1 luk6.
