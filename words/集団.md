## Notes
- [集](../characters/集.md) "collect" + [団](../characters/団.md) "group"
- This word is the viable form of [団](../characters/団.md): the character's meaning (group) is identical, but 団 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 集団 is its `stand_in` compound.

団 is the Japanese shinjitai form of 團, "round; a body of people," and 集 is "to gather," so 集団 is people (or things) brought together into a unit: a group, a body, a collective. It is a neutral, sociological word used of any organized or loosely gathered body, from 集団生活 "group living" and 集団行動 "group behavior" to 集団安全保障 "collective security," and it can also carry a hostile reading ("a gang, a mob") depending on context.

Here Chinese usage has narrowed: in Mandarin 集團 (the traditional form of the alias 集团) is mainly a business group or conglomerate (a 集團公司), and for the general sense of a group other words are common. It should be distinguished from [集合](集合.md) "to assemble, gather," the act of coming together, and from [群衆](群衆.md) "crowd, the masses," a mass of people with no internal organization, and from [団結](団結.md) "solidarity, unity," the cohesion within a group. The readings are Mandarin jítuán, Cantonese zaap6 tyun4, Japanese shūdan (しゅうだん) and Korean 집단. Dan'a'yo keeps the general sense, "group, collective," placed under [Society](../lexipedia/Society.md).
