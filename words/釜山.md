## Notes
- [釜](../characters/釜.md) "kettle, cauldron" + [山](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain" — the name of Busan, the port city at the southeastern tip of Korea.

釜山 (Busan) is the second-largest city of Korea and its chief port. Its full modern name is 釜山廣域市 (부산광역시), "Busan Metropolitan City", a separate unit apart from 慶尚南道 (see [[慶尚]]) since 1963, first as a 直轄市, and renamed 廣域市 in 1995 (from memory, not re-checked). The name is traditionally explained as "cauldron mountain", from a hill behind the old harbour said to resemble a cooking pot; an older name, 富山, is reported for the same place (from memory, not re-checked). The port was opened to Japanese trade in 1876, and the Pusan Perimeter of 1950 and the temporary wartime capital are also tied to the city (from memory, not re-checked).

Mandarin Fǔshān, Cantonese fu2 saan1 and Korean 부산 are attested: English and Chinese Wiktionary have 釜山 as the Korean city, with the Korean entry a hanja form of 부산. English Wiktionary records the modern Japanese name as プサン (from the Korean) and the historical colonial-era on'yomi readings ふさん and ふざん; ふさん is taken here as the Sino-Xenic reading. Vietnamese Phủ Sơn is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading from the character pages; no Vietnamese dictionary entry was found (from memory, not re-checked). The same Chinese string 釜山 is also, in Chinese Wiktionary, the name of several ancient mountains in northern China tied to the Yellow Emperor, unrelated to the Korean city.
