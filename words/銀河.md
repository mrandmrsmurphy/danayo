## Notes
- [銀 (char)](../characters/銀%20(char).md) "silver" + [河](../characters/河.md) "river"

銀河 is literally the "Silver River" — the arc of the Milky Way seen against the night sky, which Chinese astronomers described as a river of silver light. The image of a river across the sky is among the oldest in East Asian astronomy and poetry: the *Classic of Poetry* (詩経) already speaks of the 天漢, the "Han of heaven", and the compound 銀河 itself is familiar from Tang poetry, as in Li Bai's 疑是銀河落九天 ("I suspect the Silver River has fallen from the ninth heaven").

The name is inseparable from the legend of the Weaver Girl and the Cowherd: [[織女星]] (Vega) and [[牛郎星]] (Altair) are lovers separated by the Silver River, permitted to cross it on a bridge of magpies only once a year on [[七夕]] (the seventh night of the seventh month). 銀河 thus carries both an astronomical and a deeply romantic register in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese literary tradition.

In modern usage the word has extended to mean "galaxy" in general (especially in Japanese, where 銀河 is the standard scientific term and the Milky Way is more specifically 天の川). Korean uses 은하 for galaxy and 은하수 (Silver River Water) specifically for the Milky Way. Vietnamese retains Ngân Hà as the literary name.

In Dan'a'yo the word is kept narrower than in Japanese: 銀河 is the visible band of the Milky Way, "what one sees looking up", while the galaxy as a structure, the one our solar system sits inside, is [[銀河系]]. The two are related but not interchangeable, and 銀河系 is the bigger term.
