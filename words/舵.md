## Notes
- [舵 (char)](../characters/舵%20(char).md) "rudder" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the character 舵 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry.

Korean and Japanese both keep a native word as the everyday term for "rudder, helm" — Korean 키 (not the Sino-Korean 타 used here) and Japanese かじ (not on'yomi タ/ダ, mostly confined to compounds) — while Vietnamese lái has gone further than either: it is a fully naturalized, ancient loan from this very character (Old Chinese \*lˤajʔ), and has broadened well past boats into the ordinary verb for "to steer, to drive" in general (lái xe, "to drive a car"). The more literal Sino-Vietnamese reading đà survives only in specialized nautical compounds like đà công ("helmsman"), alongside a Cantonese-influenced variant, tài (from 舵's Cantonese reading to4), in tài công, another word for a boat pilot.

English Wiktionary glosses the character "rudder; tiller; helm" in Japanese, with the on readings だ (go'on) and た (kan'on) and the kun かじ. It traces the Japanese かじ to Old Japanese, originally a single oar-like device at the stern of a small boat, swept side to side, whose "steering device" sense later superseded the "oar" sense. It also lists the senses "the rudder of a boat or ship" and "the helm, tiller or wheel of a ship or aircraft". The Water page lists the word as "rudder, helm". The `japanese` field holds the Sino-Japanese た.

Mandarin duò and Cantonese to4 are as given in the fields. The Korean and Vietnamese observations in the paragraph above are not from Wiktionary and I have not re-checked them.

The only other word sharing the 注音 ㄉㄚ is the homophone listed above, 駄 (checked 2026-10-12).
