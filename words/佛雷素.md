## Notes
- [佛 (char)](../characters/佛%20(char).md) + [雷 (char)](../characters/雷%20(char).md) + [素](../characters/素.md) "element" — a phonetic transliteration of *Flerov*, after the Flerov Laboratory of Nuclear Reactions (itself named for physicist Georgy Flyorov), truncated to two syllables to keep the name compact rather than spelling out the full transliteration the way Mandarin's borrowed-character system would. Japanese and Korean instead simply borrow the IUPAC name as a loanword (フレロビウム, 플레로븀).

**Form, a variant of this series' usual pattern.** Unlike single-syllable coinages ([[巨金]], [[惰素]], etc.), where `mandarin`/`cantonese` hold the reading of one avoided element character, 佛雷素 is itself already a two-syllable phonetic transliteration of "Flerov" — the real Chinese element character it avoids, 鈇/𫓧 (fū/fu1), is a *single* syllable that doesn't match either half of this compound. Since this coinage's whole purpose is to spell out "Fle-rov" phonetically using two ordinary characters rather than adopt that single novel character, `mandarin`/`cantonese` above (fúléi/fat6 leoi4) instead give the direct compositional reading of 佛's own fú/fat6 and 雷's own léi/leoi4 — consistent with what this vault's own characters actually say when read aloud, rather than a reading of the avoided single-character alternative. `vietnamese` (Flerovi) follows the same "-i" pattern as [[達金]]/[[邁金]]/[[黒金]]'s Darmstadti/Meitneri/Hassi.
