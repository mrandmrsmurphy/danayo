## Notes
- [業](../characters/業%20(char).md) "deed" + [報](../characters/報%20(char).md) "repay" — "deed-return," what a deed brings back. Their own `stand_in` values: 業's is itself; 報's is itself; none points here, so 業報 is an independent compound.

業報 is the result of a deed returning on the one who did it: in Buddhism, karma's fruit. Mandarin yèbào, Cantonese jip6 bou3 and Japanese ごうほう use it, and Korean 업보 is the same word. Dan'a'yo takes the term over from its older religious home, on the vault's policy of adopting other religions' words when they carry the right feeling.

**Hebrew**: it best translates *ʿāwōn* (עָוֺן) in its sense of punishment, "my punishment is greater than I can bear" (Genesis 4:13), and the "bearing" of iniquity (*nāśāʾ ʿāwōn*, נָשָׂא עָוֺן). The Greek "wages of sin" (Romans 6:23) is the same idea. It is sin's own outcome, where [[天罰]] is a penalty God imposes and [[報応]] is God's repaying. The *power* of sin (Genesis 4:7, Romans 6) is [[業力]]. Compare [[罪業]].
