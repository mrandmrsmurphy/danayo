## Notes
- [腐](../characters/腐.md) "rotten, corrupt" + [朽](../characters/朽.md) "decayed, rotten wood"
- This word is the stand-in for [朽](../characters/朽.md), which is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo.

A compound reinforcing the meaning of organic or moral decay. In Chinese, 腐朽 describes both physical rotting (timber, organic matter) and institutional or moral corruption — 腐朽的制度 "a corrupt system." In Japanese, ふきゅう (fukyū) appears in literary and classical contexts; the chengyu [朽木糞牆](../chengyu/朽木糞牆.md) invokes 朽 to evoke a person or thing beyond redemption. The compound pair (腐 "putrefied flesh" + 朽 "rotten wood") draws on the contrast between animal and vegetable decay to convey thoroughgoing deterioration.

The Life page sets it against [腐敗](腐敗.md), a near-synonym covering the same range (literal rot and figurative corruption); the difference is mainly register, with 腐朽 literary and classical and 腐敗 the plain everyday word used in ordinary political-corruption contexts. It is a subtle distinction and not a clean semantic split. The Sino-Vietnamese form is hủ hủ, since both characters read hủ, and the Korean 부후 and Cantonese fu6 nau2 follow the same pattern. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "decay, rot, corruption," in the literary register.
