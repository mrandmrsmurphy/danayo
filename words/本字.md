## Notes
- [本](../characters/本%20(char).md) "foundation" + [字](../characters/字%20(char).md) "character" — no stand-in relationship applies (both 本 and 字's own stand-ins are themselves, each with its own independent word page).

本字 means the original character — the philological term for the character that etymologically and semantically belongs to a word, as distinct from a 假借 (phonetic loan character) borrowed to write that word for its sound alone, or a later 俗字/別字 (variant or corrupted form) that replaced it in common use. This is standard vocabulary in etymological dictionaries across the Sinosphere: identifying a word's 本字 is exactly the kind of citation this vault's own `## Notes` sections perform constantly (e.g. distinguishing [[正字]] from [[別字]]).

Mandarin běnzì, Cantonese bun2 zi6, Japanese ほんじ (honji), and Korean 본자 (bonja) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and the term is genuinely used in each language's own philological tradition. Vietnamese bản tự is a defensible compositional derivation (本's primary reading bản, well attested as in 版本 "edition"; 字's classical Sino-Vietnamese reading tự, alongside its far more common native/everyday counterpart chữ). `kwin` is false per the AND-rule: 字 is individually `kwin: false`, regardless of 本's own `kwin: true`.
