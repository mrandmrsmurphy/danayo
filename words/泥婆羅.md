## Notes
- [泥 (char)](../characters/泥%20(char).md) "mud" + [婆](../characters/婆.md) "granny" + [羅](../characters/羅.md) "Rome; net; spread" — phonetic rendering of Sanskrit *Nepāla*; the characters carry no semantic content

泥婆羅 is Nepal: a phonetic rendering of Sanskrit *Nepāla*, with 泥 "mud", 婆 "granny" and 羅 "net; spread" used for sound alone. English Wiktionary has no entry for 泥婆羅; it lists the modern Chinese 尼泊爾 (an alias; Mandarin Níbó'ěr, Cantonese nei4 bok6 ji5) as the country Nepal. Chinese Wikipedia's article on Songtsen Gampo writes the old name, in its simplified form 泥婆罗, for the kingdom in his time. Japanese ネパール and Korean 네팔 write the name in sound, so the Japanese reading でいばら here is built from the characters, and the Korean field is the real name; Vietnamese Nepal is likewise the real name.

English Wikipedia says Xuanzang travelled to the country of Nepala, near the Snow Mountains, and that Bhrikuti, a Nepali princess of the Licchavi dynasty, was a queen of King Songtsen Gampo of Tibet and is credited with bringing Buddhism to Tibet. Songtsen Gampo's other foreign wife was the Chinese princess Wencheng (文成公主), so Tibet, China and Nepal were joined by these marriages. The Licchavi are of the kingdom of Nepal. Compare [[南亜]], the region, [[獅子国]], Sri Lanka, and [[孟加拉]], Bangladesh.
