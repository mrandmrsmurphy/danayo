## Notes
- [水 (char)](../characters/水%20(char).md) "water, liquid" + [銀 (char)](../characters/銀%20(char).md) "silver" — literally "water-silver," quicksilver, reflecting mercury's liquid metallic appearance.

Mandarin shuǐyín, Cantonese seoi2 ngan4, Japanese すいぎん (suigin), Korean 수은 and Vietnamese thủy ngân are all standard, fully compositional readings across the sphere, matching 水's and 銀's own stored citations; the same "water-silver" logic is independently attested in every source language, not a Dan'a'yo-only coinage. 水銀 is already the name of the metal in the early Chinese materia medica, where it is closely associated with cinnabar, the ore from which it was extracted, and with alchemical and medicinal use; the English "quicksilver" ("living silver") rests on the same image of a liquid silver-coloured metal.

As the element name, 水銀 is the word behind the symbol Hg, and 水 serves as the single-character abbreviation for mercury in the vault's periodic-table convention.
