## Notes
- [導](../characters/導.md) "guide, lead" + [演 (char)](../characters/演%20(char).md) "perform" — to guide a performance; by extension, the person who does so.

導演 is transparent and specifically Chinese: a director *leads* (導) the *performance* (演) of a film, play, or production, and the compound covers both the act of directing and the director themself with no separate word needed for either. Japanese and Korean both reach for a completely different metaphor for the same role — 監督 (かんとく / 감독), literally "supervise, oversee," borrowed from its older sense of overseeing work or sport (a baseball 監督 is a team's manager, not its "director"). Korean also has a second native option, 연출 (演出, "staging/direction"), used especially for stage and broadcast directors as distinct from film. Vietnamese sides with Chinese here rather than Japanese/Korean, keeping the same guide-plus-perform logic in đạo diễn (導 → đạo, 演 → diễn).

English Wiktionary gives the Chinese word as a verb, "to direct (a film or play)", with 執導 as a synonym (not a word in the vault), and a countable noun, "director (of film or theatre)", with the measure words 名 and 位. It lists Vietnamese đạo diễn as its only descendant, and has no Japanese or Korean entries for 導演, which agrees with the point above that Japanese and Korean use [[監督]] instead. The `japanese` (どうえん) and `korean` (도연) fields therefore hold only the Sino-Xenic readings of the characters.

Mandarin dǎoyǎn and Cantonese dou6 jin2 are as at English Wiktionary.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄉㄚㄨ⼶ㄋ (checked 2026-10-12).
