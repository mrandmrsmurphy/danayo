## Notes
- [厉 (char)](../characters/厉%20(char).md) "whetstone" — this word is the stand-in for its own character.

厉/厲 originally meant "to sharpen" (on a whetstone), later extending metonymically to the whetstone itself, and further, by an extension of the "sharp/harsh" sense, to "strict, severe" and "fierce, vigorous" (as in 再接再厲, "to redouble one's efforts with each engagement"). The character is separately loaned for its sound alone in two unrelated senses documented on its own page: 蛎/蠣 "oyster" (hence 牡蛎/牡蠣, [[牡厉]]) and "leprosy" (hence [[厉病]]).

Mandarin lì, Cantonese lai6, and Korean 려 (ryeo) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese らい (rai) is the reading used specifically in the "leprosy/disease" sense (as in 癩 rai, historically the term for leprosy) rather than the "whetstone" sense proper — an inherited quirk of on'yomi borrowing, kept as-is since it's the only attested reading for this character. Vietnamese has two genuinely distinct attested readings, both verified via hvdic.thivien.net: lệ is the standard Hán Việt reading (matching the "strict/severe" and "whetstone" senses, and the reading used in the surname 厲/Lệ), while lẹ is a Nôm reading of the same character — kept as two separate list items rather than the single comma-joined string previously stored.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 러 diverges from Sino-Korean 려 (dropping the "j"-glide). No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄌㄝ unique among words; several characters — 例, 励, 檸, 麗 — happen to share this reading, but no other *word* does).
