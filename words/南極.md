## Notes
- [南](../characters/南.md) "south" + [極 (char)](../characters/極%20(char).md) "extremity, pole" — literally "south-extremity."

南極 names the South Pole specifically — the geographic point — distinct from [[南極洲]] ("Antarctica," the continent surrounding it), the same distinction this vault keeps for the north via [[北極]] ("North Pole"), which — unlike the South Pole — sits in open ocean rather than on a continent.

Mandarin Nánjí, Cantonese naam4 gik6, Korean 남극 (namgeuk), and Japanese なんきょく (nankyoku) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday term in its language. Vietnamese nam cực is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 南's nam and one of 極's three candidates, cực. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 남긱 diverges from Sino-Korean 남극 in the second syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄋㄚㄇㄍㄧㄎ unique among both words and characters).
