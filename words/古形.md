## Notes
- [古](../characters/古.md) "ancient, old" + [形 (char)](../characters/形%20(char).md) "shape, form" — no stand-in relationship applies: 古's own `stand_in` is [[古代]] and 形's is [[形]] itself (so 古 is bound here, as in [[古語]] and [[古文]]).

古形 means **old form, archaic form**: the earlier shape in which a word, a grammatical form, or a character was written or spoken, before a later form replaced it. It is the form-level companion of [[古語]] "archaism" (an old *word*) and [[古層]] "archaic stratum." In the writing-system chapter it names the older shapes of a character (seal-script or pre-reform forms) that are gathered, together with its [[異体]] variants, under the one [[正体]] head form, in the spirit of [[異体不容]]. It is equally usable of language: a verb ending, a pronunciation or a spelling that survives only in fixed phrases is a 古形.

Japanese こけい is a standard dictionary word glossed simply "an old form or format" (古形を保つ "to preserve an archaic form"), common in grammar and historical linguistics, and, for characters, sits near 旧字体 and 古字. Chinese gǔxíng is understood compositionally ("an ancient form"); Chinese philology more often has the narrower 古字 (ancient character) and 古文 for the pre-Qin script forms. Korean 고형 is the Sino-Korean reading, and it is a homophone of 固形 (고형, "solid form," as in 고형 연료 "solid fuel") and of other 고형 words, and Dan'a'yo keeps the collision, since 固 is also stored as go (ㄍㄛ), so a coined 固形 would read goheng too and need a homophone callout. No word 固形 exists in the vault yet, so no callout is needed now.

The reading is the plain concatenation 古 go + 形 heng = goheng (고헝); Korean 고형 differs in the second syllable (헝 against 형), hence `kwin: false`. Unverified items: Wiktionary has no page for 古形 and hvdic has no entry for it, so Cantonese gu2 jing4 and Korean 고형 are compositional readings from the constituent characters (the Japanese gloss is confirmed by goo/Weblio), and Vietnamese is omitted because no attested term was found.
