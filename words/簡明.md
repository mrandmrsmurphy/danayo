## Notes
- [簡](../characters/簡.md) "simple; brief" + [明 (char)](../characters/明%20(char).md) "bright; clear" — literally "simple and clear," that is, plain in construction and easy to see through. Neither character's `stand_in` points here (簡's is [[簡単]], 明's is itself), so no legitimizer relationship applies. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 간명 matches Sino-Korean 간명 exactly.

簡明 is a coordinate compound whose two halves both describe the same virtue. 簡 is "simple, brief," and it was originally the bamboo slip (簡牘) that early Chinese writing was cut into, so brevity is in the character's history. 明 adds "clear." The resulting word describes explanations, instructions, laws, or summaries that say what they need to say and are easy to follow. Mandarin gives it the stock phrase 简明扼要, "concise and to the point." It is close to [[簡潔]] "concise, succinct," which stresses economy and cleanliness of wording. 簡明 adds that the result is also easy to understand. It differs from [[簡単]], which means "easy" or "simple" with no claim about clarity, and from [[明瞭]] and [[明白]], which say "clear" without saying "brief."

The word is standard and fully compositional in every language. Mandarin jiǎnmíng and Cantonese gaan2 ming4 are the ordinary readings, and the Mandarin entry is of a fairly formal written register. Japanese かんめい is used as both an adjective and a noun ("terseness, conciseness"), and Korean 간명 is the same word. Vietnamese giản minh is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese reading. hvdic.thivien.net has no entry for the full compound, but its 簡 page glosses 簡明 as "rõ ràng dễ hiểu," so the reading is attested. Its alias 简明 is the mainland simplified form.

Dan'a'yo uses 簡明 in the shared CJKV sense, with no narrowing or broadening. The Dan'a'yo grammar uses it to describe the language's own design goal, a "simple, clear and inclusive system." In the Valuation domain it sits with the other words of clarity.
