## Notes
- [浙](../characters/浙.md) "Zhe River; Zhejiang" + [江](../characters/江.md) "river" — "the Zhe River", the old name of the Qiantang River, which gave its name to the province. 江's own stand-in is [[大江]]; 浙 is a 名専字 (a character restricted to proper names), and this word is where the vault uses it. 淅江 and 折江 are variant spellings listed at English Wiktionary and are kept as the word's aliases.

浙江 is first of all the historical name of the Qiantang River (錢塘江), the river that runs by [[抗州]] (Hangzhou) to the sea and is famous for its tidal bore; English Wiktionary quotes the Shiji, the Shishuo Xinyu and Zhou Mi's Wulin jiushi for this sense, the last on the bore as one of the great sights of the world. It is also Zhejiang, a province of eastern China on the East China Sea coast south of the Yangtze delta, whose capital is 抗州 (Hangzhou; real spelling 杭州) (from memory, not re-checked). The province took its name from the river.

The Japanese field holds せっこう (English Wiktionary Japanese). The Korean field holds the Sino-Korean 절강, matching the Dan'a'yo 諺文 apart from the final (ㄹ for ㄷ); the usual South Korean name today is 저장 (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Chiết Giang is the Sino-Vietnamese name. Cantonese zit3 gong1 is the Jyutping on English Wiktionary. No homophones (注音 ㄐㄝㄊㄍㄚㄫ unique across both words and characters).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary Chinese (river sense and province), Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese entries (the last three give the province only); the Chinese entry notes 浙 alone as a short form of the province and of the river.
