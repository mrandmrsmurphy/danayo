## Notes
- [両 (char)](../characters/両%20(char).md) "both"

両 is 会意: 一 ("one") plus the archaic glyph 㒳, depicting a joined pair — "both, a pair." Beyond its core "both" sense, 兩/両 was historically also a unit of weight (a "tael," roughly 37.5g, still used for gold and silver in some contexts) and, as a measure word, the classifier for vehicles (輛/辆/輌, as in 一両車 "one car"); Japanese shinjitai orthography collapsed all of these historically distinct-but-related characters — see the character page's own long `aliases` list — into the single simplified form 両.

Mandarin liǎng, Cantonese loeng2, and Vietnamese lưỡng all preserve the "both" sense directly (as in 兩者, "both parties"). Japanese りょう (ryō, on'yomi) is likewise fully productive, most familiar today as the pre-yen currency unit (両, ryō) alongside the "both" sense in words like [[両親]] (ryōshin, "parents," literally "both parents"). Korean 량 (ryang) is the North Korean reading per the standing vault convention (South Korean would shift this word-initially to 양 by 두음법칙).

This word shares its exact Dan'a'yo reading (lyang/량/ㄌ⼘ㄫ) with [[梁]] ("girder") and [[糧]] ("provisions") — the group is now fully cross-referenced on all three pages. Removed the redundant `品詞` field (duplicate of `pos`) while completing this iteration.
