## Notes
- [斜](../characters/斜.md) "slant" + [坂](../characters/坂.md) "slope" — stand-in for [坂](../characters/坂.md), which cannot appear independently: its meaning (slope, incline) is identical, but 坂 alone is too bound to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 斜 is itself bound (its own `stand_in` is [傾斜](傾斜.md)).
- This is not a standard whole-word compound in any source language; the Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional, taken from each constituent character's own stored citation. There are no Japanese or Korean fields, since even a compositional reading would misleadingly suggest the compound is standard in those languages.

Vietnamese tà bản is compositional, from 斜's tà and one of 坂's three attested readings, bản (the other two, phản and phẳng, don't semantically fit "slope" and look like unrelated corpus entries).
