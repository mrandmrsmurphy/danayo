## Notes
- [斜](../characters/斜.md) "slant" + [坂](../characters/坂.md) "slope" — this word is the viable form of [坂](../characters/坂.md): the character's meaning (slope, incline) is identical, but 坂 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. Notably, 斜 is itself bounded (its own `stand_in` is [[傾斜]]) — two bounded characters legitimizing each other.
- Not attested on Wiktionary as a standard whole-word compound in any source language; Mandarin/Cantonese/Vietnamese readings are compositional, inferred from each constituent character's own stored citation. Japanese and Korean are omitted entirely, since even a compositional reading would misleadingly suggest the compound is standard in those languages, which it is not (坂 itself has real on'yomi/Sino-Korean readings, stored on its own character page, just never combined with 斜 this way).

Vietnamese tà bản is compositional, from 斜's tà and one of 坂's three attested readings, bản (the other two, phản and phẳng, don't semantically fit "slope" and look like unrelated corpus entries). `kwin` is false, consistent with the deliberate absence of a `korean` field here: 坂's own individual `kwin` is likewise false (諺文 본 vs. Sino-Korean 판 diverge). No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄙ⼘ㄅㄛㄋ unique among both words and characters).
