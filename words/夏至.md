## Notes
- [夏 (char)](../characters/夏%20(char).md) "summer" + [至 (char)](../characters/至%20(char).md) "until; arrive"

夏至 names the summer solstice — literally "summer arrives (at its peak)" — the day the sun reaches its highest point in the sky and daylight is longest in the Northern Hemisphere, one of the 24 solar terms (二十四節気/二十四节气) of the traditional East Asian lunisolar calendar, falling around June 21. It pairs with 冬至 ("winter solstice") as the two extreme poles of the solar-term calendar, both anchoring agricultural and ritual calendars across the region.

Cross-linguistically the compound is a stable Sino-Xenic loan tied to the shared solar-term system: Sino-Korean 하지 and Japanese げし both directly concatenate the component readings, and Vietnamese hạ chí likewise uses the Sino-Vietnamese reading of both characters (rather than the native hè, "summer," which appears in ordinary Vietnamese speech but not in this fixed calendrical term) as an ordinary, freely used word for the day itself.
