## Notes
- [露 (char)](../characters/露%20(char).md) "dew" + [月 (char)](../characters/月%20(char).md) "month; moon" — "dew month"

露月 (*Lùyuè*, "dew month") is one of several traditional alternative names for the tenth lunar month, alongside [[良月]] (the principal name used in this vault) and 亥月 (named for its position in the duodecimal 地支 cycle). Where 良月 names the month for the felicity of the season — harvest complete, granaries full, the world at ease — 露月 names it for a seasonal natural phenomenon, dew (the usual reading of the name; the reason for it is not spelled out in the sources consulted). This naming pattern, in which a month receives both a "character" name and a "natural phenomenon" name, recurs across the poetic calendar; compare [[氷月]] alongside [[臘月]] for the twelfth month.

露月 survives almost entirely as a literary and calendrical curiosity in the modern languages; Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese all use the numbered month (十月/시월/October, etc.) in ordinary speech. The Japanese reading recorded here, つゆつき (*tsuyutsuki*), is the compositional native reading of the two characters (つゆ "dew" + つき "month/moon"), parallel to [[良月]]'s よいつき; it is not confirmed as an attested Japanese word, since Wiktionary's 露月 entry covers Chinese only.

The Chinese status is confirmed: Wiktionary lists 露月 as an archaic proper noun for the tenth lunar month, with 良月, 亥月, 孟冬 and 陽月 as related terms, and 菊月 and 冬月 as the neighboring months. The Calendar page's month table lists it in the tenth-month row, "fine month / dew month," next to 良月. The readings are Mandarin Lùyuè, Cantonese lou6 jyut6, Korean 로월 (the North Korean form, keeping the initial 로) and Sino-Vietnamese lộ nguyệt. Dan'a'yo keeps the sense "the tenth month, the dew month."
