## Notes
- [鼎 (char)](../characters/鼎%20(char).md) "tripod" — stand-in for [[鼎 (char)|鼎]], which cannot appear independently

鼎 is 象形 of an ancient three-legged bronze ritual cauldron, one of the most culturally weighty vessel-shapes in Chinese antiquity — used for cooking sacrificial offerings and, by extension, as a symbol of state authority (a ruling dynasty's legitimacy was traditionally spoken of in terms of possessing "the nine tripods," 九鼎). **Note**: `characters/鼎 (char).md` remains genuinely unperfected (blank `pos`, `## Notes` holding only two dangling CC-initial/final wikilinks, no `date-last-perfect`) — a tenth character page found in this state this sweep — flagged for the character-perfection sweep rather than rebuilt here.

From the literal vessel, 鼎 generalized figuratively to "grand, flourishing, at the height of power" — this vault's own chengyu 春秋鼎盛 ("in the prime of life, at the height of one's powers") uses exactly this sense. Vietnamese đỉnh, picked from the character's noisier 4-item stored set (đềnh, đểnh, đễnh, đỉnh), directly parallels this figurative career: đỉnh is the ordinary Vietnamese word for "peak, summit" (đỉnh núi "mountain peak"), the same tripod-to-prominence metaphor independently echoed in the Vietnamese lexicon rather than merely borrowed as a phonetic reading. Japanese carries a single on'yomi てい (TEI), rare in ordinary vocabulary, alongside the native かなえ (kanae), a classical/archaic term for the physical vessel itself with little life in everyday speech. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 딩 (ding, from Middle Chinese 端母 t + 青開韻 eŋ) diverges from Sino-Korean 정 in the vowel.
