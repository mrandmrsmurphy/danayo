## Notes
- [七 (char)](../characters/七%20(char).md) "seven" + [千](../characters/千.md) "thousand" — a plain compositional numeral, 7 × 1,000.

Japanese ななせん uses the native counting-number replacing しち (shichi, 七's on'yomi) with なな — the same avoidance already seen with other 七-compounds, since しち can be misheard for 一 (いち, ichi) in spoken contexts, so なな is preferred whenever clarity matters, especially in numerals. Vietnamese thất thiên is the plain compositional Hán Việt concatenation — everyday Vietnamese instead counts natively (bảy nghìn/bảy ngàn), the Sino-Vietnamese form staying confined to classical/literary contexts or as a component of larger compound numerals rather than standing alone in speech. **Real `kwin` bug found and fixed**: this word had been stamped `kwin: true`, but Dan'a'yo 칟 (CID) and Sino-Korean 칠 (CIL) are different Unicode syllables — 七's own Dan'a'yo reading retains the Middle Chinese checked coda (written -d) that Sino-Korean has regularized to -l, the same [[一 (char)]]-type root-cause pattern already confirmed on several other numeral/common characters this stretch of the sweep. Corrected `kwin` to `false` here — and, checking further, `characters/七 (char).md` itself carried the identical wrong `kwin: true`, now also corrected to `false`.

**Real corpus bug found while cross-checking this word's homophones**: `words/七万.md` ("seventy thousand," 7×10,000) had its entire content mistakenly duplicated from this page — its `mandarin`/`japanese`/`korean`/`羅馬字`/`諺文`/`注音`/`english` fields all described "seven thousand" instead of "seventy thousand," with only the `characters:` list correctly naming 万. Rewritten separately in this same session with 万's own real reading rather than 千's.
