## Notes
- [開](../characters/開.md) "open" + [拓](../characters/拓.md) "expand, open up, support, push"

開拓 means to open up, pioneer, or develop (land, territory, or by extension a field of endeavor). This word sits at the center of a documented dual-reading split on 拓 itself: the character graphically collapses two originally distinct Middle Chinese etymologies — *tuò ("expand, open up, pioneer," this word's own sense) and *tà ("inscribe, take a rubbing; push, shove," as in 拓本). This vault derives 拓's own Dan'a'yo syllable (탁/tag) from the *tà ancestry, so mandarin kāituò and korean 개척 (gaecheok) — both real attested readings for this specific word's *tuò sense — genuinely diverge from what a naive concatenation of 拓's own stored character-page fields would predict; see [[拓]]'s own Notes for the full account. Cantonese hoi1tok3 and Japanese かいたく happen to concatenate cleanly regardless, since tok3/TAKU serve both senses in those languages.

Sino-Vietnamese khai thác preserves the original "open up, develop" sense but has since drifted further in everyday modern use toward "to exploit, extract, harvest" (mining, resource extraction, data) — a semantic extension of "opening up land" to "opening up and drawing out what's inside it."
