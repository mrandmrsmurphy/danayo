## Notes
- [競](../characters/競.md) "compete, contend" + [走 (char)](../characters/走%20(char).md) "run" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (競's is [[競争]], 走's is [[走]] itself), so `#cranberry` does not apply. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 走 is individually `kwin: false`.

**Fixed a real bug**: `羅馬字` had gyengjou instead of the correct gyengsou (走's own reading is sou/솟, confirmed against `syllables/ㄙㄛㄨ.md`; `諺文`/`注音` were already correct). Also normalized `japanese` from obsolete historical kana きやうそう to modern きょうそう.

競走 means a foot race — a running competition. Notably, Japanese きょうそう (kyōsō) is a genuine real-world homophone of [[競争]] ("competition" generally), since 争 and 走 happen to share the same on-reading そう (SOU) — a coincidence unrelated to the Dan'a'yo-internal system, where the two words remain distinct (注音 ㄍ⼶ㄫㄐㄚㄫ vs ㄍ⼶ㄫㄙㄛㄨ).

Mandarin jìngzǒu, Cantonese ging6 zau2, and Korean 경주 (gyeongju, note the coincidental spelling match with the city Gyeongju, an unrelated proper noun) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese cạnh tẩu is the compositional Sino-Vietnamese form; everyday Vietnamese more often uses the native chạy đua ("running race").
