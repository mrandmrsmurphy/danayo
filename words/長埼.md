## Notes
- [長](../characters/長%20(char).md) "long" + [埼](../characters/埼.md) "peninsula"

長埼 is Nagasaki, written "long cape" (the usual spelling is 長崎, kept as an alias because 崎 is itself an alias of the parent character 埼). The name is native: 長 (なが, "long") plus 埼 (さき, "cape"), said to refer to a long promontory.

Nagasaki was Japan's only window to the West during the Edo seclusion, through the Dutch trading post on Dejima, and the second atomic bomb fell here on 9 August 1945. It holds the Glover Garden, the Hidden Christian sites, Unzen volcano and Hashima, the "Battleship Island".

In Dan'a'yo 長埼 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 나가사키 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
