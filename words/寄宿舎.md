## Notes
- [寄 (char)](../characters/寄%20(char).md) "approach; send; rely on" + [宿](../characters/宿.md) "lodging" + [舎](../characters/舎.md) "inn; hut; mansion"

寄宿舎 extends [[寄宿]] ("to lodge, board") with 舎 ("building, hall"), naming the physical building itself rather than the act of lodging in it — the standard word for a boarding house or dormitory building. None of its three constituent characters' own `stand_in` fields point here (寄's is itself, 宿's is 寄宿, 舎's is 宿舎), so unlike its shorter sibling 寄宿, this word doesn't legitimize any of them — it's a further compositional extension built once all three already had independent standing.

Japanese きしゅくしゃ (kishukusha) and Korean 기숙사 are both plain, everyday, extremely common words for "dormitory" in their respective languages — 기숙사 in particular is basic, universal vocabulary in Korean, used of school and company dormitories alike. Vietnamese kí túc xá (Wiktionary-confirmed; also commonly spelled ký túc xá, an ordinary i/y orthographic variation) is likewise the standard modern Vietnamese term, and per the finding on [[寄宿]]'s own page, is itself traced to a borrowing of this exact Japanese compound rather than a separate Sino-Vietnamese coinage. No HSK level is given for the compound; no homophones (注音 ㄍㄨㄧㄙㄨㄎㄙ⼘ unique across both words and characters).
