## Notes
- [套 (char)](characters/套%20(char).md) "covering; case" — stand-in for [[套 (char)|套]], which cannot appear independently

套 is a 會意 compound of 大 "big" and 镸 "long" — something long and large — and its earliest attested sense was a dialect word for "long, big" before it was repurposed for its now-dominant meaning: an outer covering or sheath (筆套 "pen cap," 手套 "glove," 外套 "overcoat"), and by extension a matched *set* of things, which made it one of Mandarin's most common measure words (一套書 "a set of books," 一套茶具 "a tea set," 一套理論 "a whole theory"). A further figurative layer gives "convention, formula" — 老套 "clichéd," 客套 "polite formula," 套話 "stock phrases."

The word's fortunes differ sharply across the sphere. Vietnamese sáo is fully alive in exactly the Chinese senses: thủ sáo (手套) "glove," bút sáo (筆套) "pen cap," khách sáo (客套) "polite formality," lão sáo (老套) "cliché," and nhất sáo (一套) as a native classifier "a set of." Japanese, by contrast, barely uses the character at all — it is a 人名用漢字 (name-use kanji) with on'yomi トウ, the everyday concepts being covered by カバー, 袋, and 組 — and Korean 투 (套) survives mainly in Sino-Korean compounds. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 톳 (tou) differs from Sino-Korean 투 in both vowel and coda.
