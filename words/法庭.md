## Notes
- [法](../characters/法%20(char).md) "rule, law" + [庭](../characters/庭.md) "courtyard" — neither character's own `stand_in` points here (法's own is itself; 庭's own is [[中庭]]); 法庭 is an independent compound. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: `characters/法 (char).md` is `kwin: false`, `characters/庭.md` is `kwin: false` → compound `false`.

法庭 is Dan'a'yo's general word for "court, courtroom" — the legal-proceeding sense, as distinct from [[裁判所]] "courthouse," the physical building. Mandarin fǎtíng is the standard real word for this concept (also used colloquially for "court" in general, as in 上法庭 "to go to court"), though modern Mandarin more precisely reserves it for the courtroom specifically, contrasting it with 法院 "the court as an institution." Cantonese faat3 ting4 and Korean 법정 (beopjeong, also real and standard) are directly compositional.

**A real cross-script curiosity**: the actual Japanese word for this concept is written 法廷, using [[廷]] ("hall of government, imperial court") rather than Dan'a'yo's own [[庭]] ("courtyard") — two different characters that happen to be perfect on'yomi homophones (both てい/TEI), so 法庭 and 法廷 sound identical in Japanese (ほうてい) despite being written differently. Dan'a'yo follows the Mandarin orthographic choice (庭) since that character was already established in this vault.

Vietnamese pháp đình is the compositional Hán Việt reading; living Vietnamese instead reaches for the native construction tòa án for "court" in general use.
