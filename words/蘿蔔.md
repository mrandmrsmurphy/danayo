## Notes
- [蘿](../characters/蘿.md) + [蔔](../characters/蔔.md) "radish" — this word is the `stand_in` legitimizer for [[蔔]], which cannot appear independently

**Real content bug found and fixed, explaining why this page had stayed open**: the word's own `羅馬字`/`諺文`/`注音` used the second syllable 복/bok/ㄅㄛㄎ, but `蔔`'s own character page stores a different Dan'a'yo reading for itself — 북/bug/ㄅㄨㄎ (both `ㄅㄛㄎ.md` and `ㄅㄨㄎ.md` are real syllable pages in this vault, so the mismatch wasn't a missing-syllable problem, just a wrong-syllable citation). Corrected to `labug`/`라북`/`ㄌㄚㄅㄨㄎ`, matching 蔔's own ground truth exactly. This flips `kwin`: 蔔 on its own is `kwin: false` (Dan'a'yo 북 vs Sino-Korean 복 diverge), and that divergence carries through to the compound — the word's own `korean` field (라복, a real attested Sino-Korean concatenation of 蘿's 라 + 蔔's 복) no longer matches the corrected Dan'a'yo 諺文 (라북), so `kwin` changes from the previous `true` to the correct `false`.

蘿蔔 is a synonym-compound name for the radish, attested with the alternate spellings 蘿菔/蘆菔 in classical Chinese sources — `vietnamese: la bặc` was independently confirmed via web search as a genuinely attested Hán Việt compound reading (alongside the related hồng la bặc, "carrot," literally "red radish"), not merely the mechanical concatenation of 蔔's own stored bốc. Japanese らふく is a direct on'yomi reading rather than the more colloquial native だいこん (daikon), the everyday Japanese word for radish — a case where this vault's compositional Sino-Japanese reading and ordinary spoken Japanese diverge more than usual.
