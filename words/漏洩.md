## Notes
- [漏 (char)](../characters/漏%20(char).md) "leak, drip" + [洩](../characters/洩.md) "vent, leak out"

漏洩 pairs two characters that both independently mean "to leak," giving the compound a doubled, intensified sense — most often used not for liquids but for information: 情報漏洩 (information leak), 秘密の漏洩 (disclosure of a secret), 機密漏洩 (leak of classified material). 漏 itself is built around 屚 (a roof, 尸, with rain, 雨, falling through it — a literal leaking house) plus the water radical, and survives independently in [[漏]]'s cross-linked compound [[滲漏]] ("seepage, leakage").

洩 has a graphic variant, 泄, and the compound has the matching spelling 漏泄, recorded here as a word-level alias; both forms remain common enough in different regions and registers that neither displaces the other as canonical. (渫, a different character meaning "to dredge," is not a variant of 洩.) Because the variant carries a different reading, the compound has a secondary Japanese reading ろうせつ beside the more common ろうえい, and the Cantonese reading lau6 sit3 reflects the same variant ambiguity.

The Water page lists 漏洩 only in its See Also, noting that it has drifted almost entirely away from liquids in modern usage, so the literal water sense survives mainly in [漏](漏.md) and [滲漏](滲漏.md), while 漏洩 is the standard word for a leak of information. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "leak, disclosure," with the information sense as the main one. The Korean reading recorded here, 루설, is the North Korean form (South Korean writes 누설).
