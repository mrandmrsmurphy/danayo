## Notes
- [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) "big" + [食 (char)](../characters/食%20(char).md) "eat" — "to binge eat, overeat" (大食症, the medical term for bulimia/binge-eating disorder). `vietnamese: đại thực` is compositional with the same medical-register sense; everyday Vietnamese instead says ăn nhiều/ham ăn (native) for casual overeating (domain knowledge, this session's search quota exhausted). Fixed `characters` to use `(char)` filename forms. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 대식 matches Sino-Korean 대식 exactly. No homophones (注音 ㄉㄚㄧㄙㄧㄎ unique among both words and characters).

**Tajik.** 大食 is also a proper noun in Dan'a'yo: the Tajik people, and as an adjective "Tajik". The choice is deliberate, and a return to the word's origin. English Wiktionary gives Chinese 大食 (Dàshí; Cantonese daai6 sik6) as an archaic Tang-dynasty word for the Abbasid caliphate, borrowed from Pahlavi *Tāčīk, Tāzīg* and cognate with English "Tajik", and calls it a doublet of 塔吉克, the modern transliteration of Tajik. The Chinese word was used for the Arabs and their empire, since Persian speakers used the Tajik name for the Arabs before it narrowed to the Persian-speaking people of Central Asia (from memory, not re-checked). Dan'a'yo takes the word back to Tajik, and the country is [[大食国]].

The two senses share one spelling and one reading, as other names do in this vault, and context chooses. The Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese fields hold only the "gluttony" reading (たいしょく, 대식, đại thực), following the vault's convention that those fields carry the Sino-Xenic reading; the real names of the Tajik people are タジク (Japanese) and 타지크 (Korean). `kwin` is unchanged.
