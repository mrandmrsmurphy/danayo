## Notes
- [岐](../characters/岐%20(char).md) "fork (in road)" + [阜](../characters/阜.md) "mound"

岐阜 is Gifu, a name made in 1567 when Oda Nobunaga took the castle town of Inabayama and renamed it after the Chinese Qishan (岐山), where the Zhou dynasty began, and Qufu (曲阜), the birthplace of Confucius (from memory, not re-checked). 阜 ("mound") is a character used almost only in this name, which is why it is listed among the name-only characters; the reading ぎふ is on-reading throughout.

Gifu is landlocked, with the Nagara River known for cormorant fishing, the thatched farmhouses of Shirakawa-gō, and the old town of Takayama. 

In Dan'a'yo 岐阜 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 기후 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
