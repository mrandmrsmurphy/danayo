## Notes
- [肺](../characters/肺.md) "lungs" + [府](../characters/府.md) "hollow organ" (Dan'a'yo stand-in for 腑) — 肺's own stand-in is [[肺臓]]; 府's is [[政府]].

肺腑 (written 肺府 here) has two senses.

1. the lungs and the internal organs, the lungs standing for the organs of the chest in general (literary, anatomical).
2. the innermost part of the heart, where one's deepest feelings lie, as in 肺腑之言 "words from the bottom of one's heart".

腑 has no character page of its own in Dan'a'yo, so 府 stands in for it, the same way as in [[六府]] "the six hollow organs"; 府 carries 腑 as an alias, and the spelling 肺腑 is kept as the word's alias. The sound is the same in Dan'a'yo. Wiktionary adds two more Chinese literary senses, "a trustworthy person" and, archaically, "a relative of the emperor"; Dan'a'yo does not use those. The plain word for the lungs alone is [[肺臓]].

Mandarin fèifǔ, Cantonese fai3 fu2, Japanese はいふ and Korean 폐부 are all listed on Wiktionary for 肺腑. Vietnamese phế phủ is the compositional Sino-reading and was not checked against a dictionary.
