## Notes
- [蔓](../characters/蔓.md) "spread" + [延](../characters/延.md) "prolong"
- This word is the viable form of [蔓](../characters/蔓.md): the character's meaning (to spread, grow) is identical, but 蔓 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 蔓延 is its `stand_in` compound.

蔓 is the trailing stem of a creeping plant, a vine or runner, and as a verb is to creep and trail; 延 is to extend or stretch out. 蔓延 pictures a vine's way of growth: spreading outward along the ground in all directions, with no single leading edge. It was first said of plants and still is (weeds, ivy), and by extension covers anything that spreads unchecked: disease, fire, rumor, corruption, unrest. The modern usage skews negative, and the word is a staple of news writing about epidemics and crime.

It is therefore distinct from [延長](延長.md) "extension, prolongation," which lengthens something deliberately along one line, and from [拡散](拡散.md) "diffusion, spreading out," which can describe a neutral physical or informational dispersal where 蔓延 implies an unwelcome growth. The shared readings are Mandarin mànyán, Cantonese maan6 jin4, Japanese man'en (まんえん) and Korean 만연. Dan'a'yo keeps the shared sense, "spread, proliferate," with its negative tone as ordinary usage and no formal divergence. It is placed under [Dimensions](../lexipedia/Dimensions.md).
