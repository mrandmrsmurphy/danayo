## Notes
- [陰 (char)](../characters/陰%20(char).md) "Yin, shade" + [性](../characters/性.md) "nature, gender"

陰性 has two related but distinct uses. In traditional yin-yang and grammatical/biological gender contexts, it denotes the "yin," feminine, or negative pole — paired against 陽性 ("yang, masculine, positive"). In modern medical usage, it means a negative test result (陰性反応, "negative reaction/result"), the opposite of 陽性 in that sense too. Mandarin 陰性/阴性 (yīnxìng), Cantonese 陰性 (jam1sing3), Japanese 陰性 (いんせい), Korean 음성 (eumseong), and Sino-Vietnamese âm tính all share both senses across the CJKV sphere, with the medical "negative result" sense now the far more common usage in everyday speech (especially after its wide use during infectious-disease testing).
