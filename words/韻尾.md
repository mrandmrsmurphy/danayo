## Notes
- [韻](../characters/韻.md) "rhyme" + [尾 (char)](../characters/尾%20(char).md) "tail" — no stand-in relationship applies (韻's own stand-in is [[押韻]]; 尾 is its own stand-in).

韻尾 is the "tail of the rhyme": the coda, the sound that closes a syllable after its nucleus. It is the third part of a [[韻母]] ("rhyme, final"), after the glide and the nucleus, and it is the element itself, where [[尾位]] names the *slot* it fills, as [[介音]] is the glide and 介位 its slot. In Dan'a'yo's syllable structure the coda is optional and may be one of three things: a nasal (ㄇ, ㄋ, ㄫ), a following glide (the high-vowel coda y or w, see [[滑音]]), or an unreleased voiceless stop (ㄆ, ㄊ, ㄎ; see [[放出]]). The syllable-structure table on the phonology page lists these as the 韻尾, m, n, ŋ, u, i, p, t, k, or ∅ for none. The velar nasal ㄫ occurs only here, never as an onset.

Chinese linguistics traditionally organizes the syllable around initial and final (声母/韻母), and 韻尾 is its standard term for the closing element; Wiktionary glosses it "coda" in both Chinese (Mandarin yùnwěi, Cantonese wan6 mei5, with 韵尾 the simplified form, kept as an alias) and Japanese (いんび, inbi). Korean 운미 is the compositional Sino-Korean reading of the same characters and the standard term in Korean phonology, but it was not separately verified in a dictionary. Wiktionary and hvdic have no Korean or Vietnamese entry, so the `vietnamese` field is omitted. No homophones (注音 ㄨㄋㄇㄨㄧ unique across both words and characters).
