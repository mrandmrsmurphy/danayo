## Notes
- [福](../characters/福%20(char).md) "blessing" + [岡](../characters/岡.md) "hill"

福岡 is Fukuoka, written "fortune hill". The castle town was named in 1601 by Kuroda Nagamasa after his family's ancestral village of Fukuoka in Bizen, now in Okayama (from memory, not re-checked). The reading ふくおか is the on-reading 福 (ふく) plus the native 岡 (おか).

Fukuoka is the largest city of Kyushu, with Hakata as its port and rail hub, which has traded with Korea and China since ancient times. It is known for Hakata ramen and mentaiko, the shrine Dazaifu Tenmangū.

In Dan'a'yo 福岡 is the place name only, a proper noun kept in the Japanese form; the unit it names is a [県](県.md), an ordinary prefecture. The Mandarin, Cantonese and Vietnamese readings are compositional Sino-Xenic readings of the characters (from memory, not re-checked), and the Korean 후쿠오카 is the standard Korean transliteration of the Japanese name, not a Sino-Korean reading.
