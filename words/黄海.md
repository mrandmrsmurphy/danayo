## Notes
- [黄](../characters/黄%20(char).md) "yellow" + [海](../characters/海.md) "sea, ocean" — no `#cranberry`: both 黄's and 海's own `stand_in` point elsewhere; neither points here.

Named for the yellow-brown color the Yellow River's silt sediment gives the water. Mandarin Huáng Hǎi, Cantonese wong4hoi2, Japanese こうかい, and Sino-Vietnamese Hoàng Hải are all standard, fully compositional readings. Koreans instead call it 西海 (Seohae, "West Sea"), reflecting its position relative to the Korean peninsula rather than China. **Not an alias**: [[西海]] was previously listed here as an alias, but it is a separate, independent word denoting the classical "Western Sea" of the 四海 — a distinct concept from the Yellow Sea that only happens to share this modern Korean place-name usage, as 西海's own page explicitly notes.

As a Korean proper noun, 黄海 (Hwanghae) is also the bare name of a historical province on the west coast of the peninsula, which takes 道 "province" to make 黄海道. The name joins the first characters of its two chief towns, 黃州 (Hwangju) and [[海州]] (Haeju) (from memory, not re-checked). The province was divided in 1954 into North Hwanghae (黄海北道, capital [[沙里院]] Sariwon) and South Hwanghae (黄海南道, capital Haeju), both in North Korea (from memory, not re-checked). The Korean reading 황해 is the same for the sea and the province, and English Wiktionary's Korean entry 황해 is for the Yellow Sea itself, so 西海 above is an alternative Korean name, not the only one. The province sense is from memory, not re-checked on a dictionary page.

1. the Yellow Sea, between China and the Korean peninsula.
2. Hwanghae, the historical province of Korea (now North and South Hwanghae, both in North Korea).
