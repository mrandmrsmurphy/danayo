## Notes
- [海](../characters/海.md) "sea" + [狗](../characters/狗.md) "dog" — literally "sea-dog," the fur seal (named for its dog-like bark and face).

Mandarin hǎigǒu, Cantonese hoi2 gau2, and Vietnamese hải cẩu are all standard, fully compositional readings, matching 海's and 狗's own stored citations exactly — Vietnamese hải cẩu shares the exact same "sea-dog" literal construction as Dan'a'yo's own word. **Found and fixed a real bug**: `korean` was stored as "해구,물개," mixing the real compositional Sino-Korean word (해구) together with a native vernacular alternative (물개, the far more common everyday Korean word for "seal," built from native 물 "water" + 개 "dog") — the latter belongs in Notes prose, not the `korean` field. Japanese オットセイ (ottosei) is a native/loan term, unrelated to either character's own on'yomi.

`kwin` is false: 狗 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page, which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 海 is independently kwin-true. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄚㄧㄍㄛㄨ unique among both words and characters).
