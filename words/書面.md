## Notes
- [書](../characters/書.md) "book, to write" + [面](../characters/面.md) "face, surface" — "written," as opposed to spoken.

書面 means "written, in written form" (書面語, "written language," as distinct from spoken registers; 書面通知, "written notice"). Neither constituent character's `stand_in` points to this compound ([[書]]'s own is 書本; [[面]]'s is 表面), so both stand independently of 書面.

Mandarin shūmiàn and Cantonese syu1 min6(-2) preserve the sense directly. Japanese しょめん (shomen) is standard vocabulary (書面審査, "written examination/review"). Korean 서면 (seomyeon) parallels the Sino-xenic reading and is likewise standard, especially in formal/legal contexts (서면 통지, "written notice"). Vietnamese thư diện is directly attested in Hán Nôm dictionary sources, glossed "in writing, on paper" — but it is a classical/literary Sino-Vietnamese compound rather than live colloquial vocabulary; modern Vietnamese normally says văn bản ("document, written text") or bằng văn bản ("in writing," common in legal/formal register) for this concept. On the constituent readings: 書's other candidate, thơ (native "poem, letter"), is a Nôm/vernacular word that historically borrowed 書's glyph for phonetic writing rather than a genuine Hán Việt doublet, so it was not used here; 面's other candidate, miến, is real but belongs to 面's role as the simplified form of 麵 ("noodles"), not to the "face, surface" sense relevant to this word, so diện was used instead.
