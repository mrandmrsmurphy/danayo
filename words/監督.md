## Notes
- [監](../characters/監.md) "oversee" + [督](../characters/督.md) "supervise" — this word is the stand-in that legitimizes the bound character [監](../characters/監.md), which cannot appear independently.

Japanese and Korean both broadened this compound well beyond plain oversight: かんとく/감독 cover "supervisor," a sports team's "coach/manager," and — most distinctively — a film's "director," all under the same word. Mandarin jiāndū never made that last leap; Chinese keeps 监督 strictly in the administrative/regulatory sense ("supervise, oversight, supervisor") and uses a separate word, 导演, for a film director. Cantonese gaam1 duk1 follows Mandarin here rather than Japanese/Korean. Vietnamese giám đốc narrows the compound a different way again: rather than "supervisor" in general, it's the everyday, extremely common word for a company's director or CEO — the specific business-executive sense has crowded out the broader oversight meaning in ordinary usage.

English Wiktionary corrects one point of the paragraph above. Its Chinese entry (监督) does list the film-director sense, marked as ACG (anime, comics and games) usage and explained as a wasei-kango loan from Japanese かんとく, alongside the ordinary Chinese verb "to supervise; to superintend; to oversee; to regulate" (證券監督管理機構, "securities regulatory agency") and noun "supervisor; overseer". It also notes a Wikimedia-specific verb, "to oversight" content. Its Japanese entry glosses かんとく as "director, supervisor, manager, superintendent", with examples of a film director and a baseball team's manager, and "direction; supervision; control". Chinese speakers outside ACG still say 導演 for a film director, so the paragraph's main point holds for ordinary Mandarin.

Korean 감독 and Vietnamese giám đốc are listed as descendants. Mandarin jiāndū and Cantonese gaam1 duk1 are as at English Wiktionary. The Directions page lists the word as "to direct, supervise, manage", and [[導演]] is the Dan'a'yo word for a film director.

No other word shares the 注音 ㄍㄚㄇㄉㄛㄎ (checked 2026-10-12).
