## Notes
- [閏 (char)](../characters/閏%20(char).md) "intercalary" + [年 (char)](../characters/年%20(char).md) "year" — literally "intercalary year."

閏年 names the leap year — the direct opposite of an ordinary year, [[平年]]. The intercalary concept 閏 itself generalizes to lunar calendars too, where a "leap year" instead means one with an extra intercalary *month* rather than an extra day, since a purely lunar year runs shorter than the solar year and periodically needs a whole extra month inserted to stay aligned with the seasons.

Mandarin rùnnián, Cantonese jeon6 nin4, Korean 윤년 (yullyeon), and Japanese じゅんねん (junnen) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday term for "leap year" in its language. Vietnamese nhuận niên is likewise standard Sino-Vietnamese, matching 閏's nhuận (one of two attested readings) and 年's own niên; everyday Vietnamese instead uses the calque năm nhuận, mixing the Sino-Vietnamese nhuận with native năm ("year"). `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 눈넌 diverges from Sino-Korean 윤년 in both syllables. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄋㄨㄋㄋㄝㄋ unique among both words and characters).
