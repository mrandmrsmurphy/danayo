## Notes
- [密 (char)](../characters/密%20(char).md) "dense; thick; close; intimate; secret" + [陀](../characters/陀.md) "rough terrain" + [僧](../characters/僧.md) "priest; monk; bonze"

密陀僧 is litharge — lead(II) oxide, a substance used since antiquity in glassmaking, pottery glazes, and traditional medicine. Unlike a normal compositional word, none of the three characters contribute their own meaning here: 密陀僧 is a phonetic transliteration (Wiktionary-confirmed) of Persian مردار سنگ (mordār-sang, literally "dead/impure stone"), borrowed into Chinese along the same overland trade routes that carried the mineral itself — cognate forms exist in Urdu (murdā-sang), Hindi (murdāsaṅkh), and Sanskrit. The three characters were chosen purely for their sounds (mì-tuó-sēng approximating mordar-sang), the same principle behind other sound-based transliterations rather than sense-based compounds.

**Known open reading question, not resolved here**: 僧's contribution to this word's `諺文`/`羅馬字`/`注音` (숭/sung/ㄙㄨㄫ) does not match 僧's own canonical character-level reading (승/sǝng/ㄙㄜㄫ, the regular outcome of its phonetic family 曽/増/憎/層/謄/騰/贈). [[僧]]'s own Notes already flag this exact cross-word inconsistency — the same "sung" variant also appears in [[僧侶]] and [[尼僧]], while [[僧家]]/[[阿僧祇]] use the regular "seng" form and [[僧伽]] is internally self-contradictory — and call for a dedicated word-sweep pass to resolve rather than a one-page fix. Left as originally stored here, consistent with that standing flag; the Korean field (밀타승) is unaffected, since it independently uses 僧's own regular Sino-Korean 승 rather than the Dan'a'yo-internal "sung" variant. Japanese みつだそう and Vietnamese mật đà tăng are both attested as the standard terms for litharge in their respective languages. No HSK level applies to a term this specialized; no homophones (注音 ㄇㄧㄊㄉㄚㄙㄨㄫ unique across both words and characters).
