## Notes
- [壌](../characters/壌.md) "soil" + [土 (char)](../characters/土%20(char).md) "earth"
- This word is the viable form of [壌](../characters/壌.md): the character's meaning (soil, loam) is identical, but 壌 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 壌土 is its `stand_in` compound.

壌土 is the technical word for loam: soil that is a fairly even mix of sand, silt and clay, crumbly and good for farming. Mandarin rǎngtǔ (also written 壤土), Cantonese joeng6 tou2 and Korean 양토 (yangto) are the same compound, used in agronomy and soil science. The bound character 壌 means cultivated, soft soil as opposed to bare ground; it is also the 壤 of 土壌 ("soil").

In Dan'a'yo it is the specific, technical soil-type word, narrower than [[土]] (earth in general) and drier and more fertile than [[泥]] (mud, wet earth). No narrower or shifted sense is needed beyond that.
