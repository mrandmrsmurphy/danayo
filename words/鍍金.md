## Notes
- [鍍](../characters/鍍.md) "to coat; gild" + [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "gold; metal"

鍍金 means to gild: to coat a surface with a thin layer of gold, and the gilding itself. English Wiktionary glosses Chinese 鍍金 (an alias, 镀金; Mandarin dùjīn, Cantonese dou6 gam1) as "to gild; to gold plate", and Japanese ときん as "plating, gilding; to plate" (the common Japanese word for plating is めっき). Korean 도금 is "plating, gilding". Both languages also use the word figuratively: Chinese, derogatorily, for putting on a golden facade, especially going overseas to study to polish one's qualifications without real ability, and Japanese めっき for "pretense", a thin surface over something plain.

This word is the viable form of [鍍](../characters/鍍.md): the character's meaning (to gild) is identical, but 鍍 alone is too bounded to stand as a Dan'a'yo word. 金, "gold; metal", provides the object that completes the verb-object compound.
