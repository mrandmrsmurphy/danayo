## Notes
- [締 (char)](../characters/締%20(char).md) "tie, conclude" + [切 (char)](../characters/切%20(char).md) "cut off"

締切 names the cutoff moment itself — the exact point at which submissions, applications, or an opportunity close — emphasizing the "cut off" boundary rather than the whole time period leading up to it, unlike [[期限]] (which names the bounded period/deadline more generally). Common on Japanese forms and notices: 締切日 (shimekiribi, "the cutoff date"), 応募締切 (ōbo shimekiri, "application deadline").

Note: Japanese reads しめきり (shimekiri), a native compound rather than an on'yomi reading. Korean 체절 is a standard compositional Sino-Korean reading. **`vietnamese` intentionally left blank**: no attested Sino-Vietnamese compound for this specific Japanese term could be confirmed; Vietnamese uses the native hạn chót or hạn cuối ("final deadline") instead. No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄊㄝㄧㄑㄝㄊ unique across both words and characters).
