## Notes
- [皿 (char)](../characters/皿%20(char).md) "dish; container" — stand-in for [[皿 (char)|皿]], which cannot appear independently

皿 is 象形, depicting a shallow footed vessel or dish seen in profile — the radical (itself [[Radical 108|皿]]) that anchors the corpus's general vocabulary for open containers rather than any single specific vessel type. Unlike most of the concrete-object characters covered so far in this sweep, 皿 stays almost entirely within its literal container sense across the whole CJKV sphere, without the rich figurative afterlife seen on characters like [[日]]/[[月]]/[[白]]. **Note**: `characters/皿 (char).md` is itself still genuinely unperfected — blank `pos`, no real `## Notes` content beyond two dangling, unlinked-to-any-bullet Middle Chinese initial/final wikilinks — flagged here for the separate character-perfection sweep rather than fixed inside this word-creation pass, though its individual scalar fields remained reliable ground truth for this word page.

Japanese lists two on'yomi, ばい (BAI) and みょう (MYOU), but neither is genuinely productive in ordinary usage — the native 訓読み さら (sara) dominates completely (皿洗い sara-arai "dishwashing," 灰皿 haizara "ashtray," even compounds that might be expected to use an on'yomi instead default to さら) — a more lopsided native/Sino-Japanese imbalance than any other character covered in this sweep. Vietnamese mãnh is the confirmed Sino-Vietnamese reading for this specific "dish/container" sense (per Hán Việt reference dictionaries), picked over four other variants stored on the character's own page (mạnh, mảng, mảnh, mịn) — all four are real Vietnamese words but belong to unrelated homophone characters (mạnh "strong," mảnh "fragment," etc.), the same kind of stored-field contamination already documented multiple times elsewhere in this vault's history. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 명 (myeng, from Middle Chinese 明母 m + 庚三開韻 ɣiæŋ) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 명. 皿 shares its Dan'a'yo reading with two other standalone words, [[明]] ("bright") and [[鳴]] ("cry, chirp") — a three-way coincidental convergence, unrelated etymologically to any of them.
