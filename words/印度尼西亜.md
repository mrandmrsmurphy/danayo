## Notes
- [印](../characters/印.md) "seal (India, clipped)" + [度](../characters/度.md) "degree" + [尼](../characters/尼.md) "nun (transliteration syllable)" + [西](../characters/西.md) "west (transliteration syllable)" + [亜](../characters/亜.md) "secondary (transliteration syllable)" — a full phonetic transliteration of "Indonesia," not a semantic compound; only 印 (borrowed from 印度, "India") carries any real meaning here, tracing the old geographic association between the Indonesian archipelago and India via the Sanskrit-derived name itself (Indonesia < Latin *Indus* + Greek *nesos*, "islands").

印度尼西亜 is the full transliterated name for Indonesia, most often clipped in everyday Mandarin and Cantonese to [[印尼]], much as "the United States of America" is rarely spelled out in speech. Japanese and Korean both prefer the katakana/hangul phonetic loanword directly — インドネシア and 인도네시아 respectively — over any kanji/hanja transliteration, so the kanji form 印度尼西亜 seen here is a formal, largely written convention rather than an everyday spoken form in either language. Vietnamese similarly renders the name as a syllable-by-syllable phonetic transcription, In-đô-nê-xi-a, rather than through Sino-Vietnamese readings of the individual characters.
