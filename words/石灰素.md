## Notes
- [[石灰]] "quicklime" + [素](../characters/素.md) "element" (functioning as the productive "-ium" element suffix) — literally "lime element." No cranberry: neither constituent's own `stand_in` points here.
- Avoids the Chinese invented character 鈣／钙, coining a transparent native compound instead — the same naming strategy used throughout this vault's periodic table (compare [[苦土素]] for magnesium, avoiding 鎂/镁).

石灰素 names calcium (Ca, atomic number 20), the element historically isolated from lime (石灰, calcium oxide/hydroxide) — the same lime-to-element relationship, and the same etymological logic, that produces English "calcium" itself (from Latin calx, "lime"). Pronunciation fields (seghoiso/석회소/ㄙㄝㄎㄏㄛㄧㄙㄛ) are the straightforward concatenation of each constituent's own stored reading.

Mandarin gài and Cantonese koi3 are the real, standard readings for calcium in those languages (of 鈣/钙, the character this vault's compound deliberately avoids in writing while still matching its pronunciation — the same convention already established on [[苦土素]]). Korean 칼슘 (kalsyum) and Vietnamese canxi are both the real, standard loanword terms (from English "calcium" and French "calcium" respectively), not Sino-Korean/Sino-Vietnamese compounds — Korean and Vietnamese chemical nomenclature draws on Western loanwords far more freely than Chinese or Japanese. **Corrected 2026-09-22**: `japanese` previously also listed せっかいそ (sekkaiso) alongside カルシウム — a superficially plausible on'yomi compositional reading of 石灰素's own characters, but not a real, attested Japanese chemical term (confirmed via web search: カルシウム is the sole standard Japanese name for the element); removed, matching the same real-term-only convention already established and documented on [[苦土素]] (which similarly removed a fabricated compositional reading, くどそ, in favor of the genuine マグネシウム).

No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄙㄝㄎㄏㄛㄧㄙㄛ unique among both words and characters).
