## Notes
- [石 (char)](../characters/石%20(char).md) "stone" + [灰 (char)](../characters/灰%20(char).md) "ash" (together the word [石灰](石灰.md) "quicklime") + [素](../characters/素.md) "element" (functioning as the productive "-ium" element suffix) — literally "lime element."
- Avoids the Chinese invented character 鈣／钙, coining a transparent native compound instead — the same naming strategy used throughout this vault's periodic table (compare [苦土素](苦土素.md) for magnesium, avoiding 鎂/镁).

石灰素 names calcium (Ca, atomic number 20), the element historically isolated from lime (石灰, calcium oxide/hydroxide) — the same lime-to-element relationship, and the same etymological logic, that produces English "calcium" itself (from Latin calx, "lime"). Pronunciation fields (seghoiso/석회소/ㄙㄝㄎㄏㄛㄧㄙㄛ) are the straightforward concatenation of each constituent's own stored reading.

Mandarin gài and Cantonese koi3 are the real, standard readings for calcium in those languages (of 鈣/钙, the character this vault's compound deliberately avoids in writing while still matching its pronunciation — the same convention already established on [苦土素](苦土素.md)). Korean 칼슘 (kalsyum) and Vietnamese canxi are both the real, standard loanword terms (from English "calcium" and French "calcium" respectively), not Sino-Korean/Sino-Vietnamese compounds — Korean and Vietnamese chemical nomenclature draws on Western loanwords far more freely than Chinese or Japanese.
