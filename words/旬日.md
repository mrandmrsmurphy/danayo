## Notes
- [旬](../characters/旬.md) "10-day period" + [日 (char)](../characters/日%20(char).md) "day" — stand-in for [旬](../characters/旬.md), which cannot appear independently.

旬日 names the traditional ten-day period of the Chinese lunisolar calendar, the unit each month was historically divided into three of (上旬/中旬/下旬, "early/mid/late month" — all cross-referenced on 旬's own page). Days within a 旬 were traditionally named using the 十干 heavenly stems.

Mandarin xúnrì, Cantonese ceon4 jat6, and Japanese じゅんじつ (junjitsu) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese tuần nhật is likewise compositional; notably, tuần itself has undergone semantic drift in modern Vietnamese away from its original "ten-day period" sense to mean "week" generally (tuần is the everyday Vietnamese word for "week"), a shift not shared by its Mandarin/Cantonese/Japanese/Korean cognates. Korean 순일 (sunil, from 旬's 순 + 日's 일) is the compositional Sino-Korean reading; the native 열흘 ("ten days") is held on 旬's own `korean_native` field.
