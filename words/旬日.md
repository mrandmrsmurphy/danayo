## Notes
- [旬](../characters/旬.md) "10-day period" (stand-in for this word — [[旬日]] is itself the legitimizing compound for the bound character 旬, per its own stored `stand_in`) + [日 (char)](../characters/日%20(char).md) "day."

旬日 names the traditional ten-day period of the Chinese lunisolar calendar, the unit each month was historically divided into three of (上旬/中旬/下旬, "early/mid/late month" — all cross-referenced on 旬's own page). Days within a 旬 were traditionally named using the 十干 heavenly stems.

Mandarin xúnrì, Cantonese ceon4 jat6, and Japanese じゅんじつ (junjitsu) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese tuần nhật is likewise compositional; notably, tuần itself has undergone semantic drift in modern Vietnamese away from its original "ten-day period" sense to mean "week" generally (tuần is the everyday Vietnamese word for "week"), a shift not shared by its Mandarin/Cantonese/Japanese/Korean cognates. **Corrected 2026-09-21**: the `korean` field previously stored 열흘, the real native Korean word for "ten days" (already correctly preserved as 旬's own `korean_native` field) rather than the compositional Sino-Korean reading 순일 (sunil, from 旬's 순 + 日's 일) — moved per standing convention.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 슌닏 diverges from Sino-Korean 순일 in both syllables. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄙ⼜ㄋㄋㄧㄊ unique among both words and characters).
