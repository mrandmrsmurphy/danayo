## Notes
- [日 (char)](../characters/日%20(char).md) "day, sun" + [本 (char)](../characters/本%20(char).md) "foundation" + [語](../characters/語.md) "language" — i.e. [日本](日本.md) "Japan" + 語. No stand-in relationship applies (日's own `stand_in` is bare `日`, 本's is `本`, 語's is [[言語]]); 日本語 is an independent compound. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: 日 and 語 are individually `kwin: false`.

日本語 means the Japanese language, the country-name + 語 pattern shared by [[英語]], [[中国語]] and [[韓国語]]. Japan's own name, 日本 "origin of the sun," is the source of the first two characters; the compound is the full form that Japanese itself uses, beside the clipped Sinitic 日語.

Mandarin Rìběnyǔ, Cantonese jat6 bun2 jyu5, Japanese にほんご, and Korean 일본어 are all standard, fully compositional readings. Vietnamese tiếng Nhật is the standard, everyday term (rather than a compositional 日本語 form, which is rarely used). In informal speech, this word is commonly shortened to [[日語]], which is the everyday form in Chinese (rìyǔ) and Korean (일어), while Japanese speakers overwhelmingly say 日本語. Dan'a'yo keeps both: 日本語 is the full name of the language, 日語 the short Sinitic name, and both sit together on the Grammar page. The shared CJKV sense is kept unchanged. No homophones (注音 ㄋㄧㄊㄅㄛㄋ·⼄ unique across both words and characters).
