## Notes
- [喉](../characters/喉.md) "throat" + [破 (char)](../characters/破%20(char).md) "burst" + [音](../characters/音.md) "sound" — no stand-in relationship applies (喉's own stand-in is [[咽喉]]; 破 is its own stand-in; 音's is [[音楽]]).

喉破音 is the "throat burst": the ejective, a stop produced with the glottis closed and the larynx raised, which compresses the air in the mouth and pushes it *out* in a sharp burst (a glottalic egressive stop). It is the third member of Dan'a'yo's three-way system of bursts, beside the plain outward [[破裂音]] and the inward [[入破音]]: all three share 破 "burst", and the marked two name the source of the air — the lowered larynx (入, "in") or the raised larynx (喉, "throat") — against the plain lung-driven default. Dan'a'yo's own consonant inventory has no ejectives, so the word is not used on the phonology page; it exists so that the system is complete and so that ejectives in source and neighbouring languages can be named.

喉破音 is a **Dan'a'yo coinage**, and no source language has this exact compound, so its readings are the literal readings of its characters and not native terms: Mandarin hóupòyīn, Cantonese hau4 po3 jam1, Japanese こうはおん (喉's on'yomi コウ), Korean 후파음. The ordinary Japanese term for the ejective is 放出音, which Dan'a'yo avoids because 放出 "release" is also the second element of 無放出無声塞音, the unreleased coda stop, and would blur the two. 外破音, the other candidate, was left unused in favor of the throat image, which names the source of the air. Wiktionary and hvdic have no Vietnamese entry for the compound, so that field is omitted. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄛㄨㄆㄜㄨㄇ unique across both words and characters).
