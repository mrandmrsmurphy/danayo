## Notes
- [接 (char)](../characters/接%20(char).md) "attach, connect" + [頭 (char)](../characters/頭%20(char).md) "head" + [辞](../characters/辞.md) "word, expression" — an element attached to the "head" of a word: a prefix. The mirror of [[接尾辞]], which attaches to the "tail".

接頭辞 is the ordinary Japanese and Korean linguistic term for a prefix (せっとうじ, 접두사), and Dan'a'yo keeps it in that sense. In Dan'a'yo it is also the name of a **category**, the **verbal prefixes** (動詞接頭辞), because the prefixes the grammar actually has are all verbal ([[文法 - 05形態]], §動詞形態). Four of the six copulas double as aspect prefixes: 有– and 無– mark **experiential** aspect ("has / has never done X"), and 在– and 莫– mark **progressive** aspect ("is / is not in the middle of X"). 可– marks ability. The mood prefixes (可–, 不–, 不可– and the rest) are currently listed under Modifiers in [[文法 - 97品詞]] and are described there as "prefixing"; whether they belong in this category as well is not yet decided.

The contrast with [[派生辞]] is function, not position. A 派生辞 is a suffix that derives a new lexical item from a stem, and may stack (進化論者). A 接頭辞 is a grammatical prefix on a verb that marks aspect or mood and creates no new word. [[接尾辞]] is only the general word for "suffix": Dan'a'yo's suffixes are split between 派生辞, the relational particles of [[関詞]], and the tense and aspect suffixes 已, 中 and 将, so unlike 接頭辞 it does not name a category of its own. 接辞 remains the umbrella term for every affix.

The verbal prefixes are also free words. 有, 無, 在 and 莫 are copulas and stand on their own, so each prefix is a case like 物 and 事: a free word whose bound function would get a separate page. None of these prefix pages exists yet. When they do, they would follow the convention for prefixes, the fullwidth hyphen *after* the stem position (`有－.md`), and carry the ASCII alias `有-`.

The `mandarin`, `cantonese` and `vietnamese` fields give the literal reading of the characters, as 関詞 and 派生辞 do for their own coined forms. Mandarin linguistics itself says 前缀 (qiánzhuì) and Vietnamese says tiền tố (前素) for "prefix", so these fields are not the ordinary native terms.
