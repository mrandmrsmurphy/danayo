## Notes
- [糟](../characters/糟.md) "dregs, lees" + [糠](../characters/糠.md) "chaff, bran, husks" — 糟's own `stand_in` is this exact compound, but 糠's own is [[比糠]], so transitivity fails and `#cranberry` does not apply; 糟糠 legitimizes 糟 alone. `kwin: false` per the AND-rule: both constituents are individually `kwin: false`.

糟糠 literally means "dregs and husks" — the coarsest, poorest food, eaten only by the destitute. It survives in Dan'a'yo chiefly through its figurative sense in 糟糠之妻 ("the wife of one's dregs-and-husks days"), the wife who shared a husband's hardship before he found success or wealth.

Mandarin zāokāng, Cantonese zou1 hong1, and Korean 조강 (jogang) are all standard, fully compositional readings. Japanese さうかう (saukau) is the historical kana spelling of the on-reading そうこう (sōkō). Vietnamese tao khang is the standard Sino-Vietnamese compound, attested in the same idiom (tao khang chi thê, "wife of one's poverty days"). No Dan'a'yo homophones.
