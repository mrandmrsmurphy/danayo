## Notes
- [用](../characters/用.md) "use" + [字 (char)](../characters/字%20(char).md) "character, glyph" — 用 is legitimized by [[使用]] and 字 by its own standalone word [[字]], so neither is a stand-in here.

用字 names the characters that are actually in use, and by extension the manner of using them: which characters a text, a person, a name or a country employs. It is a transparent verb-object compound (用 "to use" + 字 "character"). In Dan'a'yo it is the umbrella for the question the writing-system chapter keeps returning to: which characters are in use, and where. It is the second element of a family of compounds that sort characters by who uses them, such as 人名用字 (characters used in personal names), 特別用字, 韓国用字 and 他国用字, and it sits beside [[常用]] ("in common use", as in 常用字) and [[代用字]] ("substitute character"), which share the same 用 + 字 skeleton.

Cross-linguistically the term is standard mainly in Japanese. Japanese ようじ (用字) is a recognized technical term meaning "the way characters are used; the characters being used" (Japanese Wiktionary: 文字の使いかた。また、使用している文字), and it figures in the established phrase 用字用語 ("orthography and wording"), the stock expression of editorial style guides and the National Language Council's notation standards. Mandarin yòngzì and Cantonese jung6 zi6 are listed in English Wiktionary, where the Chinese senses are "to use words" (verb) and "usage of characters in a document; orthography" (noun), so the noun sense matches the Japanese one closely. For Korean, a reading 용자 follows compositionally from Sino-Korean 용 and 자, but a standalone Korean dictionary entry could not be located; the Korean official lists are normally called 인명용 한자 ("kanji for personal names") instead, so treat the Korean form as compositional, not separately verified. No Sino-Vietnamese entry for 用字 appears on hvdic, so no `vietnamese` field is given, and a compositional Hán-Việt form was deliberately not asserted.

`kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 용지 (字 is ji) diverges from Sino-Korean 용자. There is no homophone in the vault; the nearest neighbour [[代用字]] merely shares the tail.
