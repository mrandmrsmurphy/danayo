## Notes
- [哭 (char)](../characters/哭%20(char).md) "cry; weep"

哭 means "to cry, weep, wail" — of both ordinary crying and ritual mourning-wails at funerals (a distinct social practice in premodern China, formalized as part of funeral rites). 痛哭 ("to wail bitterly, cry one's heart out") intensifies the plain sense.

Mandarin kū and Cantonese huk1 preserve the sense directly. Japanese コク (KOKU, on'yomi, largely confined to formal/funerary contexts) alongside native なげ(く) (nage(ku), "to lament, grieve") cover the sense. Korean 곡 (gok) parallels the Sino-xenic reading — 곡하다 (gokhada) specifically denotes ritual mourning-wailing — alongside native 울다 (ulda, "to cry"). Vietnamese khốc is the Hán Việt reading, attested in thống khốc (痛哭, "to cry bitterly," directly paralleling the Mandarin compound), khốc khấp (哭泣, "to weep, sob"), and đại khốc (大哭, "to wail loudly"); the character page's other stored candidate, khóc, is not unrelated noise but a genuine doublet from an older, pre-standard borrowing layer of the same character — it has since fully vernacularized into the ordinary everyday Vietnamese verb "to cry," while khốc remains confined to the literary Sino-Vietnamese compound register. khốc is kept here as the field value since it parallels the other Sino-xenic readings; khóc is documented rather than silently dropped, given how directly it bears on this exact word's meaning.
