## Notes
- [三 (char)](../characters/三%20(char).md) "three" + [稜](../characters/稜.md) "edge, ridge" + [鏡 (char)](../characters/鏡%20(char).md) "mirror, lens" — literally "three-edge lens," precisely describing the triangular cross-section of the classic dispersing prism, as the character breakdown itself already shows.

Korean uniquely borrows the English scientific term wholesale as 프리즘 (peurijeum, "prism") rather than deriving a Sino-Korean reading compositionally — a genuine loanword choice, not a gap, distinct from every other field on this page. Vietnamese lăng kính is directly derived from 稜鏡 itself ("edge-mirror," confirmed via web search as the term's own documented etymology) and is the standard, ordinary Vietnamese word for "prism" in general — Vietnamese doesn't productively add a "three" qualifier the way this compound's Dan'a'yo/Chinese/Japanese forms do, since lăng kính alone already implies the familiar triangular shape. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 삼룽경 diverges from the loanword-based Korean field entirely, a case where the divergence isn't a coda/vowel mismatch of the usual kind but a wholesale difference in naming strategy (compositional Dan'a'yo vs. borrowed-loanword Korean). No homophones (注音 ㄙㄚㄇㄌㄨㄫㄍ⼶ㄫ unique among both words and characters).
