## Notes
- [金 (char)](../characters/金%20(char).md) "metal; gold" — stand-in for [[金 (char)|金]], which cannot appear independently. This is a highly ambiguous word: for gold particularly, see <ruby>[黄金](/words/黄金.md)<rt>ㄏ⺢ㄫㄍㄧㄇ</rt></ruby>; for metal in general, see <ruby>[金属](/words/金属.md)<rt>ㄍㄧㄇㄐㄛㄎ</rt></ruby>

金 is 形声 (Old Chinese *krɯm): the character page's own Notes trace it to semantic 士/呂 ("ritual axe" and "two blocks of metal") plus phonetic 亼 (the ancient ancestor of [[今]], sharing its own OC reading *krɯm) — the same phonetic component behind this word's own homophone relationship below. The character originally named copper specifically, first worked during the Copper Age that preceded the Bronze Age in China, before generalizing first to "metal" broadly and then narrowing again, in many contexts, to "gold" specifically — the same three-way ambiguity (copper-adjacent origin, generic metal, specific gold) that motivates this word's own disambiguating cross-references to [[黄金]] and [[金属]]. **Note**: `characters/金 (char).md` is stamped `date-last-perfect: 2026-03-12`, among the oldest surviving stamps in this vault and structurally out of step with the current checklist (its `## Notes` merges the SKIP/MC bullets into a single line, and two Middle-Chinese-initial/final wikilinks sit stray inside the `## Words` section rather than under `## Notes`) — flagged here for the character-perfection sweep rather than rebuilt inside this word-creation pass, though its individual scalar fields remained reliable.

金 is also the most common surname in Korea (김, Kim) — the character page's own first "Words" entry — a genuinely distinct onomastic use layered on top of the metal/gold vocabulary rather than derived from it. Japanese carries two on'yomi, きん (KIN, the default — 金曜日 kin'yōbi "Friday," 金色 kin'iro "gold-colored") and こん, which survives specifically in the Buddhist/classical reading of 金色 as こんじき (konjiki), a register-specific doublet of the same compound rather than a separate word. Vietnamese kim is the character's sole stored reading, thoroughly alive both in ordinary vocabulary (kim loại "metal") and as a productive naming element. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 김 (gim, from Middle Chinese 見母 k + 侵B韻 ɣiɪm) coincides exactly with Sino-Korean 김.
