## Notes
- [本 (char)](../characters/本%20(char).md) "root, origin" + [地 (char)](../characters/地%20(char).md) "land, place" — literally "the root/origin place," i.e. this locality. No stand-in relationship applies (本's own `stand_in` is itself; 地's own is itself).

本地 means local, of this locality — this vault's word for "native" in the sense of "a local," distinct from [[土著]]'s "aborigine, indigenous" sense above. Mandarin běndì and Vietnamese bản địa (a real, everyday word, người bản địa "a local/indigenous person") are both standard for this ordinary geographic sense. Cantonese bun2 dei6 and Korean 본지 are the compositional readings from each character's own attested value, not independently verified as living words in this exact sense.

**A deliberate override of Japanese's primary sense**: Japanese distinguishes this exact compound by reading — ほんじ (honji) names a major Buddhist theological concept (本地垂迹, "original ground and manifest trace," the doctrine that Shinto kami are avatars of an underlying Buddha/Bodhisattva), while the far less common ほんち (honchi) carries the plain geographic "this locality, this land" sense instead. This vault deliberately uses the ほんち reading and sense, not the historically more significant ほんじ — an explicit override of Japanese's own dominant usage in favor of the sense shared by the other four languages. `kwin` is true: both 本 and 地 are individually `kwin: true`. No homophones (注音 ㄅㄛㄋㄉㄧㄜ unique across both words and characters).
