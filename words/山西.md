## Notes
- [山](../characters/山%20(char).md) "mountain" + [西](../characters/西.md) "west" — no stand-in relationship applies (山's own is the single-character word [[山]]; 西's own is [[西方]]).

This name is a collision between a Chinese province and a Vietnamese town, so the Chinese sense comes first.

1. Shanxi, a province of northern China west of the Taihang Mountains, whose capital is [[太原]]; its name is generally explained as "west of the mountains", the mountains being the Taihang range (English and Chinese Wiktionary also record the historical senses "the region west of the Taihang Mountains" and "the region west of Mount Hua"). It is not to be confused with the neighbouring Shaanxi, which has the same pinyin letters without the tone mark.
2. Sơn Tây, a town northwest of Hanoi, which was a province of the same name in the Nguyễn era; the old province was merged into Hà Tây in 1965 and Hà Tây into Hanoi in 2008, and the town of Sơn Tây is now an urban district of [[河内]] (from memory, not re-checked). The name is Hán-Việt 山西 and the town is known for its ancient citadel and the nearby Ba Vì mountain (from memory, not re-checked).

The Chinese province's readings fill the fields: Mandarin Shānxī, Cantonese saan1 sai1, Japanese さんせい and Korean 산서 are the established readings of the Chinese province, which the vault's `japanese` and `korean` fields follow; Vietnamese Sơn Tây is the Hán-Việt name for the Vietnamese sense (and also the usual Vietnamese name for the Chinese province, as the Vietnamese Wiktionary entry says).

Attestation, checked 2026-10-11: English Wiktionary has 山西 (Chinese, Japanese さんせい, Korean 산서 as the hanja form, Vietnamese Sơn Tây); Chinese Wiktionary has 山西 as the Chinese province; Vietnamese Wiktionary has an entry (as 山西 or "sơn tây") that glosses it as the Chinese province between Hebei and Shaanxi. The Vietnamese-town sense is not recorded at any of the Wiktionaries consulted. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄚㄋㄙㄝㄧ unique across both words and characters).
