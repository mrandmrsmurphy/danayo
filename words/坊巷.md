## Notes
- [坊](../characters/坊.md) "lane, quarter, neighborhood" + [巷](../characters/巷.md) "alley, lane" — this word is itself the stand-in that legitimizes the character 巷 as an independent Dan'a'yo entry (坊's own stand-in is [[作坊]], so no `#cranberry`: only one side of the pair is a bound character).

坊巷 means "streets and lanes" collectively — a genuine, dictionary-attested classical Chinese term (漢典/zdic.net), reflecting the classical distinction that a straight thoroughfare is 街 while a curved one is 巷, with 坊 contributing the sense of a residential quarter or block (the walled neighborhood-unit system of cities like Tang-dynasty Chang'an). This word resolves a real gap found while auditing [[Buildings]]: none of the four source languages agree on a single word for "alley" in everyday modern speech — Mandarin/Cantonese lean on 巷/胡同, Japanese uses native 路地, Korean uses native 골목, and Vietnamese uses native hẻm/ngõ — so rather than force a choice among mutually exclusive modern colloquialisms, Dan'a'yo instead coins from this real classical compound, the same dispute-resolution method used for [[妓院]].

Mandarin fāngxiàng and Cantonese fong1 hong6 are both standard, fully compositional readings, matching 坊's and 巷's own stored citations exactly, and 坊巷 is itself a real attested classical Chinese word. Japanese ぼうこう, Korean 방항, and Vietnamese phường hạng are the compositional readings built from each character's own stored citations — these are Dan'a'yo-internal coinages rather than independently-attested everyday words in those three languages, since 坊巷 as a compound doesn't appear to have organically spread beyond Chinese classical usage.

`kwin` is false: 坊 alone diverges from Korean on its own character page, which alone is enough to make the AND-rule false; 巷 is independently kwin-true (Dan'a'yo's own 항 coincides exactly with real Sino-Korean 항). No homophones (注音 ㄈㄚㄫㄏㄚㄫ unique among both words and characters).
