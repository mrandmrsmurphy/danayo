## Notes
- [材](../characters/材.md) "material, stuff" + [料](../characters/料.md) "material, stuff" — `#cranberry`: both 材's own `stand_in` and 料's own `stand_in` point back to this exact compound (transitivity A=B=AB confirmed), so 材料 is the shared stand_in legitimizing both bound characters at once.

材料 names material, stuff, or the raw components of something — extending abstractly to "abstracta," the ingredients or data that go into an argument or a piece of writing. It's distinguished from the narrower 原料 ("raw material," unprocessed; not yet its own page) by covering material generally, processed or not.

Mandarin cáiliào, Cantonese coi4liu6-2 (with the well-attested colloquial changed-tone shift on 料, the same pattern seen across many other Cantonese compound nouns in this vault), Korean 재료 (jaeryo), and Japanese ざいりょう (zairyō) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday word for "material" in its language. Vietnamese tài liệu is likewise directly compositional (材's tài + 料's liệu), but has undergone a striking semantic drift in modern Vietnamese: it no longer means physical material (that sense is now covered by the unrelated compound vật liệu, from 物 "thing" + 料) but instead means "documents, data, reference materials".
