## Notes
- [桔](../characters/桔.md) "balloon flower" + [梗](../characters/梗.md) "stem; stalk"
- This word is the viable form of [桔](../characters/桔.md): the character refers to the balloon flower plant, but is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo. 桔梗 is its `stand_in` compound.

The word names the balloon flower, Platycodon grandiflorus, the bluish-purple bell-shaped flower also called the Chinese bellflower. Its senses differ across the sphere. Wiktionary gives Mandarin jiégěng only as the traditional-medicine sense, the root of the plant, with Cantonese gat1 gang2 (also git3 gang2) alongside. Japanese ききょう is the ordinary name of the flower itself, one of the seven flowers of autumn (秋の七草), and also names the dried root used in medicine, a colour-layering combination, a shade of bluish purple and a family-crest pattern.

Korean has the native 도라지 for the plant, while Sino-Korean 길경 is the name of the root as a drug, so the Korean field here is the Sino-Xenic reading. In Dan'a'yo the word takes the plant sense, as placed on the [Plants](../lexipedia/Plants.md) page, which follows the Japanese usage rather than the narrower Mandarin root sense.
