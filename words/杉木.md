## Notes
- [杉](../characters/杉.md) "cedar" + [木 (char)](../characters/木%20(char).md) "tree" — 杉木 is the stand-in word for [杉](../characters/杉.md); 杉 is too bounded to stand alone in Dan'a'yo discourse.

杉木 names the Chinese fir (*Cunninghamia lanceolata*), a fast-growing conifer native to southern China and northern Vietnam, prized for its straight-grained, rot-resistant, easily-worked timber — one of the most economically important timber species across East Asia, widely planted in reforestation and used in construction, furniture, and coffins.

Japanese さんもく and Korean 삼목 are both standard compositional Sino-readings. Vietnamese sam mộc is the formal Hán-Việt reading; the everyday Vietnamese common name is instead the colloquial variant sa mộc (also sa mu), likely a historical assimilation dropping the coda before the following m- of mộc. `kwin` is true: Dan'a'yo 삼목 matches Sino-Korean 삼목 exactly. No specific HSK level is confirmed. No homophones (注音 ㄙㄚㄇㄇㄛㄎ unique across both words and characters).
