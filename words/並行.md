## Notes
- [並 (char)](../characters/並%20(char).md) "side by side" + [行 (char)](../characters/行%20(char).md) "go; walk" — no stand-in relationship applies (並's and 行's own stand-ins are themselves).

並行 means to go side by side: two courses, processes or lines of action running together in the same direction and at the same time. It names a motion, so it is a [[事詞]], and it covers both the literal sense (walking, driving, sailing abreast) and the extended one: two events, projects or procedures carried on concurrently. It is close to [[同時]] "at the same time", but 同時 only places events at one moment, while 並行 says they keep running alongside each other. The word sits beside [[並列]] (things arranged side by side, as in a parallel circuit) and [[並立]] (to stand side by side, to coexist).

Wiktionary records the Chinese verb "to go side by side; to carry on simultaneously" (Mandarin bìngxíng, Cantonese bing6 hang4), with a computing sense "parallel" (parallel computing), and Japanese へいこう, a noun and verb (並行する) meaning concurrent or running side by side. The geometric "parallel" of two lines that never meet is more often written 平行 in Japanese, and Dan'a'yo has no word that means only that (see the note on [[Directions]]); 並行 is the concurrent-motion word, not the geometric one. Vietnamese tịnh hành is the Sino-Vietnamese descendant Wiktionary lists. Korean 병행 is the compositional Sino-Korean reading and was not checked against a dictionary.
