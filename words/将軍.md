## Notes
- [将 (char)](../characters/将%20(char).md) "general; commander" + [軍](../characters/軍.md) "armed forces" — no stand-in relationship applies (将's own `stand_in` is itself, 軍's is [[軍隊]]).

将軍 names a supreme military commander, or, in its most famous Japanese sense, the shogun — the hereditary military ruler of Japan from the Kamakura through Edo periods, whose title 征夷大将軍 ("great general who subdues the barbarians") was later shortened to plain 将軍 in common reference. Unlike [[将帥]]/[[将校]], 将 here keeps its default "will, going to" reading (jiāng/zoeng1) rather than shifting to the "commander" tone (jiàng/zoeng3) — a real, lexicalized exception rather than a predictable rule, confirmed directly via Wiktionary/CantoDict.

Korean 장군 and Vietnamese tướng quân (Wiktionary-verified, attested since at least 1774) are both standard, non-drifted readings for "general." No homophones (注音 ㄐ⺢ㄫㄍㄨㄋ unique across both words and characters).

**Correction (2026-08-27)**: `japanese` had an archaic-kana typo (しやうぐん → しょうぐん, the same historical-orthography error class as [[寺院]]'s じゐん and [[射香]]'s じゃかう); `vietnamese` was wrongly capitalized ("Tướng quân" → "tướng quân").
