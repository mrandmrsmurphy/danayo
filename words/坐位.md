## Notes
- [坐 (char)](../characters/坐%20(char).md) "to sit" + [位](../characters/位.md) "position, place"

坐位 is the everyday word for "seat" — a place to sit, whether on a train, in a theater, or at a table. 坐, a 会意 character (the 坐 character page analyses it as 留 "to stay" with 土 "ground," to remain still on the ground), is the basic verb "to sit"; 坐位 literally combines this with 位 ("place, position") to mean "the place where one sits."

Dan'a'yo builds this word on the canonical character 坐, with the extremely common variant 座 — formed by adding the roof radical 广 over 坐, distinguishing the noun "seat" (座) from the verb "to sit" (坐) in modern usage — recorded as a word-level alias (座位), following the same alias-parent pattern established for [[追遡]] and [[禿就]] elsewhere in this vault. In practice 座位 is, if anything, the more common spelling in contemporary Chinese; 坐位 and 座位 are listed as homophonous variants of the same word. [[坐席]], cross-linked from [[坐 (char)]]'s own Notes, is a near-synonym built the same way from 坐 + 席 ("mat, seat").

The Buildings page lists it as the entry "seat," which is the sense Dan'a'yo keeps: the place where one sits, with the verb "to sit" carried by 坐 alone, as the word itself explains. The readings are Mandarin zuòwèi, Cantonese zo6 wai6, Japanese zai (ざい), Korean 좌위 and Sino-Vietnamese tọa vị. In Japanese the word is chiefly a medical and technical term (the sitting position, as in 座位 in nursing and rehabilitation), and everyday Japanese says 席 or 座席 for a seat.
