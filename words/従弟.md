## Notes
- [従 (char)](../characters/従%20(char).md) "obey, follow, collateral" + [弟 (char)](../characters/弟%20(char).md) "younger brother" — literally "a collateral younger-brother [relative]," the direct pair to [[従兄]] above.

Classically, 從弟 names specifically a father's brother's son younger than oneself; modern Japanese generalizes it to "a younger male cousin" of either parent's side, and casual Japanese speech uses いとこ (itoko) for any cousin regardless of age or gender, reserving 従弟 (じゅうてい, jūtei) for formal or written contexts. Dan'a'yo follows the generalized living Japanese sense, matching [[従兄]]'s own treatment.

Mandarin cóngdì and Cantonese cung4 dai6 are real, attested, but classical/literary registers, displaced by 堂弟/表弟 in everyday Mandarin. Korean 종제 (jongje) parallels 종형 as a real but secondary term next to 사촌동생; Vietnamese tòng đệ is a real, attested Sino-Vietnamese term (em họ con nhà chú) alongside more common đường đệ (堂弟).
