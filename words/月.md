## Notes
- [月 (char)](../characters/月%20(char).md) "moon; month" — stand-in for [[月 (char)|月]], which cannot appear independently

月 is a 象形 pictograph of a crescent moon with an interior stroke marking shading or a surface feature — a Swadesh-list word (`swadesh: 148`), among the vault's most basic vocabulary. Like [[日]] (sun/day), it carries a double sense split into the celestial body itself (望月 "full moon," 蜜月 "honeymoon," 月食 "lunar eclipse," 月虹 "moonbow") and, independently, the calendar unit "month" derived from the lunar cycle — the latter generating an enormous compound family paralleling 日's: ordinal months (一月–十二月), poetic literary month-names tied to seasonal flora (杏月 "apricot-blossom month," 桂月 "osmanthus month"), and the twelve Earthly Branch month-names (寅月, 卯月, etc.) shared with the traditional sexagenary calendar.

Japanese carries a genuine on'yomi split by function: げつ (GETSU) is the general-purpose reading (月曜日 getsuyōbi "Monday," 月経 gekkei, 月給 gekkyū), while がつ (GATSU) is reserved specifically for month-counting compounds with numbers (一月 ichigatsu "January," not *ichigetsu) — a complementary distribution rather than free variation. The native 訓読み つき (tsuki) remains the ordinary standalone word for "moon" in everyday speech, parallel to 日's ひ. Vietnamese nguyệt is the standard Sino-Vietnamese reading (nguyệt san "monthly magazine," trăng is the native word for "moon" itself).

月 is also used as the periodic-table prefix for selenium via [[月素]], the same Dan'a'yo-only naming convention as 日素/helium.
