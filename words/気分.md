## Notes
- [気 (char)](../characters/気%20(char).md) "atmosphere, spirit" + [分 (char)](../characters/分%20(char).md) "portion, part" — literally "portion of atmosphere/spirit."

気分 names one's mood or feeling — the momentary emotional state or atmosphere a person carries, closely related to 気持ち-type "feeling" vocabulary in Japanese (not yet its own page) and to the more general Sino-xenic 氣氛 ("atmosphere, ambience," of a place or situation rather than a person; also not yet its own page).

Mandarin qìfēn, Cantonese hei3 fan1, Korean 기분 (gibun), and Japanese きぶん (kibun) are all standard, fully compositional readings, and each is the real everyday word for "mood, feeling" in its language. Vietnamese khí phần is likewise compositional, from 気's khí and one of 分's two attested readings, phần — though it functions mainly as a citation form for explaining the Japanese loanword kibun rather than as independent everyday Vietnamese vocabulary, where tâm trạng ("state of mind") or cảm giác ("feeling, sensation") are the natural equivalents. `kwin` is false: Dan'a'yo 킈분 diverges from Sino-Korean 기분 in the first syllable. No Dan'a'yo homophones (注音 ㄎㄧㄜㄅㄨㄋ unique among both words and characters).
