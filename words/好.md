## Notes
- [好 (char)](../characters/好%20(char).md) "good" — the stand-in for the character 好, which cannot appear independently

好 is 会意: [[Radical 038|女]] ("woman") + [[子]] ("child") — it was good for a woman to have a child, extended first to the mutual affection between mother and child and eventually generalized to "good" in the broadest sense. The compound family stays close to this generalized sense (良好 "good, fine, kind," 友好 "friendly, amicable") while 愛好 ("to like, to be fond of") shows the character's own dual life as both adjective ("good") and verb ("to like, to be fond of" — the same character reads differently by tone in Mandarin, hǎo vs. hào, though this vault's own Dan'a'yo reading doesn't distinguish the two).

Vietnamese keeps two genuinely distinct readings rather than one collapsing into the other: hảo is the general adjectival "good" (hảo hạng "high quality," paralleling this word's own primary gloss), while hiếu specifically narrows to filial piety and devotion to one's parents (hiếu thảo "filial, dutiful") — a real semantic split inherited from the character's own etymological connection between "goodness" and the mother-child bond, not contamination between unrelated words. Japanese carries a single on'yomi こう (KOU), alongside the native 訓読み この(む) (konomu, "to like, to prefer") and い-い/よ-い (ii/yoi, "good"), splitting the same adjective/verb duality found in Mandarin's tone distinction into two separate native readings rather than one.
