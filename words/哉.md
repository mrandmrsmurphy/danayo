## Notes
- [哉 (char)](../characters/哉%20(char).md) "O" — 哉 is also the `stand_in` of its own character page, i.e. the word that legitimizes the bound character 哉

哉 is the Dan'a'yo vocative (呼格) particle, "O ...!". Like all 関詞 it is a **suffix**: it attaches directly after the addressed noun or noun phrase, as in the grammar's own example 父哉! "O Father!" ([[文法 - 04句法]], under 関詞; [[文法 - 05形態]] lists it as "Vocative: -哉").

Two roles, one particle. This page documents both roles that the grammar assigns to 哉. (1) **Vocative suffix** after a noun: 父哉!. (2) **Sentence-final particle** (語気助詞) at the end of a clause, which the grammar glosses as "negative alarm (also vocative)". The grammar lists 哉 twice because it is a suffix in both roles, but the two lists sort particles by different criteria: the 関詞 list sorts by attachment (a postposition-like suffix after a noun, marking its syntactic role), while the 語気助詞 list sorts by position and function (clause-final, marking mood or emotion). The word is therefore a vocative 関詞 that also serves as a 語気助詞, and the vault's own character page agrees: it describes a vocative marker after a noun phrase and "an alarmed or exclamatory SFP (cp. 'yikes!')" at the end of an utterance. The one example of the second role in the grammar is 我 在被食中哉! "OMG, I'm being eaten!" ([[文法 - 05形態]]). The gloss "negative alarm" is the vault's own label; no source text is cited for it, and this page does not claim any wider Dan'a'yo-specific semantic range than the grammar states. This also fits the 書面体 rule that non-declarative sentences end in a sentence-final particle ([[乎]], [[矣]], 哉, etc.): 哉 is the exclamatory or alarmed choice, beside the interrogative [[乎]], the emphatic [[矣]] and the exclamative [[也]].

Classical Chinese. Wiktionary lists 哉 (zāi) as a grammatical particle, now obsolete on its own in Standard Chinese, used for emphasis: mid-sentence to mark an inverted construction and sentence-finally for exclamation, emphasis or strong intention ("how ...!"). It also lists an obsolete sense "to begin, commence", and, in Northern Wu, a separate sentence-final TAM particle. Wiktionary does not list a vocative sense for Classical Chinese; the vocative function is the Dan'a'yo grammar's own development of the exclamatory particle. A classical use that is well known (from memory, not re-checked here) is 善哉, "excellent!, how good!". Wiktionary lists the rare variants 㦲, 㢤 and 𠳆 for this particle; the character page carries no aliases, and none are added here. In Japanese, Wiktionary lists the kun reading かな, a Classical Japanese particle of wonder or postulation (as in 善きかな, from memory, not re-checked), a genuine use of the same character, and an affix さい "exclamation or excitement". Korean and Vietnamese are bare Hanja / Hán Nôm entries without definitions.

Cross-linguistic fields. The `japanese` (さい), `korean` (재, North Korean reading) and `vietnamese` (tai) fields give the character's Sino-Xenic bound readings, not equivalents of the particle. The character page also lists the Vietnamese variant tơi, which Wiktionary shows as a Hán Nôm reading; the Sino-Vietnamese form on hvdic.thivien.net is tai. Native functional equivalents (Notes only): Mandarin 啊/呀 (ā/ya, as in 朋友啊!), Cantonese 呀 (aa3), Japanese よ and ね for address and exclamation, か and かな (Classical Japanese), Korean 아/야 (the vocative suffix, as in 영수야!) and the exclamatory ending -구나, and Vietnamese ơi (Trời ơi! "Oh heavens!"). These are comparison-chart equivalents in [[文法 - 97品詞]] and the small grammar booklets. The sentences 朋友啊!, 영수야! and Trời ơi! are illustrative, written from memory, not re-checked against a source.

哉 reads jai (ㄐㄚㄧ, 재): Middle Chinese 精母 + 咍韻, as on the [character page](../characters/哉%20(char).md). The character is also a homophone of the words [[在]] and [[載]], and of the characters 才, 財 and 斎, none of which has a word page.
