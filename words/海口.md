## Notes
- [海](../characters/海.md) "sea" + [口](../characters/口%20(char).md) "mouth" — "sea mouth". No stand-in relationship applies (海's is [[海洋]]; 口 is its own stand-in).

海口 (Haikou) is the capital of Hainan province, on the northern coast of Hainan Island in southern China (from memory, not re-checked). Unlike most Chinese city names, English Wiktionary treats 海口 first as an ordinary common noun: "mouth of a river; estuary", an obsolete sense "border", "seaport", a Southern Min sense "coastal area", and the figurative senses "big, deep mouth" and "boast; brag". The figurative sense is the source of the idiom 誇下海口 "to make a big boast" (from memory, not re-checked), and it is listed after the plain estuary and seaport senses below.

Wiktionary Japanese lists かいこう only for the city; the ordinary senses are attested in Chinese only, not in Japanese or Korean. Korean 해구 is the North Korean reading derived from the character pages (English Wiktionary has no Korean entry for 海口), and the usual South Korean name today is 하이커우 (from memory, not re-checked). Vietnamese Hải Khẩu is the Sino-Vietnamese name listed on Wiktionary. The 注音 carries a syllable-boundary dot because ㄏㄚㄧㄎㄛㄨ also parses as ㄏㄚ + ㄧㄎ + ㄛ + ㄨ, among other splits. The estuary and seaport senses are placed on [Water](../lexipedia/Water.md). The page is typed 固有名詞 (one part of speech per word) even though the first three senses are common-noun senses. Attestation: English Wiktionary Chinese, Japanese and Vietnamese entries. No homophones (注音 ㄏㄚㄧ·ㄎㄛㄨ unique across both words and characters).

1. River mouth, estuary.
2. Seaport.
3. (figurative) a big mouth; boast, brag.
4. Haikou, the city.
