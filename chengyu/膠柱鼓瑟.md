## Literal Meaning
- [膠](../characters/膠.md) — glue; to glue
- [柱 (char)](../characters/柱%20(char).md) — pillar; here the bridge (peg) under a string
- [鼓 (char)](../characters/鼓%20(char).md) — drum; to strike, to play
- [瑟 (char)](../characters/瑟%20(char).md) — the se, a large zither

Literally: "To glue the bridges and play the se."

## Extended Meaning
To be rigid and unable to adapt: to stick to a rule, a method or a text without noticing that circumstances have changed. The se is tuned by moving a bridge under each string; with the bridges glued fast it can play only one pitch and cannot be retuned. The idiom is always critical, and is used for pedantry, for learning that cannot be applied, and for leaders who follow the book and ignore the situation.

## Source and Origin
The idiom comes from **《史記·廉頗藺相如列傳》** (Shiji, ch. 81). During the siege at Changping, the king of Zhao, believing a rumour spread by Qin, replaced the veteran general Lian Po with Zhao Kuo, the son of the famous Zhao She. The dying minister Lin Xiangru warned the king:

**《史記·廉頗藺相如列傳》** (compiled c. 100 BC):
> "王以名使括，若膠柱而鼓瑟耳。括徒能讀其父書傳，不知合變也。"
> (Your Majesty is employing Kuo for his name; it is like gluing the bridges and then playing the se. Kuo can only read his father's books and records, and does not know how to adapt to change.)

The king did not listen and sent Zhao Kuo to command. The four-character form 膠柱鼓瑟 is the later crystallisation; the original has 而, "膠柱而鼓瑟". Checked on zh.Wikisource, 史記 卷081, on 2026-11-09: the quotation and its attribution to Lin Xiangru are as above. The outcome of the campaign (the destruction of Zhao's army at Changping) and the description of the se are from general knowledge and were not checked against a primary text.

## Standard Form
**膠柱鼓瑟**
- 胶柱鼓瑟 — simplified Chinese (膠 → 胶)
- 膠柱而鼓瑟 — the original wording with 而, in 史記

## Cultural Notes
Chinese: a fixed literary idiom, still common in essays and in criticism of rigid thinking and rote learning. Japanese (こうちゅうこしつ), Korean (교주고슬) and Vietnamese use the same four characters with the same sense, mostly in writing. It pairs naturally with the related idiom 刻舟求劍 ("marking the boat to find the sword"), which makes the same point about ignoring change.

## Example Sentences
1. 括徒能讀其父書傳，不知合變也。
2. Following the manual to the letter in a crisis is like gluing the pegs and then playing the se.
3. 他處事膠柱鼓瑟，從不肯變通。

## Sentiment
Negative; a mild to sharp criticism of inflexibility, in a literary and slightly learned register.
