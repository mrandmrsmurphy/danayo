## Literal Meaning
- [孤](characters/孤.md) — lone, isolated
- [軍](characters/軍.md) — army, troops
- [奮](characters/奮.md) — to rouse oneself, exert vigorously
- [闘](characters/闘.md) — to fight, struggle

Literally: "A lone army fights with all its might."

## Extended Meaning
孤軍奮闘 describes struggling single-handedly, without support or reinforcement, often against overwhelming odds. The register is admiring rather than pitying: it praises the perseverance and courage of someone who keeps fighting after allies have fled or failed to arrive, whether the "fight" is literal war, a political struggle, scholarship, sports, or ordinary personal hardship. It can carry an undertone of pathos — solitary struggle is heroic partly because it is unsupported — but the core valence is positive: admiration for grit shown in isolation.

## Source and Origin
The phrase arises from military writing of the Han and Three Kingdoms periods, describing literal small forces fighting on without reinforcement. No single text coined the idiom in its fixed four-character form; the imagery recurs across chronicles of the era such as **《三國志》**, **《後漢書》**, and **《資治通鑑》**, typically to praise desperate valor or tragic resistance. By the late imperial period the phrase had generalized from its military origin into a broader metaphor for individual perseverance in adversity — political, scholarly, or moral, not only martial.

## Standard Form
**孤軍奮闘** (Japanese shinjitai 闘) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 孤軍奮鬥 — traditional Chinese form (鬥)
- 孤军奋斗 — simplified Chinese form (斗)
- 孤軍奮戰 / 孤军奋战 — traditional/simplified variant using 戰/战 ("to battle") in place of 鬥/斗 ("to fight/struggle"); same meaning, interchangeable
- 孤軍奮鬭 — historical variant using 鬭, an older form in the same 鬥/鬪/鬭/闘 variant family

## Cultural Notes
Chinese usage stresses both heroism and pathos together — a solitary person fighting on when allies have scattered or failed to appear, as in 他在改革中孤軍奮鬥 ("he fought alone for reform").

Japanese usage is especially common and unmarked: 孤軍奮闘 (kogun funtō) shows up constantly in sports commentary, political reporting, and personal narrative to mean simply "fighting valiantly alone," with less of the tragic weight it can carry in Chinese.

Korean 고군분투 (gogun buntu) and Vietnamese cô quân phấn đấu both preserve the same core sense — fighting on alone with determination — and are used similarly across military, political, and everyday contexts of solitary struggle.

## Example Sentences
1. 在困境中，他孤軍奮鬥，終於成功。("In hardship, he fought alone and finally succeeded.")
2. 彼は孤軍奮闘してチームを支えた。("He fought on alone and held the team together.")
3. 그는 외롭게 고군분투했다. ("He struggled alone in loneliness.")
4. With no one left to back her up, she was left to fight 孤軍奮闘 against the entire board.

## Sentiment
Admiring, tinged with pathos. Describes heroic isolation — the courage to keep fighting even when abandoned or unsupported — combining respect for perseverance with an awareness of how much harder the struggle is alone.
