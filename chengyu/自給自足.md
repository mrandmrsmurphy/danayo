## Literal Meaning
- [自](../characters/自.md) — self, oneself
- [給](../characters/給.md) — supply, provide
- [自](../characters/自.md) — self, oneself
- [足 (char)](../characters/足%20(char).md) — sufficient, enough; foot

Literally: "Self-supply, self-sufficient" — supply yourself, and have enough from your own supply.

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary glosses the Chinese entry "to be self-supporting and self-sufficient; autarky" (example: 實現自給自足, "to become self-sufficient"), and the Japanese entry "self-sufficiency; be self-sufficient" (example: エネルギーを自給自足する住宅, "an energy-independent home"). Its Korean 자급자족 and Vietnamese tự cấp tự túc entries are Han-form entries of the same word.

## Source and Origin
自給自足 is built from two older words, but the four-character compound itself appears to be modern.

- **自給** ("to supply oneself") is classical. A familiar example is Tao Yuanming's 《歸去來辭並序》: 「余家貧，耕植不足以自給」, "my family was poor, and farming and planting were not enough to support me."
- **自足** ("to be self-sufficient, content with oneself") is likewise an older word, close in thought to the Taoist 知足 (knowing sufficiency).

In the wikisource corpus the four-character 自給自足 appears only in modern texts: Republican-era essays and political writing (for example on grain imports and on fascist "self-sufficient" states), and modern legal and UN documents. I have not found it in a classical text, so this page no longer calls it a classical compound or places it in "classical texts". Who first joined the two words is not established here.

English Wiktionary has entries for 自給自足 in Chinese, Japanese, Korean (a hanja form of 자급자족) and Vietnamese (a Han form of tự cấp tự túc).

## Sentiment
Neutral to positive; it describes being self-sufficient.
