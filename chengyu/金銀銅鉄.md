## Literal Meaning
- [金 (char)](characters/金%20(char).md) — gold
- [銀 (char)](characters/銀%20(char).md) — silver
- [銅 (char)](characters/銅%20(char).md) — copper, bronze
- [鉄 (char)](characters/鉄%20(char).md) — iron

Literally: "Gold, silver, copper/bronze, iron."

## Extended Meaning
金銀銅鉄 refers to the descending sequence of materials in Nebuchadnezzar's statue-dream, used to describe a succession of kingdoms or eras each inferior in some respect to the one before, even while often growing harsher or more powerful. It is invoked in discussions of historical succession, the rise and fall of empires, and — in its fullest theological sense — the transience of all human power compared to the permanence of God's kingdom. Prophetic and eschatological in register; not used for ordinary material description of the four metals themselves.

## Source and Origin
**Daniel 2:31–45**, Nebuchadnezzar's dream of the great statue, as interpreted by Daniel:

> "You saw, O king, and behold, a great image... The head of this image was of fine gold, its chest and arms of silver, its middle and thighs of bronze, its legs of iron, its feet partly of iron and partly of clay." (Dan 2:31–33, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders the verses 「這像的頭是精金的，胸膛和膀臂是銀的，肚腹和腰是銅的，腿是鐵的，腳是半鐵半泥的。」 (verses 32–33, checked on cnbible.com, which shows the same wording in the 現代標點和合本; the 聖經新譯本 reads 「這像的頭是純金的，胸膛和手臂是銀的，腹和腰是銅的，腿是鐵的，腳是半鐵半泥的。」).

I found **no attestation of 金銀銅鉄 (金銀銅鐵, 金银铜铁) as a fixed idiom for the four kingdoms** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. The same four characters are the plain list of four common metals, so on its own the string needs the Daniel context to carry this meaning; as a Dan'a'yo idiom it appears to be a coinage that takes the vision's sequence of metals as a name for it. The earlier description of it as an established form in the Bible-translation tradition was unsupported and has been removed.

The descending sequence 金 (gold) → 銀 (silver) → 銅 (bronze/copper) → 鉄 (iron) symbolizes successive kingdoms, each inferior in glory to the one before it even as later kingdoms grow harder and militarily stronger — Daniel's interpretation reads the statue as a sequence of empires (one traditional identification being Babylon, Medo-Persia, Greece, and Rome; other readings exist, and I did not check this against a source), declining in splendor while increasing in raw strength. The vision culminates in a stone "cut out by no human hand" that shatters the statue and grows into a mountain filling the whole earth (see [[石山盈界]]) — representing God's eternal kingdom supplanting every human empire in succession. This makes 金銀銅鉄 and 石山盈界 companion idioms drawn from the same prophetic vision, one naming the succession of empires and the other its final overthrow.

## Standard Form
**金銀銅鉄** (Japanese shinjitai 鉄) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 金銀銅鐵 — traditional Chinese form (鐵)
- 金银铜铁 — simplified Chinese form (铜铁)

## Variants
The attested Chinese rendering is the Chinese Union Version sentence of Daniel 2:32–33 (see Source and Origin). Three variants listed here earlier (金銀銅鉄泥, 四金属国, 像之金銀銅鉄) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
金銀銅鉄 is a reference point in prophetic and apocalyptic discussion, naming the "four kingdoms" schema that has shaped Christian historical interpretation. Like its companion idiom [[石山盈界]], it is closer to a direct scriptural citation than a general secular idiom. Because it is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「這像的頭是精金的，胸膛和膀臂是銀的，肚腹和腰是銅的，腿是鐵的，腳是半鐵半泥的。」(Dan 2:32–33, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 但以理解夢，金銀銅鉄示四國相繼。("Daniel interpreted the dream: gold, silver, copper, iron showing four kingdoms in succession.")
3. 世上諸國如金銀銅鉄，由尊至賤，終必敗亡。("The world's kingdoms are like gold, silver, copper, iron — from noble to base, they will ultimately fall.")
4. Historians have long read empires through the lens of 金銀銅鉄 — each stronger in arms than the last, yet somehow diminished in splendor.

## Sentiment
Prophetic, eschatological. Used to reference the succession of earthly kingdoms, the transience of human power, or the ultimate triumph of God's kingdom over worldly empires.
