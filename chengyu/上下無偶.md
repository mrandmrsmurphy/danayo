## Literal Meaning
- [上 (char)](characters/上%20(char).md) — above
- [下 (char)](characters/下%20(char).md) — below
- [無 (char)](characters/無%20(char).md) — none, without
- [偶](characters/偶.md) — likeness, image, paired form

Literally: "Above and below, no likeness."

## Extended Meaning
A concise formulation of the prohibition against making images of God: no form in heaven above or on earth below may serve as a representation of the divine. It is used prescriptively as legal shorthand for the Second Commandment, or more broadly as a doctrinal affirmation of God's incomparability and transcendence over any created form.

## Source and Origin
**Exodus 20:4** and **Deuteronomy 5:8**, the Second Commandment:

> "Thou shalt not make unto thee any graven image, or any likeness of any thing that is in heaven above, or that is in the earth beneath..." (KJV)

上下無偶 condenses this commandment into a balanced four-character formula, with the spatial pair 上下 mirroring the Hebrew merism "heaven above... earth beneath" — a totalizing expression covering the whole of creation. 偶 is chosen for its sense of "likeness, paired form, counterpart" rather than 像 ("image, statue") alone, emphasizing that nothing in existence can be *paired with* or made equivalent to God as a representation. Traditional Chinese Bible translations (e.g. 和合本) commonly render the commandment's "likeness" as 形像 or 偶像, and the idiom is occasionally expanded to 上下無偶像 for clarity.

## Cultural Notes
The biblical prohibition arose within a Near Eastern world saturated with cult statues; Israel's distinctiveness lay precisely in the worship of an unseen, unrepresentable God. The command is theological before it is aesthetic — it is not merely a ban on sculpture but an assertion that God transcends and cannot be reduced to any created form. In Christian history this commandment has shaped iconoclasm debates, Reformation-era controversies over sacred art, and continuing differences between traditions favoring strict aniconism and those permitting regulated representational imagery (icons, statuary); 上下無偶 as a formula expresses the high-transcendence reading. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, the phrase functions as both a doctrinal statement and a memorable catechetical summary of the Second Commandment.

## Example Sentences
1. 「不可為自己雕刻偶像，也不可作甚麼形像，彷彿上天、下地...的百物。」(Ex 20:4, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 上下無偶，故不可塑形以象神。("Since there is no likeness above or below, no form may be fashioned to represent God.")
3. 十誡第二誡即上下無偶之訓。("The Second Commandment is precisely the teaching of 'above and below, no likeness.'")
4. Whatever image one might carve, 上下無偶 — nothing above or below can stand as God's likeness.

## Sentiment
Reverent, prohibitive, theological. Stresses divine transcendence and the incomparability of God to any created form.
