## Literal Meaning
- [四 (char)](characters/四%20(char).md) — four
- [面](characters/面.md) — side, face
- [楚](characters/楚.md) — Chu (a Warring States period kingdom)
- [歌](characters/歌.md) — song

Literally: "Chu songs on all four sides."

## Extended Meaning
四面楚歌 describes being completely surrounded by enemies, isolated with no allies or escape, besieged both physically and psychologically. What makes the idiom enduring is that it names not just physical siege but existential isolation — the enemy is everywhere, and even familiar sounds confirm defeat. Used metaphorically across the region to describe situations of total opposition or abandonment, in political analysis, workplace conflict, personal narrative, and historical commentary. The tone is grave and dramatic, often implying inevitability or impending collapse, emphasizing psychological collapse brought on by a perceived total loss of support — defeat that is already internal before it becomes external.

## Source and Origin
From **《史記·項羽本紀》** (Records of the Grand Historian, "Basic Annals of Xiang Yu"), written by **司馬遷** (Sima Qian), describing the final predicament of **項羽** (Xiang Yu) at Gaixia. The phrase refers to the famous stratagem in which Han forces, having encircled Xiang Yu's army, sang Chu songs through the night. Hearing his homeland's music from every direction, Xiang Yu believed Chu had already fallen and that he was utterly alone. The tactic combined military encirclement with psychological warfare, breaking morale before battle — and became the archetypal image of total, inescapable defeat.

## Cultural Notes
四面楚歌 is a fully naturalized classical idiom across Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese, used constantly outside any historical framing to describe total opposition or abandonment — a politician facing scandal from every direction, a company besieged by competitors and regulators at once, or a person isolated by circumstance. Unlike more generic expressions for difficulty, it specifically evokes the psychological dimension of Xiang Yu's story: the moment the trapped party's own morale breaks, not merely the physical fact of encirclement.

## Example Sentences
1. 「項王軍壁垓下，兵少食盡，漢軍及諸侯兵圍之數重。夜聞漢軍四面皆楚歌，項王乃大驚曰：『漢皆已得楚乎？是何楚人之多也！』」(《史記·項羽本紀》)
2. 公司近來醜聞纏身，股東與員工皆倒戈，可謂四面楚歌。("The company has been mired in scandal lately, with both shareholders and employees turning against it — truly surrounded on all sides.")
3. 정치적으로 사면초가에 몰린 그는 결국 사퇴를 선언했다. ("Politically surrounded on all sides, he ultimately announced his resignation.")
4. By the final quarter, the incumbent found himself 四面楚歌 — abandoned by his party, outspent by his rivals, and trailing in every poll.

## Sentiment
Grave, dramatic, often implying inevitable collapse. Names not just physical encirclement but the psychological moment when hope of rescue or support disappears entirely.
