## Literal Meaning
- [詛](../characters/詛.md) — to curse, pronounce judgment upon
- [地 (char)](../characters/地%20(char).md) — ground, earth
- [哀](../characters/哀.md) — sorrow, painful hardship
- [食 (char)](../characters/食%20(char).md) — to eat, obtain sustenance

Literally: "the ground cursed; in sorrow eat."

## Extended Meaning
A compact summary of the curse upon the earth and humanity's painful labor to draw sustenance from it. It emphasizes both cosmic disorder and economic hardship following the Fall, extending the curse beyond the human body into the natural environment itself.

## Source and Origin
This maxim condenses **Genesis 3:17-18**:

> "Cursed is the ground for thy sake; in sorrow shalt thou eat of it all the days of thy life; Thorns also and thistles shall it bring forth to thee; and thou shalt eat the herb of the field..." (KJV)

Traditional Chinese Bible translations render this as "地必為你的緣故受咒詛；你必終身勞苦才能從地裡得吃的" ("the ground will be cursed because of you; you will toil all your life to get food from it"). The Dan'a'yo form preserves the twofold structure of the verse: a curse upon the ground, paired with sorrowful eating as its consequence.

Genesis 3 presents these consequences in layers, each already documented in this vault's own chengyu: [[加哀痛産]] (intensified pain in childbirth), [[欲夫治汝]] (relational distortion between husband and wife), 詛地哀食 (agricultural hardship, this idiom), and [[汗食帰泥]] (toil and mortality). This layering anticipates later biblical themes, notably Romans 8:20-22, where creation itself is described as "subjected to futility" and awaiting restoration alongside humanity.

## Cultural Notes
The underlying Hebrew text stresses *adamah* ("ground, soil"), the same substance from which humanity itself was formed (Genesis 2:7) — the curse upon the ground thus mirrors and echoes the human condition, straining the relationship between person and earth. 哀 here denotes sustained structural hardship rather than a passing emotion or episodic inconvenience. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 詛地哀食 underscores the cosmic scope of the Fall — affecting ecology, labor, and material life alike — and frames history as marked by this resistance in creation until an eventual eschatological restoration.

## Example Sentences
1. 自詛地哀食以來，人勞而不息，地亦不復如初。("Since the ground was cursed and eating became sorrowful, humanity labors without rest, and the earth is no longer as it was.")
2. 「地必為你的緣故受咒詛；你必終身勞苦才能從地裡得吃的。」(創世記 3:17，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Paul's vision in Romans 8 of creation "groaning" builds directly on the reality named by 詛地哀食.
4. 農夫終日勞苦，猶見詛地哀食之實。("The farmer toils all day, still witnessing the reality of 'cursed ground, sorrowful eating.'")

## Sentiment
Somber, agrarian, existential. Conveys hardship woven into the fabric of fallen creation.
