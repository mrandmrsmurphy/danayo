## Literal Meaning
- [愛 (char)](characters/愛%20(char).md) — to love
- [隣 (char)](characters/隣%20(char).md) — neighbor
- [如 (char)](characters/如%20(char).md) — as, like
- [自](characters/自.md) — self

Literally: "Love neighbor as self."

## Extended Meaning
愛隣如自 compresses the second great commandment into four characters: love your neighbor as yourself. 愛隣 ("love-neighbor") establishes the object, while 如自 ("as-self") provides the radical standard — not mere tolerance or kindness, but love equal to self-love. The principle implies treating others' needs, dignity, and welfare with the same care and urgency one naturally extends to oneself, transcending tribal or ethnic boundaries (as illustrated in the parable of the Good Samaritan, Luke 10:25–37). Used to express the standard of Christian love, call for radical neighbor-regard, or challenge self-centeredness with the golden rule of reciprocal love.

## Source and Origin
The command originates in **Leviticus 19:18** and is repeated throughout the New Testament — **Matthew 22:39**, **Mark 12:31**, **Luke 10:27**, **Romans 13:9**, **Galatians 5:14**, **James 2:8**:

> "You shall love your neighbor as yourself." (Lev 19:18, ESV)
> "And a second is like it: You shall love your neighbor as yourself." (Matt 22:39, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本), checked on cnbible.com, renders Leviticus 19:18 as 「…卻要愛人如己。我是耶和華。」 and Matthew 22:39 as 「其次也相仿，就是要愛人如己。」 The 聖經新譯本 reads 「卻要愛你的鄰舍好像愛自己」 for Leviticus, and the 中文標準譯本 renders Matthew 22:39 as 「要愛鄰如己」. The Chinese Bible's own phrase is therefore 愛人如己, with 愛鄰如己 in the 中文標準譯本.

I found **no attestation of 愛隣如自 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage that stays close to, but is not identical with, the existing Chinese Bible phrases above, using 隣 for "neighbor" and 如自 for "as oneself."

Jesus identified this command, together with wholehearted love of God, as summarizing all the Law and the Prophets (Matthew 22:40) — making 愛隣如自 one of the most frequently cited Biblical chengyu across CJKV Christian writing.

## Variants
- 愛人如己 — the Chinese Union Version's own phrase for the command (Lev 19:18; Matt 22:39), using 人 ("people") in place of 隣 ("neighbor")
- 愛鄰如己 — the form in the 中文標準譯本 of Matthew 22:39 (要愛鄰如己), with 鄰 for 隣 and 己 for 自

Two variants listed here earlier (愛隣如愛己 and 如己愛隣) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
The command is the shorthand for the whole of Christian neighbor-ethics, cited in sermons, religious education and ethical writing; its close relative 愛人如己, the 和合本's own phrase, is the more familiar form for most Chinese readers of the Bible. I did not check how common either form is outside Christian contexts or in each daughter language, so no such claim is made here.

## Example Sentences
1. 「要愛人如己。」(Lev 19:18, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 基督教人當愛隣如自，不分貴賤親疏。("Christians should love their neighbor as themselves, without distinction of status or relationship.")
3. 律法之要義在愛神與愛隣如自。("The essential meaning of the law lies in loving God and loving neighbor as self.")
4. The charity's founding principle was simple, almost naively so: 愛隣如自 — treat every stranger's need as urgently as your own.

## Sentiment
Ethical, demanding, compassionate. Expresses the standard of Christian love, a call for radical neighbor-regard, and a challenge to self-centeredness through the golden rule of reciprocal love.
