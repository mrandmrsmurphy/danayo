## Literal Meaning
- [十 (char)](../characters/十%20(char).md) — ten
- [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) — persons
- [不 (char)](../characters/不%20(char).md) — not
- [滅 (char)](../characters/滅%20(char).md) — destroy, wipe out

Literally: "for ten persons, not destroy."

## Extended Meaning
A concise summary of Abraham's intercession for Sodom: if ten righteous people were found there, the city would be spared. By extension, it signifies the preserving power of even a small righteous minority within a corrupt community.

## Source and Origin
The idiom condenses **Genesis 18:23–33**, Abraham's intercessory bargaining with God over Sodom, especially verse 32:

> "And he said, Oh let not the Lord be angry, and I will speak yet but this once: Peradventure ten shall be found there. And he said, I will not destroy it for ten's sake." (KJV)

Traditional Chinese Bible translations render the divine reply along the lines of "為這十個的緣故，我也不毀滅那城" ("For the sake of these ten, I will not destroy that city"). The Dan'a'yo form strips away the narrative framing (Abraham's numerical descent from fifty down to ten) and preserves only the juridical core: ten righteous persons suffice to stay destruction.

## Cultural Notes
This episode sits at the intersection of divine justice and mercy — Abraham's negotiation, descending step by step from fifty to ten, reveals both bold human intercession and divine patience with that boldness. In later Jewish tradition, ten became the minimum quorum (minyan) required for certain communal religious acts, a historically distinct development that nonetheless echoes the same number's significance here. In Christian interpretation, the passage illustrates the mediatorial role of the righteous and anticipates the broader theme that the many may be spared for the sake of the few. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 十人不滅 often expresses the hope that moral integrity in even a small remnant can restrain wider corruption or judgment, underscoring that God's judgment is responsive to righteousness rather than arbitrary.

## Example Sentences
1. 一地若有義者，或可成十人不滅之恩。("If a place has righteous ones, it may yet receive the mercy of 'ten and not destroyed.'")
2. 「我不為這十個的緣故毀滅那城。」(創世記 18:32，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Abraham's bargaining down to ten in Genesis 18 gives the Dan'a'yo idiom 十人不滅 its name and its logic.
4. 十人不滅之理，示神憐憫非無條件，然亦非全然無情。("The principle of 'ten and not destroyed' shows that God's mercy is not unconditional, yet neither is it wholly without compassion.")

## Sentiment
Hopeful yet solemn. Conveys restrained mercy, intercessory boldness, and the preserving weight of righteousness.
