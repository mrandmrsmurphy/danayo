## Literal Meaning
- [画](../characters/画.md) — to draw, to paint
- [龍 (char)](../characters/龍%20(char).md) — dragon
- [点 (char)](../characters/点%20(char).md) — to dot, to add a point
- [睛](../characters/睛.md) — eye, pupil

Literally: "paint the dragon and dot the eyes."

## Extended Meaning
To add the final, decisive touch that brings something fully to life; the finishing detail that completes a work. Across the region it is used to praise a final element that perfects an argument, artwork, performance, or plan — often in literary criticism, rhetoric, and everyday commentary when a small addition transforms the whole.

## Source and Origin
The story appears in the Tang-dynasty art-historical text **《歷代名畫記》** ("Record of Famous Painters of All Dynasties") by **張彥遠** (Zhang Yanyuan), in its entry on the painter **張僧繇** (Zhang Sengyou):

> "又金陵安樂寺四白龍，不點眼睛，每雲：『點睛即飛去。』人以為妄誕，固請點之。須臾，雷電破壁，兩龍乘雲騰去上天，二龍未點眼者見在。"
> ("At Anle Temple in Jinling he also painted four white dragons, without dotting their eyes, always saying, 'If I dot the eyes, they will fly away.' People thought this absurd and insisted he do it. In a moment, thunder and lightning broke through the wall, and the two dragons whose eyes had been dotted rode the clouds up into the sky; the two dragons left without dotted eyes remained on the wall.")

Whether legendary or not, the story illustrates a principle of Chinese aesthetics: the eye is the point where vitality appears, and the final stroke does not merely complete an image but activates it.

## Cultural Notes
The idiom refers to a concluding detail that gives coherence or vitality to an entire work, highlighting completion through precision — the decisive stroke may be small, but it determines whether the work merely exists or truly comes alive. It is invoked across the region whenever a small addition is credited with elevating an otherwise complete piece of writing, art, or plan from merely competent to genuinely striking.

## Example Sentences
1. 這篇演講的結尾，真是畫龍點睛之筆。("The closing of this speech was truly the finishing touch that brought it to life.")
2. That final chord change was the 画龍点睛 the song needed.
3. 最後の一言が、まさに画龍点睛だった。("That final remark was truly the finishing touch.")
4. 這幅畫加上這一抹紅色，堪稱畫龍點睛。("Adding this touch of red to the painting truly counts as the finishing stroke.")

## Sentiment
Admiring, appreciative. Praises precision and timing — a small addition recognized as decisive rather than superfluous.
