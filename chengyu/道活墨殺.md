[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [道 (char)](../characters/道%20(char).md) — way, Word, Logos
- [活 (char)](../characters/活%20(char).md) — to give life, to make alive
- [墨](../characters/墨.md) — ink, written letter
- [殺 (char)](../characters/殺%20(char).md) — to kill

Literally: "the Way gives life; the ink kills." 墨 ("ink") stands in deliberate metaphorical contrast to 道, standing for the written letter as opposed to the living Word.

## Extended Meaning
A compressed rendering of the Pauline contrast between Spirit and letter: the living divine Word, apprehended spiritually, brings life, whereas mere written code, taken in isolation from the Spirit, brings condemnation. It is occasionally expanded in explanatory prose as 道使人活，墨使人殺 ("the Way makes people live; the ink makes people die").

## Source and Origin
The idiom condenses **2 Corinthians 3:6**:

> "...who also hath made us able ministers of the new testament; not of the letter, but of the spirit: for the letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life." (KJV)

The underlying Greek reads τὸ γὰρ γράμμα ἀποκτείνει, τὸ δὲ πνεῦμα ζωοποιεῖ ("for the letter kills, but the spirit gives life"). The Chinese Union Version (和合本), checked at [維基文庫〈聖經 (和合本)/哥林多後書〉](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/聖經_(和合本)/哥林多後書), renders 2 Corinthians 3:6 as 「他叫我們能承當這新約的執事．不是憑着字句、乃是憑着精意．因爲那字句是叫人死、精意是叫人活。」 with the note 〈精意或作聖靈〉 (that edition writes 憑着 and 因爲 and punctuates with ． and 、). An earlier version of this page also quoted the 聖經新譯本 and 中文標準譯本 and said cnbible.com had been checked; I could not reach cnbible.com in this pass and did not re-check those wordings, the KJV text or the Greek. So 字句 corresponds to "letter" and 精意 (or 聖靈) to "Spirit."

I found **no attestation of 道活墨殺 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage. The Dan'a'yo form replaces 字句 with 墨 to sharpen the image concretely — ink on the page versus a living Word — and pairs it with 道, deliberately echoing Johannine rather than only Pauline vocabulary.

## Cultural Notes
In Pauline theology the contrast is not between Scripture and Spirit, nor between the Old and New Testaments as such, but between law treated as external code and the Spirit's inward work; the verse has been invoked historically in debates over legalism, in Reformation discussions of Law and Gospel, and in critiques of rigid literalism. The choice of 道 is significant in East Asian Christian contexts, since it resonates with Johannine theology, especially **John 1:1**, where the Logos is rendered 道 in Chinese translations — 道活 thus subtly links Paul's statement to the broader biblical theme of the living Word. 墨殺, by contrast, is intentionally stark: ink is not evil in itself, and Scripture itself is written in ink, so the idiom critiques the reduction of divine revelation to static text apprehended without spiritual understanding, not writing or Scripture as such. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, the phrase serves as a warning against formalism while upholding the authority of revelation when spiritually received.

## Example Sentences
*Example 2 is the real 和合本 text; examples 1, 3 and 4 are constructed.*

1. 徒守規條，不求道之活力，終至墨殺而非道活。("Clinging only to regulations without seeking the life of the Way results in the killing letter, not the living Word.")
2. 「因爲那字句是叫人死、精意是叫人活。」(哥林多後書 3:6，和合本 Chinese Union Version; real text, checked at the Wikisource page linked above)
3. Reformers repeatedly invoked 道活墨殺 to argue against reducing faith to mere external observance.
4. 讀經若無聖靈光照，恐流於墨殺，而失道活之義。("Reading Scripture without the Spirit's illumination risks falling into the killing letter, losing the meaning of the living Word.")

## Sentiment
Admonitory, theological, reformative. Cautions against lifeless legalism while affirming the vivifying power of the Spirit.
