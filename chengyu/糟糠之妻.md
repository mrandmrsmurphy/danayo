## Literal Meaning
- [糟](../characters/糟.md) — dregs, sediment (of fermented grain)
- [糠](../characters/糠.md) — chaff, husk
- [之 (char)](../characters/之%20(char).md) — possessive particle, "of"
- [妻 (char)](../characters/妻%20(char).md) — wife

Literally: "The wife of dregs and husks" — 糟糠 (fermented grain dregs and husks) names the coarsest, poorest food, standing for the years of shared poverty a couple endured together.

## Extended Meaning
A wife who stood by her husband through hardship and poverty before he achieved success or status — and, by extension, the principle that such a wife must never be cast aside once wealth or position is attained. Carries strong moral weight, praising loyalty and gratitude over social climbing or opportunism.

## Source and Origin
**《後漢書·宋弘傳》** (Book of the Later Han, Biography of Song Hong):
> 時帝姊湖陽公主新寡，帝與共論朝臣，微觀其意。主曰：「宋公威容德器，群臣莫及。」帝曰：「方且圖之。」後弘被引見，帝令主坐屏風後，因謂弘曰：「諺言貴易交，富易妻，人情乎？」弘曰：「臣聞貧賤之知不可忘，糟糠之妻不下堂。」帝顧謂主曰：「事不諧矣。」
> (At that time the emperor's elder sister, Princess Huyang, had recently been widowed. The emperor discussed the court officials with her to gauge her feelings. The princess said: "Lord Song's dignity, bearing, and virtue are unmatched by any of the officials." The emperor said: "I shall see to it." Later, when Song Hong was received in audience, the emperor had the princess sit behind a screen and said to Song Hong: "There is a saying — when one becomes noble, one changes one's friends; when one becomes rich, one changes one's wife. Is this human nature?" Song Hong replied: "I have heard that friends made in poverty and low station must not be forgotten, and the wife who shared the dregs and husks must not be cast from the hall." The emperor turned to his sister and said: "This will not succeed.")

Emperor Guangwu of Han was testing whether the newly-powerful Song Hong could be persuaded to set aside his wife and marry the widowed Princess Huyang. Song Hong's reply — refusing outright, on principle — became the source of both this idiom and its fuller companion phrase, 糟糠之妻不下堂 ("the wife who shared the dregs and husks must not be dismissed from the hall").

## Standard Form
**糟糠之妻**
- 糟糠之妻不下堂 — the fuller form, directly quoting Song Hong's own words, still commonly cited in full
- 糟糠の妻 — the standard Japanese form, using native の (rather than a fully Sino-Japanese four-character compound) between 糟糠 and the native word for wife (つま); this mixed construction, not a bare on'yomi compound, is itself the standard citation form in Japanese

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** zāo kāng zhī qī
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** zou1 hong1 zi1 cai1
- **Japanese:** そうこうのつま (糟糠の妻) — sōkō no tsuma
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 조강지처 (jo-gang-ji-cheo)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** tao khang chi thê

Japanese notably departs from a fully compositional Sino-Japanese reading: rather than *sōkōshisai* (the bare on'yomi concatenation of all four characters), the idiom pairs the Sino-Japanese compound 糟糠 (sōkō) with native の and つま (tsuma, "wife") — a hybrid construction that is itself the idiom's standard, fixed form.

## Cultural Notes
Carries real moral force across the sphere — praising loyalty to a spouse who endured hardship, and by extension condemning the abandonment of such a spouse after success. Common in discussions of marital fidelity, gratitude, and character, often invoked specifically to criticize someone (typically a man, given the idiom's traditional framing) who leaves a devoted spouse once he no longer needs her support. Korean and Japanese usage both preserve this same moral weight, with Japanese usage leaning toward literary or reflective register rather than casual conversation.

## Example Sentences
1. 他始終沒有忘記自己的糟糠之妻，即使事業有成也未曾改變。
2. Whatever else changed, he never forgot he owed everything to his _zāo kāng zhī qī_.
3. 成功しても糟糠の妻を忘れてはいけない。
4. Abandoning his _tao khang chi thê_ after making his fortune earned him nothing but contempt.

## Sentiment
Strongly positive when praising fidelity; strongly critical when describing its violation (abandoning such a wife). A moral touchstone rather than neutral description.
