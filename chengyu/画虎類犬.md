## Literal Meaning
- [画](../characters/画.md) — draw, paint
- [虎 (char)](../characters/虎%20(char).md) — tiger
- [類](../characters/類.md) — resemble, be like; kind
- [犬 (char)](../characters/犬%20(char).md) — dog

Literally: "draw a tiger and end up resembling a dog." The full form is 畫虎不成反類狗, "draw a tiger, fail, and it looks like a dog instead."

## Extended Meaning
To aim high and imitate a model badly, so that the result is worse than if one had not tried, and is laughable: a would-be tiger that is only a dog. It is said of a person who copies a bold model's manner without his substance, and of an ambitious plan that goes wrong. The saying that goes with it, 刻鵠不成尚類鶩 ("carve a swan and fail, and it still looks like a duck"), is the milder case, where the imitation falls short but stays respectable.

## Source and Origin
The saying comes from the letter 《誡兄子嚴敦書》 (a warning to his nephews Yan and Dun) written by the general Ma Yuan (馬援) from Jiaozhi, preserved in 《後漢書·馬援傳》 (卷24). Ma Yuan holds up two men for his nephews to copy: Long Bogao, who is careful and modest, and Du Jiliang, who is dashing and generous but dangerous to imitate:

> 「效伯高不得，猶爲謹敕之士，所謂刻鵠不成尚類鶩者也。效季良不得，陷爲天下輕薄子，所謂畫虎不成反類狗者也。」
> "If you copy Bogao and fall short, you will still be a careful, disciplined man: this is what is meant by 'carve a swan and fail, and it still resembles a duck.' If you copy Jiliang and fall short, you will sink into one of the frivolous men of the world: this is what is meant by 'draw a tiger and fail, and it turns into a dog.'"

Checked on zh.Wikisource on 2026-11-09 (後漢書/卷24 and 誡兄子嚴敦書). The original has 狗 (「反類狗」); the four-character form with 犬 is a later variant, and both are in use. The same phrase appears in Cao Zhi's 與楊德祖書, again with 狗 (「譬畫虎不成，反為狗也」).

## Standard Form
**画虎類犬** — Dan'a'yo standard, with 画 the shinjitai form of 畫 and 犬 as the unrestricted word for "dog". 狗 is a 名専字 in Dan'a'yo, and it is aliased in the chengyu rather than used in the headword.
- 畫虎類犬 / 畫虎類狗 / 画虎類狗 — traditional and mixed forms
- 画虎类犬 / 画虎类狗 — simplified Chinese
- 畫虎不成反類犬 / 畫虎不成反類狗 — the full form

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** huà hǔ lèi quǎn (with 狗: huà hǔ lèi gǒu)
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** waak6 fu2 leoi6 hyun2 (with 狗: gau2)
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** がこるいけん (ga-ko-rui-ken)
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 화호류견 (South Korean 화호유견)
- **Vietnamese (Hán–Việt):** hoạ hổ loại khuyển

The 注音 takes one dot, at 類|犬, where ㄌㄨㄧㄎ⼔ㄋ also splits as ㄌㄨ + ㄧㄎ + ⼔ㄋ.

## Cultural Notes
Ma Yuan's letter is one of the best-known pieces of family advice in Chinese. The warning is aimed at the reckless admirer: imitating a flashy hero is dangerous because the surface (the stripes) is easy to copy and the substance is not. Japanese 画虎類犬 (がこるいけん) and Korean 화호류견 are literary borrowings, and Vietnamese hoạ hổ loại khuyển is the Sino-Vietnamese form; the Japanese also has the plain-speech 虎を描いて犬に類す. In English the nearest idiom is "to bite off more than one can chew," though the thought is more about imitation.

## Example Sentences
1. 效季良不得，陷為天下輕薄子，所謂畫虎不成反類狗者也。
2. 他硬要學大師的狂放，結果畫虎類犬，成了笑柄。
3. Copying a genius's recklessness without his skill is a sure way to draw a tiger and get a dog.

## Sentiment
Negative; mocking, and also a warning.
