## Literal Meaning
- [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) — person
- [山 (char)](../characters/山%20(char).md) — mountain
- [海](../characters/海.md) — sea

Literally: "people as mountains, people as seas."

## Extended Meaning
A huge throng of people; an enormous crowd filling every space, evoking the feeling of being surrounded by humanity on all sides. Though purely descriptive of scale, the idiom can carry different tones depending on context — positive (lively, prosperous, communal) or negative (overcrowded, chaotic, overwhelming).

## Source and Origin
The earliest attestation of this exact four-character form appears in the Song-dynasty **《西湖老人繁勝錄》** ("Random Notes on the Splendor of West Lake by an Old Man of West Lake"), describing the bustling markets of Hangzhou:

> "四山四海；三千三百；衣山衣海；卦山卦海；南山南海；人山人海。"
> ("Mountains and seas on every side; three thousand three hundred [stalls]; robes like mountains, robes like seas; fortune-telling stalls like mountains, like seas; south like mountains, south like seas; people like mountains, people like seas.")

The phrase was popularized in later vernacular fiction, most famously in the Ming-dynasty novel **《水滸傳》** (Water Margin), chapter 51:

> "每日有那一般打散，或是戲舞，或是吹彈，或是歌唱，賺得那人山人海價看。"
> ("Every day there were performers — some dancing, some playing instruments, some singing — drawing crowds as thick as mountains and seas to watch.")

By the late imperial period the pairing had become fully established as a fixed idiom for immense public gatherings — temple fairs, New Year crowds, or armies on parade.

## Cultural Notes
The imagery evokes not only sheer size but also the vitality and collectivity of social life, from festive joy to oppressive chaos. In Japanese, 人山人海 (jinzan jinkai) is a recognized fixed idiom meaning "teeming with people." Korean 인산인해 (insan inhae) is extremely common, regularly used in news reports about crowds at holidays or protests. Vietnamese nhân sơn nhân hải appears in literature as a poetic hyperbole for endless masses of people. It remains one of the living idioms in daily use across East Asia, moving easily from classical hyperbole to everyday news reporting.

## Example Sentences
1. 春節廟會上，人山人海，熱鬧非凡。("At the Spring Festival temple fair, there were people as mountains and seas — lively beyond measure.")
2. 祭りの会場は人山人海だった。("The festival grounds were a sea of people.")
3. 명절에 시장은 인산인해였다。("During the holiday, the market was crowded with people like mountains and seas.")
4. Lễ hội chùa Hương, người đông như nhân sơn nhân hải。("At the Perfume Pagoda festival, people thronged like mountains and seas.")

## Sentiment
Descriptive and flexible — positive when evoking lively, prosperous crowds, negative when evoking overcrowding and chaos; vivid and vast in tone either way.
