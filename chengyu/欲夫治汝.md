## Literal Meaning
- [欲](../characters/欲.md) — desire
- [夫 (char)](../characters/夫%20(char).md) — husband
- [治](../characters/治.md) — to rule, govern
- [汝 (char)](../characters/汝%20(char).md) — you

Literally: "your desire [toward] your husband; he shall rule you." The phrase compresses two clauses of the source verse into a single four-character structure.

## Extended Meaning
A sober description of the relational distortion introduced between husband and wife as part of the judgment following the Fall: a wife's desire directed toward her husband paired with his rule over her. It is not presented as a prescriptive ideal for marriage but as a diagnostic description of a broken condition — within Dan'a'yo usage it functions as an anthropological marker, acknowledging relational distortion while leaving room for redemption rather than endorsing domination.

## Source and Origin
This maxim condenses part of **Genesis 3:16**, God's judgment on Eve after the Fall:

> "...and thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee." (KJV)

Traditional Chinese Bible translations render this as "你必戀慕你丈夫，你丈夫必管轄你" ("You will desire your husband, and your husband will rule over you"). The Dan'a'yo form retains both halves of this dynamic — desire and rule — in compressed form.

## Cultural Notes
The Hebrew term rendered "desire" (תְּשׁוּקָה, *teshuqah*) appears only three times in the entire Old Testament, and its other appearance in Genesis 4:7 — where sin's "desire" is toward Cain, and he is told to "rule over it" — uses nearly identical grammar. This close parallel has led many interpreters to read Genesis 3:16 as describing a struggle for mastery within the marriage relationship rather than simple romantic longing. 治 conveys governance or administration and need not imply cruelty in isolation, but within this curse context it signals an imbalance introduced by sin rather than the created design (compare Genesis 1:27–28, where the man and woman are jointly given dominion over creation, with no such asymmetry between them). Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 欲夫治汝 is used to name this distortion candidly as part of teaching on the Fall's consequences, not as a blueprint for how marriage ought to be.

## Example Sentences
1. 墮落之後，婚姻之中或見欲夫治汝之張力。("After the Fall, tension in marriage may reflect the dynamic of desire and rule.")
2. 「你必戀慕你丈夫，你丈夫必管轄你。」(創世記 3:16，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Theologians point to 欲夫治汝 alongside Genesis 4:7 to argue the verse describes a struggle for mastery, not a design pattern.
4. 恩典之下，欲夫治汝之張力當漸得醫治。("Under grace, the tension of 'desire and rule' should gradually be healed.")

## Sentiment
Grave, realistic, relationally tense — diagnostic of a fallen condition rather than prescriptive or celebratory.
