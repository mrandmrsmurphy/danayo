## Literal Meaning
- [緑 (char)](../characters/緑%20(char).md) — green
- [林](../characters/林%20(char).md) — grove, forest
- [豪](../characters/豪.md) — bold, heroic; a strong man
- [客](../characters/客.md) — guest, visitor, traveller

Literally: "a bold guest of the green forest" — a brave man who lives in the greenwood.

## Extended Meaning
A bandit, outlaw or robber, usually with a flattering sense of boldness and chivalry: the Robin Hood kind. The 緑林 ("green forest") is the world of outlaws, and 豪客 is the bold fellow in it. In modern use the phrase can be neutral or jocular, but it can also simply mean bandits.

## Source and Origin
**緑林** itself comes from the Lülin hills (綠林山, in present-day Hubei), where in 17 AD Wang Kuang and Wang Feng's band of starving rebels hid against Wang Mang's Xin dynasty. 《後漢書·劉玄傳》 (卷11): 「新市人王匡、王鳳爲平理諍訟，遂推爲渠帥，衆數百人。……共攻離鄉聚，臧於綠林中。」 ("Wang Kuang and Wang Feng of Xinshi settled disputes, and were made chiefs of several hundred men… together they attacked the scattered villages, and hid in the Lülin hills.") Thereafter 緑林 meant outlaws.

The four-character phrase **緑林豪客** is first found in a Tang poem by Li She (李涉), 《井欄砂宿遇夜客》 (全唐詩 卷477). Li She, passing Jiujiang at Wankou, was stopped by robbers; when the robber chief learned his name, he asked for a poem instead of loot, and Li She wrote:

> 「暮雨蕭蕭江上村，綠林豪客夜知聞。他時不用逃名姓，世上如今半是君。」
> "Evening rain falls soft on the riverside village; the bold men of the greenwood know of me by night. Another day you need not hide your names: half the world now is you."

The phrase takes 豪客 from that poem; the poem's own note records the story. Checked on zh.Wikisource on 2026-11-09 (井欄砂宿遇夜客 and 後漢書/卷11). The poem has variant readings: 「春」 for 「暮」 and 「敲門」 for 「知聞」.

## Standard Form
**緑林豪客** — Dan'a'yo standard, using 緑, the shinjitai form (aliased to 綠 and 绿).
- 綠林豪客 — traditional Chinese
- 绿林豪客 — simplified Chinese

## Cultural Notes
The Greenwood outlaw is a stock figure of Chinese fiction, from 《水滸傳》 on, and the English "Robin Hood" is the usual gloss. Korean 록림 (South Korean 녹림, "green forest") also means a band of outlaws in the same way. In Japanese 緑林 is read りょくりん and means the same.

## Example Sentences
1. 綠林豪客夜知聞。
2. 這條山路上有綠林豪客出沒，商旅都結伴而行。
3. The magistrate was robbed by outlaws of the greenwood, as bold as the old stories say.

## Sentiment
Mixed; admiring or romantic when it speaks of outlaw heroes, but still a word for robbers.
