## Literal Meaning
- [汗 (char)](../characters/汗%20(char).md) — sweat
- [食 (char)](../characters/食%20(char).md) — eat
- [帰](../characters/帰.md) — to return
- [泥 (char)](../characters/泥%20(char).md) — mud

Literally: "by sweat eat; return to mud."

## Extended Meaning
A compact summary of the curse upon Adam: labor marked by toil, and mortality ending in a return to the ground. It captures both economic hardship and existential finitude — the fallen human estate as one of daily struggle bounded by eventual death.

## Source and Origin
The idiom condenses **Genesis 3:19**:

> "In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread, till thou return unto the ground; for out of it wast thou taken: for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return." (KJV)

Traditional Chinese Bible translations render this as "你必汗流滿面才得糊口...你本是塵土，仍要歸於塵土" ("By the sweat of your face you will earn your bread... for you are dust, and to dust you will return"). The Dan'a'yo form compresses "eat by sweat" into 汗食 and the return to the ground into 帰泥 — choosing 泥 ("mud") over 塵 ("dust") as the Dan'a'yo term for the ground humanity was formed from and returns to, a deliberate lexical choice rather than a mistranslation of the underlying Hebrew עָפָר ("dust, dry earth").

## Cultural Notes
Genesis 3 links labor and mortality: work itself predates the Fall, but toil and frustration intensify afterward, and the Hebrew's emphasis on "the sweat of your face" underscores bodily exertion specifically. 帰泥 carries anthropological weight — humanity is formed from the ground and returns to it — and resonates with funeral liturgy and Ash Wednesday themes ("remember that you are dust, and to dust you shall return") in the wider Christian tradition. The pairing of economic struggle and death suggests the curse touches both daily life and ultimate destiny, though within Christian theology this verse stands in tension with the later hope of bodily resurrection. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 汗食帰泥 functions as a stark anthropological maxim, used to describe the fallen human condition, comment on the dignity and burden of labor, or meditate on mortality.

## Example Sentences
1. 自墮落以來，人間常態乃汗食帰泥。("Since the Fall, the human condition has been to eat by sweat and return to dust.")
2. 「你必汗流滿面才得糊口，直到你歸了土...你本是塵土，仍要歸於塵土。」(創世記 3:19，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Ash Wednesday liturgy echoes the same theme as 汗食帰泥: labor now, dust in the end.
4. 汗食帰泥雖為咒詛，然基督徒仍盼復活之望。("Though 'sweat and eat, return to mud' is a curse, Christians still hold to the hope of resurrection.")

## Sentiment
Grave, realistic, existential. Reflects toil, limitation, and the sober awareness of death.
