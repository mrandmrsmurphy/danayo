## Literal Meaning
- [一 (char)](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one, a single
- [衣](../characters/衣.md) — garment, robe, clothing
- [帯 (char)](../characters/帯%20(char).md) — belt, sash
- [水 (char)](../characters/水%20(char).md) — water

Literally: "A single belt of water" — one narrow ribbon of water, as thin as the sash of a garment.

## Extended Meaning
Used to describe two places separated only by a narrow strip of water, such as a strait, sea or river. Wiktionary gives the example sentence 中國和日本是一衣帶水的鄰邦 ("China and Japan are neighbouring countries separated only by a narrow strip of water").

The phrase began in a speech of will and is now used for nearness. In the Nan Shi passage below, the Yangtze is "only a belt of water" and so no reason not to cross it. The modern sense is simply that the water between two places is narrow.

## Source and Origin
**《南史·陳本紀下》** (History of the Southern Dynasties, annals of the last Chen ruler, juan 10):

Emperor Wen of Sui (隋文帝 Yang Jian, r. 581–604), having unified northern China, turned his attention south. The Chen dynasty still held the Yangtze basin, and its last ruler was dissolute. The Sui emperor spoke to his minister Gao Jiong (高熲):

> 隋文帝謂僕射高熲曰：「我為百姓父母，豈可限一衣帶水不拯之乎？」命大作戰船。
> "The Emperor of Sui said to the Grand Councillor Gao Jiong: 'As the father and mother of the people, can I let a mere belt of water stop me from rescuing them?' He then ordered great warships to be built."

In 589 CE, Sui forces crossed the Yangtze and destroyed the Chen dynasty. The wording is as recorded in the 《南史》; it is often attributed loosely to the 《隋書》 (the official history of the Sui), but the passage verified here is the 《南史》 one.

## Cultural Notes
Wiktionary glosses the Chinese idiom "separated only by a narrow strip of water". It also quotes Guo Moruo's 1972 poem 《沁園春·祝中日恢復邦交》, written for the resumption of diplomatic relations between China and Japan: 「赤縣扶桑，一衣帶水，一葦可航。」

## Example Sentences
1. 「我為百姓父母，豈可限一衣帶水不拯之乎？」— 隋文帝（《南史》）
   "Can I allow a mere belt of water to prevent me from saving my people?" (Sui Wendi, before the conquest of Chen, 589 CE)
2. 日中両国は一衣帯水の隣国であり、その文化的絆は深く長い。
   "Japan and China are neighbors separated by only a belt of water; their cultural bonds are deep and long."

## Sentiment
Warm; it describes two places as near each other.
