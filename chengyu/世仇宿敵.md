[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [世仇](../words/世仇.md) — generational feud, age-long enmity
- [宿敵](../words/宿敵.md) — sworn or longstanding enemy

Literally: "an age-long feud, a sworn ancient enemy." Both 世仇 and 宿敵 are already established two-character compounds in CJK vocabulary, here joined into a single four-character theological maxim.

## Extended Meaning
A compressed expression of the primordial enmity announced in Genesis 3:15. It denotes the enduring hostility between the serpent and the seed of the woman, often interpreted in Christian theology as the conflict between Satan and the redemptive line culminating in Christ. 世仇 captures the generational continuity of the enmity; 宿敵 conveys its deep-rooted, almost primordial character. Together they frame redemptive history as structured, directed conflict rather than a momentary dispute.

## Source and Origin
The phrase reflects **Genesis 3:15**, part of God's judgment on the serpent after the Fall:

> "And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel." (KJV)

Traditional Chinese Bible translations render the core clause as "我又要叫你和女人彼此為仇" ("I will make you and the woman enemies to each other"). This verse has long been called the *protoevangelium*, the first announcement of the gospel: the enmity it establishes is judicial and redemptive, preventing any total alliance between humanity and the serpent, unfolding across the whole of history, and culminating in the bruising of the serpent's head. The Dan'a'yo formulation 世仇宿敵 intensifies the temporal dimension of that enmity — not momentary but historical and transgenerational, a struggle carried down through every generation until its resolution.

## Cultural Notes
In ordinary CJK usage, 世仇 refers to clan or family feuds transmitted across generations, and 宿敵 to an adversary whose hostility is embedded in a long shared history (rivals, nations, or individuals). By appropriating these secular terms for a theological maxim, Dan'a'yo recasts salvation history in epic, personal terms rather than abstract doctrine. The phrase must be read carefully: the biblical text does not depict two equal cosmic principles locked in dualistic balance — the enmity is established by God himself and is ordered toward an eventual, decisive victory, not perpetual stalemate. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere the phrase signals both conflict and promise, framing history as directed struggle rather than cyclical inevitability.

## Example Sentences
1. 自墮落以來，世仇宿敵未嘗止息，直至應許之子得勝。("Since the Fall, the age-long enmity has not ceased, until the promised Son prevails.")
2. 「我又要叫你和女人彼此為仇...」(創世記 3:15，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Theologians call Genesis 3:15 the protoevangelium — the first hint of the 世仇宿敵 between the serpent and the woman's seed.
4. 蛇與女人的後裔之間乃是世仇宿敵，直到基督得勝方休。("Between the serpent and the woman's seed lies an age-long enmity, an ancient foe, until Christ's final victory.")

## Sentiment
Epic, adversarial, anticipatory. Conveys long-standing hostility while implying eventual triumph — grave rather than despairing in tone.
