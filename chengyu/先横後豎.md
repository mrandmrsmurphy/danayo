[Dan'a'yo Chengyu](chengyu/Dan'a'yo%20Chengyu.md)

>[!Tip] The first line of the second couplet of the 筆順 verse, with its partner [[先撇後捺]] — see the verse on [[Stroke]]

## Literal Meaning
- [先](../characters/先.md) — first, before
- [横](../characters/横.md) — horizontal stroke
- [後](../characters/後%20(char).md) — after
- [豎](../characters/豎.md) — vertical stroke

Literally: "Horizontal first, vertical after."

## Extended Meaning
When a horizontal and a vertical stroke cross or meet, the horizontal is written first and the vertical after (十, 土, 干). It is the first of the rules that govern the order of strokes of different kinds, and it pairs with [[先撇後捺]], which governs the two falling strokes. The rule has well-known exceptions (a vertical that pierces the whole character, as in 中 and 申, is written last in Japanese teaching), but as a default it is the most widely applied rule in stroke-order instruction.

## Source and Origin
A 先X後Y phrase: 先 ("first") and 後 ("after") frame the two stroke names 横 and 豎, which are the stroke names used on [[Stroke]] (the classical names are 勒 and 努). It is the first line of the second couplet of the 筆順 verse. Coined within 単亜語 on 2026-10-03, on the model of the existing [[先題後述]].

## Cultural Notes
Like the other idioms of [[Dan'a'yo Chengyu|単亜語 origin]], this phrase is vault-internal terminology rather than a phrase with independent life across the CJKV sphere. The rule it names, however, is not Dan'a'yo's own: it belongs to the common stroke-order tradition of the whole region. The same rule is stated in Chinese stroke-order instruction as 先横后竖, in Japanese as 「横画が先」, and in Korean as 가로획 먼저, 세로획 나중.

## Example Sentences
1. 「十」は先横後豎で、横画を書いてから縦画を書く。
2. In 土 the first stroke is the horizontal: 先横後豎.

## Sentiment
Neutral and instructional; a mnemonic in the register of a school rule, intended to be recited and remembered.
