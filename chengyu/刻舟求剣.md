## Literal Meaning
- [刻](characters/刻.md) — to carve, notch
- [舟](characters/舟.md) — boat
- [求](characters/求.md) — to seek
- [剣 (char)](characters/剣%20(char).md) — sword

Literally: "Carve the boat, seek the sword."

## Extended Meaning
刻舟求劍 describes clinging rigidly to a fixed method or reference point after the circumstances that made it valid have already changed — a foolish, mechanical consistency that ignores context. It is used critically, to point out approaches that fail to adapt to changing conditions, data, or reality on the ground, whether in policy, business strategy, or personal habit.

## Source and Origin
**《呂氏春秋·察今》** (Master Lü's Spring and Autumn Annals, "Examining the Present"). A man crossing a river drops his sword overboard from a moving boat. Rather than marking the spot in the water, he carves a notch on the boat's gunwale where the sword fell, intending to dive for it later when the boat stops — failing to realize the boat has moved on while the sword remained fixed at the bottom of the river. The story was originally composed as a pointed criticism of rulers and statesmen who apply outdated laws and methods to a changed present, mistaking the letter of old policy for its original context and purpose.

## Standard Form
**刻舟求剣** (Japanese shinjitai 剣) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 刻舟求劍 — traditional Chinese form (劍)
- 刻舟求剑 — simplified Chinese form (剑)

## Cultural Notes
刻舟求劍 is a fully naturalized classical idiom across Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese, used constantly in policy criticism, business commentary, and everyday speech to call out rigid, outdated thinking. Its vivid narrative image (a visibly absurd, physically impossible plan) makes it one of the more immediately intuitive four-character idioms even for language learners, requiring little cultural background to grasp.

## Example Sentences
1. 「舟已行矣，而劍不行，求劍若此，不亦惑乎？」(《呂氏春秋·察今》)
2. 當市場環境劇變而仍沿用舊方案，無異於刻舟求劍。("When the market changes dramatically yet you keep using the old plan, it's just like 'carving the boat to seek the sword.'")
3. 시대가 바뀌었는데도 옛 방식을 고집하는 것은 각주구검과 다름없다. ("Insisting on the old way even though the times have changed is no different from 刻舟求劍.")
4. Relying on last decade's market research today is pure 刻舟求剣 — the river has moved on, but the notch on the boat hasn't.

## Sentiment
Critical, mocking. Points out foolish, rigid consistency that ignores how circumstances have changed — a cautionary example rather than a compliment.
