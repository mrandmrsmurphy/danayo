## Literal Meaning
- [数](characters/数.md) — counted, numbered
- [数](characters/数.md) — counted, numbered
- [衡](characters/衡.md) — weighed
- [分 (char)](characters/分%20(char).md) — divided

Literally: "Numbered, numbered, weighed, divided."

## Extended Meaning
数数衡分 pronounces a verdict of divine judgment: a person, ruler, or nation has been assessed by God and found wanting, with downfall as the certain, imminent consequence. It is used to describe the moment pride or wickedness meets its reckoning — invoked ominously, often as prophecy or as commentary after the fact, comparing a fallen leader or collapsed regime to Belshazzar. Never used lightly; it always carries the full weight of an irreversible, divinely-decreed sentence.

## Source and Origin
**Daniel 5:25–28** — the writing on the wall at Belshazzar's feast:

> "And this is the writing that was inscribed: MENE, MENE, TEKEL, and PARSIN. This is the interpretation of the matter: MENE, God has numbered the days of your kingdom and brought it to an end; TEKEL, you have been weighed in the balances and found wanting; PERES, your kingdom is divided and given to the Medes and Persians." (Dan 5:25–28, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders the passage 「所寫的文字是：彌尼，彌尼，提客勒，烏法珥新。講解是這樣：彌尼，就是神已經數算你國的年日到此完畢；提客勒，就是你被稱在天平裡，顯出你的虧欠；毗勒斯（與烏法珥新同義），就是你的國分裂，歸與瑪代人和波斯人。」 (verses 25–28, checked on cnbible.com).

I found **no attestation of 数数衡分 (數數衡分) as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage that parallels the four Aramaic words while conveying Daniel's interpretation rather than transliterating them: the doubled 数数 ("numbered, numbered," from 數算) mirrors the doubled MENE; 衡 ("weighed") renders TEKEL's image of the scales of divine justice; and 分 ("divided") renders PERES/UPHARSIN, the prophecy of the kingdom's partition. The earlier description of it as a "remarkable crystallization" in the Chinese Bible tradition was unsupported and has been removed.

## Standard Form
**数数衡分** (Japanese shinjitai 数) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 數數衡分 — traditional Chinese form (數)

## Variants
The attested Chinese rendering is the transliteration plus interpretation in the Chinese Union Version of Daniel 5:25–28 (see Source and Origin); I found no fixed four-character form. Three variants listed here earlier (数衡分裂, 計量分散, 数之衡之分之) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
The Belshazzar story is proverbial in English as "the writing on the wall," and 数数衡分 is meant to carry the same warning of judgment on the proud or wicked in the Dan'a'yo sphere. Because it is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「所寫的文字是：彌尼，彌尼，提客勒，烏法珥新。講解是這樣：彌尼，就是神已經數算你國的年日到此完畢；提客勒，就是你被稱在天平裡，顯出你的虧欠；毗勒斯（與烏法珥新同義），就是你的國分裂，歸與瑪代人和波斯人。」(Dan 5:25–28, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 驕王見数数衡分之文，知其國運已盡。("The proud king saw the 'numbered, numbered, weighed, divided' writing and knew his kingdom's fate was finished.")
3. 不義之國必受数数衡分之判。("An unrighteous nation will surely receive the judgment of 'numbered, weighed, divided.'")
4. Historians would later say the regime's final year was its own 数数衡分 — counted, weighed, and found wanting.

## Sentiment
Ominous, prophetic, judgmental. Used to pronounce divine verdict, warn of impending doom, or describe the inevitable downfall of the arrogant and wicked.
