## Literal Meaning
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [石](../characters/石%20(char).md) — stone
- [二](../characters/二%20(char).md) — two
- [鳥](../characters/鳥%20(char).md) — bird

Literally: "one stone, two birds."

## Extended Meaning
Achieving two results with a single action; a single effort producing double benefit. Fully standard and stylistically neutral — it appears in speech, writing, education, business, and media, carrying no literary register and no moral weight beyond efficiency. It simply names an optimization.

## Source and Origin
Unlike most chengyu, 一石二鳥 is **not** a Classical Chinese idiom. It is a modern calque of the English proverb "to kill two birds with one stone," introduced into written Chinese in the late nineteenth to early twentieth century through translation, education, and contact with Western languages. From Chinese, it spread into Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese as part of the shared modern Sino-lexical layer.

There is no premodern Chinese textual source for the phrase. Classical Chinese contains structurally similar ideas, but not this specific image — the metaphor of stone-and-birds is distinctly Western, and its adoption marks a shift in how new idioms entered the CJKV sphere: not by poetic citation or historiography, but by translation. What is notable is how smoothly the phrase naturalized: its four-character structure fits chengyu norms perfectly, and its causal logic aligns with long-standing pragmatic reasoning in Chinese prose. As a result, it is often mistaken for an ancient idiom despite its modern origin.

## Standard Form
**一石二鳥** (Dan'a'yo, using shinjitai 鳥)
- 一石二鸟 — simplified Chinese form
- Both forms current; the underlying idiom is identical, reflecting only the ordinary traditional/simplified script split rather than any separate history.

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** yì shí èr niǎo

- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** いっせきにちょう

- **Vietnamese (Sino-Vietnamese):** nhất thạch nhị điểu

## Cultural Notes
Across the region, 一石二鳥 is fully standard and stylistically neutral in all five CJKV languages that use it, appearing across registers with no special literary weight. Its simplified alias 一石二鸟 reflects the same modern-layer status — a calque doesn't accumulate the kind of orthographic history a classical idiom does, so there's little regional variation beyond the ordinary traditional/simplified split.

## Example Sentences
1. 這個方法既省時又省錢，真是一石二鳥。("This method saves both time and money — truly killing two birds with one stone.")
2. By combining the trip with the conference, she managed a 一石二鳥 outcome.
3. To kill two birds with one stone, he studied Japanese while watching anime — 一石二鳥的做法。

## Sentiment
Positive and practical; approving of efficiency rather than emotionally charged. No irony or ambivalence — unlike many classical idioms, it carries no baggage beyond "well done, that was efficient."

## Usage Note
一石二鳥 is a good example of a "false antique." Its form signals classical authority, but its history is modern — useful as a diagnostic case when distinguishing inherited literary idioms from translated conceptual vocabulary.
