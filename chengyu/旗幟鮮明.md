## Literal Meaning
- [旗](../characters/旗.md) — banner, flag
- [幟 (char)](../characters/幟%20(char).md) — pennant, standard
- [鮮](../characters/鮮.md) — vivid, bright
- [明 (char)](../characters/明%20(char).md) — clear, distinct

Literally: "Banners and pennants, vivid and clear" — battle standards bright and distinct enough to be recognized from afar.

## Extended Meaning
To take a clear, unambiguous, unmistakable stand — stating one's position, principles, or allegiance so plainly that there is no room for doubt about where one stands. 旗幟 (banners) extends metaphorically from a literal battlefield marker of identity and allegiance to any outward sign of one's stated position; 鮮明 (vivid and clear) describes how sharply that position is expressed. Frequently carries a positive connotation of conviction and openness.

## Source and Origin
Like several other four-character idioms in this set, 旗幟鮮明 fuses two originally separate two-character terms, each with its own classical source.

**旗幟** — from **《列子·黃帝》** (Liezi, "The Yellow Emperor" chapter):
> 黃帝與炎帝戰於阪泉之野，帥熊、羆、狼、豹、貙、虎為前驅，雕、鶡、鷹、鳶為旗幟。
> (The Yellow Emperor did battle with the Flame Emperor on the field of Banquan, leading bears, brown bears, wolves, leopards, lynxes, and tigers as his vanguard, with eagles, pheasants, hawks, and kites serving as his banners.)

This legendary account of the mythic battle between the Yellow Emperor and the Flame Emperor uses 旗幟 for birds of prey serving symbolically as battle standards — an early, striking instance of the word in its military-emblem sense.

**鮮明** — from **《漢書·王吉傳》** (Book of Han, Biography of Wang Ji):
> 皆好車馬衣服，其自奉養極為鮮明，而亡金銀錦繡之物。
> (They were all fond of fine carriages, horses, and clothing, keeping their own dress and bearing extremely vivid and bright, and yet possessed no gold, silver, or embroidered treasures.)

This passage describes the Wang family's generations of officials, whose outward appearance was strikingly vivid and well-kept despite their reputation for personal integrity and frugality — the origin of 鮮明 in the sense of "vivid, sharply distinct."

Note: 《史記·高祖本紀》 also contains the word 旗幟, but only in its plain, literal sense of physical battlefield flags — not yet joined with 鮮明 or extended to the figurative "clearly stated position" sense this idiom carries today. The fixed four-character idiom 旗幟鮮明, joining both terms into their modern figurative meaning, crystallized later than either individual source.

All five readings are fully compositional, matching each constituent character's own standard reading.

## Cultural Notes
Most common in modern Mandarin political and ideological contexts, where it describes stating one's stance clearly and without ambiguity — a positive quality associated with conviction and openness rather than evasiveness. Japanese and Korean usage carries the same sense of taking an explicit, publicly recognizable position, particularly in institutional or public settings. The idiom is not typically used for private or personal matters — it implies a position stated publicly enough to be "seen" the way a banner is seen.

## Example Sentences
1. 他在這個問題上態度旗幟鮮明，毫不含糊。
2. The candidate was _qí zhì xiān míng_ on the issue, leaving no doubt where she stood.
3. 彼はその問題について旗幟鮮明な立場を取った。
4. Unlike his predecessor, the new director was _kỳ xí tiên minh_ about the company's direction.

## Sentiment
Positive; praises clarity, conviction, and openness in publicly stating one's position or principles.
