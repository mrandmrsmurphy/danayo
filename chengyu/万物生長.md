## Literal Meaning
- [万](../characters/万.md) — ten thousand, myriad
- [物](../characters/物%20(char).md) — thing
- [生](../characters/生.md) — to grow, to be born
- [長](../characters/長%20(char).md) — to grow, to lengthen

Literally: "the ten thousand things grow."

## Extended Meaning
All living things flourish through the vital power of Heaven and Earth; the natural order of life's growth and renewal. Still used today, especially in seasonal prose or philosophical writing, for the growth and renewal of nature, society, or human potential more broadly.

## Source and Origin
萬物 ("the ten thousand things") together with verbs of growth and generation is a stock pattern of early Chinese cosmological writing, but I could not establish an early text that contains the fixed four-character string 萬物生長. Passages I checked against Wikisource that do carry the underlying idea:

- **《周易·繫辭下》**, chapter 4:
  > "天地絪縕，萬物化醇；男女構精，萬物化生。"
  > (Heaven and earth interweave and the myriad things are transformed and enriched; male and female join their essences and the myriad things are transformed and born.)
- **《禮記·中庸》**:
  > "萬物並育而不相害，道並行而不相悖。小德川流，大德敦化，此天地之所以爲大也。"
  > (The myriad things are nurtured together without harming one another, and the ways run side by side without conflict. Lesser virtue flows like rivers, greater virtue transforms in depth; this is why heaven and earth are great.)

Both use 化生 and 並育 rather than 生長, which is why 萬物化生 and 萬物並育 are separate expressions (see Cultural Notes).

**Corrections and what was not verified.** The earlier version of this page quoted passages that are not in the cited texts, and they have been removed: 荀子·王制 does contain 春耕、夏耘、秋收、冬藏, but followed by "四者不失時，故五穀不絕，而百姓有餘食也", not 萬物生長之時也, and the string 萬物生長 does not appear in the chapter; no 淮南子·天文訓 line "春分而後，天地氣交，萬物生長" was found, and the string 萬物生長 does not appear in that chapter; the lines "萬物方盛，天道乃定" are not in 詩經·小雅·四月; and the "萬物並育" line is from the *Zhongyong*, not the *Mencius*. 《禮記·月令》 was also named without a checked quotation. I could not check the claim about Mao Zedong's 《七律·長征》 against a text and it does not match the poem as I know it, so it has been dropped. The line 萬物生長靠太陽 is a modern slogan, not classical, and I did not trace its origin.

## Standard Form
**萬物生長** (traditional) / **万物生长** (simplified) — both current, no meaningful difference in usage; Dan'a'yo standardizes on the mixed-script 万物生長.

## Cultural Notes
The phrase carries different philosophical freight depending on tradition:
- **Confucian:** the *Zhongyong* passage above presents the nurturing of all things side by side as the greatness of heaven and earth.
- **Daoist:** reflects the spontaneous self-generation of the Dao — "the Dao gives birth to one, one to two, two to three, three to the ten thousand things" (道生一，一生二，二生三，三生萬物).
- **Modern:** the growth and renewal of nature, society, or human potential generally.

Related but distinct expressions in the same semantic field include 萬物化生 ("all things are transformed and generated"), 萬物並育 ("all things grow together"), and 生生不息 ("life begets life without ceasing") — these are separate idioms, not variant spellings of this one.

## Example Sentences
1. 春來萬物生長，草木萌發。("With spring's arrival, all things grow and plants sprout anew.")
2. 봄이 되면 만물생장한다. ("When spring comes, all things grow.")
3. When spring arrives, all things flourish — *vạn vật sinh trưởng*.

## Sentiment
Serene and life-affirming; expresses the cosmic pulse of renewal. Where idioms like 鏡花水月 or 空中樓閣 speak of illusion, 万物生長 turns to reality and vitality — the eternal spring of creation.
