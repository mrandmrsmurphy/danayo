[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [招災](../words/招災.md) — to invite disaster
- [引禍](../words/引禍.md) — to bring on calamity

Literally: "invite disaster and draw calamity." 招災 and 引禍 are each already established two-character compounds; their pairing here intensifies the sense of active causation.

## Extended Meaning
A general maxim expressing that certain actions bring about ruin. In biblical framing it often refers to rebellion against God that provokes judgment, though the phrase can also function more broadly as proverbial wisdom about moral causality.

## Source and Origin
招災引禍 is not drawn from a single verse but distills a theme that pervades biblical literature — that wrongdoing brings its own consequences:

- **Proverbs 8:36** — "But he that sinneth against me wrongeth his own soul: all they that hate me love death."
- **Jeremiah 7:19** — "Do they provoke me to anger? saith the LORD: do they not provoke themselves to the confusion of their own faces?"
- **Galatians 6:7** — "Be not deceived; God is not mocked: for whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap."

Old Testament prophetic texts frequently portray idolatry and injustice as actions that actively "bring" calamity upon a people, rather than calamity arriving as impersonal misfortune. The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders Galatians 6:7, checked on cnbible.com, as 「不要自欺，神是輕慢不得的。人種的是甚麼，收的也是甚麼。」, the Chinese Bible's own form of the "sow and reap" principle.

I found **no dictionary entry for 招災引禍 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources; the standard Chinese expression with this sense is 招災惹禍 ("to invite disaster and stir up trouble"), which a search summary of dictionary entries traces to Li Yu's 《巧團圓》 (unchecked). 招災引禍 appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character formulation close to it, built from the existing words 招災 and 引禍. The earlier remark that Chinese Bible commentary "often uses" phrases such as 自招禍患 was not verified and is not repeated.

## Cultural Notes
In classical Chinese moral discourse, 招災引禍 expresses a general principle of moral causality without necessarily specifying divine agency — it can function almost proverbially, describing any self-inflicted misfortune. In biblical theology, however, calamity is not impersonal fate: it is either the natural consequence of folly or the judicial act of a covenant God, and the language of "inviting" disaster underscores human responsibility rather than blind chance. The doubling of 招 and 引 emphasizes this active causation — ruin is not random but summoned by wrongdoing. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 招災引禍 can function broadly in wisdom contexts or more specifically as covenantal warning, depending on the surrounding discourse.

## Example Sentences
1. 背約棄義，實乃招災引禍之始。("To abandon covenant and righteousness is the beginning of inviting disaster.")
2. 「不要自欺，神是輕慢不得的。人種的是甚麼，收的也是甚麼。」(加拉太書 6:7，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. The prophets warned that idolatry would 招災引禍 upon the nation, not as arbitrary punishment but as its natural harvest.
4. 貪婪與欺詐，終將招災引禍於己身。("Greed and deceit will in the end bring disaster and calamity upon oneself.")

## Sentiment
Warning, causal, morally instructive. Stresses that actions have serious and often self-induced consequences.
