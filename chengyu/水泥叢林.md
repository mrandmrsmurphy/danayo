## Literal Meaning
- [水 (char)](../characters/水%20(char).md) — water
- [泥 (char)](../characters/泥%20(char).md) — mud
- [叢](../characters/叢.md) — thicket
- [林 (char)](../characters/林%20(char).md) — grove

Literally: "Cement jungle." It is a compound of two words, [水泥](../words/水泥.md) "cement" and [叢林](../words/叢林.md) "jungle," rather than a four-character idiom built from four separate morphemes.

## Extended Meaning
Figuratively, "concrete jungle": a dense, hard, impersonal modern city of concrete buildings, seen as a place where people must struggle to survive, like animals in a jungle. It is mostly used with a critical or weary tone about big-city life, and also in a neutral descriptive sense.

## Source and Origin
This is a modern Chinese expression with no classical source, and I give no classical citation. According to Wiktionary, Chinese 水泥叢林 (shuǐní cónglín; simplified 水泥丛林) means "concrete jungle" (figurative), listed as a derived term of both 水泥 and 叢林, with a related Cantonese form 石屎森林. Wiktionary has no Korean, Japanese or Vietnamese entry for it. From memory, not verified: it is a calque of English "concrete jungle." I did not trace the date of its first Chinese use.

In Dan'a'yo it is used in the rendering of 躺平即是正义 on [China](../lookup/HSK/China.md) (sentence 12, 為 水泥叢林 ...).

## Standard Form
**水泥叢林**
- 水泥丛林 — simplified form (叢 → 丛)
- 水泥森林 — a variant listed by Wiktionary under 水泥 (not checked further); Cantonese uses 石屎森林 "concrete forest"

## Cultural Notes
**In Chinese**, the phrase is a neutral-to-critical set phrase in journalism, essays and everyday talk about big cities. Cantonese has its own form, 石屎森林.

**In Japanese**, the usual expression is コンクリートジャングル (from memory, not re-checked); 水泥 is not a Japanese word, since cement is セメント.

**In Korean and Vietnamese**, no native attestation of this four-character form was found; the readings above are compositional.

## Example Sentences
1. 他厭倦了水泥叢林裡的生活。
2. 水泥叢林之中，很難見到一片綠地。
3. She left the concrete jungle of the city for a small village.

## Sentiment
Mostly negative or weary; casual to journalistic in register.
