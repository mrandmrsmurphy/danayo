[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [剣 (char)](characters/剣%20(char).md) — sword
- [生](characters/生.md) — live
- [死](characters/死.md) — die

Literally: "Sword, live; sword, die" — by the sword one lives, by the sword one dies.

## Extended Meaning
The idiom states a principle of reciprocal, karmic violence: one who makes the sword — force, aggression, coercion — the instrument of one's life will in turn be destroyed by that same instrument. It is not a literal prediction so much as a moral warning, used to describe warlords, tyrants, gang violence, arms races, or any cycle of retaliation. Always negative or cautionary in valence; never used approvingly. It appears in political commentary, historical narrative, and religious/ethical teaching against vengeance and militarism.

## Source and Origin
**Matthew 26:52** (Gospel of Matthew), set at Jesus's arrest in the Garden of Gethsemane. When one of his companions (identified as Peter in John 18:10) drew a sword and struck the high priest's servant, Jesus rebuked him:

> "Then Jesus said to him, 'Put your sword back into its place. For all who take the sword will perish by the sword.'" (Matt 26:52, ESV)

Greek: *πάντες γὰρ οἱ λαβόντες μάχαιραν ἐν μαχαίρῃ ἀπολοῦνται* — "for all who take up the sword will perish by the sword."

The saying condenses an older Biblical theme (cf. Genesis 9:6, Revelation 13:10) that violence returns upon the violent. Note that the verse itself says those who *take* the sword will *perish* by it; the familiar English "live by the sword, die by the sword," which the four-character form mirrors, is a later paraphrase.

Chinese Bibles render the verse 「收刀入鞘吧！凡動刀的，必死在刀下。」 (RCUV, checked), and Japanese sources title the saying 剣を取る者は剣によって滅ぶ. I found **no attestation of 剣生剣死 (劍生劍死, 剑生剑死) as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character rendering of the verse, like the other Biblical chengyu on the grouping page, shaped to the four-character idiom mold native to the region (compare the native karmic-retribution idiom 自業自得) even though its source is the New Testament rather than a classical Chinese text. The earlier claim on this page that it crystallized in East Asian Bible translation and commentary was unsupported and has been removed.

## Standard Form
**剣生剣死** (Japanese shinjitai 剣) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 劍生劍死 — traditional Chinese form (劍)
- 剑生剑死 — simplified Chinese form (剑)

## Variants
The attested renderings of the verse are longer phrases rather than a single fixed idiom:
- 凡動刀的，必死在刀下 — RCUV wording of Matthew 26:52
- 剣を取る者は剣によって滅ぶ — the Japanese form of the saying

Three four-character-or-longer variants listed here earlier (取剣死剣, 用剣必亡剣, 以剣生者以剣死) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
The idea that violence returns on the violent is intelligible well beyond Christian circles, because it fits a pre-existing native concept of karmic retribution (因果報應, 自業自得), so the form reads as immediately clear to non-Christian audiences. The saying itself is best known in Christian communities as the words of Jesus at his arrest. Because 剣生剣死 is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「凡動刀的，必死在刀下。」(Matt 26:52, RCUV 和合本修訂版, "All who draw the sword will die by the sword.")
2. 暴君剣生剣死，終遭反噬。("The tyrant lived by the sword and died by the sword, ultimately suffering reversal.")
3. 基督教人勿剣生剣死，当以和平待人。("Christ teaches people not to live by the sword and die by the sword, but to treat others with peace.")
4. Every warlord who built his rule on the blade eventually learned the truth of 剣生剣死 the hard way.

## Sentiment
Cautionary, karmic. Used to warn against violence, express the inevitability of retribution, or describe the self-destructive nature of aggression.
