## Literal Meaning
- [瑠](characters/瑠.md) — lapis lazuli
- [璃 (char)](characters/璃%20(char).md) — glass, glaze
- [清](characters/清.md) — clear
- [天 (char)](characters/天%20(char).md) — heaven, sky

Literally: "Lapis lazuli, clear heaven."

## Extended Meaning
瑠璃清天 evokes a vision of divine majesty: a brilliant blue, gemlike surface as clear and vast as the sky itself. It is used to describe divine glory, heavenly beauty, sacred visions, or the overwhelming majesty of God's presence — mystical and awe-inspiring rather than merely decorative or descriptive of an ordinary gemstone.

## Source and Origin
**Exodus 24:10**, describing the theophany on Mount Sinai, where Moses and the elders of Israel saw God and survived:

> "And they saw the God of Israel. There was under his feet as it were a pavement of sapphire stone, like the very heaven for clearness." (Ex 24:10, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本), checked at [維基文庫〈聖經 (和合本)/出埃及記〉](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/聖經_(和合本)/出埃及記), renders the verse 「他們看見以色列的　神、他脚下彷彿有平鋪的藍寶石、如同天色明淨。」 (that edition writes 脚, punctuates with 、 and leaves a space before 神). An earlier version of this page also quoted the 聖經新譯本 wording and said cnbible.com had been checked; I could not reach cnbible.com in this pass and did not re-check that wording or the ESV text above. The Chinese Bible's own words are therefore 藍寶石 ("sapphire") and 天色明淨.

I found **no attestation of 瑠璃清天 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage that substitutes 瑠璃 for the Chinese Bible's 藍寶石.

瑠璃 ("lapis lazuli," the established Japanese reading of this compound, るり) evokes the brilliant blue stone, while 清天 ("clear heaven") captures the extraordinary clarity "like the very heaven" of the original text. The sapphire pavement beneath God's feet suggests both transcendent beauty and the firmament of heaven itself.

## Variants
The attested Chinese phrases are 平鋪的藍寶石 and 如同天色明淨 (Chinese Union Version of Exodus 24:10). Three variants listed here earlier (蒼玉清天, 瑠璃舗天, 足下瑠璃如天) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
瑠璃清天 is a reference to this rare Sinai theophany, suited to sermons and religious writing on visions of divine glory. I did not check how common it is in any country. 瑠璃 itself (as "glass" or "lapis lazuli") carries independent aesthetic associations in Buddhist art and East Asian material culture more broadly, giving the idiom an extra layer of resonance for readers already familiar with 瑠璃 as a treasured, luminous material.

## Example Sentences
*Example 1 is the real 和合本 verse; examples 2–4 are constructed.*

1. 「他們看見以色列的　神、他脚下彷彿有平鋪的藍寶石、如同天色明淨。」(Ex 24:10, 和合本 Chinese Union Version; a real quotation, checked at the Wikisource page linked above)
2. 摩西等見神，其足下有瑠璃清天之景。("Moses and the others saw God, and under his feet was the vision of lapis clear as heaven.")
3. 神之榮光如瑠璃清天，超越人之所能想。("God's glory is like lapis clear as heaven, surpassing what humans can imagine.")
4. The stained glass caught the afternoon light and for a moment the whole nave seemed 瑠璃清天 — luminous, blue, impossibly clear.

## Sentiment
Mystical, awe-inspiring, transcendent. Used to describe divine glory, heavenly beauty, sacred visions, or the overwhelming majesty of God's presence.
