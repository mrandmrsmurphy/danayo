## Literal Meaning
- [諸 (char)](../characters/諸%20(char).md) — various, all
- [法 (char)](../characters/法%20(char).md) — dharma, phenomenon, law
- [無 (char)](../characters/無%20(char).md) — without, lacking
- [我 (char)](../characters/我%20(char).md) — self, I

Literally: "All dharmas are without self." 法 here translates Sanskrit *dharma* in its technical Buddhist sense — any phenomenon, mental or physical — not its ordinary sense of "law, rule."

## Extended Meaning
No phenomenon, mental or physical, possesses an independent, permanent, unchanging self or essence. What appears to be a fixed "self" — a person, a thing, even a thought — is in fact a composite of interdependent, ever-changing processes. This is a formal doctrinal claim, not a general observation about humility or ego; it is used almost exclusively in religious, philosophical, and literary registers.

## Source and Origin
Together with [[諸行無常]] and [[涅盤寂静]], this idiom names one of the **Three Dharma Seals** (三法印) — the three-part formula by which Nāgārjuna (龍樹) identifies genuine Buddhist teaching. **《大智度論》卷二十二** states:
> 佛法印有三種：一者一切有為法念念生滅皆無常，二者一切法無我，三者寂滅涅槃。
> (The seals of the Buddha-dharma are three: first, that all conditioned phenomena are impermanent, arising and ceasing moment to moment; second, that all dharmas are without self; third, that cessation is nirvana.)

The second clause, **一切法無我** ("all dharmas are without self"), is the direct doctrinal source of this idiom; 諸法無我 is a synonymous crystallization using 諸 ("the various, all") in place of 一切 ("all, every"). The same formula, again using 一切法無我, recurs in the treatise's fifteenth fascicle. The underlying teaching (Sanskrit *sarva-dharmā anātmānaḥ*, "all phenomena are without self") appears throughout the earlier Āgama literature and is foundational to both Theravāda and Mahāyāna doctrine — Nāgārjuna's formula fixes it as one of the three doctrinal seals rather than originating the concept itself.

All five readings are fully compositional, matching each constituent character's own standard reading.

## Cultural Notes
In Chinese Buddhist thought, 諸法無我 is a technical term central to discussions of emptiness (空) and dependent origination (緣起) — it is cited alongside 諸行無常 as one half of the doctrinal foundation for the third seal, nirvana. In Japanese, the underlying concept of 無我 (*muga*) has also influenced broader cultural ideas of selflessness and egolessness, often in a softened ethical sense outside strictly doctrinal contexts. In Korean Buddhist teaching, 제법무아 remains a standard doctrinal expression. Unlike many chengyu, this phrase is not metaphorical but philosophical and analytical — it names a formal conclusion of Buddhist reasoning rather than describing a situation or trait.

## Example Sentences
1. 佛教強調諸法無我，認為萬物皆無固定自性。
2. Buddhism holds that _zhū fǎ wú wǒ_ — no phenomenon possesses a fixed, independent self.
3. 諸法無我は仏教の基本的な教えの一つである。
4. 불교에서는 제법무아를 중요한 진리로 본다.

## Sentiment
Reflective and analytical rather than emotional; a foundational statement of Buddhist doctrine, denying any fixed self to persons or phenomena alike.
