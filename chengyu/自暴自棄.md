## Literal Meaning
- [自](../characters/自.md) — self
- [暴 (char)](../characters/暴%20(char).md) — to violate, to ruin
- [棄](../characters/棄.md) — to discard, abandon

Literally: "to violate oneself and cast oneself away."

## Extended Meaning
To give up on oneself; to fall into despair or reckless self-neglect. It describes a state of discouragement in which a person stops trying to improve their situation or behavior, emphasizing self-directed defeat — the obstacle is not external hardship but the decision to abandon one's own capacity for change.

## Source and Origin
From **《孟子·離婁上》**, where Mencius distinguishes two related failures of moral character:

> "自暴者，不可與有言也；自棄者，不可與有為也。言非禮義，謂之自暴也；吾身不能居仁由義，謂之自棄也。仁，人之安宅也；義，人之正路也。曠安宅而弗居，舍正路而不由，哀哉！"
> ("One who violates himself cannot be reasoned with; one who abandons himself cannot be worked with. To speak against propriety and righteousness is called self-violation; to be unable to dwell in benevolence and follow righteousness is called self-abandonment. Benevolence is people's peaceful dwelling; righteousness is people's proper path. To leave the peaceful dwelling unoccupied and abandon the proper path untaken — how lamentable!")

暴 here means to violate or ruin, while 棄 means to discard; both verbs describe acts directed at oneself, and the doubling (自...自...) intensifies the sense of self-inflicted abandonment. In the Mencian context the phrase describes people who forfeit their own moral potential by refusing correction or instruction — the fault lies not in initial ignorance but in rejecting the very possibility of moral cultivation, which for Mencius blocks the development of innate moral tendencies.

## Cultural Notes
Across the region, 自暴自棄 describes a state of discouragement in which a person stops trying to improve their situation or behavior, appearing in educational settings, psychological discussion, journalism, and everyday speech, with a tone that can be sympathetic or admonitory depending on context. Though the idiom's ultimate root is a fairly technical Confucian distinction between two specific moral failures, modern usage has broadened it into a general term for any self-defeating loss of hope or effort.

## Example Sentences
1. 考試失利後，他一度自暴自棄，不肯再努力。("After failing the exam, he fell into despair for a while and refused to try again.")
2. Don't let one setback push you into 自暴自棄 — get back up and try again.
3. 一度の失敗で自暴自棄になってはいけない。("You mustn't fall into self-abandonment over a single failure.")
4. 자포자기하지 말고 다시 도전해 보세요。("Don't give up on yourself — try again.")

## Sentiment
Sympathetic to admonitory, depending on context. Names a self-defeating loss of hope or effort, often with an implicit call to resist it.
