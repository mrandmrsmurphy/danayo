## Literal Meaning
- [禍](../characters/禍.md) — calamity, disaster
- [延](../characters/延.md) — extend, prolong
- [子孫](../words/子孫.md) — descendants ([子](../characters/子.md) + [孫](../characters/孫.md))

Literally: "calamity extends to descendants."

## Extended Meaning
A warning that the consequences of serious, entrenched wrongdoing are not confined to the wrongdoer but extend into the lives of their descendants. Applied biblically, it names the covenantal, conditional principle that persistent sin carries generational weight — without teaching mechanical fate, denying individual responsibility, or overriding the possibility of repentance.

## Source and Origin
禍延子孫 is itself a pre-existing classical-style Chinese expression, attested in Chinese moral and historical discourse describing political downfall, clan punishment, or the long shadow of wrongdoing within family lines, without inherent theological specification. The family maxim collection **《朱柏廬治家格言》** (17th century) contains the line "匿怨而用暗箭，禍延子孫" (read at [維基文庫〈朱子家訓〉](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hant/%E6%9C%B1%E5%AD%90%E5%AE%B6%E8%A8%93), where the sentence reads 「見色而起淫心，報在妻女；匿怨而用暗箭，禍延子孫」 with a semicolon between the two clauses; the page of that title on Wikisource is the Zhu Bolu text, not the Zhu Xi work of similar name, per the search listing, and I read only this sentence there) ("harboring a grudge while striking in secret brings calamity down upon one's descendants"), and a related conceptual pairing appears in **《周易·坤·文言》** ([維基文庫〈周易/坤〉](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/周易/坤), 文言曰 section, line read there as 「積善之家，必有餘慶；積不善之家，必有餘殃。」): "積善之家，必有餘慶；積不善之家，必有餘殃" ("a household that accumulates good deeds will have surplus blessing; a household that accumulates ill deeds will have surplus calamity") — not the exact four-character formula, but the same older concern that a family's fortune or misfortune extends beyond any one individual's life.

Dan'a'yo repurposes this pre-existing secular idiom (which is why `origin: Bible` records the Dan'a'yo biblical sense and not the earliest source of the four characters, as with 骨肉相連) for a specifically biblical principle, articulated in **Exodus 20:5**, **Deuteronomy 5:9**, and **Exodus 34:7**:

> "...visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children unto the third and fourth generation of them that hate me." (Exodus 20:5, KJV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本), checked at [維基文庫〈聖經 (和合本)/出埃及記〉](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/聖經_(和合本)/出埃及記), renders it 「恨我的、我必追討他的罪、自父及子、直到三四代．」 (that edition punctuates with 、 and ．). An earlier version of this page also quoted the 聖經新譯本 and said cnbible.com had been checked; I could not reach cnbible.com in this pass and did not re-check that wording or the KJV text above. So the Chinese Bible's own phrase is 追討他的罪，自父及子，直到三四代. In biblical theology this generational extension is covenantal and conditional: it applies specifically to those who persist in hatred of God, and is immediately contrasted in the same passage with mercy shown "unto thousands" of those who love him — an asymmetry in which mercy far outweighs judgment. Later prophetic texts further nuance the principle: **Ezekiel 18** emphasizes personal accountability directly, and **Jeremiah 31:29–30** explicitly rejects simplistic generational blame ("the son shall not bear the iniquity of the father"), so that in Scripture generational consequence coexists with, rather than overriding, individual moral agency.

## Cultural Notes
Because 禍延子孫 predates its biblical application, its adoption into Dan'a'yo represents a deliberate theological reorientation: in classical discourse it describes inherited misfortune generically, while in biblical application it describes covenantal judgment within a moral framework governed by a just and merciful God. Standing alone, the phrase can sound fatalistic; within the larger biblical arc it is restrained by divine patience, the promise of surpassing mercy, and the possibility of repentance and restoration. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, the expression serves as a sober warning about the historical weight of sin rather than a doctrine of inherited guilt.

## Example Sentences
*Example 2 is the real 和合本 text; examples 1, 3 and 4 are constructed.*

1. 若一族長久悖逆，或見禍延子孫，然悔改亦可止其延。("If a household persists in rebellion, calamity may extend to descendants; yet repentance may arrest its course.")
2. 「恨我的、我必追討他的罪、自父及子、直到三四代．」(出埃及記 20:5，和合本 Chinese Union Version; a real quotation, the last clause of the verse, checked at the Wikisource page linked above)
3. Ezekiel 18 tempers the older warning of 禍延子孫 by insisting each soul answers for its own sin.
4. 家風敗壞，恐禍延子孫；然神之憐憫，遠勝於怒。("A corrupted family legacy risks calamity extending to descendants; yet God's mercy far exceeds his anger.")

## Sentiment
Sobering, cautionary, historically conscious. Warns that actions reverberate across generations, yet remains bounded by the larger biblical arc of mercy.
