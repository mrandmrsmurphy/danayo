## Literal Meaning
- [六 (char)](characters/六%20(char).md) — six
- [作 (char)](characters/作%20(char).md) — to work
- [一 (char)](characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [止](characters/止.md) — to stop, cease

Literally: "Six work, one stop."

## Extended Meaning
六作一止 compresses the Sabbath commandment into a compact, balanced formula: six units of labor followed by one unit of complete cessation. It is used prescriptively to describe the discipline of Sabbath-keeping and, by extension, any healthy rhythm alternating sustained work with real, complete rest — not partial slowdown but full cessation. Invoked in religious instruction, discussions of work-rest balance, and reflections on the creation pattern itself.

## Source and Origin
**Exodus 20:9–10** and **Deuteronomy 5:13–14**, the Fourth Commandment:

> "Six days you shall labor, and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the LORD your God. On it you shall not do any work." (Ex 20:9–10, ESV)

六作一止 renders the commandment's six-work/one-rest ratio directly, with 止 ("stop, cease") chosen specifically to emphasize complete cessation from work rather than merely reduced activity. This rhythm mirrors God's own pattern in creation (Genesis 2:2–3), establishing the weekly cycle of labor and sacred rest as a pattern with cosmic rather than merely practical origin — a principle that extends beyond rest itself to include worship, remembrance of God's creative and redemptive work, and trust in divine provision, and which has shaped both Jewish and Christian practice, applied somewhat differently in each tradition.

## Variants
- 六日作一日止 — a fuller rendering making the units of time explicit ("six days work, one day rest")
- 六労一息 — emphasizing cessation with a different pairing of characters ("six-labor, one-rest")
- 作六息一 — an alternate word order with the same sense

## Cultural Notes
六作一止 functions across the CJKV Christian sphere as a direct reference to the Fourth Commandment, invoked in sermons and religious education on Sabbath observance. It also resonates more broadly in modern discussion of work-life balance and rest discipline, where the six-to-one ratio is cited as a model of sustainable rhythm independent of its specifically religious framing.

## Example Sentences
1. 「六日要勞碌做你一切的工，但第七日是向耶和華你神當守的安息日。」(Ex 20:9–10, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 神命六作一止，使人得安息敬拜。("God commanded six days of work, one day of rest, so that people might have rest and worship.")
3. 守安息日者行六作一止之律，記念創造之工。("Those who keep the Sabbath follow the law of six work, one rest, commemorating the work of creation.")
4. Even outside any religious framing, she found that 六作一止 — six days of real effort, one day of real rest — kept her from burning out.

## Sentiment
Prescriptive, rhythmic, restorative. Used to express the pattern of work and rest, the discipline of Sabbath-keeping, or the divine rhythm of labor and worship.
