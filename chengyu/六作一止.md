## Literal Meaning
- [六 (char)](characters/六%20(char).md) — six
- [作 (char)](characters/作%20(char).md) — to work
- [一 (char)](characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [止](characters/止.md) — to stop, cease

Literally: "Six work, one stop."

## Extended Meaning
六作一止 compresses the Sabbath commandment into a compact, balanced formula: six units of labor followed by one unit of complete cessation. It is used prescriptively to describe the discipline of Sabbath-keeping and, by extension, any healthy rhythm alternating sustained work with real, complete rest — not partial slowdown but full cessation. Invoked in religious instruction, discussions of work-rest balance, and reflections on the creation pattern itself.

## Source and Origin
**Exodus 20:9–10** and **Deuteronomy 5:13–14**, the Fourth Commandment:

> "Six days you shall labor, and do all your work, but the seventh day is a Sabbath to the LORD your God. On it you shall not do any work." (Ex 20:9–10, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders Exodus 20:9–10 as 「六日要勞碌做你一切的工，但第七日是向耶和華你神當守的安息日。這一日你和你的兒女、僕婢、牲畜，並你城裡寄居的客旅，無論何工都不可做。」 (checked on cnbible.com, which shows the same wording in the 現代標點和合本; the 聖經新譯本 reads 「六日要勞碌，作你一切工作。」). The Chinese Bible's own phrases are therefore 勞碌做…工 for the six days and 安息日 for the seventh.

I found **no attestation of 六作一止 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage that renders the commandment's six-work/one-rest ratio directly, with 止 ("stop, cease") chosen to stress complete cessation from work rather than reduced activity. The earlier description of it as an established Bible-translation form was unsupported and has been removed.

The rhythm mirrors God's own pattern in creation (Genesis 2:2–3), establishing the weekly cycle of labor and sacred rest as a pattern with cosmic rather than merely practical origin — a principle that extends beyond rest itself to include worship, remembrance of God's creative and redemptive work, and trust in divine provision, and which has shaped both Jewish and Christian practice, applied somewhat differently in each tradition.

## Variants
The attested Chinese rendering is the Chinese Union Version of Exodus 20:9–10 (see Source and Origin). Three variants listed here earlier (六日作一日止, 六労一息, 作六息一) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
六作一止 is a reference to the Fourth Commandment, suited to sermons and religious education on Sabbath observance. It also resonates in modern discussion of work-life balance and rest discipline, where the six-to-one ratio is cited as a model of sustainable rhythm independent of its religious framing. Because it is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「六日要勞碌做你一切的工，但第七日是向耶和華你神當守的安息日。」(Ex 20:9–10, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 神命六作一止，使人得安息敬拜。("God commanded six days of work, one day of rest, so that people might have rest and worship.")
3. 守安息日者行六作一止之律，記念創造之工。("Those who keep the Sabbath follow the law of six work, one rest, commemorating the work of creation.")
4. Even outside any religious framing, she found that 六作一止 — six days of real effort, one day of real rest — kept her from burning out.

## Sentiment
Prescriptive, rhythmic, restorative. Used to express the pattern of work and rest, the discipline of Sabbath-keeping, or the divine rhythm of labor and worship.
