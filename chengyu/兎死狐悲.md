## Literal Meaning
- [兎 (char)](../characters/兎%20(char).md) — rabbit, hare
- [死](../characters/死.md) — die
- [狐](../characters/狐.md) — fox
- [悲 (char)](../characters/悲%20(char).md) — grieve, be sad

Literally: "The hare dies; the fox grieves."

## Extended Meaning
To grieve over the fall of one's own kind: when someone like you meets disaster, you feel its shadow fall on yourself. It is said of people in the same camp or the same trade, especially of rulers, warlords or villains, who mourn a peer's defeat because they fear the same end. The word 物傷其類 ("creatures hurt for their kind") is its usual companion.

## Source and Origin
The earliest form is **狐死兔泣** ("when the fox dies, the hare weeps"), in **《宋史·李全傳下》** (卷477). In 1227 (寶慶三年), after the rebel leader Li Quan was reported dead, his wife Lady Yang sent a messenger to Xia Quan, Li Quan's rival, to win him over:

> 「將軍非山東歸附耶？狐死兔泣，李氏滅，夏氏寧獨存？願將軍垂盼。」
> "Are you not a man of Shandong who came over to us? When the fox dies, the hare weeps: if the Li house is destroyed, how could the Xia house survive alone? I hope you will look kindly on us."

The modern word order 兔死狐悲 is later. 《通俗編》 (卷28) cites the Song Shi line and remarks that today's phrase is 兔死狐悲. Checked on zh.Wikisource on 2026-11-09: 宋史/卷477 for the quote and 通俗編/28 for the remark. Wikisource marks the 宋史 page as only 25% proofread, so the wording is worth rechecking against a printed edition.

## Standard Form
**兎死狐悲** — Dan'a'yo standard, using 兎, the form Dan'a'yo prefers for the rabbit character.
- 兔死狐悲 — with 兔 (兔 is an alias of 兎)
- 狐死兔泣 — the older form from the Song Shi, with the animals reversed and 泣 for 悲

## Cultural Notes
A common idiom in modern Chinese, usually in newspaper and political commentary, often with a faint sneer. It is the same thought as 物傷其類, and it differs from 唇亡齒寒, which is about shared practical danger, because this one is about fellow feeling between those of a kind. Korean 토사호비 (also 토사호읍, from 狐死兔泣) and Vietnamese thố tử hồ bi are the usual forms in those languages.

## Example Sentences
1. 狐死兔泣，李氏滅，夏氏寧獨存？
2. 看到老同行的店倒閉了，他兎死狐悲，連夜算自己的賬。
3. The tyrants of the region grieved as one when their neighbour fell: hare dies, fox weeps.

## Sentiment
Mildly negative; the grief is sympathetic but self-interested, and is often used with irony.
