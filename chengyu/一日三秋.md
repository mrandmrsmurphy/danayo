[Misc. Chengyu](chengyu/Misc.%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [日](../characters/日%20(char).md) — day
- [三](../characters/三%20(char).md) — three
- [秋](../characters/秋%20(char).md) — autumn(s)

Literally: "one day [is like] three autumns." Each autumn comes once a year, so "three autumns" stands for about three years, and the phrase expresses an extreme dilation of perceived time.

## Extended Meaning
A single day feels as long as three years. Used to describe intense longing, especially in separation from a loved one, friend, or ruler — it conveys emotional impatience, yearning, or anxious anticipation, not literal duration.

## Source and Origin
The source is the **《詩經·王風·采葛》** ("Book of Odes," *Airs of Wang*, "Gathering Kudzu"), checked against the Wikisource text. The poem has three stanzas that differ only in the plant gathered and the span of time, each longer than the last:

> "彼采葛兮，一日不見，如三月兮。
> 彼采蕭兮，一日不見，如三秋兮。
> 彼采艾兮，一日不見，如三歲兮。"
> ("She is off gathering kudzu — one day without seeing her is like three months. She is off gathering southernwood (蕭) — one day without seeing her is like three autumns. She is off gathering mugwort (艾) — one day without seeing her is like three years.")

三秋 is therefore in the **second** stanza, where the plant is 蕭, not 葛; the poem's title comes from the first stanza. The chengyu condenses that line (一日不見，如三秋) into the fixed four characters 一日三秋. The progression from 三月 to 三秋 to 三歲 also shows that "autumn" is being used as a unit of about a year.

**Not verified.** I did not find the earliest text in which the four-character form 一日三秋 appears as a fixed phrase, so how and when it crystallized from the poem is not stated here.

## Standard Form
**一日三秋** — no common orthographic variants; the form is consistent across Traditional and Simplified Chinese and appears identically in Japanese, Korean Hanja, and Vietnamese literary contexts. 一日千秋 (with 千 "thousand" for 三 "three") is a genuine, more hyperbolic alias carrying the same sense.

## Cultural Notes
- Strongly associated with classical poetic diction.
- Often appears in romantic or elegiac contexts.
- The emotional tone is more refined and literary than casual speech.
- While understandable in modern usage, it carries a distinctly classical resonance rather than feeling like ordinary conversation.

## Example Sentences
1. 別後思君，一日三秋。("Since parting and thinking of you, one day feels like three autumns.")
2. Since she left, each day feels like *yī rì sān qiū*.
3. Awaiting news from the front, the family felt as though one day dragged on like three years.

## Sentiment
Deeply emotional, nostalgic, and often romantic. Positive in affection, but rooted in longing and absence rather than joy.
