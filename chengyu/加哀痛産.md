[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [加](../characters/加.md) — to add, increase
- [哀](../characters/哀.md) — sorrow
- [痛](../characters/痛.md) — pain
- [産](../characters/産.md) — childbirth, bringing forth

Literally: "to increase sorrow and pain in childbirth."

## Extended Meaning
Refers to the divine judgment upon Eve after the Fall: the intensification of pain and sorrow in childbearing. By extension, it can denote the burden of consequences following disobedience, especially consequences tied to family life, motherhood, or the created order more broadly.

## Source and Origin
The phrase condenses **Genesis 3:16**, part of God's judgment on Eve after the Fall:

> "Unto the woman he said, I will greatly multiply thy sorrow and thy conception; in sorrow thou shalt bring forth children..." (KJV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders the verse 「又對女人說：我必多多加增你懷胎的苦楚；你生產兒女必多受苦楚。你必戀慕你丈夫；你丈夫必管轄你。」 (checked on cnbible.com, which shows the same wording in the 現代標點和合本; the 聖經新譯本 reads 「我要大大增加你懷胎的痛苦，你必在痛苦中生產兒女；…」). The Chinese Bible's own phrase is therefore 加增…懷胎的苦楚 ("increase the suffering of pregnancy") with 生產兒女必多受苦楚 for the bringing forth of children.

I found **no attestation of 加哀痛産 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage, a compact theological summary rather than a verbatim biblical quotation: 加 ("to add, to increase") mirrors the verse's emphasis on intensification, so that pain is multiplied, not invented from nothing. The earlier remark that this aligns with an "emphatic doubling construction of the underlying Hebrew" is a common reading of the Hebrew that I did not check in this pass.

## Cultural Notes
This idiom stands within the larger Christian doctrine of the Fall — not merely a biological description but a covenantal judgment within the Genesis narrative. Christian theology has interpreted the verse in several overlapping ways: as literal physical pain in childbirth, as encompassing the broader emotional anguish of motherhood, and as part of a wider distortion of male–female relations introduced in Genesis 3 (the verse continues with "thy desire shall be to thy husband, and he shall rule over thee"). Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, the four-character form is used to highlight the judicial aspect of the event within broader teaching on the Fall and its consequences.

## Example Sentences
1. 人類背逆之後，始有加哀痛産之罰。("After humanity's rebellion, there came the penalty of multiplied sorrow in childbirth.")
2. 「我必多多加增你懷胎的苦楚，你生產兒女必多受苦楚。」(創世記 3:16，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Preachers cite 加哀痛産 when teaching on the consequences of the Fall for family life.
4. 加哀痛産之言，提醒世人罪之代價。("The words 'sorrow multiplied in childbirth' remind humanity of the cost of sin.")

## Sentiment
Solemn, judicial, tragic. Evokes the gravity of divine judgment while implying the broader narrative of fall and redemption.
