## Literal Meaning
- [有 (char)](../characters/有%20(char).md) — have, possess; there is
- [備](../characters/備.md) — prepare; preparation, readiness
- [無 (char)](../characters/無%20(char).md) — lack; there is no
- [患 (char)](../characters/患%20(char).md) — disaster, calamity, affliction, trouble

Literally: "Having preparation, there is no calamity" — if you have prepared, trouble cannot reach you.

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary glosses the Chinese idiom and the Japanese idiom "to be prepared against adverse events; just in case". Its Korean entry 유비무환 is a hanja-form noun, "the state of being prepared and worry-free".

## Source and Origin
**《尚書·説命中》** (Shujing / Book of Documents, Yuming, Counsels of Shuo):

> 「…惟事事乃其有備，有備無患。無啟寵納侮，無恥過作非。」
> "…In every matter have your preparation; having preparation, there is no disaster. Do not invite favoritism and invite insult; do not be ashamed of a fault and so commit wrong."

The 《說命中》 line is verified on Wikisource (尚書/說命中). Traditionally it is Fu Yue's counsel to King Wu Ding of Shang.

A caution on this source: the 《說命》 chapters belong to the "Old Text" (古文) portion of the received 尚書, which since the Qing scholar 閻若璩 has been widely regarded as a later reconstruction rather than a surviving Shang text. The saying is therefore canonical in the received 尚書 but is not firmly datable to the Shang period, and the attribution to Fu Yue is the tradition's, not an established fact.

The same saying is also attested in the 《左傳·襄公十一年》, where the minister Wei Jiang (魏絳) advises Duke Dao of Jin:

> 書曰：『居安思危。』思則有備，有備無患。敢以此規。
> "The Documents say: 'When at peace, think of danger.' Thinking of it, one will be prepared; being prepared, there is no disaster. I venture to offer this as my admonition."

Here only 居安思危 is cited as a quotation from the 《書》 (a lost passage, as later commentators note), and 思則有備，有備無患 is Wei Jiang's own continuation. So the "full chain" peace → foresight → preparation → safety is the 《左傳》 wording, not a single line of the 尚書.

## Cultural Notes
English Wiktionary has Chinese and Japanese entries, with Japanese ゆうびむかん, Korean 유비무환 as descendants. The 《左傳》 quotation of 書曰「居安思危」 is verified in Siku Quanshu texts on Wikisource (for example 春秋臣傳 juan 16).

## Example Sentences
1. 《尚書·説命中》:「有備無患。」
   "Having preparation, there is no disaster."
2. 《左傳·襄公十一年》:「書曰：『居安思危。』思則有備，有備無患。」
   "When at peace, think of danger; thinking of danger, you will be prepared; being prepared, there is no disaster."
## Sentiment
Affirmative; it says that preparation prevents disaster.
