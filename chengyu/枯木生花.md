## Literal Meaning
- [枯 (char)](../characters/枯%20(char).md) — withered
- [木 (char)](../characters/木%20(char).md) — tree
- [生](../characters/生.md) — grow, put forth
- [花](../characters/花.md) — flower

Literally: "A withered tree puts forth flowers." It is made of two words, [枯木](../words/枯木.md) "withered tree" and [生花](../words/生花.md) "to bloom".

## Extended Meaning
A hopeless case comes back to life: an old or dying thing flowers again, a barren period ends, a failing person or cause has a sudden second spring. It is also used for an unexpected stroke of good luck. In Chan (Zen) Buddhism the dead tree is the mind that has died to self, and its blossoming is awakening.

## Source and Origin
The line is attributed to the Chan master Dongshan Liangjie (洞山良价, 807–869), who said it to his disciple Shushan Kuangren (疎山匡仁) in 《五燈會元》 卷13 (and in the 洞山語錄, and later in the 指月錄 卷18):

> 山他日上堂曰：「欲知此事，直須如枯木生花，方與他合。」
> "Another day Dongshan taught in the hall: 'If you want to know this matter, you must be like a withered tree putting forth flowers; only then will you be in accord with it.'"

Checked on zh.Wikisource on 2026-11-09 (五燈會元/卷第十三, 指月錄/卷18). The phrase has an older, darker use in Chinese omen texts, where a dead tree flowering is a sign of ruin (「枯木生花，有喪其國」, quoted in the 庶徵典 of the 古今圖書集成); that is not the sense the idiom now carries.

## Standard Form
**枯木生花** — one standard form; traditional and simplified Chinese are written alike. Variant forms such as 枯木開花 exist, but they are not aliased.

## Cultural Notes
A Chan image first, and then a popular one. Dongshan's dead tree is not a sad picture: the point of the line is that the mind must have died to ordinary grasping before it can flower. Later speech turned it into a plain word for a revival, and it is used both of recovery from illness or ruin and of unexpected good fortune. The Japanese 枯木に花 (かれきにはな, "flowers on a dead tree") and the story of Hanasaka Jijii (花咲爺, "the old man who made the cherries bloom") are related images. Korean 고목생화 and Vietnamese khô mộc sinh hoa are the standard forms.

## Example Sentences
1. 欲知此事，直須如枯木生花。
2. 那家老店經過改革後，竟然枯木生花，生意比從前還好。
3. After years of illness she was well again, a withered tree in flower.

## Sentiment
Positive; hopeful and a little surprised, a revival that nobody expected.
