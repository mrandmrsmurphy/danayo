## Literal Meaning
- [落](../characters/落.md) — fall, drop; fallen
- [花](../characters/花.md) — flower, blossom
- [流](../characters/流.md) — flow, flowing
- [水 (char)](../characters/水%20(char).md) — water

Literally: "Fallen blossoms, flowing water" — the image of petals that have left the branch and are now drifting on a current, carried wherever the water goes.

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary's Chinese entry gives three senses: (of late spring) "in decline; waning", "desolate; dilapidated", and (by extension) "utterly defeated; in a sorry state", with a line from the Chinese Internationale, 「舊世界打個落花流水」 ("smash the old world into pieces"). Its Japanese entry gives "falling flowers and flowing water" and "mutual love". The literal meaning is "falling blossoms and flowing water".

## Source and Origin
The four-character 落花流水 is already found in Tang poetry. Examples checked on wikisource include the opening of 高駢《訪隱者不遇》, 「落花流水認天台，半醉閒吟獨自來」, and 趙嘏《寄遠》, 「無限春愁莫相問，落花流水洞房深」, where it names the scene of spring passing and the lover far away. A Tang poem of mourning has 「落花流水共添悲」 (《聞逝者自驚》, in the 《全唐詩》).

## Standard Form
落花流水 is orthographically identical across all scripts — no aliases needed.

## Cultural Notes
English Wiktionary has a Chinese entry and a Japanese entry (らっかりゅうすい, analysed as 落花 + 流水). It has no Korean or Vietnamese entry.

## Example Sentences
1. 落花流水認天台，半醉閒吟獨自來。（高駢，《訪隱者不遇》）

## Sentiment
Varies by sense: wistful for waning spring, negative for defeat.
