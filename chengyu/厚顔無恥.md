## Literal Meaning
- [厚 (char)](../characters/厚%20(char).md) — thick
- [顔](../characters/顔.md) — face
- [無 (char)](../characters/無%20(char).md) — without
- [恥](../characters/恥.md) — shame

Literally: "Thick-faced and without shame."

## Extended Meaning
Describes someone utterly shameless or brazen — behaving improperly, dishonestly, or offensively without any trace of embarrassment, and often continuing to justify or deny wrongdoing even when it is obvious to everyone else. Strongly negative and frequently directed at public figures (corrupt officials, dishonest businesspeople, hypocrites) who show no remorse or self-awareness.

## Source and Origin
### Stage 1: the image of a "thick face" — 《詩經·小雅·巧言》 (Classic of Poetry, c. 11th–7th century BCE)
> 蛇蛇碩言，出自口矣。巧言如簧，顏之厚矣。
> (Grandiose, boastful words come pouring from his mouth. Clever talk, smooth as a reed-pipe's tune — how thick is his face!)

This ode condemns slanderers at the Zhou court whose glib, dishonest speech is matched by their shameless audacity — "顏之厚矣" ("his face is thick indeed") is the origin of the "thick face" = shamelessness metaphor that underlies 厚顏 itself.

### Stage 2: 厚顏 and 蒙恥 paired — 孔稚珪《北山移文》 (Kong Zhigui, Southern Qi dynasty, c. 5th century CE)
> 豈可使芳杜厚顏，薜荔蒙恥。
> (How could one let the fragrant thoroughwort bear a thickened face, or the creeping fig suffer such shame?)

This celebrated parallel-prose satire mocks a court official, Zhou Yong (周顒), who had postured as a reclusive hermit on North Mountain before returning to worldly office — personifying the mountain's own plants as ashamed on the hypocrite's behalf. Here 厚顏 appears as a fixed compound ("thickened face," i.e. shamelessness) alongside 蒙恥 ("to suffer shame"), the conceptual precursor to 無恥. The specific four-character compound 厚顏無恥 crystallized later, joining 厚顏 (Stage 1's legacy) with 無恥 as its own fixed compound, rather than being a direct quotation from either classical source.

## Standard Form
**厚顔無恥** (Dan'a'yo standard, using shinjitai 顔)
- 厚顏無恥 — fully traditional form (顏)
- 厚颜无耻 — fully simplified form
- 厚顏 alone is also current as a standalone word/adjective meaning "brazen, shameless"

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** hòu yán wú chǐ
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** hau5 ngaan4 mou4 ci2
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** こうがんむち
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 후안무치 (hu-an-mu-chi)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** hậu nhan vô sỉ

All five readings are fully compositional, with no rendaku, voicing shift, or non-default character reading involved — a straightforward case among this batch.

## Cultural Notes
Used across all five languages as a strong moral condemnation, most often aimed at public figures — corrupt officials, dishonest businesspeople, public liars — who show no shame even when confronted with clear evidence of wrongdoing. It carries more force than a milder word like "shameless" in English; describing someone as 厚顔無恥 is a serious, cutting accusation, common in political commentary, journalism, and everyday complaint alike, but rarely used lightly or humorously.

## Example Sentences
1. 他犯了錯卻還狡辯，真是厚顏無恥。
2. The senator's flat denial, despite the evidence, was breathtakingly _hòu yán wú chǐ_.
3. そんな厚顔無恥な言い訳は誰も信じない。
4. Her _hậu nhan vô sỉ_ excuse only made the scandal worse.

## Sentiment
Strongly negative; a serious moral condemnation of brazen shamelessness, dishonesty, or hypocrisy. Not used humorously or lightly.
