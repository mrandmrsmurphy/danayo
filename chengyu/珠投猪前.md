## Literal Meaning
- [珠](../characters/珠.md) — pearl
- [投 (char)](../characters/投%20(char).md) — to cast, throw
- [猪](../characters/猪.md) — swine
- [前 (char)](../characters/前%20(char).md) — before, in front of

Literally: "cast pearls before swine."

## Extended Meaning
To offer something precious to those incapable of appreciating it. By extension, it cautions against exposing sacred or valuable things to contempt or careless disregard.

## Source and Origin
This idiom condenses **Matthew 7:6**, from the Sermon on the Mount:

> "Give not that which is holy unto the dogs, neither cast ye your pearls before swine, lest they trample them under their feet, and turn again and rend you." (KJV)

Traditional Chinese Bible translations render the relevant clause as "也不要把你們的珍珠丟在豬前" ("do not throw your pearls before swine"). The Dan'a'yo form compresses the syntax into a balanced four-character maxim, dropping the parallel "holy things to dogs" clause and retaining only the pearls-and-swine image.

## Cultural Notes
Within its original context the saying addresses discernment: it does not license contempt toward others but warns against the imprudent disclosure of sacred teaching to those unable or unwilling to value it. In Christian interpretation the "pearls" often symbolize the gospel, wisdom, or holy mysteries, and the warning concerns both the protection of what is holy and the avoidance of unnecessary provocation. The structure parallels existing East Asian idioms about misplaced instruction, most notably [[対牛弾琴]] ("playing the lute to a cow"); 珠投猪前 carries additional moral and covenantal weight from its biblical origin and its explicit pairing of holy value with rejection. In Dan'a'yo usage the proverb typically signals prudential restraint rather than mere frustration, affirming that truth is precious and that context and audience matter.

## Example Sentences
1. 對頑梗之人講至理，無異珠投豬前。("To speak profound truth to the obstinate is like casting pearls before swine.")
2. 「不要把你們的珍珠丟在豬前，恐怕它踐踏了珍珠，轉過來咬你們。」(馬太福音 7:6，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Sharing deep theology with an unreceptive audience risks becoming 珠投猪前.
4. 智者知何時當言，何時當默，免珠投猪前之憾。("The wise know when to speak and when to stay silent, to avoid the regret of casting pearls before swine.")

## Sentiment
Cautionary, discerning, restrained. Counsels wisdom in speech and stewardship of what is sacred.
