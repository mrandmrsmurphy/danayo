## Literal Meaning
- [流](../characters/流.md) — flow
- [言 (char)](../characters/言%20(char).md) — speech, words
- [飛 (char)](../characters/飛%20(char).md) — fly
- [語](../characters/語.md) — language, talk

Literally: "Flowing words, flying talk" — speech that spreads like water and moves through a population as swiftly as something airborne.

## Extended Meaning
Baseless rumors, gossip, or unfounded reports circulating quickly through a community — talk without factual grounding, often damaging to reputations or provoking unrest. Neutral-to-negative; almost always used to dismiss or warn against unverified claims rather than to describe true information.

## Source and Origin
The idiom fuses two originally separate two-character terms, each with its own classical source.

**流言** — from **《書經·金縢》** ("The Metal-Bound Coffer" chapter, Book of Documents):
> 武王既喪，管叔及其羣弟乃流言於國，曰：「公將不利於孺子。」
> (After King Wu's death, Guanshu and his brothers spread rumors throughout the state, saying: "The Duke [of Zhou] intends harm to the young prince.")

This records events after the death of King Wu of Zhou: with the young King Cheng newly on the throne and the Duke of Zhou acting as regent, King Wu's brothers Guanshu and Caishu suspected the Duke of scheming to usurp power, and spread rumors to that effect throughout the state — rumors that proved false, though they triggered a real rebellion the Duke had to suppress.

**蜚語** (an interchangeable variant of **飛語**) — traced to **《鶡冠子·武靈王》** (Heguanzi, "King Wuling" chapter), a Warring States-era philosophical text. The exact classical wording of this second source was not independently verified for this entry (the primary text is not readily accessible outside specialist databases), but the attribution is corroborated by multiple independent dictionary sources.

The two terms — 流言 ("rumors that spread/flow") and 蜚語/飛語 ("talk that flies about") — were later joined into the fixed four-character idiom 流言蜚語/流言飛語, with 蜚 and 飛 (both meaning "to fly," and near-homophones) used interchangeably.

## Standard Form
**流言飛語** (using 飛, the Dan'a'yo standard form here)
- 流言蜚語 — using 蜚 in place of 飛, equally standard and arguably closer to the term's classical-text origin; both forms are current in modern usage
- 流言飞语 — simplified form

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** liú yán fēi yǔ
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** lau4 jin4 fei1 jyu5
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** りゅうげんひご
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 유언비어 (yu-eon-bi-eo)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** lưu ngôn phi ngữ

Korean 유언비어 shows the regular word-initial 두음법칙 (initial-sound rule) shift of 流's reading from 류 to 유 — a systematic, predictable shift for word-initial ㄹ+ㅠ in modern (South) Korean orthography, not an irregular or unexpected reading.

## Cultural Notes
Used across all five languages to describe or dismiss unfounded rumors and gossip — common in news reporting, legal contexts, and everyday warnings not to trust unverified claims. Korean usage in particular often appears in news and legal contexts specifically discussing the harm caused by circulating false rumors. The idiom carries no positive sense in any of the five languages; it is always used to characterize speech as unreliable.

## Example Sentences
1. 不要相信那些流言飛語，等官方消息公布後再做判斷。
2. Don't put any stock in the office _liúyánfēiyǔ_ until there's an official statement.
3. 流言飛語に惑わされてはいけない。
4. The scandal turned out to be nothing but _lưu ngôn phi ngữ_.

## Sentiment
Negative; dismissive of unfounded claims and cautionary about the harm rumors can cause.
