## Literal Meaning
- [生](characters/生.md) — life
- [机](characters/机.md) — mechanism, vital force
- [勃](characters/勃.md) — surging, vigorous
- [勃](characters/勃.md) — surging, vigorous

Literally: "Full of throbbing life-force."

## Extended Meaning
生机勃勃 describes something brimming with vitality — flourishing, full of energy, growth, and creative power. It is used very positively of landscapes, economies, youth, or even ideas as being full of vitality and promise. In this vault it is grouped among the Biblical chengyu because of its extended use in modern Christian Chinese writing to describe creation, resurrection, or spiritual renewal, even though the idiom itself is not a translation of any specific Biblical verse.

## Source and Origin
The 成語字典 page ([chinesewords.org: 生機勃勃](https://www.chinesewords.org/idiom/show-22654.html), read directly) gives as the documented use a line from Zhang Tianmin's (張天民) modern novel 《創業》 (the page punctuates it 「油田一片生機勃勃，繁忙興旺的景象。」). Taiwan's Ministry of Education [《成語典》 entry](https://dict.idioms.moe.edu.tw/idiomView.jsp?ID=17009&webMd=1&la=1), also read directly, does not cite 《創業》 or 張天民; it gives only the definition 「形容充滿生氣活力，生命力旺盛」 and the example 「花園裡百花盛開，呈現生機勃勃的景象」. The 《創業》 line:

> 「油田一片生機勃勃，繁忙興旺的景象。」
> ("The oil field was a scene of thriving life, busy and prosperous.")

and the dictionaries treat 生氣勃勃 as a near synonym. I did not retrieve the novel itself, so the 《創業》 line rests on that one dictionary page, and I found no earlier use, so the four-character form appears to be a modern idiom, not a classical one. Its components 生機 ("life force") and 勃勃 ("surging, vigorous") are older words.

**Corrected.** The earlier version of this page said that 生機 appears in the 《莊子》 and 《淮南子》, that Ming and Qing writers "began pairing the two into the fixed compound," and quoted 「春意融融，生機勃勃」 as Ming–Qing literary usage. I could not verify any of those, so they are removed, along with Example 1's Ming–Qing attribution.

The idiom's life-affirming imagery later made it a natural fit for modern Christian Chinese writers describing creation, resurrection and spiritual renewal (a use I did not check). That is the reason for its place among the Biblical chengyu in this vault, and `origin` stays `Bible` so the page keeps that grouping, although the phrase itself is not a translation of any verse.

## Standard Form
**生机勃勃** (simplified 机) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 生機勃勃 — traditional Chinese form (機)

## Variants
- 生氣勃勃 / 生气勃勃 — nearly synonymous, using 氣/气 ("energy, spirit") in place of 機/机 ("life-force, mechanism")
- 生機盎然 — "teeming with vitality"

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** shēng jī bó bó
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** sang1 gei1 but6 but6
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** せいきぼつぼつ (sei-ki-botsu-botsu; rare — more common synonyms are 活気に満ちる or 生命力あふれる)
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 생기발발 (saeng-gi-bal-bal)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** sinh cơ bột bột

## Cultural Notes
Chinese usage is very positive, describing landscapes, economies, youth, or ideas as full of vitality and promise. Korean 생기발발 retains the same meaning — vigorous life, energy, or morale. Vietnamese sinh cơ bột bột describes flourishing development, especially in literature or ecology. Japanese せいきぼつぼつ is not common as a set yojijukugo, but is recognized in academic or poetic registers.

## Example Sentences
*Example 1 is quoted from a dictionary page; examples 2–4 are constructed.*

1. 「油田一片生機勃勃，繁忙興旺的景象。」(張天民《創業》, as quoted on the 成語字典 page linked in Source and Origin; the novel itself was not read)
2. 春天的田野生機勃勃，一片翠綠。("In spring the fields are full of life, a sea of green.")
3. 그 도시는 생기발발하다. ("The city is full of vibrant life.")
4. Even after the fire, the forest returned within a decade, 生机勃勃 once again.

## Sentiment
Vigorous, flourishing, pulsing with the energy of life. Expresses a joyful vision of the world's renewal — a concept equally at home in Daoist cosmology, Confucian natural ethics, or Christian creation and resurrection theology.
