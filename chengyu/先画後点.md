[Dan'a'yo Chengyu](chengyu/Dan'a'yo%20Chengyu.md)

>[!Tip] The last line of the 筆順 verse, with its partner [[先中後両]] — see the verse on [[Stroke]]

## Literal Meaning
- [先](../characters/先.md) — first, before
- [画](../characters/画.md) — stroke
- [後](../characters/後%20(char).md) — after
- [点](../characters/点%20(char).md) — dot

Literally: "Strokes first, dots after."

## Extended Meaning
A dot that stands to the upper right of a character, or inside it, is written last, after the strokes it accompanies (犬, 太, 玉). The rule closes the verse and is the one that says what is not part of the main frame of the character. A dot at the head of a character is the opposite case: it is written first, as the top of the character (主, 方).

## Source and Origin
A 先X後Y phrase with 画 in the sense of the lines of a character (the word [[筆画]]) opposed to 点, the dot stroke. It is the last line of the 筆順 verse on [[Stroke]]. Coined within 単亜語 on 2026-10-03.

All five readings are compositional: each is the concatenation of the constituent characters' own readings, since the phrase is a coinage and not an attested idiom.

## Cultural Notes
Like the other idioms of [[Dan'a'yo Chengyu|単亜語 origin]], this phrase is vault-internal terminology rather than a phrase with independent life across the CJKV sphere. The rule it names, however, is not Dan'a'yo's own: it belongs to the common stroke-order tradition of the whole region. The same rule is stated in Chinese stroke-order instruction as 点在最后 or 右上点最后写, and in Japanese as 「右上の点は最後」.

## Example Sentences
1. 「犬」は先画後点で、点を最後に書く。
2. In 太 the dot comes last: 先画後点.

## Sentiment
Neutral and instructional; a mnemonic in the register of a school rule, intended to be recited and remembered.
