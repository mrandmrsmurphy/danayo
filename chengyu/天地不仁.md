## Literal Meaning
- [天 (char)](../characters/天%20(char).md) — heaven; sky
- [地 (char)](../characters/地%20(char).md) — earth; ground
- [不 (char)](../characters/不%20(char).md) — not
- [仁](../characters/仁.md) — benevolent; humane; compassionate

Literally: "Heaven and Earth are not benevolent."

## Extended Meaning
"Heaven and Earth are not benevolent": in the 《道德經》 passage, Heaven and Earth treat the ten thousand things as straw dogs, and the sage treats the people the same way. English Wiktionary has no entry for 天地不仁.

## Source and Origin

**《道德經·第五章》** (attributed to Laozi, composed or compiled c. 6th–4th century BC; received text established by the Han):

> 天地不仁，以萬物為芻狗；聖人不仁，以百姓為芻狗。
> "Heaven and Earth are not benevolent — they treat the ten thousand things as straw dogs. The sage is not benevolent — he treats the people as straw dogs."

The Wang Bi commentary on this chapter (verified on Wikisource) begins: 「天地任自然，無為無造，萬物自相治理，故不仁也」, "Heaven and Earth leave things to nature, neither acting nor making; the ten thousand things govern themselves, and so they are 'not benevolent'." It adds that benevolence needs establishing, transforming and favouring, which would make things lose their true nature.

The phrase is the opening line of 《道德經》 chapter 5. English Wiktionary has no entry for it.

## Example Sentences

1. 《道德經·第五章》：天地不仁，以萬物為芻狗；聖人不仁，以百姓為芻狗。
   "Heaven and Earth are not benevolent, treating the ten thousand things as straw dogs; the sage is not benevolent, treating the people as straw dogs."

## Sentiment
Philosophical; it states that Heaven and Earth show no partiality.
