## Literal Meaning
- [不 (char)](characters/不%20(char).md) — not
- [遠 (char)](characters/遠%20(char).md) — far
- [千](characters/千.md) — thousand
- [里 (char)](characters/里%20(char).md) — li (a traditional unit of distance)

Literally: "Not considering a thousand li to be far."

## Extended Meaning
不遠千里 describes being willing to travel a great distance out of respect, commitment, or eagerness — valuing a purpose enough that hardship and distance are disregarded. The negation 不遠 does not deny distance as a fact; it denies its moral weight, reframing value around motive rather than convenience. The construction is deliberately understated rather than boastful: it normalizes sacrifice, and distance recedes in importance when the aim is rightly ordered. It functions relationally — less about the traveler's toughness than about honoring the value of the destination or the person sought.

## Source and Origin
From **《孟子·梁惠王上》** (Mencius, "King Hui of Liang I"), the opening exchange, checked against the Wikisource text. It is King Hui who speaks the line, greeting Mencius on his arrival:

> 孟子見梁惠王。王曰：「叟！不遠千里而來，亦將有以利吾國乎？」孟子對曰：「王！何必曰利？亦有仁義而已矣。」
> (Mencius went to see King Hui of Liang. The king said, "Venerable sir, since you have not counted a thousand li too far to come, you must surely have some way to benefit my state?" Mencius replied, "Why must Your Majesty speak of benefit? There is only humaneness and righteousness.")

So the idiom begins as a courteous greeting that carries a hint of expectation, and Mencius's reply turns the conversation from 利 (benefit) to 仁義. In later use the phrase keeps only the respectful sense of making a long journey without counting the distance.

**Corrected.** The earlier version of this page attributed the phrase to 《孟子·滕文公下》 and gave a line in which Mencius says it to 戴不勝. I checked that chapter: it contains the 戴不勝 passage (about hiring a Qi or a Chu tutor) but not the string 不遠千里, and in the real source the speaker is King Hui, not Mencius.

## Cultural Notes
Across the CJKV region, 不遠千里 is used to commend effort motivated by respect, learning, duty, or relationship. It appears in formal speech, writing, and polite acknowledgment, especially when welcoming a guest who has traveled far or praising someone's initiative in seeking out a teacher, mentor, or cause. The tone is respectful and mildly elevated, and the idiom is more commonly encountered in literary or formal register than in casual speech across all four daughter languages.

## Example Sentences
1. 「叟！不遠千里而來，亦將有以利吾國乎？」(《孟子·梁惠王上》)
2. 他不遠千里，從國外趕來參加恩師的葬禮。("He traveled a thousand li without hesitation, coming from abroad to attend his beloved teacher's funeral.")
3. 많은 학생들이 불원천리하고 이 스승을 찾아왔다. ("Many students traveled great distances without regard to the hardship, to seek out this teacher.")
4. She'd flown halfway across the world for the reunion — 不遠千里, as the old phrase goes, because some things are worth the distance.

## Sentiment
Respectful, mildly elevated, admiring. Commends the disregard of distance and hardship in pursuit of something valued — duty, learning, loyalty, or relationship.
