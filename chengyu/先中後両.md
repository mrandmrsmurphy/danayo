[Dan'a'yo Chengyu](chengyu/Dan'a'yo%20Chengyu.md)

>[!Tip] The first line of the fourth couplet of the 筆順 verse, with its partner [[先画後点]] — see the verse on [[Stroke]]

## Literal Meaning
- [先](../characters/先.md) — first, before
- [中](../characters/中%20(char).md) — middle
- [後](../characters/後%20(char).md) — after
- [両](../characters/両%20(char).md) — both

Literally: "The middle first, both after."

## Extended Meaning
When a character is symmetric about a central stroke, the central stroke is written first, then the stroke on one side, then the stroke on the other (小, 水, 山). The rule is the exception to the plain left-to-right default of [[左由右至]]: the middle comes before either side, and the two sides then follow in left-to-right order.

## Source and Origin
A 先X後Y phrase, with 両 ("both") standing for the two flanking sides. Spelling out "left, then right" would need five characters, and the order of the sides is already supplied by [[左由右至]]. It is the first line of the fourth couplet of the 筆順 verse on [[Stroke]]. Coined within 単亜語 on 2026-10-03.

All five readings are compositional: each is the concatenation of the constituent characters' own readings, since the phrase is a coinage and not an attested idiom.

## Cultural Notes
Like the other idioms of [[Dan'a'yo Chengyu|単亜語 origin]], this phrase is vault-internal terminology rather than a phrase with independent life across the CJKV sphere. The rule it names, however, is not Dan'a'yo's own: it belongs to the common stroke-order tradition of the whole region. The same rule is stated in Chinese stroke-order instruction as 先中间后两边, in Japanese as 「中が先」, and in Korean as 가운데 먼저, 양쪽 나중.

## Example Sentences
1. 「小」は先中後両で、まず中央の縦画を書く。
2. In 水 the middle stroke is written first, then its two sides: 先中後両.

## Sentiment
Neutral and instructional; a mnemonic in the register of a school rule, intended to be recited and remembered.
