## Literal Meaning
- [三 (char)](../characters/三%20(char).md) — three
- [綱](../characters/綱.md) — guiding cord; bond
- [五 (char)](../characters/五%20(char).md) — five
- [常](../characters/常.md) — constant

Literally: "the three bonds and the five constants."

## Extended Meaning
三綱五常 names the complete framework of imperial Confucian ethics: [[三綱]], the Three Bonds that structure hierarchical relationships (ruler–subject, father–son, husband–wife, each pairing assigning the first party a dominant role the second must obey), and [[五常]], the Five Constant Virtues that should govern personal conduct (仁 benevolence, 義 righteousness, 禮 propriety, 智 wisdom, 信 trustworthiness). Used together, the phrase stands for the entire traditional moral-political order — invoked either with reverence, as the bedrock of civilized society, or critically, as shorthand for rigid patriarchal hierarchy, depending on the speaker's stance toward Confucian tradition.

## Source and Origin
**Caveat on checking.** I could not load the primary texts below (the Wikisource pages for the 白虎通義, 春秋繁露 and the 論語 commentary returned errors), so the quotations are the standard ones, not re-read here, and the attribution of the first four-character use rests on secondary sources: Chinese Wikipedia's article 三綱五常 and a search summary, which agree.

- **三綱** as a term goes back to Western Han scholar Dong Zhongshu (董仲舒, c. 179–104 BC) in the *Chunqiu fanlu* (《春秋繁露·基義》), conventionally quoted as 「王道之三綱，可求於天。」 ("The three bonds of the kingly way may be sought in Heaven.") Chinese Wikipedia cites the same chapter for 「君臣父子夫婦之義皆取諸陰陽之道，君為陽，臣為陰；父為陽，子為陰；夫為陽，婦為陰。」, grounding the three pairings in yin-yang cosmology.
- The authoritative definition is in the Eastern Han **《白虎通義·三綱六紀》**, the record of the White Tiger Hall conference (白虎觀會議) convened by Emperor Zhang in 79 AD. It begins 「三綱者何謂也？謂君臣、父子、夫婦也」 and states that the ruler is the bond (綱) for the subject, the father for the son, the husband for the wife. (Chinese Wikipedia quotes the three clauses in the order 君、夫、父; I did not settle the order in the original.)
- 五常 (仁義禮智信) is older individually, traceable to core Confucian and Mencian texts.
- The combined term **三綱五常** is credited to the Eastern Han scholar **馬融** (Ma Rong), whose comment on the Analects passage 「殷因於夏禮，所損益可知也」 (《論語·為政》) is reported as 「所因，謂三綱五常也」 ("What they adapted refers to the Three Bonds and Five Constants"), preserved in the He Yan collected commentary. This is the earliest use of the four-character string I could find, and it is secondary-sourced.

**Corrected.** The earlier version of this page said 三綱 and 五常 were "formally paired" by the White Tiger Hall synthesis. The sources I found credit Ma Rong's commentary with first pairing them, and the 白虎通義 passage defines 三綱 and 六紀, not the combined phrase, so `origin` is now `"馬融: 論語注"` instead of the 白虎通義.

## Cultural Notes
In Mandarin and Cantonese contexts the phrase is now almost always historical or critical in register — invoked in discussions of Confucian tradition, gender roles, or as a byword for outdated patriarchal hierarchy in modern critique, rather than as a live prescriptive standard. Japanese さんこうごじょう and Korean 삼강오상 carry the same historical-Confucian register, encountered mainly in scholarly, historical, or classical-education contexts. Vietnamese tam cương ngũ thường likewise survives as the standard term in discussions of traditional Confucian ethics in Vietnamese scholarship, paired exactly as in the source Chinese formula.

## Example Sentences
1. 舊社會強調三綱五常，女性地位低下。
2. Traditional society emphasized *sāngāngwǔcháng*, leaving women with a subordinate status.
3. 儒家倫理的核心可以用三綱五常四個字概括。
4. The core of Confucian ethics can be summed up in the four characters *sāngāngwǔcháng*.

## Sentiment
Mixed to negative in contemporary usage; historically neutral-to-reverent as a description of orthodox Confucian order, but now most often invoked critically as shorthand for rigid, patriarchal hierarchy.
