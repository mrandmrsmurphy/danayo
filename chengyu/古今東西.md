## Literal Meaning
- [古](characters/古.md) — ancient, old
- [今 (char)](characters/今%20(char).md) — present, now
- [東](characters/東.md) — east
- [西](characters/西.md) — west

Literally: "Ancient and modern, east and west."

## Extended Meaning
古今東西 means "across all times and places; universally." It epitomizes a philosophical instinct toward totality — a claim that transcends boundaries of time (古今) and space (東西) at once. The idiom is used to introduce sweeping comparisons or universal truths, framing whatever follows as valid regardless of era or location.

## Source and Origin
古今東西 is a compound of two ancient binary pairs, 古今 ("ancient and modern") and 東西 ("east and west"), the first standing for totality of time and the second for totality of space; listing opposed terms to mean "everything between" is a common Chinese rhetorical pattern. I could not establish a classical text that contains the fixed four-character string 古今東西. Japanese reference sources I found (a search summary of several of them) say the pattern derives from Chinese classical usage and cite as an early example Nakamura Masanao's 「古今東西一致道徳の説」 in the *Tōkyō Gakushiin zasshi* of 1889, a Meiji-period use that I did not check against the journal. English Wiktionary lists the term for Chinese, Japanese and Korean as a juxtapositional idiom without giving an etymology.

**Corrected.** The earlier version of this page said the compound "became common in Tang and Song prose" and gave the origin as "Tang–Song period." I found no support for that, so the origin is recorded as not established. The related form 東西古今 and the Chinese 古今中外 (below) are the usual alternatives, and which of these is older in Chinese I did not determine.

## Standard Form
**古今東西** (traditional) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 古今东西 — simplified Chinese form

## Variants
- 古今中外 — "across all times and lands," substituting 中外 ("China and abroad") for 東西
- Some texts substitute 中外 for 東西 more broadly, extending the scope to the entire world rather than the specifically east/west axis

## Cultural Notes
Readers can hear in 古今東西 the universality of 理 (*li*) in Confucian thought, the unity of the Dao in Daoist language, or the interpenetration of all dharmas across time and space in Mahayana Buddhism; these are interpretive resonances, not documented uses, and I did not check them. Japanese ここんとうざい (kokontōzai) is used as a rhetorical preface meaning "throughout the world and all ages," Korean 고금동서 appears in learned prose, and Vietnamese cổ kim đông tây serves the same function; I did not check how common it is in each language. The earlier comparison with a quotation attributed to Augustine (*veritas una et eadem est ubique et semper*) could not be verified and has been removed.

## Example Sentences
1. 古今東西，善惡之理不變。("In all times and places, the principles of good and evil do not change.")
2. 古今東西の詩人が愛を詠んだ。("Poets of all ages and lands have sung of love.")
3. 고금동서를 막론하고, 진리는 하나이다. ("In all times and places, truth is one.")
4. Cổ kim đông tây, lòng người chẳng đổi. ("Across time and continents, the human heart remains the same.")

## Sentiment
Sweeping, affirming, universalizing. Frames a claim as timeless and placeless — true across all eras and horizons.
