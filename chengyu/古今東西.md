## Literal Meaning
- [古](characters/古.md) — ancient, old
- [今 (char)](characters/今%20(char).md) — present, now
- [東](characters/東.md) — east
- [西](characters/西.md) — west

Literally: "Ancient and modern, east and west."

## Extended Meaning
古今東西 means "across all times and places; universally." It epitomizes a philosophical instinct toward totality — a claim that transcends boundaries of time (古今) and space (東西) at once. The idiom is used to introduce sweeping comparisons or universal truths, framing whatever follows as valid regardless of era or location.

## Source and Origin
古今東西 is a classical compound of two independently ancient binary pairs — 古今 ("ancient and modern") and 東西 ("east and west") — each deeply rooted in early Chinese cosmology and historiography as ways of expressing totality of time and totality of space respectively. The specific four-character compound became common in Tang and Song prose as a way to denote universality, used by literary and philosophical writers to frame truths or values as valid across time and space.

## Standard Form
**古今東西** (traditional) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 古今东西 — simplified Chinese form

## Variants
- 古今中外 — "across all times and lands," substituting 中外 ("China and abroad") for 東西
- Some texts substitute 中外 for 東西 more broadly, extending the scope to the entire world rather than the specifically east/west axis

## Cultural Notes
In Confucian thought, 古今東西 echoes the universality of 理 (*li*), the cosmic principle present throughout all ages and realms — what was right for the ancients remains right today, though its expression may vary. In Daoist language it resonates with the unity of the Dao, flowing through all directions and times. In Buddhist usage it approximates the Mahayana view of non-obstruction between past and present, all dharmas interpenetrating across temporal and spatial planes.

Japanese ここんとうざい (kokontōzai) carries nearly identical usage as a rhetorical preface meaning "throughout the world and all ages," common in academic or literary writing. Korean 고금동서 appears in learned prose, sermons, and essays to imply universality. Vietnamese cổ kim đông tây serves the same function in moral discourse or essays. The idea also has a Western philosophical parallel in the concept of *universalia* — truths valid in all places and times — echoed in Augustine's *veritas una et eadem est ubique et semper* ("truth is one and the same everywhere and always").

## Example Sentences
1. 古今東西，善惡之理不變。("In all times and places, the principles of good and evil do not change.")
2. 古今東西の詩人が愛を詠んだ。("Poets of all ages and lands have sung of love.")
3. 고금동서를 막론하고, 진리는 하나이다. ("In all times and places, truth is one.")
4. Cổ kim đông tây, lòng người chẳng đổi. ("Across time and continents, the human heart remains the same.")

## Sentiment
Sweeping, affirming, universalizing. Frames a claim as timeless and placeless — true across all eras and horizons.
