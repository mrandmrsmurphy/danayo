[Dan'a'yo Chengyu](chengyu/Dan'a'yo%20Chengyu.md)

>[!info] For a demonstration of this principle in grammatical context, see [[文法 - 04句法]]

## Literal Meaning
- [先](../characters/先.md) — first, prior
- [題](../characters/題.md) — topic, that which is under discussion
- [後 (char)](../characters/後%20(char).md) — afterward
- [述](../characters/述.md) — to state, to comment, predicate

Literally: "Topic first, comment after." The form is itself programmatic: establish the topic, then provide the statement about it.

## Extended Meaning
先題後述 names Dan'a'yo's foundational rule of clause structure: every clause, main or subordinate, divides into a **題** (topic — the frame, what is being talked about) followed by a **述** (comment — what is asserted about that topic). This governs word order at a level more fundamental than subject-verb-object: many Dan'a'yo clauses resemble SVO on the surface, but this is incidental — when the topic aligns with the agent, SVO emerges; when it does not, the clause diverges from SVO without violating the rule. All embedded structures (relative clauses, complement clauses, adverbial clauses) follow the same pattern, each internally organized as topic-comment before being embedded into a larger clause. Because [[修飾先行]] additionally requires modifiers to precede their heads, an entire topic-comment subclause can appear pre-nominal, linked by 之, while remaining internally topic-comment. The topic is identified by position (initial placement) and context rather than by a fixed grammatical role like "subject" — subject and object are not primary organizing categories in Dan'a'yo clause structure at all.

## Source and Origin
先題後述 is a Dan'a'yo original design principle, not a phrase borrowed from Classical Chinese or any other existing tradition. It elevates topic-prominence — a well-documented typological feature shared by Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese, and Korean alike (in the sense described by linguists as "topic-prominent" languages, contrasting with strictly subject-prominent languages) — into a single universal, formally stated rule governing every clause in the language, rather than leaving it as a loose discourse tendency layered on top of a subject-predicate grammar. By stating topic-comment as the one and only clause-structuring rule, Dan'a'yo deliberately eliminates competing structural principles: even [[修飾先行]] (the modifier-first rule) operates within this larger constraint, since ordering is ultimately resolved at the level of topic versus comment. The name itself enacts the rule it describes — 先題 ("topic first") 後述 ("comment after") is itself topic-then-comment in miniature.

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** xiān tí hòu shù
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** sin1 tai4 hau6 seot6
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** せんだいこうじゅつ
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 선제후술 (seon-je-hu-sul)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** tiên đề hậu thuật

All five readings are fully compositional, matching each constituent character's own standard reading; 後 takes こう (KOU) rather than ご (GO) here, matching the real Japanese word 後述 (kōjutsu, "stated below"), which independently combines the same two characters.

## Cultural Notes
Within Dan'a'yo, 先題後述 functions as a technical grammatical term — the name used to teach and invoke the topic-comment rule in grammatical discussion, and the single most structurally consequential rule for constructing a Dan'a'yo clause correctly. It has no meaning or currency outside the Dan'a'yo grammar tradition; unlike classical chengyu, it carries no independent Chinese, Japanese, Korean, or Vietnamese literary usage. Linguistically, it aligns Dan'a'yo with the broader Sinospheric tendency toward topic-prominent clause structure — a feature Li and Thompson's typology identifies across Mandarin and, to varying degrees, Japanese and Korean — but formalizes what is elsewhere a flexible discourse tendency into an absolute rule with no exceptions.

## Example Sentences
1. 魚 此人捕已。(Topic: 魚 "the fish"; Comment: 此人捕已 "this man has caught [it].") Even when reordered for naturalness, the underlying organization remains [Topic] → [what is said about it].
2. Dan'a'yo syntax rests on one rule above all others: _xiān tí hòu shù_, topic before comment.
3. 先題後述の原則により、あらゆる節は主題と説明の二部構造を持つ。
4. 선제후술의 원칙은 단아어 문장 구조의 근본이다.

## Sentiment
Technical and definitional, not commendatory or critical. Within Dan'a'yo grammatical discourse the phrase is authoritative — invoking it settles a word-order question — and among learners it carries the weight of a foundational rule: not emotional, but load-bearing.
