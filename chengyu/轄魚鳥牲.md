[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [轄 (char)](../characters/轄%20(char).md) — to govern, control, exercise authority over
- [魚 (char)](../characters/魚%20(char).md) — fish
- [鳥 (char)](../characters/鳥%20(char).md) — birds
- [牲](../characters/牲.md) — beasts, domesticated animals

Literally: "to rule fish, birds, and beasts."

## Extended Meaning
A compact expression of humanity's dominion mandate: the charge to exercise authority over the animal creation. By extension, it can signify responsible stewardship of the natural world rather than unrestrained exploitation.

## Source and Origin
The idiom condenses **Genesis 1:26–28**:

> "...and let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, and over the fowl of the air, and over the cattle, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creepeth upon the earth." (KJV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本), checked at [維基文庫〈聖經 (和合本)/創世記〉](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/聖經_(和合本)/創世記), renders Genesis 1:26 as 「神說、我們要照着我們的形像、按着我們的樣式造人、使他們管理海裏的魚、空中的鳥、地上的牲畜、和全地、並地上所爬的一切昆蟲。」 (that edition writes 照着, 按着 and 海裏 and punctuates with 、). I could not reach cnbible.com and did not check the 聖經新譯本 wording or the KJV text above. So the Chinese Bible's own verb is 管理 and its phrase 海裏的魚、空中的鳥、地上的牲畜.

I found **no attestation of 轄魚鳥牲 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage. The Dan'a'yo form selects 轄 specifically to emphasize jurisdictional authority exercised within an assigned scope, rather than mere day-to-day management or outright ownership.

## Cultural Notes
Genesis presents human dominion as derivative authority: humanity rules as image-bearer under divine sovereignty, so the command is royal yet bounded rather than autonomous. The choice of 轄 is significant here — it connotes governance within an assigned jurisdiction, not unconditional possession, helping guard against readings that equate dominion with license to exploit. The triad 魚鳥牲 mirrors the Hebrew merism spanning sea, sky, and land, signaling comprehensive scope rather than an exhaustive zoological taxonomy. This mandate has shaped later theological debate about ecology, agriculture, technology, and animal ethics, with some traditions stressing mastery and others stewardship — the Genesis text itself combines authority with accountability. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 轄魚鳥牲 functions both as doctrinal shorthand for the creation mandate and as a reminder that this rule is delegated, not self-originating.

## Example Sentences
*Example 2 is the real 和合本 text; examples 1, 3 and 4 are constructed.*

1. 人受造之初，即蒙命轄魚鳥牲，而非任意毀壞。("From the beginning humanity was commanded to rule fish, birds, and beasts, not to destroy them at will.")
2. 「使他們管理海裏的魚、空中的鳥、地上的牲畜、和全地、並地上所爬的一切昆蟲。」(創世記 1:26，和合本 Chinese Union Version; real text, checked at the Wikisource page linked above)
3. Environmental ethics discussions increasingly return to 轄魚鳥牲 to argue that dominion implies stewardship, not exploitation.
4. 轄魚鳥牲之命，非許人恣意宰制，乃命人善加看守。("The mandate to rule fish, birds, and beasts does not license willful domination but commands careful stewardship.")

## Sentiment
Dignified, vocational, covenantal. Affirms human authority while implying responsibility and restraint.
