## Literal Meaning
- [財](characters/財.md) — wealth, money
- [愛 (char)](characters/愛%20(char).md) — love
- [悪 (char)](characters/悪%20(char).md) — evil
- [根 (char)](characters/根%20(char).md) — root

Literally: "Money-love, evil's root."

## Extended Meaning
財愛悪根 states that it is not wealth itself but the love of wealth that is the source from which many kinds of moral corruption grow. It is used to warn against greed and materialism, and to explain moral failure — corruption, betrayal, exploitation — as ultimately traceable to an attachment to money rather than to money's mere existence. Cautionary and moralistic; invoked in ethical, religious, and social commentary on avarice.

## Source and Origin
**1 Timothy 6:10**:

> "For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils. It is through this craving that some have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many pangs." (1 Tim 6:10, ESV)

財愛 ("money-love") precisely preserves the verse's crucial distinction — it is the *love* of money that is condemned, not money itself. 悪根 ("evil-root") renders the verse's botanical metaphor directly: 根 (root) names the source from which various evils spring, exactly as in the English "a root of all kinds of evils." This distinction between money and the love of money is central to the Biblical text and is fully preserved in the chengyu's structure.

## Variants
- 貪財悪根 — emphasizing the aspect of greed ("greed-for-money, evil-root")
- 愛財万悪根 — a fuller rendering ("love of money, root of ten thousand evils")
- 財愛諸悪根 — an alternate emphasizing totality ("love of money, root of all evils")

## Cultural Notes
財愛悪根 is used across the CJKV Christian sphere as a direct citation warning against greed, and it resonates readily with pre-existing East Asian Buddhist and Confucian warnings against attachment and avarice — Buddhism's teaching on 貪 (greed) as a root affliction and Confucian moral suspicion of profit-seeking (見利忘義, "forgetting righteousness at the sight of profit") both prepare the ground for the idiom's easy adoption outside strictly religious contexts. As with other Biblical chengyu built on a pre-existing native moral vocabulary (cf. [[骨肉相連]], [[血誓盟約]]), 財愛悪根 functions comfortably as both scriptural citation and generally intelligible moral maxim.

## Example Sentences
1. 「貪財是萬惡之根。有人貪戀錢財，就被引誘離了真道，用許多愁苦把自己刺透了。」(1 Tim 6:10, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 財愛悪根，多人因此離道入迷。("The love of money is the root of evil; many people because of this leave the way and enter delusion.")
3. 貪腐之官皆証財愛悪根之理。("Corrupt officials all prove the principle that the love of money is the root of evil.")
4. Every scandal in the company traced back to the same old truth: 財愛悪根.

## Sentiment
Cautionary, moralistic. Used to warn against greed, materialism, or to explain moral corruption stemming from avarice.
