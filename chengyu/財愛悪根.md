## Literal Meaning
- [財](characters/財.md) — wealth, money
- [愛 (char)](characters/愛%20(char).md) — love
- [悪 (char)](characters/悪%20(char).md) — evil
- [根 (char)](characters/根%20(char).md) — root

Literally: "Money-love, evil's root."

## Extended Meaning
財愛悪根 states that it is not wealth itself but the love of wealth that is the source from which many kinds of moral corruption grow. It is used to warn against greed and materialism, and to explain moral failure — corruption, betrayal, exploitation — as ultimately traceable to an attachment to money rather than to money's mere existence. Cautionary and moralistic; invoked in ethical, religious, and social commentary on avarice.

## Source and Origin
**1 Timothy 6:10**:

> "For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evils. It is through this craving that some have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many pangs." (1 Tim 6:10, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders the verse 「貪財是萬惡之根。有人貪戀錢財，就被引誘離了真道，用許多愁苦把自己刺透了。」 (checked on cnbible.com, which shows the same wording in the 現代標點和合本; the 聖經新譯本 reads 「因為貪財是萬惡之根。…」 and the 中文標準譯本 reads 「貪愛金錢是萬惡之根」). The Chinese Bible's own phrase is therefore 貪財是萬惡之根, and its wording is "the root of all evils" (萬惡之根).

I found **no attestation of 財愛悪根 (財愛惡根, 财爱恶根) as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage: 財愛 ("money-love") is meant to keep the verse's distinction that it is the *love* of money that is condemned, not money itself, and 悪根 ("evil-root") renders the botanical metaphor of a root from which evils spring. The earlier description of it as an established Bible-translation form was unsupported and has been removed.

## Variants
The attested Chinese phrase is 貪財是萬惡之根 (Chinese Union Version of 1 Timothy 6:10). Three variants listed here earlier (貪財悪根, 愛財万悪根, 財愛諸悪根) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
The warning against greed resonates with pre-existing East Asian moral vocabulary — Buddhism's teaching on 貪 (greed) as a root affliction and Confucian suspicion of profit-seeking (見利忘義, "forgetting righteousness at the sight of profit") — so 財愛悪根 reads as both a scriptural citation and a generally intelligible moral maxim (cf. [[骨肉相連]], [[血誓盟約]]). Because it is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「貪財是萬惡之根。有人貪戀錢財，就被引誘離了真道，用許多愁苦把自己刺透了。」(1 Tim 6:10, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 財愛悪根，多人因此離道入迷。("The love of money is the root of evil; many people because of this leave the way and enter delusion.")
3. 貪腐之官皆証財愛悪根之理。("Corrupt officials all prove the principle that the love of money is the root of evil.")
4. Every scandal in the company traced back to the same old truth: 財愛悪根.

## Sentiment
Cautionary, moralistic. Used to warn against greed, materialism, or to explain moral corruption stemming from avarice.
