## Literal Meaning
- [温](../characters/温.md) — warm, reheat; to revisit
- [故](../characters/故.md) — old, former, the past
- [知 (char)](../characters/知%20(char).md) — know, understand
- [新 (char)](../characters/新%20(char).md) — new

Literally: "Warm the old; know the new" — to revisit what one has already learned and discover fresh understanding within it.

The image in 温 ("to warm, to reheat") is of old knowledge not studied again from scratch but *warmed*, like reheating food. The early commentary glosses 温 more plainly as 尋 (to go over again, to work through): in the 《論語註疏》 the gloss is 「溫，尋也。尋繹故者，又知新者，可以為人師矣」. The reheating image is the popular reading of the character, not the commentary's gloss.

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary glosses the Chinese idiom "reviewing learnt knowledge helps one understand it better; reviewing the past helps one understand the present", and the Japanese おんこちしん "one should be able to derive new understanding while revising what he has learned; learning from the past".

## Source and Origin
**《論語·爲政篇·第十一章》** (Analects, Book II: For Government, Chapter 11):

> 「溫故而知新，可以爲師矣。」
> "He who warms the old and thereby comes to know the new — such a one may be deemed a teacher."

The saying is attributed to Confucius (孔子). The 《論語註疏》 introduces the passage as 「此章言為師之法」, "this chapter speaks of the method of being a teacher". Zhu Xi's school reads it the same way: 「故者，舊所得也；新者，新所進也」, "the old is what one has already gained; the new is what one newly advances in", and the point is that the teacher's worth lies in this daily renewal.

The four-character form 温故知新 drops the connecting particle 而 of the original sentence.

## Standard Form
**温故知新** (Dan'a'yo standard; uses 温, the form shared with Japanese and Mainland China)
- 溫故知新 — traditional Chinese form (溫 for 温); dominant in Taiwan and classical scholarship; aliased in this vault

No further orthographic variants. The four characters are stable across all scripts.

## Cultural Notes
English Wiktionary has entries for 溫故知新 and 温故知新 (Chinese, and Japanese おんこちしん). Both quote the same 《論語·爲政》 line as the etymology, and the Japanese entry is derived from Classical Chinese. Korean 온고지신 and Vietnamese ôn cố tri tân are listed as descendants.

Positive; it commends learning by reviewing the old.
