[Dan'a'yo Chengyu](chengyu/Dan'a'yo%20Chengyu.md)

>[!Tip] The second line of the third couplet of the 筆順 verse, with its partner [[先外後内]] — see the verse on [[Stroke]]

## Literal Meaning
- [先](../characters/先.md) — first, before
- [填](../characters/填%20(char).md) — fill in
- [後](../characters/後%20(char).md) — after
- [閉](../characters/閉.md) — close, shut

Literally: "Fill in first, close after."

## Extended Meaning
For an enclosed character, the inside is written before the enclosure is sealed with its last stroke. In 国 the frame is drawn without its bottom bar, the inner strokes are written, and the bottom bar closes the box; in 日 and 田 likewise. It is the second rule of the enclosure couplet, and refines [[先外後内]]: first the outer frame, then the filling, then the closing stroke.

## Source and Origin
A 先X後Y phrase using the verbs 填 and 閉: 填 names the work of the inside and 閉 the sealing stroke, which is why this phrase pairs with [[先外後内]] as the third couplet of the 筆順 verse on [[Stroke]]. Coined within 単亜語 on 2026-10-03.

## Standard Form
**先填後閉**
- 先填后闭 — variant orthography (traditional, simplified or Japanese form of a constituent character)

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** xiān tián hòu bì
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** sin1 tin4 hau6 bai3
- **Japanese (on-yomi):** せんてんごへい
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 선전후폐
- **Vietnamese (Hán–Việt):** tiên điền hậu bế

All five readings are compositional: each is the concatenation of the constituent characters' own readings, since the phrase is a coinage and not an attested idiom.

## Cultural Notes
Like the other idioms of [[Dan'a'yo Chengyu|単亜語 origin]], this phrase is vault-internal terminology rather than a phrase with independent life across the CJKV sphere. The rule it names, however, is not Dan'a'yo's own: it belongs to the common stroke-order tradition of the whole region. The same rule is stated in Chinese stroke-order instruction as 先里头后封口, and in Japanese as 「外側が先、閉じるのは最後」.

## Example Sentences
1. 「国」は先填後閉で、最後に下の横画を書いて閉じる。
2. Write 日 as 先填後閉: frame, then the middle bar, then the closing bottom stroke.

## Sentiment
Neutral and instructional; a mnemonic in the register of a school rule, intended to be recited and remembered.
