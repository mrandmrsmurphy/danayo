[Dan'a'yo Chengyu](chengyu/Dan'a'yo%20Chengyu.md)
>[!Tip] The guiding maxim of [[文法 - 06語用]].

## Literal Meaning
- [百 (char)](characters/百%20(char).md) — hundred
- [家](characters/家.md) — school (of thought)
- [共 (char)](characters/共%20(char).md) — together, in common
- [承](characters/承.md) — to receive, inherit, carry forward

Literally: "The hundred schools jointly inherit," or more fluidly, "all schools share in the inheritance."

## Extended Meaning
百家共承 asserts that intellectual traditions do not arise from nothing: rival schools of thought still stand within a shared civilizational inheritance, and disagreement between them does not negate that continuity. It pushes against two extremes — sectarian absolutism on one side, cultural amnesia on the other — holding instead that plurality exists within continuity. Applied broadly, it implies that traditions argue within a shared grammar, that even revolutions inherit categories from what they reject, and that intellectual life is less a battlefield than a generational relay. It does not deny real disagreement; it denies rootlessness.

## Source and Origin
百家共承 is a Dan'a'yo-coined idiom (単亜語), the guiding maxim for pragmatics and stylistic breadth in the language (see [[文法 - 06語用]]). 百家 ("the hundred schools") evokes the intellectual plurality of the Warring States period and the later retrospective phrase 百家爭鳴 ("the hundred schools contend"). The deliberate substitution of 承 for 爭鳴 is the idiom's central move: where 爭鳴 emphasizes contention, 共承 emphasizes transmission — reframing the inherited metaphor from noise to lineage. This maxim makes explicit Dan'a'yo's pragmatic philosophy: not to limit its lexicon and registers to one lineage, but to acknowledge and inherit them all in common.

Two named sub-principles flow directly from this maxim, both stated in the same section of the grammar:
1. [[詞彙兼容]] ("lexical inclusivity") — welcoming terms from every school of thought and cultural source.
2. [[文体並存]] ("stylistic coexistence") — allowing classical elegance, formal clarity, and modern brevity to share one framework.

## Cultural Notes
百家共承 is grammar-policy language rather than an idiom in general circulation, functioning as the overarching statement from which [[文体並存]] and [[詞彙兼容]] both descend, and standing alongside Dan'a'yo's other founding mottos ([[合漢再決]], [[東亜自通]], [[覧昭和決]], [[毎字明意]], [[朝鮮正音]], [[文言現代]]). Applied to education or cultural theory more broadly, the principle would justify teaching rival schools as part of one continuous story, recovering suppressed traditions without rejecting modern ones, and building new syntheses while still acknowledging prior debts — conservative in its respect for inheritance, without being homogenizing.

## Example Sentences
1. 単亜語の語彙政策は、百家共承の理念を体現している。("Dan'a'yo's lexical policy embodies the principle of 百家共承.")
2. Rather than picking one philosophical lineage as authoritative, the language follows 百家共承 — every school gets a hearing, and none gets erased.
3. 이 언어는 백가공승의 정신에 따라 다양한 사상적 전통을 받아들인다. ("This language embraces diverse intellectual traditions in the spirit of 백가공승.")

## Sentiment
Inclusive, conservative, continuity-affirming. Rejects both sectarian exclusivity and rootless eclecticism in favor of shared, acknowledged inheritance.
