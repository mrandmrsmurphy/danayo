## Literal Meaning
- [春 (char)](../characters/春%20(char).md) — spring
- [秋 (char)](../characters/秋%20(char).md) — autumn
- [鼎 (char)](../characters/鼎%20(char).md) — the bronze tripod cauldron; here, "just, right at" (see Source and Origin)
- [盛](../characters/盛.md) — flourishing; full; at its peak

Literally: "Spring and autumn just at their fullness" — one's years are at their height and one's powers at their peak.

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary glosses the Chinese idiom "to be in the prime of life". In its source it describes the emperor as young and vigorous.

## Source and Origin

The phrase comes from **賈誼** (*Jiǎ Yì*, 200–168 BC), the Western Han scholar and court academician under **漢文帝** (Emperor Wen of Han), in his memorial known as the 《治安策》 (*Policy for Peace and Order*), preserved in **《漢書·賈誼傳》** (Book of Han, ch. 48):

> 「天子春秋鼎盛，行義未過，德澤有加焉，猶尚如是，況莫大諸侯，權力且十此者乎！」
> ("The Son of Heaven is in the full vigor of his years; his conduct has been without fault and his kindness has only grown, and still it is thus — how much more so with the great feudal lords, whose power is ten times as great!")

Jia Yi's argument is a warning, not a flattery: even with the emperor in his prime and governing well, the feudal kings are already plotting and rebelling, so the danger will only grow when those kings come of age. The words 春秋鼎盛 here simply describe the emperor as young and vigorous.

**春秋** ("spring and autumn") is a classical metonym for *years* or *age*, and also names the chronicle 《春秋》. For **鼎盛**, the Han commentator 應劭 glosses 鼎 as 方 ("just, at the moment of"), so 鼎盛 means "right at the height of flourishing". The popular reading of 鼎 as the sovereign's bronze cauldron (the Nine Tripods of Yu) is a later picture, not the original sense of the word here. The four-character form 春秋鼎盛 appears already in the original sentence and later became a set phrase.

## Cultural Notes
English Wiktionary has a Chinese entry only, and lists a Japanese calque 春秋鼎に盛んなり (しゅんじゅうまさにさかんなり). It has no Korean or Vietnamese entry.
