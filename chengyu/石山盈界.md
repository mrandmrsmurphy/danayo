## Literal Meaning
- [石 (char)](characters/石%20(char).md) — stone
- [山 (char)](characters/山%20(char).md) — mountain
- [盈](characters/盈.md) — to fill, be full
- [界](characters/界.md) — boundary, world

Literally: "Stone [becomes] mountain, fills the world."

## Extended Meaning
石山盈界 describes something small and seemingly powerless that grows to overwhelm and outlast every great human power arrayed against it. It is used specifically of God's kingdom in Christian eschatology — invoked to express confident, triumphant hope in the ultimate, universal, and permanent victory of divine purpose over worldly empires, however dominant those empires appear in the moment. Reverent and hopeful in tone, always eschatological in scope rather than describing any ordinary present-day victory.

## Source and Origin
**Daniel 2:34–35, 44–45** — the climax of Nebuchadnezzar's dream of the great statue, as interpreted by Daniel:

> "As you looked, a stone was cut out by no human hand, and it struck the image on its feet of iron and clay, and broke them in pieces... But the stone that struck the image became a great mountain and filled the whole earth." (Dan 2:34–35, ESV)
> "And in the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom that shall never be destroyed." (Dan 2:44, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders Daniel 2:35 as 「於是金、銀、銅、鐵、泥都一同砸得粉碎，成如夏天禾場上的糠秕，被風吹散，無處可尋。打碎這像的石頭變成一座大山，充滿天下。」 (checked on cnbible.com, which shows the same wording in the 現代標點和合本; the 聖經新譯本 reads 「那打碎這像的石頭卻變成一座大山，充滿全地。」). The source's own words for the image are therefore 石頭 / 大山 / 充滿天下.

I found **no attestation of 石山盈界 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage that compresses the prophecy's central image: 石 (stone), 山 (mountain), and 盈界 ("fills the world"). The stone "cut out by no human hand" is read in Christian interpretation as a kingdom of divine rather than human origin, which would then, as a messianic prophecy, supplant every earthly power. The earlier description of the form as a "crystallization" of the passage in the tradition was unsupported and has been removed.

## Variants
The attested rendering is the Chinese Union Version sentence above. Three variants listed here earlier (石成大山盈天下, 非手之石山盈界, 山石充満天下) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
石山盈界 is a specific reference to Daniel's prophecy, suited to sermons, eschatological discussion and religious commentary, to express confidence that God's kingdom will ultimately prevail over every human empire. Like [[指記二碑]], it is closer to a direct scriptural citation than a general secular idiom. Because it is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「打碎這像的石頭變成一座大山，充滿天下。」(Dan 2:35, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 神國如石山盈界，終必勝萬邦。("God's kingdom is like the stone-mountain filling the world; it will ultimately triumph over all nations.")
3. 基督之國始雖小，終石山盈界，無有窮盡。("Christ's kingdom, though small at first, will ultimately become a stone-mountain filling the world, without end.")
4. What began as a handful of believers in an upper room would, the prophecy promised, become 石山盈界 — a mountain filling the whole earth.

## Sentiment
Triumphant, eschatological, hope-filled. Used to express the ultimate victory and universal scope of God's kingdom, or the inexorable growth of divine purpose.
