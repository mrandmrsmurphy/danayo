## Literal Meaning
- [一 (char)](characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [網 (char)](characters/網%20(char).md) — net
- [打](characters/打.md) — to strike, cast
- [尽](characters/尽.md) — to exhaust, finish completely

Literally: "One cast of the net, [catch] all."

## Extended Meaning
一網打尽 describes wiping out or capturing an entire group in a single decisive action, replacing repeated pursuit with one complete, efficient stroke. The idiom foregrounds method and completeness rather than justification — it praises decisiveness and thoroughness, leaving ethical evaluation to context. Used in both literal and figurative senses across law enforcement reporting, military history, business strategy, and everyday speech; the tone is forceful and sometimes aggressive, marking an endpoint rather than a process.

## Source and Origin
The fixed four-character phrase is found in the Song notebook **《東軒筆錄》** (*Dongxuan bilu*) by Wei Tai (魏泰), volume 4, which is the passage Taiwan's Ministry of Education *Dictionary of Chinese Idioms* gives as its documented usage. Checked against the Wikisource text:

**《東軒筆錄·卷之四》**:
> "劉侍制元瑜既彈蘇舜欽，而連坐者甚衆，同時俊彥，為之一空。劉見宰相曰：『聊為相公一網打盡。』是時南郊大禮，而舜欽之獄，斷於赦前數日。"
> (After the censor Liu Yuanyu impeached Su Shunqin, a great many were implicated with him, and the outstanding men of the day were swept away. Liu told the chief minister, "I have netted them all for Your Excellency in one cast." This was the time of the great suburban sacrifice, and Su Shunqin's case was settled a few days before the amnesty.)

So the idiom began as a boast about a political purge — Liu Yuanyu's impeachment in the Su Shunqin affair — not as a neutral description.

The phrase also occurs in **《宋史·卷三一四·范純仁傳》** (checked on Wikisource): "造謗者公相慶曰一網打盡，此事未遠，願陛下戒之。" ("Those who spread the slander openly congratulate one another that they have 'netted them all in one cast'; this was not long ago — I beg Your Majesty to take warning.") Both passages are genuine; I did not establish which is earlier as a written record, and this page keeps 《東軒筆錄》 as `origin` because it is the passage the Ministry cites.

The Ministry's entry names as its deeper allusion source (典源) the net story in **《呂氏春秋·孟冬紀·異用》**, where Tang of Shang sees a man setting nets on four sides and praying that everything from heaven, earth and the four directions fall into them; Tang remarks "嘻，盡之矣" ("Ah, that would take everything"). I relied on the Ministry's entry for this and did not retrieve the *Lüshi chunqiu* text, so treat the connection as the dictionary's, not independently checked here.

**Corrected.** The earlier version of this page described the 《宋史》 occurrence as a later traditional attribution and credited the *Hanyu Da Cidian* for the earlier one. I found no support for that ordering or for the dictionary claim, and removed them.

## Standard Form
**一網打尽** (Japanese shinjitai 尽) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 一網打盡 — traditional Chinese form (盡)
- 一网打尽 — simplified Chinese form (网、尽)

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** yì wǎng dǎ jìn (tone-sandhi 一 realized as *yì* before the non-4th-tone 網; frontmatter uses the uninflected citation form)
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** jat1 mong5 daa2 zeon6
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** いちもうだじん (ichi-mō-da-jin)
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 일망타진 (il-mang-ta-jin)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** nhất võng đả tận

## Cultural Notes
一網打尽 is a fully naturalized, everyday idiom across Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese, with no special religious or philosophical framing needed — it appears constantly in news reporting on police raids and crackdowns, in military and business narrative, and in casual speech about decisively finishing off a whole group of problems, rivals, or targets at once.

## Example Sentences
1. 警方經過長期跟蹤，終於將整個犯罪集團一網打尽。("After long surveillance, police finally rounded up the entire criminal syndicate in one sweep.")
2. 一夜の作戦で、敵の残党を一網打尽にした。("In a single night's operation, the remaining enemy forces were wiped out entirely.")
3. 검찰은 비리 관련자 전원을 일망타진했다. ("Prosecutors rounded up every person involved in the corruption case in one sweep.")
4. The sting operation was designed to 一網打尽 — arrest every member of the ring in a single coordinated raid.

## Sentiment
Forceful, decisive, sometimes aggressive. Praises thoroughness and finality in dealing with an entire group at once, without particular concern for nuance or fairness in the method.
