## Literal Meaning
- [光 (char)](../characters/光%20(char).md) — light, radiant
- [明 (char)](../characters/明%20(char).md) — bright, clear
- [正 (char)](../characters/正%20(char).md) — correct, upright
- [大 (char)](../characters/大%20(char).md) — great, magnanimous

Literally: "Bright, clear, upright, and great." [[光明]] ("brightness, clarity") pairs with 正大 ("uprightness and moral greatness") to describe a character fit to be seen fully in the open.

## Extended Meaning
Open and aboveboard; honest, upright, and free of hidden motives or concealment. Describes conduct, character, or process that has nothing to hide — actions taken in the full light of scrutiny rather than through intrigue or deception. Almost always positive, and often invoked by explicit contrast with underhanded or secretive dealing.

## Source and Origin
The fixed four-character form appears in **《朱子語類》卷十五** (*Classified Conversations of Master Zhu*, a record of Zhu Xi's 朱熹 teachings compiled by his students), in a passage on the *Great Learning*'s progression from the rectification of the mind (正心) to self-cultivation, household order, and good government:
> 到心正，則胸中無些子私蔽，洞然光明正大，截然有主而不亂，此身便修，家便齊，國便治，而天下可平。
> (Once the mind is rectified, there is not the slightest selfish obstruction within it — it becomes thoroughly bright, clear, upright, and great, decisively self-possessed and unconfused; then the self is cultivated, the household is ordered, the state is governed, and the world may be at peace.)

Zhu Xi uses 光明正大 to describe the transparent, unclouded state of a mind free of private bias — the condition that makes proper self-cultivation and, by extension, good governance possible. The phrase draws on two established two-character terms: [[光明]] ("brightness, clarity, openness") and 正大 ("uprightness, moral greatness"), together expressing that a properly cultivated mind acts in a way fit to be seen in the open, without concealment or distortion.

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** guāng míng zhèng dà
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** gwong1 ming4 zing3 daai6
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** こうめいせいだい
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 광명정대 (gwang-myeong-jeong-dae)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** quang minh chính đại

All five readings are fully compositional, matching each constituent character's own standard reading.

## Cultural Notes
In Chinese, 光明正大 often emphasizes transparency and moral legitimacy, especially in contrast to deceit or intrigue. In Japanese, the phrase is used in formal contexts to describe fair and honorable conduct, especially in public life. In Korean, 광명정대 similarly refers to actions that are honest and beyond reproach. Compared with the close synonym [[公明正大]] ("公," public + impartial), this form leans slightly more toward moral clarity and openness as an inner quality, rather than institutional or procedural impartiality.

## Example Sentences
1. 他光明正大地承認了自己的錯誤。
2. He openly and honestly admitted his mistake — acting _guāng míng zhèng dà_.
3. 光明正大なやり方で勝負すべきだ。
4. 그는 광명정대한 태도로 일을 처리했다.

## Sentiment
Strongly positive; praises conduct that stands fully in the light, without concealment or hidden motive.
