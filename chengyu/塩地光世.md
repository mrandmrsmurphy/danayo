## Literal Meaning
- [塩 (char)](../characters/塩%20(char).md) — salt
- [地 (char)](../characters/地%20(char).md) — earth, land
- [光 (char)](../characters/光%20(char).md) — light
- [世](../characters/世.md) — world, age

Literally: "salt of the earth, light of the world."

## Extended Meaning
A concise expression of Jesus's description of his disciples as preserving, purifying, and illuminating influences within the world. It denotes moral distinctiveness and public witness — a calling to visible, transformative presence within society rather than withdrawal from it.

## Source and Origin
This idiom condenses **Matthew 5:13–14**, from the Sermon on the Mount:

> "Ye are the salt of the earth: but if the salt have lost his savour, wherewith shall it be salted?... Ye are the light of the world. A city that is set on an hill cannot be hid." (KJV)

The underlying Greek distinguishes γῆ ("earth, land") in v.13 from κόσμος ("world, cosmos") in v.14. I checked the Chinese versions on cnbible.com: the Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders both clauses with 世上, 「你們是世上的鹽」 (v.13) and 「你們是世上的光」 (v.14), a flattening of the distinction, but the 聖經新譯本 and the 中文標準譯本 render v.13 as 「你們是地上的鹽」, following the Greek, while keeping 世上 or 世界 in v.14. So the flattening is specific to the 和合本, not to "Traditional Chinese Bible translations" generally, as the earlier version of this page said.

I found **no attestation of 塩地光世 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage that pairs the two metaphors. The Dan'a'yo form preserves the Greek two-term distinction (地 "earth" for v.13, 世 "world" for v.14) rather than following the 和合本's flattening, uniting both metaphors into a single balanced four-character pairing.

## Cultural Notes
Salt in the ancient world preserved food, enhanced flavor, and symbolized covenant fidelity (see also the "covenant of salt" in Numbers 18:19); light signifies revelation, visibility, and moral clarity. Both metaphors are vocational rather than merely descriptive — a calling, not simply a state of being. The imagery also carries an implicit warning: salt can lose its savor and become useless, and light can be hidden under a bushel rather than displayed. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 塩地光世 functions as an ethical summons, compressing Christian identity and public mission into a single balanced maxim.

## Example Sentences
1. 信徒當自省，是否仍為塩地光世，而非隱匿無用。("Believers should examine whether they remain salt of the earth and light of the world, rather than hidden and ineffective.")
2. 「你們是世上的鹽...你們是世上的光。」(馬太福音 5:13-14，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. The sermon reminded the congregation that to be 塩地光世 is a calling, not merely a compliment.
4. 教會當如塩地光世，於黑暗之世發光，於腐敗之地防腐。("The church should be as salt of the earth and light of the world — shining in a dark age, preserving in a corrupt land.")

## Sentiment
Encouraging yet exhortative. Affirms a calling while pressing responsibility and visibility.
