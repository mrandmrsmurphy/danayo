## Literal Meaning
- [腹 (char)](../characters/腹%20(char).md) — belly
- [行](../characters/行.md) — to go, to walk
- [食](../characters/食.md) — to eat
- [塵](../characters/塵.md) — dust

Literally: "Go on the belly, eat dust."

## Extended Meaning
The curse on the serpent after the Fall: it is condemned to crawl on its belly and eat dust all its days. As a four-character idiom it names total humiliation and defeat, 腹行 for the humiliating posture of crawling and 食塵 for the permanent degradation, and it is used of disgrace, subjugation, or divine judgment.

## Source and Origin
**Genesis 3:14**, God's curse on the serpent after the temptation (和合本, 創世記 3:14):

> 耶和華神對蛇說：你既作了這事，就必受咒詛，比一切的牲畜野獸更甚，你必用肚子行走，終身吃土。
> "The LORD God said to the serpent, 'Because you have done this, cursed are you above all livestock and above all beasts of the field; on your belly you shall go, and dust you shall eat all the days of your life.'" (Gen 3:14, ESV)

The 和合本 has 吃土 ("eat earth") for the Hebrew "dust", and the vault's 食塵 uses 塵 ("dust") instead. The phrase 腹行食塵 is a Dan'a'yo compression of the verse. The image of eating or licking dust as defeat recurs in the Bible, for example Psalm 72:9 and Micah 7:17.

## Variants
- 以腹行而食塵 — "go on the belly and eat dust", a fuller form
- 腹行終身食塵 — "go on the belly, eat dust all one's life", stressing perpetuity
- 蛇行食土 — "snake-crawl, eat earth", an alternate form closer to the 和合本's 吃土

These are alternate phrasings of the same text, not orthographic variants of one fixed idiom.

## Cultural Notes
In its biblical setting the curse marks the serpent's fall from its former standing and is read, in Jewish and Christian tradition, as both an explanation of why snakes crawl and a sign of the defeat of the tempter. Eating dust stands in the Bible for total subjugation. In Dan'a'yo the idiom is available as a metaphor for disgrace or divine judgment, with its biblical source in the Fall narrative kept in view.

## Example Sentences
1. 蛇受咒，腹行食塵，以示其墮落之深。
   "The serpent was cursed to crawl on its belly and eat dust, to show the depth of its fall."
2. 惡者終必如腹行食塵，極其卑賤。
   "The wicked will in the end be like one who crawls on his belly and eats dust, utterly debased."

## Sentiment
Condemnatory and humiliating. Used to describe utter defeat, degradation, divine curse, or the consequences of pride and rebellion.
