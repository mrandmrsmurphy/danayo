## Literal Meaning
- [阿 (char)](../characters/阿%20(char).md) — (phonetic, from Avīci)
- [鼻 (char)](../characters/鼻%20(char).md) — (phonetic, from Avīci)
- [叫 (char)](../characters/叫%20(char).md) — to cry out
- [喚 (char)](../characters/喚%20(char).md) — to shout, call out

Literally: "Avīci [and] Wailing" — the fused names of two of the Eight Hot Hells.

## Extended Meaning
Scenes of extreme suffering, chaos, or torment; uproarious shouting and lamentation as if issuing from hell itself. Across the region it is used metaphorically for situations filled with shouting, panic, or extreme disorder — riots, battlefield confusion, disasters, or even comedic exaggerations of noisy turmoil — with tone ranging from dramatic to ironic depending on context.

## Source and Origin
阿鼻叫喚 is not, as its surface reading might suggest, a single hell's name (阿鼻) paired with a description of the sounds within it (叫喚). It instead fuses the names of **two distinct hells** among the Eight Hot Hells (八熱地獄) of Buddhist cosmology: 叫喚地獄 ("Wailing Hell," the fourth) and 阿鼻地獄 (Avīci, "the Hell of No Interval," the eighth and most severe). 阿鼻 is itself a phonetic transliteration of Sanskrit Avīci, "without interval," referring to uninterrupted suffering.

The idiom's specific formulation and the enumeration of the Eight Hot Hells that connects these two names is best traced to **《往生要集》** (Ōjōyōshū, "Essentials of Rebirth," 985 AD), compiled by the Japanese Tendai monk Genshin (源信), which catalogs the eight hells in order — 等活・黒縄・衆合・叫喚・大叫喚・焦熱・大焦熱・無間（阿鼻） — with vivid, influential descriptions of each that shaped later Japanese Buddhist art and literature. The broader hell cosmology itself, including the concept of Avīci, is older still, appearing in earlier Abhidharma literature such as the 《阿毘達磨俱舍論》 (Abhidharmakośa), but the specific fused four-character idiom and its popularization trace most directly to Genshin's synthesis.

## Cultural Notes
Buddhist descriptions of hell frequently emphasize sound — screams, wailing, and lamentation convey the scale of suffering more vividly than physical description alone, and 叫喚 ("shouting and crying out") captures this auditory imagery directly by name. In religious texts the reference is literal; in later literary usage it becomes figurative, applying the imagery of hell to worldly scenes of chaos or distress. The idiom preserves this specifically Buddhist cosmological reference even when used metaphorically in secular contexts — a disaster site or riot described as 阿鼻叫喚 retains an echo of infernal suffering, not merely generic loud chaos.

## Example Sentences
1. 大地震發生後，現場一片阿鼻叫喚。("After the great earthquake, the scene was one of utter agonized chaos.")
2. The aftermath of the disaster was pure 阿鼻叫喚 — screaming, panic, and confusion everywhere.
3. 戦場は阿鼻叫喚の巷と化した。("The battlefield turned into a scene of hellish screaming and chaos.")
4. 混乱の中、阿鼻叫喚の声が響き渡った。("Amid the chaos, cries of agony echoed everywhere.")

## Sentiment
Grim, chaotic, vividly negative. Describes scenes of extreme suffering or disorder, ranging in register from tragic to hyperbolic depending on context.
