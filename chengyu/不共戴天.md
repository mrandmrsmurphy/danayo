## Literal Meaning
- [不 (char)](characters/不%20(char).md) — not
- [共 (char)](characters/共%20(char).md) — together, shared
- [戴 (char)](characters/戴%20(char).md) — to bear, wear upon oneself
- [天 (char)](characters/天%20(char).md) — heaven, sky

Literally: "Not sharing the same heaven."

## Extended Meaning
不共戴天 describes an enmity so total and absolute that coexistence between the two parties is impossible — not a dispute to be negotiated or a grievance to be settled by compensation, but a categorical state placing the relationship outside ordinary moral repair. It does not describe mere intensity of feeling; once invoked, it signals finality rather than anger. Used to describe enemies whose opposition is fundamental and enduring, in historical narration, political rhetoric, and literary writing — the tone is severe and elevated, and the idiom is rarely used lightly.

## Source and Origin
From **《禮記·曲禮上》** (Book of Rites, "Summary of the Rules of Propriety, Part I"). The phrase appears in ritual and moral discourse concerning blood vengeance and ultimate moral opposition, where certain offenses (particularly the killing of one's father) sever the possibility of shared social or cosmic order.

戴天, "to bear heaven above one's head," is a basic image of human existence within an ordered cosmos. To say that two parties 不共戴天 is to deny not merely social harmony but shared placement under the same moral sky — the conflict is framed as total. In the ritual worldview of the Book of Rites, such language is reserved for extreme cases, especially those involving kinship violation or grave injustice, encoding a boundary beyond which reconciliation is no longer ritually or morally intelligible.

## Standard Form
**不共戴天** is the Dan'a'yo standard form; stable across traditional/simplified script.

## Variants
- 不倶戴天 — an alternate form substituting 倶 ("together, jointly") for 共, with the same meaning

## Cultural Notes
不共戴天 is a fully naturalized classical idiom across Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese, used to describe enemies whose opposition is fundamental and enduring — a mortal or blood enemy (不共戴天之仇, "an enemy one cannot share heaven with") rather than an ordinary rival. It appears across the region in historical narration, political rhetoric, and literary writing, always with severe, elevated register.

## Example Sentences
1. 「父之讎，弗與共戴天。」(《禮記·曲禮上》, "One does not share heaven with the enemy of one's father.")
2. 兩國積怨已深，幾成不共戴天之勢。("The two nations' mutual resentment had grown so deep it had nearly become an irreconcilable enmity.")
3. 그와 나는 불공대천의 원수가 되었다. ("He and I became mortal enemies who cannot share the same sky.")
4. After the betrayal, the two former partners became 不共戴天 rivals, and no reconciliation was ever possible.

## Sentiment
Severe, elevated, final. Describes an absolute, categorical enmity beyond ordinary moral repair — signals finality rather than mere anger.
