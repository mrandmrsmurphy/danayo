## Literal Meaning
- [家](characters/家.md) — house, household
- [分 (char)](characters/分%20(char).md) — divided, split
- [不 (char)](characters/不%20(char).md) — not
- [立 (char)](characters/立%20(char).md) — stand

Literally: "A house, divided, does not stand."

## Extended Meaning
家分不立 warns that any group bound together by shared purpose — a household, a nation, an organization, a church — will collapse if it turns against itself internally. The idiom is used prescriptively rather than merely descriptively: it is invoked to argue for unity and against factionalism, applied equally to politics, family relationships, business, and religious community. Always cautionary; never used to praise division.

## Source and Origin
The saying appears in near-identical form in all three Synoptic Gospels — **Matthew 12:25**, **Mark 3:25**, and **Luke 11:17** — spoken by Jesus in response to the accusation that he cast out demons by the power of Satan:

> "Every kingdom divided against itself is laid waste, and no city or house divided against itself will stand." (Matt 12:25, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders the verse 「凡一國自相紛爭，就成為荒場；一城一家自相紛爭，必站立不住。」 (checked on cnbible.com, which shows the same wording in the 現代標點和合本); the 聖經新譯本 renders it 「如果一個國家自相紛爭，就必定荒涼；一城一家自相紛爭，必站立不住。」

I found **no attestation of 家分不立 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character rendering of the "house divided" clause, like the other Biblical chengyu on the grouping page. Its logical structure — subject (家, "house") + state (分, "divided") + consequence (不立, "cannot stand") — mirrors the terse cause-and-effect phrasing of Chinese maxims, which is why it reads naturally as a chengyu despite its New Testament origin. The earlier claim on this page that it was the "East Asian Bible translation's crystallization" of the clause was unsupported and has been removed.

## Variants
Attested Chinese renderings are longer phrases rather than a fixed idiom:
- 一城一家自相紛爭，必站立不住 — Chinese Union Version of Matthew 12:25

A "fuller Classical Chinese rendering" listed here earlier (家若分争，必站不住) could not be found in any source and has been removed.

## Cultural Notes
As with other Biblical chengyu built on a pre-existing native logical pattern (cf. [[剣生剣死]]), the warning against internal division resonates with the Confucian concern for household and social harmony, so the thought is intelligible beyond Christian circles. Because 家分不立 is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「凡一國自相紛爭，就成為荒場；一城一家自相紛爭，必站立不住。」(Matt 12:25, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 教會家分不立，當求合一之道。("If the church is divided against itself, it must seek the way of unity.")
3. 此國家分不立，終必敗亡。("This nation divided against itself will ultimately fall.")
4. The coalition's infighting proved the old warning true: 家分不立, and within a year it had collapsed entirely.

## Sentiment
Cautionary, admonitory. Used to warn against internal conflict and to argue for the necessity of unity, whether in a family, a church, a company, or a nation.
