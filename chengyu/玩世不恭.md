## Literal Meaning
- [玩](characters/玩.md) — to play with, toy with
- [世](characters/世.md) — the world, life
- [不 (char)](characters/不%20(char).md) — not
- [恭](characters/恭.md) — respectful, reverent

Literally: "to toy with the world, not reverent [toward it]" — treating life and the world with a playful, unserious contempt.

## Extended Meaning

An unserious, irreverent, mocking attitude toward life and the world, typically adopted out of dissatisfaction with reality rather than genuine lightheartedness — this vault's closest match for English "cynical" as a worldview or personality trait, distinct from [[冷笑]] (the sneering laugh itself) and [[皮肉]] (the ironic/critical mode of speech). Carries a mildly negative connotation (贬义) in the source tradition, though it can also describe a detached, above-it-all equanimity — a wry, self-protective disengagement rather than active malice, especially for someone who has already suffered real setbacks.

## Source and Origin

### Earliest precedent (Han dynasty) — the idea, not yet the fixed idiom
**《漢書·東方朔傳贊》** (Ban Gu's closing appraisal of the biography of Dongfang Shuo, the witty, unconventional court advisor to Emperor Wu of Han):
> 依隱玩世，詭時不逢。
> (Relying on a kind of hidden reclusion, he played with the world — cunningly, he never quite met his own times.)

This establishes the core idea — a clever man's playful, semi-withdrawn relationship with a world he doesn't fully respect or fit into — without yet fusing 玩世 and 不恭 into one fixed phrase.

### Fixed four-character form (Ming dynasty)
The earliest attestation of the exact idiom is Ming writer Li Kaixian's (李開先) **《雪蓑道人傳》** ("Biography of the Snow-Cape Daoist"):
> 醉後高歌起舞，更有風韻，只是玩世不恭，人難親近耳。
> (After drinking he'd sing loudly and dance, with real flair — it's just that he was so cynically irreverent toward the world that people found him hard to get close to.)

## Cultural Notes

Live in modern Mandarin, most often describing a person's general life-attitude (a description of character) rather than a single action — 他一向玩世不恭 ("he's always been cynically irreverent"). Not independently confirmed as a standard four-character idiom in living Japanese or Korean usage; Dan'a'yo still gives it the systematic cross-CJKV reading this vault provides for every chengyu, matching the treatment already given to other Chinese-specific-origin idioms elsewhere in this vault.

## Example Sentences
1. 他對什麼都玩世不恭，好像沒有一件事值得認真對待。
2. After the scandal, he grew _wán shì bù gōng_, treating every serious conversation as a joke.

## Sentiment
Mildly negative to mixed — carries a real note of disapproval (贬义) in its classical grammar function, but can also read as a wry, self-protective detachment rather than active cruelty, especially for someone already worn down by disappointment.
