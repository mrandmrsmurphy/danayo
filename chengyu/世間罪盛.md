[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [世間](../words/世間.md) — the world, the present age, the human sphere
- [罪 (char)](characters/罪%20(char).md) — sin, transgression
- [盛](characters/盛.md) — abundant, flourishing, overflowing

Literally: "in the world, sin abounds."

## Extended Meaning
A compact declaration of pervasive sinfulness in the human order. It emphasizes not isolated wrongdoing but systemic moral saturation — the diagnosis that sin is not merely present in the world but ascendant, flourishing, proliferating. It can function historically (describing a specific corrupt age, as in the pre-Flood narrative) or diagnostically, describing any age marked by moral decay.

## Source and Origin
世間罪盛 is a Dan'a'yo-coined theological maxim rather than a direct quotation of a single verse; it draws together several biblical themes rather than translating one text:

- **Genesis 6:5** — "And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth..." (the pre-Flood corruption of humanity)
- **Psalm 14:3** — "There is none that doeth good, no, not one."
- **Romans 5:20** — "But where sin abounded, grace did much more abound" (Greek: *ἐπλεόνασεν ἡ ἁμαρτία*, "sin increased/abounded" — the closest structural parallel to 罪盛)
- **1 John 5:19** — "And we know that we are of God, and the whole world lieth in wickedness."

The deliberate choice of 世間 — a term widely used in Buddhist and general CJK vocabulary for "the world" or "the mundane, conditioned realm" — carries layered resonance: in Buddhist discourse 世間 denotes the impermanent realm of suffering, and by appropriating the term for a biblical maxim, Dan'a'yo reframes the human predicament as moral rebellion rather than mere impermanence or ignorance. Unlike Buddhist pessimism about the world's nature, biblical theology pairs this diagnosis with redemptive intervention rather than leaving it as a bare description of suffering.

## Cultural Notes
Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 世間罪盛 functions as an anthropological and cultural assessment rather than a fatalistic complaint — it names corruption candidly while implicitly preparing the ground for the announcement of redemption, paralleling how Romans 5:20 immediately pairs "sin abounded" with "grace did much more abound." It is used diagnostically in preaching and moral reflection, applicable to any age or society judged to be in a state of pervasive moral decline, not narrowly to the antediluvian world of Genesis alone.

## Example Sentences
1. 當世間罪盛之時，更顯救贖之需。("When sin flourishes in the world, the need for redemption becomes clearer.")
2. 「耶和華見人在地上罪惡很大...」(創世記 6:5，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. The preacher warned that 世間罪盛 was no excuse for despair, but a reason to seek grace all the more.
4. 罪雖然世間罪盛，恩典卻更加澎湃。("Though sin abounds in the world, grace overflows all the more.")

## Sentiment
Somber, diagnostic, morally sober. Acknowledges pervasive corruption while implicitly preparing for divine remedy — grave rather than despairing.
