[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [指](characters/指.md) — finger
- [記](characters/記.md) — inscribe, record
- [二 (char)](characters/二%20(char).md) — two
- [碑](characters/碑.md) — stone tablet, stele

Literally: "Finger-inscribed two tablets."

## Extended Meaning
指記二碑 refers to the two stone tablets of the Ten Commandments, inscribed by God's own finger at Mount Sinai. It is used to invoke divine law, covenant, and authoritative, unchanging moral standard — reverently, in religious, legal-philosophical, or moral-authority contexts, never casually or ironically. It functions less as a general-purpose idiom than as a fixed reference to a specific, singular object of Biblical history, similar to how "the tablets of stone" functions in English.

## Source and Origin
The tablets appear across several passages: **Exodus 31:18**, **32:15–16**, **34:1–4**, and **Deuteronomy 4:13**, **5:22**, **9:10–11**. The foundational verse:

> "When he finished speaking with him on Mount Sinai, he gave to Moses the two tablets of the testimony, tablets of stone, written with the finger of God." (Ex 31:18, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders the verse 「耶和華在西乃山和摩西說完了話，就把兩塊法版交給他，是神用指頭寫的石版。」 (checked on cnbible.com, which shows the same wording in the 現代標點和合本; the 聖經新譯本 reads the same except for 西奈山上與摩西). The Chinese Bible's own words for the tablets are therefore 法版 ("law tablets") and 石版 ("stone tablets"), and "written with the finger of God" is 神用指頭寫的.

I found **no attestation of 指記二碑 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage that compresses the detail that the tablets were inscribed by God's finger (指記, "finger-inscribed") with 二 "two" and 碑 "stele," like the other Biblical chengyu on the grouping page. The earlier claim that 碑 was chosen over 版 for its monumental connotation was an interpretation of the coinage, not something I could verify, and has been removed.

## Standard Form
**指記二碑** (traditional 記) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 指记二碑 — simplified Chinese form (记)

## Variants
The attested Chinese terms for the tablets are 法版 and 石版 (Chinese Union Version of Exodus 31:18). Three variants listed here earlier (法版二碑, 石版二碑, 誡命二版) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
指記二碑 is a reverent, specific reference to the Sinai tablets, close to a direct scriptural citation, and it is suited to sermons, religious education and discussion of Biblical law and covenant. Because it is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「耶和華在西奈山和摩西說完了話，就把兩塊法版交給他，是神用指頭寫的石版。」(Ex 31:18, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 神以指記二碑，示其律永恒不變。("God with his finger inscribed the two tablets, showing that his law is eternally unchanging.")
3. 摩西受指記二碑於西乃山。("Moses received the two tablets inscribed by the finger [of God] at Mount Sinai.")
4. The preacher held up two stones and said, "Remember 指記二碑 — God's law was never meant to be a matter of human invention."

## Sentiment
Reverent, foundational. Used to reference divine law, covenant, or authoritative moral standards.
