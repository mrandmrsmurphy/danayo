## Literal Meaning
- [欣](../characters/欣.md) — happy, glad
- [喜 (char)](../characters/喜%20(char).md) — delighted, pleased
- [雀](../characters/雀.md) — sparrow
- [躍](../characters/躍.md) — to leap

Literally: "Joyful and delighted, leaping like a sparrow."

## Extended Meaning
To be overjoyed to the point of visible, physical excitement — not just inward happiness but joy that shows itself in motion, like a sparrow hopping energetically. Implies delight that is difficult to contain, often at sudden good news.

## Source and Origin
The fixed four-character idiom appears verbatim in **《水滸傳》第一〇八回** (*Water Margin*, chapter 108):
> 宋江聞報，把那憂國家、哭兄弟的病症，退了九分九釐，欣喜雀躍，同眾將拔寨都起。
> (When Song Jiang heard the report, the affliction of grieving for his country and weeping for his brothers receded nine parts in ten — overjoyed, he leapt for joy, and set out to break camp together with all his generals.)

The idiom's two halves each carry older pedigrees of their own. **雀躍** ("sparrow-leaping") traces back to **《莊子·在宥》** ("Letting Be, Leaving Alone"):
> 鴻蒙方將拊髀雀躍而遊。
> (Vast Obscurity was slapping his thigh and hopping about like a sparrow, wandering freely.)

In this passage, the sage-figure Hongmeng ("Vast Obscurity") meets the traveler Yunjiang ("Cloud General") in a state of carefree, playful abandon — slapping his thigh and hopping in place, refusing serious engagement and simply declaring "遊!" ("Wander! Play!"). **欣喜** ("joy, delight") is separately attested in the much earlier **《春秋左氏傳》** (Zuo Zhuan). The two halves were joined into the fixed four-character idiom by the time of *Water Margin* (compiled during the Ming dynasty from earlier oral and written material).

## Standard Form
**欣喜雀躍**
- 欣喜雀跃 — simplified form

## Pronunciations

All five readings are fully compositional, matching each constituent character's own standard reading.

## Cultural Notes
Describes open, exuberant happiness — especially joy that is difficult to contain and shows itself physically, not merely felt inwardly. Common in Mandarin for describing a group or individual's reaction to sudden good news. In Japanese, the idiom is understood but relatively literary — everyday speech tends to reach for simpler expressions instead. In Korean, the exact four-character form is less common in casual use than the underlying imagery of "leaping for joy," though it remains a recognized literary idiom.

## Example Sentences
1. 聽到這個好消息，大家都欣喜雀躍。
2. The whole office was _xīn xǐ què yuè_ when the merger fell through and their jobs were saved.
3. 彼は合格の知らせに欣喜雀躍した。
4. News of the reunion left her _hân hỉ tước dược_, unable to sit still.

## Sentiment
Strongly positive; describes exuberant, visible, physically-expressed joy.
