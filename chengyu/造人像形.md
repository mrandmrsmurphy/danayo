## Literal Meaning
- [造](../characters/造.md) — to create, make
- [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) — human being
- [像](../characters/像.md) — image
- [形 (char)](../characters/形%20(char).md) — likeness, form

Literally: "create man in image and likeness."

## Extended Meaning
A compressed expression of the doctrine that humanity is created in the image of God (*imago Dei*). It signifies dignity, representation, rationality, and covenant vocation — a status that grounds ethics and human worth generally.

## Source and Origin
The idiom condenses **Genesis 1:26–27**:

> "And God said, Let us make man in our image, after our likeness: and let them have dominion..." (KJV)

Traditional Chinese Bible translations typically render this as "我們要照著我們的形像，按著我們的樣式造人" ("Let us make man according to our image, in our likeness"). The Dan'a'yo form removes the surrounding syntax and preserves the paired theological terms 像 and 形, reflecting the underlying Hebrew's own dual vocabulary (צֶלֶם *tselem*, "image," and דְּמוּת *demuth*, "likeness").

## Cultural Notes
The pairing 像形 preserves this twofold Hebrew structure without collapsing the two terms into one. Historically, Christian theology has debated whether "image" and "likeness" denote genuinely distinct faculties or are simply rhetorical parallelism restating the same idea — patristic writers sometimes distinguished rational capacity (image) from moral conformity (likeness), while later theologians often treated the two terms as overlapping and mutually reinforcing. In contrast to surrounding ancient Near Eastern cultures, where only kings were said to bear the divine image, Genesis universalizes the concept to all humanity — a democratization of royal dignity that undergirds later doctrines of human equality and moral accountability. 造 emphasizes intentional, personal craftsmanship rather than impersonal emanation: the act is deliberate. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 造人像形 serves as an anthropological cornerstone grounding ethics, vocation, and the doctrine of redemption — the Fall is understood to distort but not erase this status, a tension developed further elsewhere in biblical theology.

## Example Sentences
1. 人之尊嚴，本於造人像形之旨。("Human dignity rests upon the truth that man was created in image and likeness.")
2. 「神說：我們要照著我們的形像、按著我們的樣式造人。」(創世記 1:26，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Christian ethicists appeal to 造人像形 as the theological basis for the inherent worth of every human being.
4. 縱使墮落，造人像形之尊嚴猶存，未曾全然抹去。("Even after the Fall, the dignity of being made in image and likeness endures, never wholly erased.")

## Sentiment
Elevated, foundational, dignifying. Affirms human worth while implying responsibility before the Creator.
