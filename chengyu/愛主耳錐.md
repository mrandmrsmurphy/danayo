[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [愛 (char)](characters/愛%20(char).md) — to love
- [主](characters/主.md) — master, lord
- [耳 (char)](characters/耳%20(char).md) — ear
- [錐](characters/錐.md) — awl

Literally: "Loving the master, [pierce the] ear [with an] awl."

## Extended Meaning
愛主耳錐 refers to the voluntary bondservant who chooses lifelong service out of love rather than obligation, marked permanently by a pierced ear. In Christian interpretation, it becomes a powerful metaphor for voluntary, loving devotion to God — believers willingly becoming "bondservants of Christ" (Romans 1:1, Philippians 1:1). The physical mark symbolizes total commitment and joyful surrender rather than coerced slavery, and the idiom is invoked devotionally to express lifelong, freely chosen dedication.

## Source and Origin
**Exodus 21:5–6** and **Deuteronomy 15:16–17**:

> "But if the slave plainly says, 'I love my master, my wife, and my children; I will not go out free,' then his master shall bring him to God, and he shall bring him to the door or the doorpost. And his master shall bore his ear through with an awl, and he shall be his slave forever." (Ex 21:5–6, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本), checked on cnbible.com, renders verse 5 as 「倘或奴僕明說：我愛我的主人和我的妻子、兒女，不願意自由出去」 and verse 6 as 「他的主人就要帶他到審判官那裡，又要帶他到門前，靠近門框，用錐子穿他的耳朵，他就永遠服事主人。」 (the 聖經新譯本 reads 「…他的主人要用錐子刺穿他的耳朵，他就要永遠服事他的主人。」). The Chinese Bible's own phrases are 我愛我的主人 and 用錐子穿他的耳朵.

I found **no attestation of 愛主耳錐 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage compressing the two clauses.

愛主 ("love [the] master") captures the servant's voluntary devotion, while 耳錐 ("ear-awl") refers to the physical mark of perpetual servitude — a practice that created a visible, permanent sign of willing bondage, chosen rather than imposed.

## Variants
The attested Chinese phrases are 我愛我的主人 and 用錐子穿他的耳朵 (Chinese Union Version of Exodus 21:5–6). Three variants listed here earlier (錐耳愛主, 僕愛主耳錐, 永僕耳記) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
愛主耳錐 is a scriptural reference suited to sermons and theological writing on voluntary discipleship and lifelong devotion to God, drawing on the New Testament's self-description of believers, and especially apostles, as "bondservants" (δοῦλος) of Christ. Because it is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「他的主人就要帶他到審判官那裡，又要帶他到門前，靠近門框，用錐子穿他的耳朵，他就永遠服事主人。」(Ex 21:6, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 僕甘愛主耳錐，終身不離其家。("The servant willingly underwent the ear-piercing out of love for his master, never leaving his household for life.")
3. 信徒如愛主耳錐之僕，自願永遠事奉基督。("Believers are like the servant with the ear-piercing of love for the master, voluntarily serving Christ forever.")
4. He liked to say his years of unpaid service to the ministry were his own kind of 愛主耳錐 — a bond he'd chosen, not one imposed on him.

## Sentiment
Devotional, self-surrendering, covenantal. Used to express voluntary lifelong commitment, joyful servitude to God, or complete dedication born of love rather than obligation.
