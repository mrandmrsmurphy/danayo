## Literal Meaning
- [涅](../characters/涅.md) — (transliteration syllable, no meaning of its own here)
- [盤 (char)](../characters/盤%20(char).md) — (transliteration syllable, no meaning of its own here)
- [寂](../characters/寂.md) — silent, still
- [静](../characters/静.md) — quiet

Literally: "Nirvana is silent and still." [[涅盤|涅槃]] is a Chinese phonetic transliteration of Sanskrit *nirvāṇa* — 涅 and 盤 contribute only sound, not sense — while [[寂静]] ("silent, tranquil") is a fully native Chinese compound.

## Extended Meaning
The ultimate state of peace beyond suffering: the complete cessation of craving, aversion, and the arising-and-passing of conditioned existence. Unlike most chengyu, this is not a metaphor extended to ordinary situations — it names a specific doctrinal endpoint of Buddhist practice, and it is used almost exclusively in religious, philosophical, or literary registers rather than casual speech.

## Source and Origin
The underlying teaching — that nirvana is defined by stillness, the cessation of all conditioned arising — is one of the **Three Dharma Seals** (三法印), the three-part formula by which Nāgārjuna (龍樹) identifies genuine Buddhist teaching. **《大智度論》卷二十二** states:
> 佛法印有三種：一者一切有為法念念生滅皆無常，二者一切法無我，三者寂滅涅槃。
> (The seals of the Buddha-dharma are three: first, that all conditioned phenomena are impermanent, arising and ceasing moment to moment; second, that all dharmas are without self; third, that cessation is nirvana.)

Note that this earliest fixed-formula source states the third seal as **寂滅涅槃** ("cessation-nirvana"), with the two halves in the reverse order from the idiom used today; a related early formulation in the **《根本說一切有部毘奈耶》** gives **寂靜涅槃** ("still-nirvana"), also reversed. The now-standard four-character form **涅槃寂靜**, presenting nirvana as the subject and stillness as its predicate, crystallized later as the fixed idiom of common usage — paired with its two doctrinal companions, [[諸行無常]] ("all conditioned things are impermanent") and [[諸法無我]] ("all dharmas are without self"), to form the complete Three Dharma Seals as they are cited today.

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** niè pán jì jìng
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** nip6 pun4 zik6 zing6
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** ねはんじゃくじょう
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 녈반적정 (nyeol-ban-jeok-jeong)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** niết bàn tịch tĩnh

涅槃 is read ねはん (*nehan*) in Japanese — a fixed, lexicalized Buddhist-loanword reading (NE for 涅, HAN for 盤/槃) rather than either character's primary on'yomi — and 寂静 is read じゃくじょう (*jakujō*), using the Buddhist-register on'yomi JAKU/JOU rather than the everyday SEKI/SEI readings; both are inherited unchanged from the constituent words [[涅盤]] and [[寂静]]. The Korean reading 녈반적정 preserves 涅's word-initial ㄴ per this vault's standing North Korean/문화어 convention (matching [[涅盤]]'s own corrected reading), rather than the South Korean 두음법칙-softened 열반 seen in most South Korean dictionaries.

## Cultural Notes
In Chinese Buddhist usage, the phrase retains a strictly doctrinal and philosophical meaning — nirvana specifically, not calmness in general. In Japanese, it is encountered mainly in doctrinal study and classical literature, where it emphasizes the stillness beyond the cycle of birth and death. In Korean Buddhist teaching, 녈반적정 (열반적정 in South Korean orthography) carries the same technical sense. Across all three, the expression functions as a fixed technical term rather than a flexible figure of speech, distinguishing it from most other chengyu.

## Example Sentences

2. Buddhism holds that _niè pán jì jìng_ — nirvana is peace — is the highest state of liberation.
3. 涅槃寂静は仏教における究極の安らぎを表す。
4. 열반적정은 모든 고통이 사라진 상태를 의미한다.

## Sentiment
Solemn and reverent rather than merely calm; names a specific doctrinal endpoint — the stillness beyond all arising and passing — rather than describing an ordinary emotional state.
