## Literal Meaning
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [攫](../characters/攫.md) — to seize, to grab
- [千](../characters/千.md) — thousand
- [金](../characters/金%20(char).md) — gold

Literally: "seize a thousand pieces of gold in one grab."

## Extended Meaning
To get rich suddenly; to make a huge profit through a single, often risky or opportunistic act. 攫 means to snatch or grab with the hand — a physical, abrupt verb, unlike verbs of earning or cultivation. Paired with 千金, the image becomes intentionally jarring: enormous wealth obtained without proportional labor.

## Source and Origin
The image behind the idiom is an old story in the *Liezi*, though the four-character form is not in it.

**《列子·說符》**:
> "昔齊人有欲金者，清旦衣冠而之市，適鬻金者之所，因攫其金而去。吏捕得之，問曰：『人皆在焉，子攫人之金何？』對曰：『取金之時，不見人，徒見金。』"
> (Long ago a man of Qi who craved gold dressed in his best one morning, went to the market, walked up to a gold-seller's stall, and snatched the gold and left. The officers caught him and asked, "There were people all around; why did you grab another man's gold?" He answered, "When I took the gold I didn't see people, only gold.")

The passage supplies the verb 攫 ("to seize") and the picture of a grab at gold, and it is the likely inspiration for the idiom, but it does not contain the characters 一攫千金, and the "thousand pieces" and the "single grab" are later additions. The moral of the original is one of blind greed, which is why the idiom keeps a faint note of recklessness.

**Not verified.** I could not locate the earliest text in which the four-character form 一攫千金 itself appears. Some reference pages (for example Japanese Wikipedia) name the *Shiji* as the source but quote no passage, and I did not find the phrase in it, so that attribution is not repeated here. The earlier claim on this page that the phrase arose in late Ming and Qing vernacular fiction was likewise unsupported and has been removed.

## Standard Form
**一攫千金**
- 一獲千金 — a variant writing seen in Japanese usage, with 獲 ("to obtain") for 攫 ("to seize"); listed as an alias. 攫 is the form that keeps the "grab" image of the *Liezi* story.

## Cultural Notes
Across the region, 一攫千金 is used to describe speculative success, windfalls, or get-rich-quick schemes. It appears frequently in economic commentary, journalism, and everyday speech. The tone is often skeptical or cautionary, though it can also be purely descriptive or ironic — it contrasts sharply with idioms that praise gradual cultivation or moral causality, and its persistence reflects a social reality that never disappeared: wealth sometimes comes not by process, but by timing.

## Example Sentences
1. 他靠炒股一攫千金，卻很快又輸光了。("He made a fortune overnight speculating on stocks, but soon lost it all again.")
2. The startup's founders dreamed of a 一攫千金 exit, not steady growth.
3. Lottery advertising sells the fantasy of *yī jué qiān jīn* — instant, effortless wealth.

## Sentiment
Mixed to cautionary. Descriptive rather than approving; often carries an undertone of skepticism about the durability or legitimacy of sudden wealth.
