## Literal Meaning
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- 攫 — to seize, to grab (no vault character page yet; attested at CC rank 3210)
- [千](../characters/千.md) — thousand
- [金](../characters/金%20(char).md) — gold

Literally: "seize a thousand pieces of gold in one grab."

## Extended Meaning
To get rich suddenly; to make a huge profit through a single, often risky or opportunistic act. 攫 means to snatch or grab with the hand — a physical, abrupt verb, unlike verbs of earning or cultivation. Paired with 千金, the image becomes intentionally jarring: enormous wealth obtained without proportional labor.

## Source and Origin
The expression emerges from late Ming and Qing vernacular literature rather than the classical canon. It belongs to the linguistic world of popular fiction, drama, and anecdotal prose, where commercial life, speculation, banditry, and sudden reversals of fortune were common narrative material. This reflects the social imagination of the late imperial period, when monetization, urban markets, and mobility made sudden enrichment both visible and morally ambiguous. Because the phrase did not originate in moral philosophy or historiography, it lacks the normative framing typical of earlier idioms — it describes a phenomenon rather than endorsing it, and the sudden gain often carries an undertone of instability, luck, or dubious means.

## Cultural Notes
Across the region, 一攫千金 is used to describe speculative success, windfalls, or get-rich-quick schemes. It appears frequently in economic commentary, journalism, and everyday speech. The tone is often skeptical or cautionary, though it can also be purely descriptive or ironic — it contrasts sharply with idioms that praise gradual cultivation or moral causality, and its persistence reflects a social reality that never disappeared: wealth sometimes comes not by process, but by timing.

## Example Sentences
1. 他靠炒股一攫千金，卻很快又輸光了。("He made a fortune overnight speculating on stocks, but soon lost it all again.")
2. The startup's founders dreamed of a 一攫千金 exit, not steady growth.
3. Lottery advertising sells the fantasy of *yī jué qiān jīn* — instant, effortless wealth.

## Sentiment
Mixed to cautionary. Descriptive rather than approving; often carries an undertone of skepticism about the durability or legitimacy of sudden wealth.
