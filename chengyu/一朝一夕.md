## Literal Meaning
- [一 (char)](characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [朝 (char)](characters/朝%20(char).md) — morning (here read *zhāo*, distinct from the character's "dynasty, court" sense)
- [一 (char)](characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [夕](characters/夕.md) — evening

Literally: "One morning and one evening."

## Extended Meaning
一朝一夕 names a very short period of time. It is most often used in negative constructions to indicate that something cannot be accomplished quickly — the common implication is "not in a day or two." Frequently used in formal or literary contexts, it carries a sober, measured tone, and appears constantly in moral, political, and educational discourse emphasizing gradual effort, as in the fixed collocation 非一朝一夕之功 ("not the achievement of a single morning and evening").

## Source and Origin
The pairing of 朝 and 夕 as a merism for a full day is classical. The fixed four-character form itself traces to the **《易經》**'s commentary on the Kun hexagram (坤卦), in the 文言傳:

> 「臣弒其君，子弒其父，非一朝一夕之故，其所由來者漸矣，由辨之不早辨也。」
> "That a minister murders his ruler, or a son murders his father, is not the cause of a single morning and evening — what brings it about accumulates gradually, from a failure to discern the trouble early."

The commentary uses the phrase to argue that catastrophic betrayals do not arise suddenly but accumulate from small, undiscerned beginnings — the same passage that supplies the related image "履霜，堅冰至" ("tread on frost, and solid ice will follow"). From this origin, 一朝一夕 became standardized as a chengyu expressing brevity or suddenness, almost always in the negative — "not accomplished in a morning and an evening."

## Cultural Notes
一朝一夕 is written identically in traditional and simplified Chinese, with no meaningful orthographic variants, and the same characters are used across Japanese, Korean hanja contexts, and Vietnamese classical usage. It is fully naturalized across the CJKV sphere, used in formal writing, moral instruction, and political commentary alike to emphasize that meaningful achievement — learning, reform, trust — requires sustained effort over time rather than a single quick action.

## Example Sentences
1. 「臣弒其君，子弒其父，非一朝一夕之故，其所由來者漸矣。」(《易經·坤卦·文言傳》)
2. 學問非一朝一夕之功。("Scholarship is not the achievement of a single morning and evening.")
3. 신뢰는 일조일석에 쌓이는 것이 아니다. ("Trust is not built in a single morning and evening.")
4. Reform of institutions this entrenched was never going to happen 一朝一夕 — it took a generation.

## Sentiment
Neutral in itself, often used in serious or reflective contexts. Emphasizes patience, duration, and the necessity of long-term effort.
