## Literal Meaning
- [血 (char)](characters/血%20(char).md) — blood
- [誓](characters/誓.md) — oath, vow
- [盟](characters/盟.md) — covenant, alliance
- [約](characters/約.md) — agreement, bond

Literally: "Blood oath, covenant agreement."

## Extended Meaning
血誓盟約 refers to a covenant ratified or sealed by blood — a binding, sacred agreement whose solemnity is marked by the shedding of blood rather than by words alone. In Christian usage it references both the Mosaic covenant at Sinai and the New Covenant instituted by Christ, and by extension is invoked in theological and liturgical discussion of covenant, atonement, and sacrament. Solemn and reverent; never used lightly or outside of a genuinely binding, sacred context.

## Source and Origin
The phrase draws together two covenant-ratification scenes. First, **Exodus 24:8**, at Sinai:

> "And Moses took the blood and threw it on the people and said, 'Behold the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you.'" (Ex 24:8, ESV)

Second, the words of institution at the Last Supper — **Matthew 26:28**, **Mark 14:24**, **Luke 22:20**, and **1 Corinthians 11:25**:

> "This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood." (Luke 22:20, ESV)

The Chinese Union Version (和合本) renders Exodus 24:8 as 「摩西將血灑在百姓身上，說：你看！這是立約的血，是耶和華按這一切話與你們立約的憑據。」 (checked on cnbible.com, which shows the same wording in the 現代標點和合本 with 「你看，」; the 聖經新譯本 reads 「…這是立約的血，這約是耶和華按著這一切話與你們立的。」). The Chinese Bible's own phrase is therefore 立約的血 ("the blood that establishes the covenant").

I found **no attestation of 血誓盟約 as a fixed idiom** in Chinese, Japanese or Korean sources. It appears to be a Dan'a'yo four-character coinage: 血誓 ("blood-oath") captures the solemnity of ratification through blood, and 盟約 ("covenant, sworn agreement") supplies the binding, mutual-commitment sense. The coinage links the Mosaic covenant, ratified by sacrificial blood, with the Last Supper, where Jesus calls the cup "the new covenant in my blood," a typological echo of the Exodus scene. The earlier description of it as an established form in the Bible-translation tradition was unsupported and has been removed.

## Standard Form
**血誓盟約** (traditional 約) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 血誓盟约 — simplified Chinese form (约)

## Variants
The attested Chinese phrase is 立約的血 (Chinese Union Version of Exodus 24:8). Three variants listed here earlier (盟約之血, 新血誓盟約, 血立之約) could not be found in any source and have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
血誓盟約 is a theological reference linking the Old and New Testaments, suited to sermons, systematic theology and discussion of the Eucharist, drawing the Sinai / Last Supper parallel. Its imagery also recalls older East Asian traditions of blood-oath alliance (歃血為盟, "smearing blood to swear a covenant"; I did not check that expression's earliest source in this pass), giving it a double intelligibility similar to other Biblical chengyu built on pre-existing native concepts (cf. [[骨肉相連]]). Because 血誓盟約 is not attested as a fixed idiom, no claim is made here about how common it is in any particular country.

## Example Sentences
1. 「摩西將血灑在百姓身上，說：『你看！這是立約的血，是耶和華按這一切話與你們立約的憑據。』」(Ex 24:8, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 主以血誓盟約，救贖其民。("The Lord by blood-oath covenant redeemed his people.")
3. 舊約以牲血立，新約以基督血誓盟約成全。("The Old Covenant was established by sacrificial blood; the New Covenant was fulfilled by the blood-oath covenant of Christ.")
4. The preacher reminded the congregation that theirs was a 血誓盟約 — a covenant that had cost blood, not merely words.

## Sentiment
Solemn, sacred, covenantal. Used to reference divine covenant, sacrificial atonement, or the sacramental mysteries of faith.
