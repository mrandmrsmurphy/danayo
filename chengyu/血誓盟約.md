## Literal Meaning
- [血 (char)](characters/血%20(char).md) — blood
- [誓](characters/誓.md) — oath, vow
- [盟](characters/盟.md) — covenant, alliance
- [約](characters/約.md) — agreement, bond

Literally: "Blood oath, covenant agreement."

## Extended Meaning
血誓盟約 refers to a covenant ratified or sealed by blood — a binding, sacred agreement whose solemnity is marked by the shedding of blood rather than by words alone. In Christian usage it references both the Mosaic covenant at Sinai and the New Covenant instituted by Christ, and by extension is invoked in theological and liturgical discussion of covenant, atonement, and sacrament. Solemn and reverent; never used lightly or outside of a genuinely binding, sacred context.

## Source and Origin
The phrase draws together two covenant-ratification scenes. First, **Exodus 24:8**, at Sinai:

> "And Moses took the blood and threw it on the people and said, 'Behold the blood of the covenant that the LORD has made with you.'" (Ex 24:8, ESV)

Second, the words of institution at the Last Supper — **Matthew 26:28**, **Mark 14:24**, **Luke 22:20**, and **1 Corinthians 11:25**:

> "This cup that is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood." (Luke 22:20, ESV)

血誓 ("blood-oath") captures the solemnity of covenant ratification through blood; 盟約 ("covenant, sworn agreement") supplies the binding, mutual-commitment sense. The four-character form thus links the Mosaic covenant, ratified by sacrificial animal blood, with Christ's institution of the Eucharist, where he declares the cup to be "my blood of the covenant" — a deliberate typological echo of the Exodus scene. The idiom resonates with pre-existing East Asian oath-taking traditions (blood oaths sealed alliances long before Christian contact) while carrying distinctly Biblical sacrificial theology.

## Standard Form
**血誓盟約** (traditional 約) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 血誓盟约 — simplified Chinese form (约)

## Variants
- 盟約之血 — reversed emphasis ("the blood of the covenant")
- 新血誓盟約 — specifying the New Covenant explicitly
- 血立之約 — emphasizing ratification ("the covenant established by blood")

## Cultural Notes
血誓盟約 functions in the CJKV Christian sphere as a direct theological reference linking the Old and New Testaments — invoked in sermons, systematic theology, and Eucharistic liturgy to draw the Sinai/Last-Supper typological parallel. Its imagery also resonates independently with older East Asian traditions of blood-oath alliance (歃血為盟, "smearing blood to swear a covenant"), giving it a double intelligibility similar to other Biblical chengyu built on pre-existing native concepts (cf. [[骨肉相連]]). Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese usage remains close to the source text, occurring mainly in explicitly religious and theological contexts.

## Example Sentences
1. 「摩西將血灑在百姓身上，說：『你看！這是立約的血，是耶和華按這一切話與你們立約的憑據。』」(Ex 24:8, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 主以血誓盟約，救贖其民。("The Lord by blood-oath covenant redeemed his people.")
3. 舊約以牲血立，新約以基督血誓盟約成全。("The Old Covenant was established by sacrificial blood; the New Covenant was fulfilled by the blood-oath covenant of Christ.")
4. The preacher reminded the congregation that theirs was a 血誓盟約 — a covenant that had cost blood, not merely words.

## Sentiment
Solemn, sacred, covenantal. Used to reference divine covenant, sacrificial atonement, or the sacramental mysteries of faith.
