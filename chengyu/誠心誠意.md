## Literal Meaning
- [誠](../characters/誠.md) — sincere
- [心](../characters/心%20(char).md) — heart
- [誠](../characters/誠.md) — sincere
- [意](../characters/意.md) — intention

Literally: "sincere heart, sincere intention" — the same "sincere" gloss reduplicated across both nouns for emphasis.

## Extended Meaning
With complete sincerity; wholeheartedly; in utmost good faith. Used to describe genuine goodwill, earnest effort, or a wholehearted apology or commitment — the emotional and intentional sincerity behind an action, not merely its outward correctness. Positive in every register; rarely if ever used ironically.

## Source and Origin
The idiom is rooted in Confucian moral vocabulary, specifically the pairing of 誠 ("sincerity") and 意 ("intention") from the "eight items" (八條目) of the *Great Learning* (大學, a chapter of the *Book of Rites*, 禮記):

**《禮記·大學》**:
> "古之欲明明德於天下者，先治其國；欲治其國者，先齊其家；欲齊其家者，先修其身；欲修其身者，先正其心；欲正其心者，先誠其意；欲誠其意者，先致其知；致知在格物。"
> ("Those of antiquity who wished to make bright virtue shine forth in the world first governed their states well; wishing to govern their states well, they first brought order to their households; ... wishing to rectify their minds, they first made their intentions sincere...")

This passage establishes [[誠意]] ("making one's intentions sincere") as a foundational step in the classical program of self-cultivation, directly preceding 正心 ("rectifying the mind"). The exact four-character form 誠心誠意 does not appear verbatim in the *Great Learning* itself — [[誠心]] as a paired two-character term is a separate, independently attested classical collocation ("a sincere heart") — and the fixed idiom crystallizes later, combining 誠心 and 誠意 into a single reduplicated expression of total sincerity in both heart and intent.

## Pronunciations

- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 성심성의 (seongsim-seongui)
- **Vietnamese (Hán–Việt):** thành tâm thành ý

## Cultural Notes
- In Chinese, 誠心誠意 is common in both formal and everyday registers, describing genuine goodwill and wholehearted effort — from apologies to gift-giving to service.
- In Japanese, 誠心誠意 appears frequently in formal speech, apologies, and customer-service contexts (誠心誠意対応させていただきます, "we will respond with the utmost sincerity"), carrying a somewhat more ceremonial weight than in everyday Mandarin usage.
- In Korean, 성심성의 (often as 성심성의껏, "to the utmost of one's sincerity") conveys devoted, wholehearted effort, especially in fulfilling a duty or obligation.

## Example Sentences
1. 他誠心誠意地向大家道歉。("He apologized to everyone with complete sincerity.")
2. 誠心誠意対応させていただきます。("We will respond with the utmost sincerity.")
3. She rebuilt the trust between them *chéngxīnchéngyì* — one sincere gesture at a time.

## Sentiment
Strongly positive; earnest and warm in tone, without irony. Commonly formulaic in apologies, service contexts, and expressions of devotion.
