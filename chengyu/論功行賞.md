## Literal Meaning
- [論](characters/論.md) — to discuss, assess
- [功](characters/功.md) — merit, achievement
- [行](characters/行%20(char).md) — to carry out
- [賞](characters/賞%20(char).md) — reward

Literally: "Discuss merit, carry out reward."

## Extended Meaning
論功行賞 describes evaluating achievements and distributing rewards accordingly — rewarding merit justly. It is a standard idiom in historical and official contexts, describing the fair distribution of honors, offices, or material rewards after success in war, politics, or collective enterprise, and stands for a political ethic of meritocracy and justice: rewarding accomplishment rather than birth or favoritism.

## Source and Origin
Taiwan's Ministry of Education *Dictionary of Chinese Idioms* gives as the source (典源) the **《隋書·卷四一·蘇威傳》** (*Book of Sui*, volume 41, biography of Su Wei):

> 「及大業末年，尤多征役，至於論功行賞，威每承望風旨，輒寢其事。」
> (By the end of the Daye era there were many more levies and campaigns, and when it came to discussing merit and awarding rewards, Su Wei always took his cue from the emperor's wishes and set the matter aside.)

The same entry lists a later documented use in the Qing play 《長生殿》 (洪昇), scene 26, and the variants 論功封賞 and 論功行封. A search summary of idiom references also mentions earlier uses, in the 《史記·蕭相國世家》 account of Liu Bang rewarding his officials and in 《三國志·吳書·顧譚傳》; I did not check those. I checked the quotation against the Ministry's entry, not the text of the *Book of Sui*, and I did not establish which use is earliest.

論功 means to assess contributions (literally "discuss merit"), and 行賞 means to carry out rewards. Together they express a ethic of rewarding accomplishment rather than birth or favoritism (the page's gloss).

**Corrected.** The earlier version of this page gave the 《晉書》 as the source and quoted 「於是論功行封，賜公卿以下帛各有差」 as "the account of rewards following the conquest of Wu." I could not verify that quotation, and the source the Ministry documents is the 《隋書》, so `origin` has changed and the quotation is replaced. Example 1 is replaced accordingly.

## Standard Form
**論功行賞** (traditional) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 论功行赏 — simplified Chinese form

## Variants
- 論功封賞 and 論功行封 — variant forms listed in the Ministry's entry ("to discuss merit and bestow rewards / enfeoffments")
- 功過相抵 — "to weigh merit and fault" (a related but distinct idea, balancing achievement against wrongdoing rather than simply rewarding merit)

## Cultural Notes
In Chinese usage, 論功行賞 remains a standard idiom in historical and official contexts. Japanese 論功行賞 (ronkō kōshō) retains exactly this bureaucratic sense and is still used in politics, business, and military history to mean "rewarding merit." Korean 론공행상/논공행상 and Vietnamese luận công hành thưởng carry the same sense; I did not check how common they are or the claim about Confucian royal proclamations.

In Confucian ethics, the idiom reflects fairness and gratitude — those who serve the state should be duly recognized, maintaining moral order. In Legalist thought, it expresses the pragmatic principle that rewards and punishments should follow measurable results (賞罰分明). A loose Biblical parallel in tone, though not in scope, is "The laborer is worthy of his wages" (Luke 10:7) — both share a justice-based logic of reward tied to contribution.

## Example Sentences
1. 「至於論功行賞，威每承望風旨，輒寢其事。」(《隋書·蘇威傳》)
2. 戰後，朝廷論功行賞，賜爵有差。("After the battle, the court discussed merits and awarded titles accordingly.")
3. 전쟁이 끝난 뒤 왕은 논공행상을 실시했다. ("After the war, the king carried out merit-based rewards.")
4. When the project wrapped, the manager made sure to 論功行賞 — bonuses and promotions matched exactly who had actually done the work.

## Sentiment
Just, orderly, appreciative. Stands for recognizing and rewarding true merit, each according to their deeds — a core principle of fair governance and management.
