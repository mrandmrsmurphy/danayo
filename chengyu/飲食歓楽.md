## Literal Meaning
- [飲 (char)](../characters/飲%20(char).md) — to drink
- [食 (char)](../characters/食%20(char).md) — to eat
- [歓](../characters/歓.md) — joy, rejoicing
- [楽](../characters/楽.md) — pleasure, delight

Literally: "drink, eat, rejoice, delight" — comparable to the English expression "eat, drink, and be merry."

## Extended Meaning
To indulge in food and pleasure without regard to higher or future concerns. It often conveys a spirit of short-sighted revelry, especially when set against the certainty of mortality or coming judgment.

## Source and Origin
The phrase reflects biblical language found in several places:

> "And behold joy and gladness, slaying oxen, and killing sheep, eating flesh, and drinking wine: let us eat and drink; for to morrow we shall die." (Isaiah 22:13, KJV)

> "...let us eat and drink; for to morrow we die." (1 Corinthians 15:32, KJV, quoting the same sentiment)

> "And I will say to my soul, Soul, thou hast much goods laid up for many years; take thine ease, eat, drink, and be merry." (Luke 12:19, KJV — the Parable of the Rich Fool)

## Cultural Notes
In Christian discourse this saying is rarely neutral. In the Greco-Roman world, similar sentiments were associated with Epicurean resignation in the face of death; the biblical usage is typically ironic or critical, highlighting spiritual blindness rather than endorsing the sentiment. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere the idiom functions as a compact moral warning, standing in tension with ascetic strands of Christian tradition while also contrasting with legitimate feasting traditions such as Passover or the Messianic banquet motif — the phrase carries an implicit theological contrast between temporal indulgence and eschatological hope. Because the wording overlaps with ordinary Chinese compounds for food and joy, it may superficially appear benign in broader CJK contexts; its biblical resonance, however, shifts the tone toward admonition rather than celebration.

## Example Sentences
1. 世人但求飲食歓楽，不念来世之審判。("Many in the world seek only to eat, drink, and rejoice, without regard for the judgment to come.")
2. 「你吃喝快樂吧，因為明天要死了。」(以賽亞書 22:13，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. The parable of the rich fool warns against a life of mere 飲食歓楽, unprepared for eternity.
4. 富人積財自恃，飲食歓楽，卻不知死期將至。("The rich man trusted in his wealth, ate, drank, and made merry, unaware that his death was near.")

## Sentiment
Cautionary, morally critical, often tinged with irony.
