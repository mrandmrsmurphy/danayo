## Literal Meaning
- [戦](../characters/戦.md) — to tremble; battle (appears twice)
- [恐](../characters/恐.md) — fear, dread (appears twice)

Literally: "Trembling, trembling, fearful and fearful" — shaking with fear and anxiety.

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary glosses 戰戰兢兢 (Chinese, ideophonic) "trembling with fear; apprehensive and careful; fearful and cautious", and gives the same gloss for the Japanese adjective 戦々恐々.

## Source and Origin
From the Classic of Poetry, 《詩經·小雅·小旻》 (Lesser Elegantiae: Xiǎo Mǐn), whose last stanza ends:

> 不敢暴虎，不敢馮河。人知其一，莫知其他。戰戰兢兢，如臨深淵，如履薄冰。
> "We dare not fight a tiger with bare hands, nor wade a river on foot; people know only the one danger and not the others. Trembling, trembling, fearfully, fearfully, as if standing at the edge of a deep abyss, as if treading on thin ice."

The Mao preface reads the poem as a grandee's satire of King You of Zhou (《毛詩序》: 「《小旻》，大夫刺幽王也」), for heeding bad counsel, and the last stanza as the speaker's own cautious stance in a dangerous time. The 毛傳 glosses the pair: 「戰戰，恐也；兢兢，戒也」, "戰戰 is fear, 兢兢 is caution", and 「臨淵恐墜也，履冰恐陷也」, "at the edge of an abyss one fears to fall, on ice one fears to break through".

The lines are the best-known use of the pair outside the Odes in 《論語·泰伯》 (8.3), where the dying Zengzi calls his disciples: 「啟予足！啟予手！詩云：『戰戰兢兢，如臨深淵，如履薄冰。』而今而後，吾知免夫！小子！」, "Uncover my feet! Uncover my hands! The Odes say, 'trembling, trembling, as if at the edge of an abyss, as if treading on thin ice.' From now on I know I am spared, my children!"

## Standard Form
The classical Chinese form is **戰戰兢兢** (zhàn zhàn jīng jīng). Dan'a'yo substitutes:
- 戦 for 戰 (Japanese shinjitai simplification; aliased in [[戦]])
- 恐 for 兢 (semantic substitution; see [[恐恐]] "fearfully, with trepidation, alias: 兢兢")

## Cultural Notes
English Wiktionary has entries for 戰戰兢兢 (simplified 战战兢兢) and for the Japanese 戦々恐々; its 戰戰兢兢 entry quotes a 《漢書》 edict: 「朕承宗廟之重，戰戰兢兢，懼失天心」. It has no entry for the Dan'a'yo form 戦戦恐恐, which substitutes 恐 for 兢.
