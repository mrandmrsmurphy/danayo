## Literal Meaning
- [盛](../characters/盛.md) — flourish, prosper, be at the height of power
- [者](../characters/者.md) — one who; those who (nominalizer)
- [必 (char)](../characters/必%20(char).md) — certainly, inevitably, without exception
- [衰](../characters/衰.md) — decline, wane, diminish

Literally: "Those who flourish will certainly decline" — prosperity is not a stable state but a peak from which descent is inevitable.

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary glosses the Chinese idiom (Buddhism) "everything prosperous will eventually decline", and the Japanese proverb (Buddhism) with the same gloss. Its Chinese entry gives the full sutra line as 「盛者必衰，實者必虛」, "Everything prosperous will eventually decline. Everything full will eventually become empty."

## Source and Origin
The principle of impermanence (諸行無常, *sabbe saṅkhārā anicca*) is foundational Buddhist teaching. The specific wording 盛者必衰 appears in the 《仁王護國般若波羅蜜經》 (*Renwang Jing*, Sutra for Humane Kings), in the chapter 護國品 (juan 2). A Dharma master addresses King Pumin (普明王), who is about to be sacrificed by King Banzu, with a verse on the impermanence of kingdoms:

> 有本自無，因緣成諸，盛者必衰，實者必虛。
> 眾生蠢蠢，常如幻居，聲響俱空，國土亦如。
> "What exists was originally non-existent, and arises from conditions; what flourishes must decline, what is full must become empty. Beings, swarming, live as in an illusion; sound and echo are both empty, and so are kingdoms."

The phrase achieved its most enduring literary expression in the opening of **《平家物語》** (The Tale of the Heike, Chapter 1.1, as Wiktionary quotes it):

> 「祇園精舎の鐘の声、諸行無常の響きあり。沙羅双樹の花の色、盛者必衰の理をあらわす。おごれる人も久しからず、ただ春の夜の夢のごとし。」
>
> "The sound of the Gion Shōja bells echoes the impermanence of all things. The colour of the sāla flowers reveals the truth that the prosperous must decline. The proud do not endure; they are like a dream on a spring night."

## Cultural Notes
English Wiktionary traces the Chinese idiom to the 《仁王經》 (Humane King Sutra) and gives the Japanese proverb the readings じょうしゃひっすい, しょうじゃひっすい and しょうしゃひっすい, quoting the 《平家物語》 opening. It lists 諸行無常 and 会者定離 under See also.
