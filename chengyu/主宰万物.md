## Literal Meaning
- [主宰](../words/主宰.md) — lord, master; to govern, rule
- [万物](../words/万物.md) — the ten thousand things; all created entities

Literally: "lord and governor of all things."

## Extended Meaning
Affirms God's absolute sovereignty over creation — comprehensive authority, providential governance, and ultimate ownership of all that exists. It functions both doxologically (as an act of worship and confession) and doctrinally (as a statement about the nature of God's rule over history, nature, and human affairs).

## Source and Origin
主宰万物 is a Dan'a'yo-coined theological maxim rather than a direct quotation of a single verse; it summarizes multiple biblical affirmations of divine sovereignty:

- **Genesis 1:1** — God as Creator of heaven and earth.
- **Psalm 24:1** — "The earth is the LORD's, and the fulness thereof; the world, and they that dwell therein."
- **Daniel 4:35** — "...he doeth according to his will in the army of heaven, and among the inhabitants of the earth: and none can stay his hand..."
- **Revelation 4:11** — "Thou art worthy, O Lord, to receive glory and honour and power: for thou hast created all things..."

Traditional Chinese Bible translations often render similar ideas with expressions like 萬有之主 ("Lord of all things") or 掌管萬有 ("governing all things"). The Dan'a'yo form deliberately employs 宰 ("to govern, to administer") rather than a term of mere possession, stressing active, ongoing governance over creation rather than static ownership.

## Cultural Notes
The pairing 主宰 carries long-standing philosophical resonance in classical East Asian discourse, where it can denote an ultimate authority or controlling principle (as in debates over 天 or 道 as cosmic "master"). In Christian theology, by contrast, this sovereignty is personal and covenantal — the sovereign is a God who speaks, judges, and redeems — not an impersonal fate or abstract principle. 万物, meanwhile, signals totality much like the Hebrew merism "heaven and earth," expressing universal scope rather than a literal count. Within Dan'a'yo theology, 主宰万物 serves as a foundational confession bearing on questions of providence, predestination, and human freedom: creation, history, and destiny are not autonomous but held under a single sovereign will.

## Example Sentences
1. 信者承認神為主宰万物，不獨人心，亦包括歴史與自然。("The faithful confess God as Lord of all things, not only of hearts, but of history and nature.")
2. 「地和其中所充滿的，世界和住在其間的，都屬耶和華。」(詩篇 24:1，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. To call God 主宰万物 is to say that nothing in heaven or on earth lies outside his governance.
4. 神主宰万物，凡事皆按祂旨意而行。("God governs all things; everything unfolds according to his will.")

## Sentiment
Majestic, confessional, comprehensive. Proclaims divine sovereignty with reverent assurance.
