## Literal Meaning
- [殺 (char)](characters/殺%20(char).md) — to kill, murder
- [姦](characters/姦.md) — adultery
- [窃](characters/窃.md) — to steal
- [偽 (char)](characters/偽%20(char).md) — falsehood, lying

Literally: "Murder, adultery, theft, lying."

## Extended Meaning
殺姦窃偽 compresses four consecutive commandments from the Decalogue into a single memorable phrase: prohibitions against murder, adultery, theft, and lying, representing fundamental violations of human dignity and social order. These prohibitions protect life, marriage, property, and truth — the foundational structures of a just society — and the compression demonstrates how Biblical law addresses both vertical obligations (to God) and horizontal obligations (to neighbor). Used to reference core moral law, condemn fundamental wrongdoing, or establish the baseline of righteous conduct.

## Source and Origin
**Exodus 20:13–16** and **Deuteronomy 5:17–20**, the sixth through ninth of the Ten Commandments:

> "You shall not murder. You shall not commit adultery. You shall not steal. You shall not bear false witness against your neighbor." (Ex 20:13–16, ESV)

殺 (murder/kill), 姦 (adultery/sexual immorality), 窃 (theft/stealing), and 偽 (falsehood/lying) render the four commandments in their original order, each name reduced to a single character — a remarkably compact rendering of prohibitions that are, notably, nearly universal across human ethical systems regardless of religious tradition.

## Standard Form
**殺姦窃偽** (Japanese shinjitai 殺/窃) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 殺姦竊偽 — traditional Chinese form (竊)
- 杀奸窃伪 — simplified Chinese form (杀奸伪)

## Variants
- 勿殺勿姦勿窃勿偽 — an imperative form making each prohibition explicit ("do not kill, do not commit adultery, do not steal, do not lie")
- 殺姦窃偽証 — including the ninth commandment's "false witness" more explicitly
- 四誡禁令 — a descriptive form ("the four commandments' prohibitions")

## Cultural Notes
殺姦窃偽 functions across the CJKV Christian sphere as a direct scriptural citation summarizing the "second table" of the Decalogue — the commandments governing obligations to one's neighbor rather than to God directly. It is invoked in catechesis, sermons, and ethical writing as compact shorthand for the moral baseline shared, in substance if not in exact wording, by most systems of law and ethics.

## Example Sentences
1. 「不可殺人。不可姦淫。不可偷盜。不可作假見證陷害人。」(Ex 20:13–16, 和合本 Chinese Union Version)
2. 神禁殺姦窃偽，立人倫之基。("God forbids murder, adultery, theft, and lying, establishing the foundation of human ethics.")
3. 守法者避殺姦窃偽，行義於世。("Those who keep the law avoid murder, adultery, theft, and lying, practicing righteousness in the world.")
4. Every legal code ever written, in one form or another, comes back to 殺姦窃偽 — the same four lines humanity keeps having to draw.
