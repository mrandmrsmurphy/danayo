## Literal Meaning
- [内](../characters/内.md) — inside
- [柔 (char)](../characters/柔%20(char).md) — soft, tender
- [外](../characters/外.md) — outside
- [剛](../characters/剛.md) — rigid, firm

Literally: "Soft within, firm without."

## Extended Meaning
Describes a person who is gentle, kind-hearted, or emotionally tender at their core, while presenting a strong, resolute, or stern exterior. In modern usage it is a positive character trait — the outward firmness is seen as discipline or strength of will layered over genuine inner gentleness, often praised in leaders, parents, or anyone who must project authority while remaining compassionate underneath.

## Source and Origin
**《易經·否卦》** (the Tuàn commentary on Hexagram 12, 否 "Obstruction"):
> 否之匪人，不利君子貞。大往小來，則是天地不交，而萬物不通也；上下不交，而天下無邦也。內陰而外陽，內柔而外剛，內小人而外君子。小人道長，君子道消也。
> (Pi [Obstruction] is not the way for a person; it does not favor the constancy of the noble. The great departs and the small arrives — heaven and earth do not intercommunicate, and the myriad things do not connect; high and low do not intercommunicate, and the world has no true state. Yin within and yang without; soft within and firm without; petty people within and noble persons without. The way of the petty grows, and the way of the noble diminishes.)

The phrase 內柔而外剛 already appears verbatim in this passage, but in a strikingly different sense from its modern one: here it is part of a description of the inauspicious Pi hexagram, where the configuration of "soft within, firm without" (alongside "yin within, yang without" and "petty people within, noble persons without") symbolizes a state of cosmic and political stagnation — heaven and earth failing to communicate, the small displacing the great. The phrase was not originally a compliment. Its modern sense — praising a person as gentle-hearted yet outwardly resolute — is a later, positive reinterpretation that detaches the phrase entirely from its original hexagram context.

## Standard Form
**内柔外剛** (Dan'a'yo standard form, mixing shinjitai 内 with traditional 剛)
- 內柔外剛 — fully traditional form
- 内柔外刚 — fully simplified form
- 外柔內剛 — a common word-order variant ("firm within, soft without" reversed to "soft outside, firm within"), carrying the same core meaning but reordering which quality is presented outwardly first; both orders are current and interchangeable in practice

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** nèi róu wài gāng
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** noi6 jau4 ngoi6 gong1
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** ないじゅうがいごう
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 내유외강 (nae-yu-oe-gang)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** nội nhu ngoại cương

Japanese ないじゅうがいごう selects each character's less-common on'yomi rather than the more frequent alternate (内 ナイ over ダイ; 柔 ジュウ over ニュウ) — both readings are independently real and attested for each character (cf. 柔道 jūdō "judo," which shares 柔's じゅう reading), and this idiom simply lexicalizes that pairing.

## Cultural Notes
A genuinely positive character trait in all five languages — describing someone as 内柔外剛 (or its reverse-order variant) is a compliment, praising the coexistence of firmness and compassion rather than either quality alone. Common in descriptions of respected leaders, parents, and mentors: someone who must project strength and discipline outwardly but whose underlying motivation is understood to be care rather than harshness. This is a case where the idiom's modern meaning stands in real tension with its classical source — few speakers using it today are aware that the same phrase, in its original context, described a hexagram of political and cosmic breakdown.

## Example Sentences
1. 他看起來嚴厲，其實內柔外剛，對待下屬其實非常體貼。
2. Her leadership style was _nèi róu wài gāng_ — firm decisions delivered with real warmth.
3. 彼女は内柔外剛の性格で、部下から慕われている。
4. He is a _nội nhu ngoại cương_ father — strict in rules, gentle in heart.

## Sentiment
Positive; praises a balance of outward strength/discipline with inward gentleness or compassion. Contrasts favorably with either pure harshness or pure softness alone.
