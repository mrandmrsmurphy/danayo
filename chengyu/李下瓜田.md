## Literal Meaning
- [李](../characters/李.md) — plum tree
- [下 (char)](../characters/下%20(char).md) — beneath, under
- [瓜](../characters/瓜.md) — melon, cucumber
- [田](../characters/田.md) — field, cultivated land

Literally: "Under a plum tree; in a melon field" — two locations where an innocent action looks like theft.

The full proverb from which this four-character form condenses is:

> 「瓜田不納履，李下不整冠。」
> "In a melon field, do not stop to adjust your sandals; under a plum tree, do not raise your hands to straighten your hat."

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary's Chinese entry for the standard form 瓜田李下 glosses it "a position that invites suspicion; suspicious circumstances or surroundings" (literally "under a plum tree or in a melon patch"), and gives the longer proverb as its origin. Its entry for 李下瓜田 is a synonym pointer. Its Japanese entry for 李下瓜田 (りかかでん) is "be careful not to invite suspicion".

## Source and Origin
The saying comes from the anonymous old yuefu lyric **《君子行》** (The Way of the Gentleman), filed as a 古辭 (old lyric) in the **《樂府詩集》** (Collection of Yuefu Poetry, compiled by 郭茂倩 in the Song dynasty, juan 32). Its date is not fixed; "Han" is the traditional guess for such 古辭, and I cannot confirm it. The text, as printed in the 《樂府詩集》:

> 君子防未然，不處嫌疑間。
> 瓜田不納履，李下不正冠。
> 嫂叔不親授，長幼不比肩。
> 勞謙得其柄，和光甚獨難。
> 周公下白屋，吐哺不及餐。
> 一沐三握髮，後世稱聖賢。
>
> "The gentleman forestalls what has not yet happened; he does not place himself in positions of doubt.
> In a melon field do not stop to put on your shoes; under a plum tree do not straighten your hat.
> Brother-in-law and sister-in-law do not hand things directly to each other; elder and younger do not walk shoulder to shoulder.
> Humility and diligence win the seat of power, yet to blend one's light with the crowd is hard indeed.
> The Duke of Zhou went down into the humble hut and spat out his food before he had finished a meal;
> three times in one washing he gripped his hair, and so later ages call him a sage."

Wiktionary's etymology gives the proverb as 「瓜田不納履，李下不整冠」, with 整冠 rather than 正冠. The poem's other lines are not checked here.

The four-character compound condensed the poem's central image: 瓜田李下 in Chinese (melon field + plum-tree-beneath) or 李下瓜田 in the Japanese arrangement used in this vault (plum-tree-beneath + melon field).

## Standard Form
Dan'a'yo uses **李下瓜田**, following the Japanese yojijukugo convention (李下 comes first). The standard Chinese form is **瓜田李下** (aliased in this vault).

## Sentiment
Cautionary; it advises against putting oneself where one will be suspected.
