## Literal Meaning
- [天 (char)](characters/天%20(char).md) — heaven, sky
- [圓 (char)](characters/圓%20(char).md) — round, circular
- [地 (char)](characters/地%20(char).md) — earth, ground
- [方](characters/方.md) — square

Literally: "Heaven round, Earth square."

## Extended Meaning
天圓地方 names the classical Chinese conception of the cosmos: Heaven above is round and encompassing, Earth below is square and stable, symbolizing cosmic order, harmony, and hierarchy. Heaven (天) represents the infinite, transcendent, cyclical, and divine; Earth (地) represents the finite, stable, measured, and human — their relationship embodies harmony through complementarity, roundness for movement and continuity, squareness for order and stability. In ritual and imperial symbolism, the Emperor (天子, "Son of Heaven") mediated between round Heaven and square Earth, reflected architecturally in Beijing's circular Temple of Heaven (天壇) and square Temple of Earth (地壇). The formula expresses a worldview where cosmic order mirrors moral order: harmony between Heaven and Earth implies harmony in human society, achieved through virtue and ritual. Even in modern usage, 天圓地方 can be invoked metaphorically for "the structure of the world" or "the natural order of things."

## Source and Origin
The four characters 天圓地方 occur together in the **《淮南子·天文訓》** (Huainanzi, "Treatise on Astronomy"), checked against the Wikisource text:

> 「天道曰圓，地道曰方。方者主幽，圓者主明。」
> 「天圓地方，道在中央。日為德，月為刑。」
> ("The way of Heaven is called round, the way of Earth is called square. The square rules the dim, the round rules the bright." / "Heaven round, Earth square, the Way lies in the middle. The sun is virtue, the moon is punishment.")

The line is therefore not 「天圓而地方」, the wording this page gave for the *Huainanzi*; that string does not occur in the 天文訓.

An earlier discussion of the same idea is in the **《大戴禮記·曾子天圓》**, which I also checked on Wikisource. There a questioner asks whether it is true that "heaven is round and earth is square" (天圓而地方者，誠有之乎), and Zengzi answers that 天道曰圓，地道曰方, treating roundness and squareness as the *principles* of Heaven and Earth, linked with brightness (圓曰明) and obscurity (方曰幽), rather than as literal shapes. So the idiom has a metaphysical reading in the sources, not only a literal cosmographic one.

The vivid image 「天圓如張蓋，地方如棋局」 ("Heaven is round like a stretched canopy; Earth is square like a game board") is attributed in my search results to the **《晉書·天文志》**, which reports it as the saying of the 周髀 school (周髀家云), the "gaitian" or canopy-heaven theory. The earlier version of this page gave it as a quotation from the 《周髀算經》 itself; I could not load that text, and the sources I found say the 《周髀算經》 records a later, quantified form of the theory, so the attribution has been moved to the 晉書 as reported there, unverified against the text. I did not establish when 天圓地方 first appears as a fixed four-character phrase, or whether the 《淮南子》 passage is its earliest use.

Later tradition treated the formula as a standard cosmological doctrine, symbolizing the dual order of Heaven (天) and Earth (地) — the overarching spiritual and the grounded material.

## Standard Form
**天圓地方** (traditional 圓) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 天圆地方 — simplified Chinese form (圆)

## Variants
- 天圓如張蓋，地方如棋局 — the fuller image, "Heaven round like a stretched canopy, Earth square like a game board" (as reported from the 《晉書·天文志》 above)
- 上圓下方 — "round above, square below"; an existing related phrase that I did not check against a source

A variant 天圓如蓋，地方如棋 listed here earlier could not be found in any source and has been removed.

## Cultural Notes
天圓地方 is reflected in Chinese urban planning (square city layouts aligned to the cardinal directions) and in ritual vessels (round heaven-offering vessels paired with square earth-offering altars), and remains a common metaphor for cosmic harmony or the encompassing nature of Heaven's mandate.

The idiom invites a cross-cultural comparison (an interpretation, not something I checked here): Greek cosmology placed round celestial spheres around an enclosed Earth, and the ancient Hebrew worldview is often described as a dome of heaven over a flat Earth, so a "round Heaven, flat or square Earth" picture recurs across civilizations. The distinguishing feature of the Chinese formulation is its emphasis on balance and complementary order rather than creation ex nihilo.

## Example Sentences
1. 「天圓地方，道在中央。」(《淮南子·天文訓》)
2. 古人認為天圓地方，萬物由此生成。("The ancients believed Heaven was round and Earth square, and from this all things were generated.")
3. 고대 중국인은 천원지방의 우주관을 가졌다. ("The ancient Chinese held a worldview of Heaven round and Earth square.")
4. The old cosmology of 天圓地方 still echoes in the architecture of Beijing's ritual temples, circular altars for Heaven set against square ones for Earth.

## Sentiment
Reverent, cosmological, harmonious. A succinct formula of cosmic geometry and moral harmony — the ancient Chinese vision of the universe, echoed in art, ritual, and governance.
