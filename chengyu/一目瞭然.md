## Literal Meaning
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [目](../characters/目%20(char).md) — eye, glance
- [瞭](../characters/瞭.md) — clear, distinct
- [然](../characters/然%20(char).md) — (adjectival/adverbial suffix, "-ly, appearing as")

Literally: "clear at a single glance."

## Extended Meaning
Immediately obvious; easily understood; perfectly clear without need for further explanation. Often used to describe arguments, charts, organization, logic, or visible facts — anything whose structure or meaning is grasped the instant it's seen.

## Source and Origin
Taiwan's Ministry of Education *Dictionary of Chinese Idioms* gives as the source (典源) a passage of the Song dynasty *Zhuzi yulei* (《朱子語類》, the recorded sayings of Zhu Xi), chapter 137, "戰國漢唐諸子":

**《朱子語類·卷一三七·戰國漢唐諸子》**:
> "見得道理透後，從高視下，一目瞭然。今要去揣摩，不得。"
> (Once you see the principle through, looking down from a height, it is clear at a glance. Trying to guess at it now will not do.)

The same entry gives a modern example from **《孽海花》** chapter 19: "卻說這中堂正對著那圍場，四扇大窗洞開，場上的事，一目瞭然。" ("The central hall faced the enclosure squarely, with four great windows thrown open, so everything happening on the field was clear at a glance.")

The first four-character use I can document is therefore Zhu Xi's. I checked the Ministry's entry; I did not retrieve the text of the *Zhuzi yulei* itself. 瞭然 ("clear") is the older word on which the idiom is built.

**Corrections.** The earlier version of this page quoted three lines that I could not verify and have removed. A line attributed to 《世說新語·言語》 ("王夷甫神識清澄，瞭然自得") is not in that chapter, which I searched for 瞭然 without finding any. The 《朱子語類》 quotation given before was not the passage the Ministry cites. A line attributed to 《醒世恆言》 chapter 23 could not be found. The claim that the idiom crystallized in Ming–Qing vernacular fiction was unsupported.

## Standard Form
**一目瞭然**
- 一目了然 — common simplified form (瞭 → 了); dominant in Mainland China
- Both forms are current; Taiwan and Japan tend to keep 瞭.

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** yī mù liǎo rán
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** jat1 muk6 liu4 jin4
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** いちもくりょうぜん
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 일목료연 (il-mok-ryo-yeon)
- **Vietnamese (Hán–Việt):** nhất mục liễu nhiên

## Cultural Notes
- Common in academic, administrative, and explanatory contexts.
- Frequently used to praise clear writing, clean design, or logical structure.
- In Korean, the idiom is very common and often used in formal writing. South Korean orthography spells it 일목요연; the Sino-Korean reading kept here follows the character 瞭 (료), the North Korean form 일목료연.
- In modern Chinese, the simplified character 了 has largely replaced 瞭 in everyday usage — see Standard Form above.

## Example Sentences
1. 其理甚明，一目瞭然。("The principle is entirely clear, obvious at a glance.")
2. The diagram makes the whole process *yī mù liǎo rán*.
3. His argument was structured so clearly that the conclusion was obvious at a glance.

## Sentiment
Strongly positive. Conveys clarity, order, and intellectual precision.
