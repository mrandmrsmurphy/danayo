## Literal Meaning
- [孝 (char)](../characters/孝%20(char).md) — filial devotion
- [親](../characters/親.md) — parent
- [天 (char)](../characters/天%20(char).md) — heaven
- [賜](../characters/賜.md) — to grant, bestow

Literally: "honor parents, heaven bestows [blessing]."

## Extended Meaning
A reformulation of the Fifth Commandment that emphasizes divine blessing rather than territorial inheritance. It connects filial obedience with promised life and favor from God, framing obedience as the pathway to divinely bestowed blessing rather than mere legal obligation.

## Source and Origin
The primary source is **Exodus 20:12** (and its restatement in **Deuteronomy 5:16**):

> "Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee." (KJV)

The New Testament expands the promise's scope in **Ephesians 6:2–3**:

> "Honour thy father and mother; (which is the first commandment with promise;) That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth." (KJV)

The Apostle Paul universalizes the promise beyond the specific land of Canaan promised to Israel. Traditional Chinese Bible translations render the Exodus promise in territorial terms ("在耶和華你神所賜你的地上得以長久"), while the Ephesians restatement broadens it to simply "在世長壽" ("long life in the world"). 孝親天賜 reflects this widening: the blessing is framed not as geographic possession specifically but as a divine gift of enduring life more generally, shifting emphasis from "land" to "heaven-bestowed" while preserving continuity with the original commandment — a maxim that tracks the wider biblical trajectory from a particular, land-bound inheritance toward a universal hope.

## Cultural Notes
孝 (filial piety) is deeply rooted in East Asian moral philosophy as a social and familial virtue; in biblical theology, however, filial honor is grounded not merely in social harmony but in obedience to God himself, the first commandment carrying an explicit promise attached to it. 天賜 deliberately avoids narrowing the promise to a specific political land grant, evoking providence and gift more broadly, and so the phrase bridges the Old Testament's covenantal land-promise and the New Testament's wider, trans-national application. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 孝親天賜 functions as a softened yet faithful rendering of the Fifth Commandment's promise, preserving its seriousness while allowing for this broader theological resonance.

## Example Sentences
1. 家風若立於孝親天賜之道，則代代蒙福。("If a household stands upon the way of honoring parents under heaven's gift, generations receive blessing.")
2. 「要孝敬父母，使你得福，在世長壽。」(以弗所書 6:2-3，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Paul cites the Fifth Commandment as "the first commandment with promise," the very promise captured in 孝親天賜.
4. 孝親天賜之訓，古今皆宜，非獨屬以色列一族。("The teaching of 'honor parents, heaven bestows' applies across all ages, not to Israel alone.")

## Sentiment
Encouraging, covenantal, stabilizing. Frames obedience as the pathway to divinely bestowed blessing rather than mere legal obligation.
