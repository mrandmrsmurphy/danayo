[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [尊敬](../words/尊敬.md) — to honor, revere, treat with esteem
- [父母](../words/父母.md) — father and mother

Literally: "honor and respect father and mother." The compound joins two established two-character words, 尊敬 and 父母, into a single four-character imperative.

## Extended Meaning
A concise formulation of the filial commandment. It enjoins reverence, obedience, and grateful acknowledgment toward one's parents, and by extension can signify respect for generational authority and covenant continuity more broadly.

## Source and Origin
This idiom condenses the Fifth Commandment, **Exodus 20:12** and **Deuteronomy 5:16**:

> "Honour thy father and thy mother: that thy days may be long upon the land which the LORD thy God giveth thee." (KJV)

The underlying Hebrew verb for "honor" (כַּבֵּד, *kabbēd*) literally means "to give weight to" — implying seriousness, material support, and public respect, not merely inward feeling. Traditional Chinese Bible translations typically render the command as "當孝敬父母" ("one should honor one's parents with filial reverence"). The Dan'a'yo form selects 尊敬 rather than 孝敬 to foreground general reverent esteem rather than the specifically Confucian filial-piety terminology of 孝, while remaining fully consonant with the biblical imperative — a companion idiom to [[孝親天賜]], which renders the same commandment together with its attached promise.

## Cultural Notes
In the Decalogue this commandment bridges duties toward God and duties toward neighbor, and is the first commandment accompanied by an explicit promise. In East Asian contexts filial piety is already culturally central, and the biblical command aligns with but is not identical to Confucian structures — it is grounded in covenant relationship and divine authority rather than social harmony alone. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, 尊敬父母 functions as both moral law and cultural anchor, underscoring generational continuity and gratitude while implying that parental authority is itself accountable to a higher, divine law.

## Example Sentences
1. 社會之穩固，常繫於尊敬父母之風。("The stability of society is often bound to the practice of honoring father and mother.")
2. 「當孝敬父母，使你的日子在耶和華你神所賜你的地上得以長久。」(出埃及記 20:12，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Sunday school lessons on the Ten Commandments often begin the section on human relationships with 尊敬父母.
4. 尊敬父母者，蒙神應許長壽之福。("Those who honor their father and mother are promised by God the blessing of long life.")

## Sentiment
Reverent, stabilizing, covenantal. Calls for enduring gratitude and structured respect within family life.
