[Dan'a'yo Chengyu](chengyu/Dan'a'yo%20Chengyu.md)
>[!Tip] The named sub-principle of [[義重於音]], cited in [[文法 - 06語用]].

## Literal Meaning
- [義](../characters/義.md) — meaning, righteousness
- [以 (char)](../characters/以%20(char).md) — by means of, on the basis of
- [立 (char)](../characters/立%20(char).md) — to establish, to set upright
- [名 (char)](../characters/名%20(char).md) — name, term, designation

Literally: "By meaning, one establishes names" — or more fully, "Names are established on the basis of meaning."

## Extended Meaning
義以立名 states that a name or term's legitimacy comes from the concept it expresses, not from the sound it happens to carry. It governs vocabulary creation specifically — as distinct from its parent maxim [[義重於音]], which governs translation choice more broadly. Where 義重於音 says "when sound and sense conflict, sense wins," 義以立名 goes further: even when coining an entirely new term, the coiner should build it from the concept's own defining structure rather than borrowing or inventing a sound arbitrarily. Phonetic borrowing is treated as secondary — acceptable only when a concept genuinely lacks analyzable structure to build a semantic calque from.

This echoes the older Chinese concern with 正名 ("rectification of names," the idea that names must correspond correctly to the things they designate) without repeating that vocabulary directly — but it shifts the grounding of naming from social/political order (正名's original Confucian concern) to semantic coherence specifically, a narrower and more linguistic application of the same underlying instinct.

## Source and Origin
義以立名 is a Dan'a'yo-coined idiom (単亜語), a named sub-principle flowing directly from the language's guiding translation maxim, [[義重於音]] ("meaning outweighs sound") — documented together in [[文法 - 06語用]]. Where 義重於音 is stated as the general rule for resolving conflicts between sound and sense in translation, 義以立名 extracts and names the specific consequence for vocabulary formation: when coining new terms (whether native Dan'a'yo coinages or loanword adaptations), build the term from the concept's defining function rather than importing a foreign sound.

The maxim has direct practical implications for how Dan'a'yo itself is built: (1) when coining a new term, derive it from the concept's defining structure; (2) when borrowing a foreign term, prefer a semantic calque over phonetic imitation unless the concept genuinely lacks analyzable structure; (3) when an existing term becomes misleading, rename it according to its actual operative meaning rather than preserving a now-inaccurate sound. This principle is treated as especially important in technical, scientific, or doctrinal vocabulary, where sound-based borrowing can actively obscure conceptual clarity across the language family Dan'a'yo serves.

## Cultural Notes
Like its parent maxim 義重於音, this is grammar-policy language rather than an idiom in general circulation — cited specifically within Dan'a'yo's own design discussions when explaining the language's approach to coining new vocabulary. It stands alongside the language's other founding mottos ([[義重於音]], [[合漢再決]], [[東亜自通]], [[覧昭和決]], [[毎字明意]], [[朝鮮正音]], [[文言現代]], [[百家共承]], [[文体並存]], [[信達雅化]]) as part of the same family of design-principle statements governing how Dan'a'yo is built and translated.

## Example Sentences
1. 単亜語の造語は義以立名の原則に従い、意味から名前を立てる。("Dan'a'yo's word-coining follows the principle of 義以立名, establishing names from meaning.")
2. When a new scientific concept needs a Dan'a'yo term, 義以立名 says: build the word from what the concept *does*, not from how a foreign name happens to *sound*.
3. 이 언어의 명명 원칙은 의이립명에 근거한다. ("This language's naming principle is grounded in 의이립명.")

## Sentiment
Principled and constructive; a positive statement of method rather than a criticism. Frames good vocabulary design as a matter of conceptual fidelity.
