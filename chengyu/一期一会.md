## Literal Meaning
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [期](../characters/期%20(char).md) — period, lifetime, appointed time
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [会](../characters/会%20(char).md) — meeting

Literally: "one lifetime, one meeting."

## Extended Meaning
Each encounter is unique and will never occur in exactly the same way again; one should therefore treasure the present meeting as something singular and unrepeatable. It expresses awareness of impermanence and attentiveness to the moment, and in modern Japanese is used broadly — for meaningful encounters, graduations, farewells, or chance meetings — not only in the tea-ceremony context that produced it.

## Source and Origin
Composed of classical Chinese characters, but Japanese-coined rather than a classical Chinese chengyu in origin. The idea comes from the tea ceremony (茶道) of the Sen no Rikyū (千利休, 1522–1591) circle, and the fixed four-character form is a later formulation:

1. **《山上宗二記》** (*Yamanoue Sōji ki*) — Rikyū's disciple Yamanoue Sōji (山上宗二, d. 1590) records Rikyū's teaching in the section 茶湯者覚悟十躰, in a form that is not yet four characters:
   > "常ノ茶湯ナリトモ、路地ヘ入ルヨリ出ヅルマデ、一期ニ一度ノ会ノヤウニ、亭主ヲ敬ヒ畏ルベシ、世間雑談無用ナリ。"
   > (Even at an ordinary tea gathering, from entering the garden path until leaving, revere the host as though this were a once-in-a-lifetime meeting, and avoid idle gossip.)
2. **《南方録》** (*Nanpōroku*) — a record long credited to Rikyū's secret teachings contains the related phrase 一座一會ノ心 ("the spirit of one sitting, one meeting"). Modern scholarship regards the *Nanpōroku* as probably an Edo-period compilation, so this phrase cannot be traced to Rikyū himself.
3. **《茶湯一会集》** (*Chanoyu ichie-shū*) — the daimyo and tea master Ii Naosuke (井伊直弼, 1815–1860) uses the fixed form in the preface of this treatise. He began it in his early thirties and brought it to a fair copy around 1858, shortly before he became *tairō*:
   > "抑、茶湯の交会は、一期一会といひて、たとへハ幾度おなじ主客交会するとも、今日の会にふたゝひかへらさる事を思へハ、実に我一世一度の会也。"
   > (A tea gathering is "one lifetime, one meeting": however many times the same host and guest meet, today's gathering can never come again, so it is truly a once-in-a-lifetime meeting.)

This is the earliest text in which I found the four-character form 一期一会, and Naosuke's own gloss shows it derives from Sōji's 一期ニ一度ノ会. I verified these passages through quotations on secondary pages (Japanese Wikipedia, the Omotesenke site and a Hikone-related page), not through a facsimile of the manuscripts, and I did not search exhaustively for earlier uses.

## Standard Form
**一期一会**
No character variants in standard usage. The phrase is written identically in Japanese and Chinese contexts; it is primarily a Japanese cultural expression, and in Chinese it is understood but marked as Japanese in flavor. 一期一會 (traditional characters) is listed as an alias for cross-script search purposes only.

## Cultural Notes
- Central to Japanese tea aesthetics: host and guest treat each gathering as once-in-a-lifetime.
- Reflects Buddhist-influenced sensitivity to impermanence.
- Commonly appears in calligraphy scrolls displayed in tea rooms.
- In modern Japanese, used broadly for meaningful encounters, graduations, farewells, or chance meetings.
- In Chinese contexts, it is recognized but retains a distinctly Japanese cultural resonance.

## Example Sentences
1. 茶席之會，當以一期一会之心待之。
2. Their reunion felt like *ichigo ichie* — a moment that would never come again.
3. Graduation day reminded them that friendship, too, is 一期一会.

## Sentiment
Reflective and poignant. Positive in tone, but tinged with transience and quiet solemnity.
