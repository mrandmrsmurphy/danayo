## Literal Meaning
- [千](characters/千.md) — thousand
- [差](characters/差.md) — discrepancy, deviation
- [万](characters/万.md) — ten thousand
- [別 (char)](characters/別%20(char).md) — distinction, separation

Literally: "A thousand differences, ten thousand distinctions."

## Extended Meaning
千差万別 describes something extremely diverse — differing in countless ways, with great variety and no uniform pattern. 差 emphasizes deviation or discrepancy while 別 emphasizes distinction or separation; the two overlap but are not redundant. The paired numerals 千 and 万 are not quantitative but rhetorical, marking scale beyond enumeration — the phrase does not merely say "many kinds," but asserts that variation is intrinsic and pervasive. It functions less as an observation than as a warning against premature simplification, often appearing where a writer wants to acknowledge complexity before proceeding with classification or argument. It is neutral in tone and carries no evaluative force by itself, widely used in academic writing, journalism, education, and formal speech.

## Source and Origin
Taiwan's Ministry of Education *Dictionary of Chinese Idioms* documents the idiom in the Song-dynasty **《朱子語類》** (*Zhuzi yulei*, the recorded sayings of Zhu Xi), volume 60, on the Mencius chapter 盡心上:

> 「今人直是差處多，只一條大路，其餘千差萬別，皆是私路。」
> ("People today go wrong in many places; there is only one great road, and the thousands of other differing ways are all private paths.")

The same entry gives the variant 萬別千差. I checked the quotation against the Ministry's entry, not against the text of the *Yulei*, and it is the earliest occurrence the Ministry documents; I did not search for earlier uses.

The earlier version of this page said the idiom has no single citation and "emerges from medieval to late-imperial usage, especially in Buddhist-influenced prose," and gave the origin as "Pre-Song Period." I could not verify that account, so it has been replaced by the documented use above; the phrase may well appear in earlier Buddhist writing, which I did not check. The usage in the *Yulei* shows 千差萬別 already working as a compact way to say that something is irreducibly varied, and it has since been used in expository, philosophical and everyday prose.

## Standard Form
**千差万別** (Japanese shinjitai 万) is the Dan'a'yo standard form.
- 千差萬別 — traditional Chinese form (萬)
- 千差万别 — simplified Chinese form (别)

## Cultural Notes
千差万別 is fully naturalized and widely current across Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and Vietnamese, appearing constantly in analytic and academic registers whenever a writer wants to block premature generalization — it asserts diversity as a starting condition to be acknowledged, not a conclusion to be argued for.

## Example Sentences
1. 人的性格千差萬別，難以一概而論。("People's personalities are endlessly varied; they can't be generalized in one stroke.")
2. 各国の文化は千差万別で、単純に比較することはできない。("Each country's culture is so varied that they can't simply be compared.")
3. 사람마다 생각이 천차만별이다. ("Everyone's thinking varies enormously from person to person.")
4. Ask ten economists for a forecast and you'll get ten different answers — opinions on this are 千差万別.

## Sentiment
Neutral, analytic. Acknowledges complexity and diversity as a starting condition, without praising or criticizing the variety it describes.
