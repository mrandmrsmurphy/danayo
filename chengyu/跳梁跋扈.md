## Literal Meaning
- [跳](../characters/跳.md) — jump, leap
- [梁](../characters/梁%20(char).md) — roof beam, rafter
- [跋](../characters/跋.md) — trample, stride recklessly
- [扈](../characters/扈.md) — retinue; insolently overbearing

Literally: "Leaping on the beams, trampling everything underfoot."

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary has the Japanese entry 跳梁跋扈 (ちょうりょうばっこ), analysed as 跳梁 + 跋扈. It glosses the noun "anarchy, disorder, people with ill intent going rampant" and the suru-verb "be in a state of chaos, anarchy to rule". Its Korean entry 도량발호 is a hanja-form noun. It has no Chinese entry for the four-character form.

## Source and Origin

The chengyu fuses two independent classical phrases with distinct origins.

**跳梁** comes from the 《莊子·逍遙遊》, in Zhuangzi's answer to Huizi, who complains that his big tree is useless:

> 子獨不見狸狌乎？卑身而伏，以候敖者；東西跳梁，不避高下，中於機辟，死於罔罟。
> "Have you never seen the wildcat? It crouches low and waits for what wanders by; it leaps about east and west, avoiding neither high nor low, until it is caught in a trap and dies in the net."

The 跳梁 is the wildcat's reckless leaping, unmindful of danger, and the passage ends in its destruction. The later idiom keeps the sense of reckless, unchecked leaping about.

**跋扈** comes from the 《後漢書·梁冀傳》. The young Emperor Zhi (質帝, r. 145–146), seeing Liang Ji (梁冀) at court, said 「此跋扈將軍也」, "this is a domineering general". Liang Ji, who had enthroned the child emperor, heard it, and had poison put in the emperor's cake. The Tang commentator 章懷太子 glosses 跋扈 as 「猶彊梁也」, "like 彊梁 (stubborn and violent)". The word is older than that anecdote: it is already used in the 《後漢書·朱浮傳》 (「赤眉跋扈長安」) and in Zhang Heng's 《西京賦》, and the 《資治通鑑釋文》 glosses it simply as 「跋扈跳梁也」, so even the dictionaries explain each by the other. The four-character compound 跳梁跋扈 is attested in Song writing, for example in 葉適《習學記言》 (of Cao Cao: 「欲因此跳梁跋扈自同問鼎」), and in Ming and Qing prose, where it is applied to rebels and warlords. I have not found an earlier attestation.

## Sentiment
Condemnatory; it describes rampant lawlessness.
