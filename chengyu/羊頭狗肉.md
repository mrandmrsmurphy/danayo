## Literal Meaning
- [羊](../characters/羊.md) — sheep
- [頭 (char)](../characters/頭%20(char).md) — head
- [狗](../characters/狗.md) — dog, hound
- [肉 (char)](../characters/肉%20(char).md) — meat, flesh

Literally: "A sheep's head; dog meat" — displaying the premium product while delivering the inferior one.

Wiktionary's Chinese entry makes 羊頭狗肉 a synonym of the fuller phrase **掛羊頭賣狗肉** (hang a sheep's head, sell dog meat).

## Extended Meaning
Wiktionary's Chinese entry glosses the fuller phrase 掛羊頭賣狗肉 "to cry wine and sell vinegar; to use a good cause as a cover for doing something bad". Its Japanese entry 羊頭狗肉 glosses it "false advertising, crying wine and selling vinegar".

## Source and Origin
The four-character form derives from the longer saying **懸羊頭賣狗肉** (also written 掛羊頭賣狗肉), "hang up a sheep's head and sell dog meat". The earliest Chan (Zen) text in which I could verify it is the **《無門關》** (Wumen Guan, compiled by 無門慧開, 1229 CE), in Wumen's comment on the first koan, the Buddha holding up a flower (case 6):

> 【無門曰】黃面瞿曇傍若無人，壓良為賤，懸羊頭賣狗肉，將謂多少奇特。
> "[Wumen comments:] The yellow-faced Gautama behaves as if no one were there, forcing the good into servitude, hanging up a sheep's head and selling dog meat, and he thinks it a marvel."

The saying is also found in other Chan records and in Ming monks' biographies (for example the 《大明高僧傳》, where a monk says of his own teaching life, 「坐曲彔床，懸羊頭，賣狗肉」). In Chan usage it is a rueful or ironic charge of passing off the ordinary under a grand sign, often turned against the speaker's own school. I could not find the saying in the 《景德傳燈錄》, so this page no longer cites that work.

An older image of the same idea exists: in the 《晏子春秋·內篇雜下》 Yanzi tells Duke Ling of Qi that to forbid women's wearing men's dress outside while allowing it inside the palace is 「猶懸牛首於門，而賣馬肉於內也」, "like hanging an ox's head at the door and selling horse meat inside". Later sources (for example the 《說苑》) retell it. The animals changed, but the shape of the charge, a sign that does not match the goods, is the same.

## Standard Form
**羊頭狗肉** (standard form across traditional Chinese and Dan'a'yo)
- 羊头狗肉 — simplified Chinese form; aliased in this vault

The longer form **懸羊頭賣狗肉 / 掛羊頭賣狗肉** (挂羊头卖狗肉) remains current in Chinese, especially in formal or emphatic contexts. The four-character reduction is the standard set phrase.

## Example Sentences
1. 懸羊頭賣狗肉，將謂多少奇特。（無門慧開，《無門關》）

## Sentiment
Accusatory; it charges that the sign does not match the goods.
