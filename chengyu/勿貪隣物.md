## Literal Meaning
- [勿 (char)](../characters/勿%20(char).md) — do not
- [貪 (char)](../characters/貪%20(char).md) — covet, greedily desire
- [隣 (char)](../characters/隣%20(char).md) — neighbor
- [物 (char)](../characters/物%20(char).md) — thing, possession

Literally: "do not covet the neighbor's goods."

## Extended Meaning
A concise expression of the prohibition against coveting what belongs to another. It addresses inward desire rather than outward theft, targeting the root of envy and discontent rather than any visible act.

## Source and Origin
This idiom condenses the Tenth Commandment, **Exodus 20:17** and **Deuteronomy 5:21**:

> "Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house, thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's wife, nor his manservant, nor his maidservant, nor his ox, nor his ass, nor any thing that is thy neighbour's." (KJV)

Traditional Chinese Bible translations often render the opening clause as "不可貪戀你鄰舍的房屋" ("You shall not covet your neighbor's house"). The Dan'a'yo form replaces 不可 with the more compact classical negative 勿, and compresses the commandment's extended list (house, wife, servants, livestock) into the single general term 物, encompassing all possessions alike.

## Cultural Notes
Unlike the commandments regulating visible actions, this one governs internal disposition, exposing the moral depth of the law — in Christian theology it is often cited to show that sin begins in the heart, not merely in outward conduct. 貪 carries a strong connotation of grasping greed; the focus on 隣 underscores social proximity, since coveting disrupts covenant community among neighbors specifically. Historically the Tenth Commandment has been linked to discussions of desire, contentment, and the roots of economic injustice, and in Pauline theology (notably Romans 7:7) coveting becomes the paradigmatic example of inward transgression the law exposes. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, the phrase serves both as legal shorthand and as a tool of moral introspection, highlighting the internal dimension of righteousness.

## Example Sentences
1. 心若生妬，即犯勿貪隣物之戒。("If envy arises in the heart, one has already violated the command not to covet a neighbor's goods.")
2. 「不可貪戀人的房屋，也不可貪戀人的妻子、僕婢、牛驢，並他一切所有的。」(出埃及記 20:17，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Paul's confession in Romans 7:7 makes 勿貪隣物 the very commandment that awakened his awareness of sin.
4. 勿貪隣物之訓，教人知足常樂。("The teaching 'do not covet your neighbor's goods' instructs people in contentment.")

## Sentiment
Restrained, searching, ethically penetrating. Calls for inward discipline and contentment rather than merely outward conformity.
