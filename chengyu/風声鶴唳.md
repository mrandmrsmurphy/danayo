## Literal Meaning
- [風 (char)](../characters/風%20(char).md) — wind
- [声](../characters/声.md) — sound
- [鶴 (char)](../characters/鶴%20(char).md) — crane
- [唳 (char)](../characters/唳%20(char).md) — cry (of a bird)

Literally: "the sound of the wind, the cry of cranes."

## Extended Meaning
To panic at imagined threats; to mistake harmless signs for danger due to fear or tension. It does not describe objective danger but subjective overreaction — the fragility of morale under stress rather than any real external threat.

## Source and Origin
From the **《晉書》** (Book of Jin), in accounts of the aftermath of the Battle of Fei River (淝水之戰, 383 AD). The historical episode concerns the collapse of the Former Qin army after its defeat by Eastern Jin forces: in the rout, morale deteriorated so completely that ordinary environmental noises — wind in the grass, cranes calling — were interpreted by the fleeing soldiers as sounds of pursuing enemy troops. The phrase is often paired with 草木皆兵 ("every bush and tree looks like an enemy soldier"), another expression drawn from the same narrative episode, reinforcing the shared theme of fear-induced misperception.

## Cultural Notes
The imagery is precise rather than merely ornamental: 風聲 and 鶴唳 are not inherently ominous sounds, and their menace is entirely projected by the frightened hearer. The idiom therefore captures psychological fragility under stress rather than describing any actual danger. Across the region today, 風聲鶴唳 is used to describe collective anxiety, rumor-driven panic, or hypersensitivity in tense situations, appearing in journalism, political commentary, and literary prose. Its tone is descriptive and often mildly critical, pointing to nervous overinterpretation rather than justified alarm.

## Example Sentences
1. 敗軍風声鶴唳，草木皆以為敵兵。("The defeated army was so panicked by every sound that they mistook the very grass and trees for enemy soldiers.")
2. After the scandal broke, the office was gripped by 風声鶴唳 — every phone call felt like bad news.
3. 市場風声鶴唳，投資人稍有風吹草動便恐慌拋售。("The market was jittery with panic; investors sold off in a frenzy at the slightest disturbance.")
4. 戒厳令下，市民風声鶴唳，人人自危。("Under martial law, the citizens lived in constant nervous alarm, each fearing for their own safety.")

## Sentiment
Negative; describes anxious, fear-driven overreaction. Often carries a mildly critical or pitying tone toward the overreaction itself.
