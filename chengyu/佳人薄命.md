## Literal Meaning
- [佳 (char)](characters/佳%20(char).md) — fine, beautiful
- [人 (char)](characters/人%20(char).md) — person
- [薄](characters/薄.md) — thin, meager
- [命](characters/命.md) — fate, life

Literally: "A beautiful person, a thin fate."

## Extended Meaning
佳人薄命 expresses the melancholy observation that beauty and misfortune often go hand in hand — that talented or lovely people, especially women, tend to meet tragic or short-lived fates. It carries sympathy and lamentation rather than blame, functioning as a shorthand for the tragic destiny of beauty and talent under social constraint, especially female genius oppressed by fate or circumstance. It expresses both grief for impermanence and admiration for fleeting beauty — a distinctly East Asian aesthetic combining tragedy, destiny, and grace.

## Source and Origin
The line behind the idiom is from the Northern Song poet **蘇軾** (Su Shi, 1037–1101)'s poem **《薄命佳人》**, whose text I checked on Wikisource:

> "雙頰凝酥發抹漆，眼光入簾珠的皪。故將白練作仙衣，不許紅膏汙天質。吳音嬌軟帶兒癡，無限閑愁總未知。自古佳人多命薄，閉門春盡楊花落。"
> (Cheeks like congealed cream, hair like lacquer, eyes that light the curtain like bright pearls. She wears plain white silk as an immortal's robe and will not let rouge stain her natural beauty. Her Wu speech is soft and childlike, and she knows nothing yet of endless sorrow. "Since ancient times, beautiful women have had thin fates: behind a closed door, spring ends and the willow-down falls.")

The closing couplet's 自古佳人多命薄 is the source of the idiom 佳人薄命; the four-character form is a compression of that line. A Japanese reference source notes that documented uses of the four-character form are later than the poem, so I do not claim the compression was Su Shi's own; I did not trace when 佳人薄命 first appears as a fixed phrase.

**Corrected.** The earlier version of this page attributed the line 「自古佳人多命薄」 to Cao Pi's (曹丕) 《燕歌行》. I checked the 《燕歌行》 on Wikisource: it does not contain 佳人 or 命薄, and the line is Su Shi's. The claims about Tang poems on courtesans and about a 《紅樓夢》 preface calling its heroines 「紅顏薄命之女」 were also unverified, so they have been removed.

## Cultural Notes
In Chinese usage, 佳人薄命 carries sympathy and melancholy — an aesthetic of tragedy through beauty. In Japanese, 佳人薄命 (kajin hakumei) is a literary idiom for tragic heroines or artists (I did not check the claim that it arose in the Edo period). In Korean, 가인박명 is used proverbially to describe the sad destiny of a gifted or beautiful woman, often in historical dramas or literature. In Vietnamese, giai nhân bạc mệnh retains the same sense and I did not check the earlier claim that it describes the figure of Kiều in Nguyễn Du's *Truyện Kiều*.

## Example Sentences
1. 「自古佳人多命薄，閉門春盡楊花落。」(蘇軾《薄命佳人》)
2. 自古佳人薄命，紅顏難得長壽。("Since ancient times, fair women have had short fates; beauty rarely lives long.")
3. 그녀의 죽음은 가인박명이었다. ("Her death was a case of 'beauty and short fate.'")
4. The critics called her the very picture of 佳人薄命 — brilliant, beautiful, and gone far too soon.

## Sentiment
Melancholy, sympathetic, aesthetic. Expresses lamentation for impermanence alongside admiration for fleeting beauty — tragedy, destiny, and grace held together.
