## Literal Meaning
- [佳 (char)](characters/佳%20(char).md) — fine, beautiful
- [人 (char)](characters/人%20(char).md) — person
- [薄](characters/薄.md) — thin, meager
- [命](characters/命.md) — fate, life

Literally: "A beautiful person, a thin fate."

## Extended Meaning
佳人薄命 expresses the melancholy observation that beauty and misfortune often go hand in hand — that talented or lovely people, especially women, tend to meet tragic or short-lived fates. It carries sympathy and lamentation rather than blame, functioning as a shorthand for the tragic destiny of beauty and talent under social constraint, especially female genius oppressed by fate or circumstance. It expresses both grief for impermanence and admiration for fleeting beauty — a distinctly East Asian aesthetic combining tragedy, destiny, and grace.

## Source and Origin
The sentiment originates in classical poetry, especially from the Tang dynasty and later, where it became a fixture of tragic romanticism. A clear early example appears in **Cao Pi's (曹丕, 187–226)** *《燕歌行》*:

> 「自古佳人多命薄」 — "Since ancient times, beautiful women have been ill-fated."

The idea was expanded in later literature: Tang poetry frequently laments the short lives of courtesans and consorts, and Qing fiction gives it its most famous elaboration in **Cao Xueqin's 《紅樓夢》** (*Dream of the Red Chamber*), whose preface calls its heroines 「紅顏薄命之女」 — "fair-faced girls of fragile fate." The crystallized four-character form 佳人薄命 compresses Cao Pi's line into a standalone idiom, becoming shorthand for beauty's tragic vulnerability across the tradition that followed it.

## Variants
- 紅顏薄命 — "beauty (lit. rosy face) has a short fate," a common poetic variant, interchangeable and slightly more prevalent in later usage
- 美人薄命 — a similar modern form used in vernacular contexts

## Cultural Notes
In Chinese usage, 佳人薄命 carries sympathy and melancholy — an aesthetic of tragedy through beauty. In Japanese, 佳人薄命 (kajin hakumei) became a literary idiom during the Edo period, often describing tragic heroines or artists. In Korean, 가인박명 is used proverbially to describe the sad destiny of a gifted or beautiful woman, often in historical dramas or literature. In Vietnamese, giai nhân bạc mệnh retains the same sense and appears in poetry — for instance describing the figure of Kiều in Nguyễn Du's *Truyện Kiều*.

## Example Sentences
1. 「自古佳人多命薄。」(曹丕《燕歌行》)
2. 自古佳人薄命，紅顏難得長壽。("Since ancient times, fair women have had short fates; beauty rarely lives long.")
3. 그녀의 죽음은 가인박명이었다. ("Her death was a case of 'beauty and short fate.'")
4. The critics called her the very picture of 佳人薄命 — brilliant, beautiful, and gone far too soon.

## Sentiment
Melancholy, sympathetic, aesthetic. Expresses lamentation for impermanence alongside admiration for fleeting beauty — tragedy, destiny, and grace held together.
