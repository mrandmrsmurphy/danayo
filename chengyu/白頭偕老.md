## Literal Meaning
- [白 (char)](characters/白%20(char).md) — white
- [頭 (char)](characters/頭%20(char).md) — head
- [偕](characters/偕.md) — together
- [老 (char)](characters/老%20(char).md) — to grow old

Literally: "To grow old together with white heads."

## Extended Meaning
白頭偕老 describes living together in lasting marital harmony until old age — lifelong conjugal fidelity. It reflects fidelity, mutual respect, and the Confucian virtue of constancy (貞), and functions socially as a wish or blessing given at weddings, still used in modern Chinese, Korean, and Japanese ceremonies. Aesthetically it evokes calm domestic harmony and the natural passage of life — aging as beauty rather than decay.

## Source and Origin
The phrase's most famous early occurrence is in **《詩經·邶風·擊鼓》**, where a soldier vows fidelity to his beloved:

> 「死生契闊，與子成說，執子之手，與子偕老。」
> "Through life and death, union or separation, I have pledged with you: holding your hand, I will grow old with you."

I checked the couplet against the Wikisource text of 〈擊鼓〉, which has it with a comma after 成說. The couplet 「與子偕老」 also occurs in another Odes poem, 〈鄭風·女曰雞鳴〉, which I could not load.

Taiwan's Ministry of Education *Dictionary of Chinese Idioms* (via a search summary) describes 白頭偕老 as a combination of two older expressions: 偕老, from 《詩經·鄭風·女曰雞鳴》, a poem of a loving couple who vow to stay together to old age, and 白頭, from the Han yuefu song 〈白頭吟〉. It documents the fixed four-character form in the Ming dynasty, in Lou Jian's 〈祭殷宜人李氏文〉, Lu Cai's 《懷香記》 scene 23, and later in Shen Fu's 《浮生六記》 vol. 1; I did not check these against the texts or search for earlier uses. The entry also lists the variant 白頭到老.

**Corrected.** The earlier version of this page said 白頭 and 偕老 "merged over time" from this line and that "by the Tang and Song periods" it was the standard poetic blessing for marriage. I could not verify those statements, and the dictionary's account is that 白頭 comes from 〈白頭吟〉 and the earliest documented fixed form is Ming. `origin` records the Odes poem that I verified for the couplet 與子偕老.

## Standard Form
**白頭偕老** (traditional) is the historically standard form.
- 白头偕老 — simplified Chinese form

## Variants
- 白頭到老 — "to remain together till both are white-haired"
- Related in sense (though distinct idioms): 同甘共苦 ("to share sweetness and bitterness together"), 永結同心 ("forever united in heart"), 結髮夫妻 ("bound-hair couple," married since youth), 相敬如賓 ("mutual respect as between honored guests"), 偕老百年 ("to live together for a hundred years")

## Cultural Notes
Chinese usage is common in toasts, wedding scrolls, and epitaphs — 祝你們白頭偕老，永結同心 ("may you grow old together with white heads, forever of one heart"). Japanese 白頭偕老 (hakutō kairō) and Korean 백두해로 (baekdu haero) carry the same sense; I did not check how common they are in each language. Vietnamese bạch đầu giai lão is the classical expression for "to grow old together," often paired with the native saying đầu bạc răng long ("white hair, long teeth," i.e. ripe old age).

## Example Sentences
1. 「死生契闊，與子成說，執子之手，與子偕老。」(《詩經·邶風·擊鼓》)
2. 願新人白頭偕老，永浴愛河。("May the newlyweds grow old together and bathe forever in the river of love.")
3. 두 사람이 백두해로하기를 기원합니다. ("We pray that the two will live together to white-haired old age.")
4. Chúc hai bạn bạch đầu giai lão, trăm năm hạnh phúc. ("Wishing you to grow old together, a hundred years of happiness.")

## Sentiment
Warm, tender, enduring. A phrase of love's endurance — serene, mutual, and faithful — wishing that two people hold hands until both their heads are white, to the end of their days.
