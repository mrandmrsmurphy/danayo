## Literal Meaning
- [孤](characters/孤.md) — isolated, alone
- [立 (char)](characters/立%20(char).md) — standing
- [無 (char)](characters/無%20(char).md) — without
- [援](characters/援.md) — aid, support

Literally: "Standing alone, without aid."

## Extended Meaning
孤立無援 describes being alone with no help or support — cut off from assistance physically, socially, or morally. Originally used in military and political contexts, the idiom broadened to mean any kind of helpless isolation, emotional, moral, or intellectual. Depending on tone it can convey either pathos or nobility: tragic helplessness of the forsaken, or the steadfast independence of someone isolated precisely because they hold to a principle others have abandoned (a reading I did not trace to a source).

## Source and Origin
The four characters occur in the **《後漢書》** (*Book of the Later Han*), in the "班梁列傳" (the joint biography of Ban Chao and Liang Jin, volume 47), checked against the Wikisource text. The passage is about Ban Chao (班超) in the Western Regions after the Han court was in mourning:

> 「焉耆以中國大喪，遂攻沒都護陳睦。超孤立無援，而龜茲、姑墨數發兵攻疏勒。」
> (Yanqi, taking advantage of the great mourning in China, attacked and destroyed the Protector-General Chen Mu. Ban Chao was isolated and without help, while Kucha and Gumo repeatedly sent troops to attack Shule.)

This is a documented occurrence of 孤立無援 in a military setting; I did not search for an earlier one. A search summary also cites 《史記》 for the separate words 孤立 and 無援, which I did not check.

**Corrected.** The earlier version of this page quoted 《後漢書》 as 「孤立無援，勢窮力竭。」 I checked volume 47: the string 勢窮力竭 does not occur there, and the real sentence is the one above. The claims that 《史記·蘇秦列傳》 uses "similar imagery" and that the fixed phrase "becomes common in Han–Tang historical writing" were unverified, so they are removed.

## Cultural Notes
Chinese usage spans politics, warfare, and moral reflection — 他在改革中孤立無援，卻不曾退縮 ("he was isolated in his reform efforts, yet never retreated") shows the idiom's capacity to carry admiration alongside hardship. Japanese 孤立無援 (koritsu muen) and Korean 고립무원 (gorip muwon) carry the same sense of being utterly isolated; I did not check how common they are in each language. Vietnamese cô lập vô viện retains the same straightforward sense, "isolated with no assistance."

Related in sense (though not variants of the same phrase) are 孤軍奮鬥 ("a lone army fighting bravely"), 寡不敵眾 ("the few cannot withstand the many"), 眾叛親離 ("abandoned by all, betrayed even by kin"), and 孤苦伶仃 ("utterly alone and pitiful").

## Example Sentences
1. 「超孤立無援，而龜茲、姑墨數發兵攻疏勒。」(《後漢書·班梁列傳》)
2. 他在敵人包圍中孤立無援。("He was surrounded by the enemy, alone and without help.")
3. 그는 고립무원의 처지에 놓였다. ("He found himself isolated and without support.")
4. After the vote, she found herself politically 孤立無援 — every ally she'd counted on had quietly slipped away.

## Sentiment
Alone and cut off, yet often still standing. A phrase of hardship and endurance — depending on tone, it can express pity, admiration for courage, or stoic resolve.
