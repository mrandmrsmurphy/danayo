## Literal Meaning
- [傍 (char)](../characters/傍%20(char).md) — beside
- [若 (char)](../characters/若%20(char).md) — as if, like
- [無 (char)](../characters/無%20(char).md) — without, lacking
- [人 (char)](../characters/人%20(char).md) — person

Literally: "As if there were no one beside [him]."

## Extended Meaning
Describes someone who acts brazenly, arrogantly, or without any self-consciousness in front of others — speaking loudly, behaving domineeringly, or otherwise carrying on as though nobody else were watching. It is almost always critical or disapproving in modern usage (arrogance, inconsiderate self-absorption), though the original anecdote it comes from used it more neutrally, even sympathetically, to describe unselfconscious emotional abandon between close friends rather than arrogance toward strangers.

## Source and Origin
**《史記·刺客列傳》** (Sima Qian 司馬遷, c. 91 BCE):
> 荊軻嗜酒，日與狗屠及高漸離飲於燕市，酒酣以往，高漸離擊筑，荊軻和而歌於市中，相樂也，已而相泣，旁若無人者。
> (Jing Ke loved wine, and daily drank in the markets of Yan with a dog-butcher and with Gao Jianli. When the wine took hold, Gao Jianli would strike the zhu-lute and Jing Ke would sing along in the middle of the market — merry together at first, then in tears together — as if there were no one else around them.)

This passage introduces Jing Ke (荊軻), later famous as the would-be assassin of the King of Qin, describing his close friendship with the musician Gao Jianli (高漸離) before the assassination attempt that made him legendary. Drinking and singing together in the public market, swinging from joy to tears without self-consciousness, the two behaved 旁若無人 — the original text uses 旁, not 傍, though the two characters are near-homophonous and semantically overlapping ("beside"), and 傍若無人 became the more common orthography in later and modern usage. The four-character sequence already appears verbatim in the original — a direct quotation, not a later crystallization. Notably, the original anecdote is not really about arrogance at all; the modern idiom's critical, disapproving sense of "self-important disregard for others" developed later, diverging from Sima Qian's own more sympathetic framing of two friends lost in their own emotion.

## Standard Form
**傍若無人** (dominant modern orthography, used here as the Dan'a'yo standard)
- 旁若無人 — the original classical form, as it actually appears in 《史記》; still current, especially in more formal or literary writing
- 傍若无人 / 旁若无人 — simplified forms of each

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** bàng ruò wú rén
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** bong6 joek6 mou4 jan4
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** ぼうじゃくぶじん
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 방약무인 (bang-yak-mu-in)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** bàng nhược vô nhân

Japanese ぼうじゃくぶじん selects 無's alternate on'yomi ぶ (BU) rather than the more common む (MU) — both are independently attested readings of 無 (cf. 無事 buji, 無精 bushou), and this idiom is simply one of the words that lexicalizes the BU reading rather than MU; not a case of rendaku voicing. Korean uses 약 for 若 here (its dominant general-use reading, as in 만약 "if"), not the special reading 야 reserved for the Buddhist loanword 般若 (banya/prajna).

## Cultural Notes
Consistently critical/disapproving across all five languages in modern usage — describing someone as behaving 傍若無人 is never a compliment. It commonly appears in complaints about rude public behavior (talking loudly on the phone, cutting in line, ignoring social norms) as well as broader criticism of arrogant, self-important people in positions of power. Register spans from everyday complaint to formal social commentary; it is not restricted to literary writing the way some other idioms in this set are.

## Example Sentences
1. 他在圖書館裡旁若無人地大聲講電話，惹來眾人側目。
2. The CEO strode through the office _bàng ruò wú rén_, never once acknowledging his staff.
3. 彼は電車内で傍若無人に振る舞い、周囲の顰蹙を買った。
4. Her _bàng nhược vô nhân_ attitude at the meeting alienated the entire team.

## Sentiment
Negative; critical of arrogance, inconsiderate self-absorption, or brazen disregard for social norms. Rarely used sympathetically in modern speech, in contrast to the more neutral tone of the original anecdote.
