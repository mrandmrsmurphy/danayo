[Dan'a'yo Chengyu](chengyu/Dan'a'yo%20Chengyu.md)
>[!Tip] A guiding principle of [[文法 - 06語用]], flowing from [[百家共承]].

## Literal Meaning
- [詞](characters/詞.md) — word
- [彙 (char)](characters/彙%20(char).md) — vocabulary, collection
- [兼 (char)](characters/兼%20(char).md) — concurrently, without exclusion
- [容 (char)](characters/容%20(char).md) — to contain, admit

Literally: "Vocabulary accommodates inclusively," or more precisely, "the lexicon is capable of inclusion."

## Extended Meaning
詞彙兼容 asserts that a living cultural tradition does not purge its lexicon when schools of thought differ — it absorbs. Competing schools may use overlapping terminology, the same word may carry layered histories, and vocabulary need not be purified to preserve identity. It pushes against linguistic sectarianism: a civilization confident in itself can borrow terms, retain archaic expressions, and allow semantic accretion, rather than demanding terminological quarantine. This is historically visible in Confucian terms adopted into Buddhist discourse, Buddhist terms reinterpreted in Neo-Confucianism, and Western philosophical vocabulary naturalized into modern Sinitic usage — the lexicon becomes a shared field, not a fenced territory.

兼容 does not mean semantic collapse, and it does not imply that distinctions disappear; it implies the capacity to host difference without lexical fragmentation. The test of whether the principle is working is whether shared vocabulary remains intelligible despite divergent metaphysical commitments among the traditions using it.

## Source and Origin
詞彙兼容 is a Dan'a'yo-coined idiom (単亜語), one of two named sub-principles (alongside [[文体並存]]) flowing from the language's guiding pragmatic maxim [[百家共承]] (see [[文法 - 06語用]]). Where 百家共承 states civilizational continuity in general, and 文体並存 addresses stylistic register specifically, 詞彙兼容 addresses the lexical dimension: if 百家共承 is civilizational continuity, 詞彙兼容 is linguistic permeability.

## Cultural Notes
詞彙兼容 is grammar-policy language rather than an idiom in general circulation, cited when explaining why Dan'a'yo's vocabulary freely draws on Confucian, Buddhist, Daoist, and modern secular sources side by side rather than favoring one intellectual lineage's terminology over another's. It sits alongside the language's other founding mottos ([[合漢再決]], [[東亜自通]], [[覧昭和決]], [[毎字明意]], [[朝鮮正音]], [[文言現代]], [[義重於音]]) as part of the same family of design-principle statements.

## Example Sentences
1. 単亜語の語彙は詞彙兼容の原則により、儒教・仏教・道教・近代思想の用語を併せ持つ。("Dan'a'yo's vocabulary, under the principle of 詞彙兼容, holds together terminology from Confucian, Buddhist, Daoist, and modern thought alike.")
2. Rather than picking a single philosophical school's vocabulary as standard, the language practices 詞彙兼容 — every tradition's terms are welcome, so long as they remain mutually intelligible.
3. 이 언어의 어휘 정책은 사휘겸용의 정신을 따른다. ("This language's vocabulary policy follows the spirit of 사휘겸용.")

## Sentiment
Open, absorptive, confident. Affirms that a rich, layered vocabulary is a sign of civilizational continuity, not incoherence.
