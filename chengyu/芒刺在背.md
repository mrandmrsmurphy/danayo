## Literal Meaning
- [芒](../characters/芒.md) — awn, bristle; a sharp point
- [刺 (char)](../characters/刺%20(char).md) — thorn; to prick
- [在 (char)](../characters/在%20(char).md) — to be at, on
- [背 (char)](../characters/背%20(char).md) — back

Literally: "Awns and thorns on one's back."

## Extended Meaning
To feel acutely uneasy, as if something sharp were pricking one's back: the discomfort of being watched, or of having someone formidable close behind. It is used for fear of a powerful person, for guilt under another's gaze, and for general restlessness. It describes a feeling and is never used of a real thorn.

## Source and Origin
The idiom comes from **《漢書·霍光金日磾傳》** (Book of Han, ch. 68), in the biography of the regent Huo Guang. When Emperor Xuan had just come to the throne he went to the ancestral temple of Gaozu, with Huo Guang riding beside him as escort:

**《漢書·霍光金日磾傳》** (compiled 1st c. AD):
> "宣帝始立，謁見高廟，大將軍光從驂乘，上內嚴憚之，若有芒刺在背。後車騎將軍張安世代光驂乘，天子從容肆體，甚安近焉。"
> (When Emperor Xuan had just been enthroned and went to see the ancestral temple, the General-in-chief Guang rode with him as escort. The emperor privately feared and respected him, as if he had thorns on his back. Later, when Zhang Anshi took Guang's place as escort, the Son of Heaven sat at ease, stretched his limbs, and felt very much at peace.)

The chapter goes on to say that Huo Guang's clan was destroyed after his death, and quotes the saying that the one whose power overawes the ruler is not kept, so that the Huo disaster began with that ride. The four-character form 芒刺在背 is the shortening of the original 若有芒刺在背. Checked on zh.Wikisource, 漢書 卷六十八, on 2026-11-09: the quotation is as above. That Wikisource text is marked as only partly proofread, so the wording is worth confirming against a printed edition.

## Standard Form
**芒刺在背**
- 如芒在背 — a common variant that says "like an awn on one's back", dropping 刺

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** máng cì zài bèi
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** mong4 ci3 zoi6 bui3
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** ぼうしざいはい
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 망자재배 (mang-ja-jae-bae)
- **Vietnamese (Hán–Việt):** mang thích tại bối

The Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese forms are compositional readings of the four characters (Korean follows the North Korean reading).

## Cultural Notes
Chinese: a standard idiom in speech and writing, often used for the pressure of being watched or judged. Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese know it through the same Book of Han story, mainly in writing. It sits beside native phrases for being on pins and needles; the picture of a thorn in the back is distinct, and the fear it names is usually of a person, not a situation.

## Example Sentences
1. 上內嚴憚之，若有芒刺在背。
2. Under the director's silent gaze, the new clerk felt thorns on her back.
3. 老闆站在身後看著，他如芒在背，連字都寫不好。

## Sentiment
Negative; anxious and uncomfortable, and a literary but common register.
