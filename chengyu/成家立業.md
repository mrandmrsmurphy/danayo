## Literal Meaning
- [成 (char)](characters/成%20(char).md) — to become
- [家](characters/家.md) — family
- [立 (char)](characters/立%20(char).md) — to establish
- [業 (char)](characters/業%20(char).md) — career, enterprise

Literally: "To establish a family and set up a career."

## Extended Meaning
成家立業 means to settle down in life — to marry and build one's livelihood. It combines two ancient moral milestones in the Confucian order of life: marriage (成家) and occupation (立業), together forming a succinct goal of completing one's social and personal duty. To 成家 is not merely to marry, but to enter the moral order — to become a spouse and eventually a parent continuing the ancestral line (孝, filial piety). To 立業 is not simply to find employment, but to establish a life of purpose and virtue — a livelihood that supports others and expresses one's character (德). Commonly used to describe the mature stage of life when one has both family and career, and often invoked as an expected life milestone or a marker of dependable adulthood.

## Source and Origin
成家 ("to form a family") appears in the **Book of Rites (《禮記·昏義》)**:

> 「昏禮者，將合二姓之好，上以事宗廟，而下以繼後世也。」
> "The marriage rite unites two families in harmony — above, to serve the ancestors; below, to continue the generations."

立業 ("to establish one's enterprise") echoes the broader Confucian concern for a stable livelihood as the foundation of moral character, articulated in **Mencius (《孟子·離婁下》)**:

> 「有恆產者有恆心。」
> "Only those who have a stable livelihood can maintain a stable heart."

Together the phrase 成家立業 took form in the Han and later dynasties as a compact statement of adult responsibility, echoing the broader Confucian progression laid out in the **Great Learning (《大學》)**: 「修身齊家治國平天下」 ("cultivate the self, regulate the family, govern the state, bring peace to the world"). 成家立業 names the middle stage of that arc — between self-cultivation and public service — the responsible fulfillment of one's earthly and familial duties.

## Variants
- 成家立业 — simplified Chinese form
- 成室立功 — a poetic variant found in Han-era texts ("to form a household and attain merit")
- Related in sense (though distinct idioms): 安家立業 ("to settle down and make a living"), 成家之道 ("the way of establishing a household"), 功成名就 ("to achieve success and fame"), 事業有成 ("to accomplish a career")

## Cultural Notes
In Daoist reflection, 成家立業 can also mark the tension between simplicity and attachment — a worldly accomplishment that can distract from the freedom of the Dao, standing as the necessary but temporary "nest" of human life rather than its ultimate goal.

Japanese せいかりつぎょう (seika ritsugyō) is a formal expression, often appearing in biographies or moral writing, paralleling the Confucian notion of 修身斉家. Korean 성가립업 (seongga rip-eop) appears in classical-style essays and Confucian family teachings, reflecting the expected life path of a mature adult. Vietnamese thành gia lập nghiệp is still widely used and valued as a traditional life goal.

## Example Sentences
1. 「有恆產者有恆心。」(《孟子·離婁下》)
2. 他已經成家立業，是個可靠的人。("He's already married and established his career — a dependable man.")
3. 남자는 성가립업하여 부모를 기쁘게 해야 한다. ("A man should marry and make his career to bring joy to his parents.")
4. Anh ấy đã thành gia lập nghiệp, cuộc sống ổn định và hạnh phúc. ("He has married and established his career — a stable, happy life.")

## Sentiment
Warm, affirming, milestone-marking. To root one's life in love and labor — commonly regarded as the true measure of maturity.
