## Literal Meaning
- [一 (char)](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one, a single
- [触](../characters/触.md) — to touch, to contact
- [即 (char)](../characters/即%20(char).md) — immediately
- [発 (char)](../characters/発%20(char).md) — to discharge, to erupt

Literally: "at one touch, immediately erupt."

## Extended Meaning
On the verge of explosion; in a highly volatile state where the slightest trigger will set off conflict or crisis. 触 denotes contact or stimulus, 即 marks immediacy, and 発 indicates discharge or eruption — the sequence compresses causation into near-simultaneity, describing no process between trigger and outcome. The phrase therefore captures instability rather than violence itself: imminence, not inevitability.

## Source and Origin
Traditionally attributed to the Ming-dynasty writer **李開先** (Li Kaixian) in his essay **《原性堂記》** (included in his collected works, 《李中麓閒居集》). Li recounts renaming a room in his home from "面山" ("Facing the Mountain") to "原性" ("Tracing Nature") after a visitor's remark stirred a twenty-year-old memory of a friend who had entrusted him with two essays before his death. He writes:

> "予方有意，觸而即發，不知客何所見，適投其機乎？"
> ("I already had this thought in mind — touched, it immediately sprang forth; I do not know what the visitor perceived, that he happened to strike exactly the right chord.")

The essay uses the clause 觸而即發 ("touched, it immediately sprang forth") to describe this sudden welling-up of long-dormant feeling; the fixed four-character form 一觸即發 later crystallized from this phrase, shifting in usage from describing a sudden emotional response to describing any tense situation poised to erupt at the slightest provocation.

## Cultural Notes
In late Ming discourse, such language often described social tension, factional rivalry, or volatile temperament, and the idiom's compressed structure suits political commentary well — it conveys that conditions are already primed, requiring only minimal provocation to erupt. Across the region today, 一触即発 is common in journalism, diplomatic reporting, and narrative prose to describe tense military standoffs, labor disputes, political crises, or interpersonal conflict. Its force lies precisely in this compression of cause and effect into a single instant: the idiom does not describe the explosion itself, only the condition immediately preceding it.

## Example Sentences
1. 兩國邊境局勢一觸即發，隨時可能爆發衝突。("The situation at the two countries' border was on a hair-trigger, liable to erupt into conflict at any moment.")
2. Negotiations broke down and the standoff became 一触即発 — one wrong word could set it off.
3. 会議は一触即発の雰囲気に包まれていた。("The meeting was wrapped in an atmosphere ready to explode at any moment.")
4. 罷工前夕，勞資雙方關係一觸即發。("On the eve of the strike, relations between labor and management were at a breaking point.")

## Sentiment
Tense, urgent, alarmed. Neutral in moral valence but heavy with anticipated danger.
