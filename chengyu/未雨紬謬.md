## Literal Meaning
- [未 (char)](../characters/未%20(char).md) — not yet
- [雨 (char)](../characters/雨%20(char).md) — rain
- [紬](../characters/紬.md) — here standing for 綢, silk cord/binding material
- [謬](../characters/謬.md) — here standing for 繆, "to bind, wind around, entangle" (not 謬's own primary "error" sense — see note below)

Literally: "Before the rain, bind fast [the windows and doors]." Dan'a'yo cites 紬 and 謬 as the vault's existing characters for this idiom's real orthography 綢繆: 紬 is a dictionary-attested variant of 綢 in its silk-fabric sense; 謬 is dictionary-attested as an interchangeable variant of 繆, but *only* for 繆's rare "error" reading (as in 紕繆) — this idiom instead needs 繆's distinct "bind, entangle" reading (móu, as in 綢繆), a different sense that 謬's own character page does not itself carry. The citation is therefore orthographically defensible but semantically atypical; readers should understand 謬 here as "bind," not "err."

## Extended Meaning
To prepare thoroughly in advance, taking precautions before trouble arises rather than scrambling to react once it has already struck. The image is concrete and domestic: a bird reinforcing its nest against a coming storm, not waiting until the rain has already begun. Used broadly for foresight and prudent planning in governance, business, education, and personal conduct alike.

## Source and Origin
**《詩經·豳風·鴟鴞》** ("The Owl," Airs of Bin, Classic of Poetry):
> 迨天之未陰雨，徹彼桑土，綢繆牖戶。
> (While the sky has not yet clouded over with rain, I strip the mulberry roots' bark and bind fast the windows and door.)

The poem is voiced by a mother bird pleading with an owl that has already taken her young, urging it not to destroy the nest itself while she works desperately to reinforce it — gathering mulberry-root bark and binding the openings tight before the rain arrives. From this concrete image of advance preparation, 綢繆 (later fixed together with 未雨 as the four-character idiom) came to mean thorough foresight and preparation in general, well beyond its literal origin in nest-repair.

## Standard Form
**未雨綢繆** (the standard orthography in real-world usage; Dan'a'yo cites the vault's existing characters 紬/謬 as documented aliases — see Literal Meaning above)
- 綢繆未雨 — a fully attested reversed-order variant, equally current
- 未雨紬繆 — an intermediate mixed form, listed among this entry's own aliases

## Pronunciations
- **Mandarin (Pinyin):** wèi yǔ chóu móu
- **Cantonese (Jyutping):** mei6 jyu5 cau4 mau4
- **Japanese (on-yomi, hiragana):** みうちゅうびゅう
- **Korean (Sino-Korean):** 미우주무 (mi-u-ju-mu)
- **Vietnamese (Hán-Việt):** vị vũ trù mưu

These reflect the real, standard pronunciation of 未雨綢繆 as actually used — chóu/mau4/ちゅう/주/trù for 綢, and móu/mau4/びゅう/무/mưu for 繆 in its "bind" reading, not 謬's own "error" reading (miù).

## Cultural Notes
A standard, widely used expression for prudence and advance planning across all five languages — common in governance, business risk management, education, and personal conduct alike. It reflects a broader classical value: the wise person anticipates disorder and prepares beforehand rather than reacting to a crisis already underway. Unlike some idioms in this set, it carries no negative or critical undertone — it is straightforwardly a compliment when applied to a person or institution's foresight.

## Example Sentences
1. 企業應該未雨綢繆，以應對未來的風險。
2. Smart governments practice _wèi yǔ chóu móu_, funding disaster preparedness long before disaster strikes.
3. 問題が起こる前に未雨綢繆することが重要だ。
4. Her habit of _vị vũ trù mưu_ meant the family was never caught off guard by a crisis.

## Sentiment
Positive; praises foresight, prudence, and thorough advance preparation.
