[Biblical Chengyu](chengyu/Biblical%20Chengyu.md)

## Literal Meaning
- [邪](../characters/邪.md) — crooked, perverse, evil
- [心 (char)](../characters/心%20(char).md) — heart, inner disposition
- [常](../characters/常.md) — continually, constantly
- [悪 (char)](../characters/悪%20(char).md) — evil, wicked

Literally: "the crooked heart is continually evil."

## Extended Meaning
A summary statement of human depravity: the persistent inclination of the human heart toward evil. It emphasizes habitual disposition rather than isolated acts — a diagnosis of the human condition rather than a description of any one wrongdoing.

## Source and Origin
This idiom condenses two key passages in Genesis:

> "And GOD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every imagination of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually." (Genesis 6:5, KJV)

> "...for the imagination of man's heart is evil from his youth..." (Genesis 8:21, KJV)

Traditional Chinese Bible translations render these as "人心裡所思想的盡都是惡" ("everything the human heart thinks is evil") and "人從幼年心裡懷著惡念" ("from youth the human heart harbors evil thoughts"). The Dan'a'yo formulation captures the repeated biblical focus on 心 ("heart") together with the temporal emphasis on continual, habitual evil (常).

## Cultural Notes
Genesis presents a striking narrative tension: in chapter 6 the pervasive corruption of humanity precipitates the Flood, and in chapter 8, after judgment has fallen, God acknowledges that the human heart remains inclined toward evil yet promises restraint in future judgment. This repetition makes a theological point — the Flood did not eradicate sin at its root, since the problem is internal rather than merely environmental — and Christian theology has drawn on these verses in articulating doctrines of original sin and total depravity. The pairing of 邪心 and 常悪 stresses a habitual moral bent rather than occasional wrongdoing; in biblical anthropology the heart is the seat of will and intention, not merely emotion. Within the Dan'a'yo Christian sphere, this chengyu often functions as a sober diagnosis of the human condition, especially when contrasted with later themes of covenant mercy and redemption.

## Example Sentences
1. 洪水之前，邪心常悪；洪水之後，亦未改其本性。("Before the Flood the heart was continually evil; after the Flood, its nature was not fundamentally changed.")
2. 「耶和華見人在地上罪惡很大，終日所思想的盡都是惡。」(創世記 6:5，和合本 Chinese Union Version)
3. Theologians cite 邪心常悪 as early scriptural grounding for the doctrine of total depravity.
4. 雖知邪心常悪，然神仍應許憐憫，不再以洪水滅世。("Though the heart is known to be continually evil, God still promised mercy, never again to destroy the world by flood.")

## Sentiment
Somber, diagnostic, theologically weighty. Conveys moral realism and underscores the depth of human fallenness.
