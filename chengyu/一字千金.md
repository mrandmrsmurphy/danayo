## Literal Meaning
- [一](../characters/一%20(char).md) — one
- [字](../characters/字%20(char).md) — character, word
- [千](../characters/千.md) — thousand
- [金](../characters/金%20(char).md) — gold

Literally: "one character worth a thousand pieces of gold."

## Extended Meaning
Writing of exceptional value and precision; words so carefully chosen that even a single character is priceless. Unlike expressions that praise verbosity or ornament, 一字千金 exalts restraint — its ideal is density of meaning, not abundance of words, where any alteration would diminish rather than improve the whole.

## Source and Origin
**《史記·呂不韋列傳》** ("Records of the Grand Historian, Biography of Lü Buwei", juan 85). The page previously named the biography of Lord Chunshen (春申君列傳); I read both chapters on wikisource and the passage is in the biography of Lü Buwei.

Lü Buwei, chancellor of Qin, kept three thousand guests and, to rival the lords of the other states, had them each write down what they knew:

> 呂不韋乃使其客人人著所聞，集論以為八覽、六論、十二紀，二十餘萬言。以為備天地萬物古今之事，號曰呂氏春秋。布咸陽市門，懸千金其上，延諸侯游士賓客有能增損一字者予千金。
> "Lü Buwei had his guests each write what they had heard, and collected the discussions into eight 覽, six 論 and twelve 紀, more than two hundred thousand characters, reckoning that they covered all matters of heaven and earth, the myriad things, past and present, and called it the 《呂氏春秋》. He had it posted at the gate of the Xianyang market, with a thousand pieces of gold hung above it, and invited the wandering scholars and guests of the lords: whoever could add or remove a single character would be given the thousand gold."

The 《史記》 does not say that anyone claimed the gold. The four-character 一字千金 condenses the anecdote.

The anecdote is performative as much as literary: the offer was less an invitation to emend than a declaration of confidence. In a culture where texts were copied by hand and authority rested on transmission, claiming immunity from correction was audacious. The hyperbole 一字千金 converts textual exactness into economic value, collapsing scholarship, authorship, and reputation into a single gesture. It also reflects a transitional moment in intellectual history: the *Lüshi Chunqiu* was a syncretic work attempting to gather and systematize rival schools of thought, so the boast of uncorrectability stakes a claim not merely to elegance but to comprehensive adequacy.

## Cultural Notes
Across the region, 一字千金 is used to praise writing, editing, or speech marked by extreme concision and weight. It appears in literary criticism, education, and rhetoric, often to commend careful wording or authoritative formulation. The tone is admiring and formal — used primarily in written and evaluative contexts rather than casual speech.

## Example Sentences
1. 此文一字千金，無可增刪。("This essay is worth a thousand gold per character — nothing can be added or removed.")
2. The editor praised the poem as *yī zì qiān jīn*, every word irreplaceable.
3. A good closing line in advertising copy is truly 一字千金.

## Sentiment
Strongly positive and admiring; a formal, literary register reserved for genuinely exceptional wording rather than everyday praise.
