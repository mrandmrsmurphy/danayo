[Dan'a'yo Chengyu](chengyu/Dan'a'yo%20Chengyu.md)
>[!Tip] A guiding principle of [[文法 - 06語用]], flowing from [[百家共承]].

## Literal Meaning
- [文](characters/文.md) — writing, style
- [体](characters/体.md) — body, form
- [並 (char)](characters/並%20(char).md) — side by side, together
- [存](characters/存.md) — to exist, persist

Literally: "Writing-forms coexist side by side."

## Extended Meaning
文体並存 states the principle of stylistic coexistence: classical elegance, formal clarity, and modern brevity are all permitted to share one linguistic framework rather than one register displacing the others. It governs how Dan'a'yo handles register — when to write in a classical, literary, or modern mode, and how speakers are expected to perceive the difference — including allowances for texting and internet-speech conventions (dropping 之, dropping 於, using 注音符号-only spelling) alongside fully classical prose. Politeness conventions borrowed from Korean and Japanese tradition also fall under this same principle of coexisting registers rather than a single mandated style.

## Source and Origin
文体並存 is a Dan'a'yo-coined idiom (単亜語), one of two named sub-principles (alongside [[詞彙兼容]]) flowing from the language's guiding pragmatic maxim [[百家共承]] ("all schools jointly inherited," see [[文法 - 06語用]]). Where 百家共承 states the general openness of Dan'a'yo's lexicon and registers to the full range of East Asian tradition and modernity, 文体並存 names specifically the stylistic dimension of that openness: no single register — classical, literary, or modern — is privileged to the exclusion of the others.

## Cultural Notes
文体並存 is grammar-policy language rather than an idiom in general circulation, cited specifically when explaining why Dan'a'yo texts can range from dense classical-style prose to casual, contracted internet-register writing without any one style being deemed the "correct" default. It sits alongside the language's other founding mottos (see [[合漢再決]], [[東亜自通]], [[覧昭和決]], [[毎字明意]], [[朝鮮正音]], [[文言現代]]) as part of the same family of design-principle statements.

## Example Sentences
1. 単亜語の文体並存の原則により、古典的な文章と口語的な文章が共存できる。("Under Dan'a'yo's principle of 文体並存, classical and colloquial writing can coexist.")
2. A formal essay and a casual text message can both be correct Dan'a'yo — 文体並存 means the register serves the occasion, not the other way around.
3. 이 언어는 문체병존의 원칙에 따라 다양한 문체를 허용한다. ("This language permits a variety of styles in accordance with the principle of 문체병존.")

## Sentiment
Inclusive, pragmatic, tolerant. Affirms that stylistic range is a design virtue, not an inconsistency to be corrected.
