## Literal Meaning
- [電](../characters/電.md) — lightning
- [光 (char)](../characters/光%20(char).md) — light, flash
- [石 (char)](../characters/石%20(char).md) — stone
- [火 (char)](../characters/火%20(char).md) — fire, spark

Literally: "lightning flash, spark struck from stone."

## Extended Meaning
Extreme speed; instantaneous action; something happening in a flash. Unlike idioms about haste or rashness, 電光石火 describes speed without judgment — it can signal efficiency, decisiveness, or sheer rapidity, with no inherent negative connotation.

## Source and Origin
The pairing of these two images appears in medieval Chan (Zen) Buddhist texts, where lightning and the spark struck from flint were conventional metaphors for sudden awakening or immediate insight, occurring without gradual mediation. The Song-dynasty **《景德傳燈錄》** (Jingde Chuandeng Lu, 1004 AD) contains the line "石火電光，已經塵劫" ("[in the time of] a spark from flint or a flash of lightning, kalpas of dust have already passed"), and the closely related **《五燈會元》** (Wudeng Huiyuan) states "此事如擊石火，似閃電光" ("this matter is like striking a spark from stone, like a flash of lightning"). The expression circulated widely in Song-era religious prose before being absorbed into general literary usage; it does not derive from a single canonical historical anecdote but from this shared metaphorical vocabulary common across Chan discourse.

## Cultural Notes
Lightning and the spark from stone both emphasize brevity and intensity — the image is not merely fast movement but sudden illumination, and in Chan contexts the metaphor often described enlightenment occurring instantaneously rather than through gradual cultivation. 電光 and 石火 are parallel images of momentary brilliance, so the compound doubles the idea of immediacy for emphasis. Over time the religious connotations faded, leaving a neutral idiom for extraordinary quickness: across the region today, 電光石火 is used in sports commentary, journalism, narrative prose, and everyday speech to describe swift action or reaction. It is stylistically elevated but not archaic, and carries no necessary spiritual meaning in modern contexts.

## Example Sentences
1. 他電光石火般出拳，對手根本來不及反應。("He threw his punch like lightning and flint-spark — his opponent had no time to react.")
2. The striker's finish was 電光石火 — the goalkeeper never saw it coming.
3. 決断は電光石火、迷いのかけらもなかった。("The decision was made in a flash, without a shred of hesitation.")
4. 電光石火の早業で、犯人はその場から姿を消した。("With lightning speed, the culprit vanished from the scene.")

## Sentiment
Positive to neutral; admiring of speed, decisiveness, or sharpness. Energetic and vivid rather than solemn.
