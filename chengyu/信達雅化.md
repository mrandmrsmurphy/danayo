## Literal Meaning
- [信](characters/信.md) — faithful
- [達 (char)](characters/達%20(char).md) — to reach, intelligible
- [雅](characters/雅.md) — elegant
- [化 (char)](characters/化%20(char).md) — -ization, transformation

Literally: "Faithful, intelligible, elegant -ization."

## Extended Meaning
信達雅化 reframes Yan Fu's three translation criteria (信達雅) as an ongoing discipline rather than a static checklist. It does not add a fourth evaluative pillar alongside faithfulness, intelligibility, and elegance; instead, appending 化 ("-ization," transformation, cultivation) raises the whole triad to a meta-level, shifting the concern from whether a given translation satisfies three separate standards to whether the translator is engaged in the disciplined, ongoing formation that makes such satisfaction possible. Under 信達雅化, translation is not a technical procedure to be checked off but successful cultural transplantation — a text made to live naturally within its new linguistic and cultural setting — and the whole project is framed as a lifelong practice rather than a one-time achievement.

## Source and Origin
信達雅化 is a Dan'a'yo-coined idiom (単亜語) built directly on a real historical source: **Yan Fu's (嚴復, 1854–1921)** famous translation triad **信達雅**, articulated in the preface to his translation of Huxley's *Evolution and Ethics* (天演論, 1898). Yan described translation as governed by three difficulties — 信 (faithfulness to the original's meaning), 達 (intelligibility and fluency in the target language), and 雅 (stylistic refinement and literary propriety) — famously insisting that "if it is not readable, it is as though it were not translated." The triad became foundational in modern Chinese translation theory and still frames debates about literalism, readability, and literary style.

信達雅化 itself is not Yan Fu's own coinage — the added 化 (used here in its modern sense as a suffix comparable to "-ization," as in 近代化 "modernization" or 合理化 "rationalization") is 単亜語's own extension, proposed as a conservative elaboration rather than a revision: it does not replace the triad or introduce a competing fourth virtue such as precision or economy, but names the disciplined, lifelong practice of becoming capable of the three virtues Yan Fu described.

## Cultural Notes
Because 信達雅化 is a Dan'a'yo extension rather than a term with independent circulation in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, or Vietnamese translation studies, it is read transparently by Sino-Xenic pronunciation in each language rather than functioning as a fixed idiom any of those traditions would recognize on its own. It is cited specifically in discussions of translation practice and of Dan'a'yo's own design philosophy, alongside the language's other founding mottos such as [[毎字明意]].

The discipline the idiom names can be framed as a recurring process: establish 信 by identifying the precise claim of the original passage; secure 達 by testing readability with someone unfamiliar with the source language; refine 雅 by determining the appropriate stylistic register; and complete 化 by reexamining every adjustment made for clarity or style to ensure it remains defensible in light of fidelity.

## Example Sentences
1. 「譯事三難：信、達、雅。」(嚴復《天演論》譯例言, 1898)
2. 好的翻譯不僅要信達雅，更要信達雅化——成為一種持續的修養。("A good translation must not only be faithful, intelligible, and elegant, but 信達雅化 — a continuous discipline.")
3. 그는 평생 신달아화의 정신으로 번역에 임했다. ("He approached translation his whole life in the spirit of 신달아화.")
4. She never called herself a finished translator; 信達雅化 was less a credential than a practice she returned to with every new page.

## Sentiment
Disciplined, aspirational, reflective. Frames translation as a lifelong craft of cultivated judgment rather than a checklist to be satisfied once and set aside.
