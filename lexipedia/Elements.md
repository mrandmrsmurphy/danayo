# Elements

Elements vocabulary covers the classical elements of pre-scientific cosmology — earth, water, fire, wood, metal, air — rather than the modern chemical elements, which have their own dedicated page (see [Periodic Table](../lexipedia/Periodic%20Table.md)). This domain also documents 五行 (Wu Xing), the Chinese Five Elements/Phases theory, since it's the specific classical-elements framework Dan'a'yo's own source languages actually use. Rosenfelder's "stone" and "ether" have no Dan'a'yo word yet, flagged inline below rather than skipped silently.

## Core Vocabulary (A1–A2)

- <ruby>[水](../words/水.md)<rt>ㄙㄨ</rt></ruby>: water.
- <ruby>[火](../words/火.md)<rt>ㄏ⺢</rt></ruby>: fire.
- <ruby>[木](../words/木.md)<rt>ㄇㄛㄎ</rt></ruby>: tree, wood.
- <ruby>[土](../words/土.md)<rt>ㄊㄛ</rt></ruby>: earth.
- <ruby>[金](../words/金.md)<rt>ㄍㄧㄇ</rt></ruby>: metal, gold.
- <ruby>[風](../words/風.md)<rt>ㄈㄨㄫ</rt></ruby>: wind — the moving air, and the classical "air" in this list; the air itself is [[空気]].
- <ruby>[空気](../words/空気.md)<rt>ㄎㄛㄫㄎㄧㄜ</rt></ruby>: air, the atmosphere — cross-listed from Substances and Astronomy.
- <ruby>[大気](../words/大気.md)<rt>ㄉㄚㄧㄎㄧㄜ</rt></ruby>: the atmosphere, the air of the sky — Chinese also uses 大氣 for "grandeur"; see the homophone callout on the word page.
- <ruby>[石](../words/石.md)<rt>ㄙㄝㄎ</rt></ruby>: stone, rock — cross-listed from Substances.
- <ruby>[岩石](../words/岩石.md)<rt>ㄚㄇㄙㄝㄎ</rt></ruby>: rock, crags — cross-listed from the vault.
- <ruby>[五行](../words/五行.md)<rt>ㄛㄏㄚㄫ</rt></ruby>: Wu Xing, the Five Elements/Phases — 木 Wood, 火 Fire, 土 Earth, 金 Metal, 水 Water, the cyclical framework underlying traditional Chinese cosmology, medicine, astrology, and divination.

## Intermediate (B1–B2)

### The Generative and Destructive Cycles

五行 isn't just a list of five substances — it's organized around two opposite cycles connecting them.

- <ruby>[相生](../words/相生.md)<rt>ㄙㄚㄫㄙㄚㄫ</rt></ruby>: mutual generation — 木 feeds 火, 火 leaves behind 土 (ash), 土 bears 金 (ore), 金 collects 水 (condensation), and 水 nourishes 木 again.
- <ruby>[相克](../words/相克.md)<rt>ㄙㄚㄫㄎㄨㄎ</rt></ruby>: mutual overcoming — 木 parts 土 (roots splitting soil), 土 dams 水, 水 extinguishes 火, 火 melts 金, and 金 (as a blade) chops 木.
- <ruby>[相乗](../words/相乗.md)<rt>ㄙㄚㄫㄙㄨㄫ</rt></ruby>: mutual multiplication — the third cycle, overacting, where one phase overwhelms another; a Dan'a'yo technical use built on Wiktionary's "multiply each other".

## Semantic Range Notes

**This page's "elements" and the Periodic Table's are different things, on purpose**: 木火土金水 are the *classical* elements — a five-part cosmological framework for describing cycles of change, not a claim about chemical composition. Wood, for instance, isn't "an element" in the modern sense at all. Keeping the two domains separate avoids conflating pre-scientific cosmology with modern chemistry, the same way English keeps "the four humors" separate from "the periodic table."

**相生/相克 are still alive in ordinary modern vocabulary**: both 상생 (sangsaeng) and 상극 (sanggeuk) are everyday Korean words — 상생 for "win-win, mutual benefit" (common in business and politics), 상극 for "a bad match, mutually incompatible things" — extended metaphorically from this exact cosmological pairing.

**Notes on sources and placement**: 相克 is supported on Wiktionary under 相剋 ("to repel one another; to be mutually destructive; to be ill-matched"); 相生's Chinese entry there has no definition and its Japanese entry is a place name, so the sense given for it here rests on the vault, not Wiktionary. The four tree-wood words (梓木, 楠木, 樺木, 杉木) read as Plants vocabulary and 乾溜 ("dry distillation") has no clear tie to the classical elements; they are kept here for now. 火炎 and 火焰 are near-duplicates.
