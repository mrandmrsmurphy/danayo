# Conflict

Conflict vocabulary runs from quarrel to war: enmity and dispute, violence and cruelty, weapons and tactics, conquest and capture, victory, defeat and loss, revenge, threat and insult, rebellion, and the peace, truce and reconciliation that follow. It also holds the defense and protection words, accusation, plots and betrayal, espionage, and — for want of a sports page — contests and sport. The organized-war side is housed on [[War]], and the killing and anger words on [[Sin]] and [[Emotions]]; see See Also. "Insult" is covered by [[侮辱]], below.

## Semantic Range Notes

**英雄 vs. 俊傑 vs. 豪傑 vs. 侠客 — four "heroes," each a different kind**: [[英雄]] is the general, unmarked word for hero. [[俊傑]] leans toward outstanding intellect and talent rather than action or military prowess. [[豪傑]] names a similarly gifted, larger-than-life figure ("magnificent" + "masterful"). [[侠客]] is narrower and more specific: the wandering, chivalrous swordsman of wuxia fiction — a righteous outsider beholden to no lord, not a hero in the general sense at all.

**喪失 vs. 損失 vs. 滅失 vs. 自失 vs. 遺 — five words for "to lose," none interchangeable**: [[喪失]] leans toward the loss of abstract qualities — confidence, rights, memory. [[損失]] is the standard term for concrete financial or material loss (damages, casualties), spanning a range from gradual erosion of value to sudden deprivation. [[滅失]] is a specifically legal term for the total loss or destruction of property. [[自失]] means "to lose oneself" — a temporary collapse of composure or bearings under shock, not a loss of property or quality at all. [[遺]] is the plainest, most general word, "to lose, leave behind."

**安慰 vs. 慰安 — the same two morphemes, reversed**: [[安慰]] ("peaceful" + "comfort") and [[慰安]] ("comfort" + "peaceful") are near-synonymous reverse-order siblings, the same pattern seen elsewhere in this vault (紹介/介紹). One caution: 慰安 is also the root of 慰安婦 ("comfort women"), the euphemism for the WWII Japanese military's forced-prostitution victims — a real historical weight worth knowing even though 慰安 alone, unmodified, is simply "to comfort, console."

**勝利 covers both "victory" and "triumph"**: English separates the two, but Dan'a'yo does not — one word serves both senses.

**威脅 vs. 脅迫 — threatening someone vs. forcing them**: [[威脅]] is the act of threatening itself, and doubles as the noun "a threat" — the more common form in both Mandarin and Korean specifically for the verb. [[脅迫]] is a step further: not just the threat but using it (or other pressure) to force or coerce someone into an action, especially in legal/criminal registers.

**威脅 vs. 脅威 — reverse-order siblings, both real**: [[威脅]] (on this page) and [[脅威]] (listed just below it) share the same two morphemes in opposite order — the same pattern as [[安慰]]/[[慰安]]. 脅威 leans toward the noun "a threat, a danger" in the abstract; 威脅 is more often the verb "to threaten," which is why it's the form used here.

**侮辱 vs. 屈辱 vs. 恥辱 — insult, humiliation-through-defeat, and disgrace**: [[侮辱]] is an act one person does to another — to insult, to humiliate on purpose. [[屈辱]] names the humiliation itself, especially one bound up with submission or defeat (屈, "to bend"). [[恥辱]] (see [[Society]]) is broader still: disgrace or shame as a state, not necessarily inflicted by anyone in particular.

**衝突 vs. 紛争 vs. 戦闘 vs. 口論 vs. 喧嘩 — a ladder of conflict**: [[衝突]] is the general clash, from a collision to a conflict of interests. [[口論]] is a spoken quarrel; [[喧嘩]] is a brawl that may come to blows. [[紛争]] is a dispute that has gone on and tangled; [[戦闘]] is fighting itself; [[戦争]] (see [[War]]) is the state of organized war.

**攻撃 vs. 襲撃 vs. 進撃 vs. 挟撃 — kinds of attack**: [[攻撃]] is the general word, for an attack of any kind. [[襲撃]] is the sudden raid on a place or a person. [[進撃]] is the advance of a force that charges forward. [[挟撃]] and [[挟攻]] attack from two sides, and [[掩襲]] is the ambush.

**暴力 vs. 暴行 vs. 暴動 vs. 虐殺 vs. 虐待 — the words for violence**: [[暴力]] is violence as such, the use of force. [[暴行]] is an act of it done to a person, an assault. [[暴動]] is violence by a crowd, a riot. [[虐殺]] is the cruel killing of many, a massacre, and [[虐待]] is the cruel treatment of one who is in one's power, abuse.

**復讐 vs. 報復 — personal revenge and retaliation**: [[復讐]] is revenge for a wrong, a deliberate, personal vengeance. [[報復]] is the strike back for a blow received, often between states or groups. The grudge that drives either is housed on [[Emotions]].

**降伏 vs. 降参 vs. 停戦 — giving up and stopping**: [[降伏]] is the formal surrender of an army or a people to an enemy. [[降参]] (see [[War]]) is the ordinary "I give up". [[停戦]] is a ceasefire by agreement, a pause in the fighting that does not end the war.

**防衛 vs. 防御 vs. 保衛 vs. 国防 — defense**: [[防衛]] is defense as a duty or a policy. [[防御]] is the defensive act or posture in a fight. [[保衛]] is to defend something dear, and [[国防]] is the defense of a nation.

**被害 vs. 傷害 vs. 負傷 — harm from three sides**: [[被害]] is harm seen from the one who suffers it (the person is the [[被害者]]). [[傷害]] is injury as an offense done by another. [[負傷]] is the state of being wounded.

**勝負 vs. 勝利 vs. 勝者 vs. 敗者 — the contest and its ends**: [[勝負]] names the contest and its result as one thing, victory or defeat. [[勝利]] is the win alone, [[敗北]] is the defeat, and [[勝者]] and [[敗者]] are the people on each side.
