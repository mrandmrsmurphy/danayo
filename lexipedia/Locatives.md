# Locatives

Locatives vocabulary covers static spatial relation — where something is, rather than which way it faces or moves. This includes general place-nouns (location, whereabouts, the space inside or outside something), the verbs of occupying and surrounding a place, and the small set of case particles that mark spatial relations grammatically. It complements [[Directions]], which covers orientation and movement (up/down, the cardinal points, forward), and overlaps [[Grammar]], which houses several of the same case particles as function words in their own right.

## Core Vocabulary (A1–A2)

- <ruby>[位置](../words/位置.md)<rt>⼔ㄧㄑㄧ</rt></ruby>: a location, place.
- <ruby>[地点](../words/地点.md)<rt>ㄉㄧㄜㄉㄝㄇ</rt></ruby>: a point, spot.
- <ruby>[境界](../words/境界.md)<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄫㄍ⼶</rt></ruby>: a boundary, zone.
- <ruby>[上](../words/上.md)<rt>ㄙ⼘ㄫ</rt></ruby>: above.
- <ruby>[前](../words/前.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄋ</rt></ruby>: front, in front of — the spatial sense specifically; see [[Calendar]] for this same word's separate temporal "prior" citation.
- <ruby>[背後](../words/背後.md)<rt>ㄅㄛㄧㄏㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>: behind, the rear.
- <ruby>[内部](../words/内部.md)<rt>ㄋㄛㄧㄅㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: the inside, interior.
- <ruby>[外部](../words/外部.md)<rt>⺢ㄧㄅㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: the outside, exterior.
- <ruby>[周囲](../words/周囲.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄛㄨㄧ</rt></ruby>: the area around something, surroundings.
- <ruby>[周辺](../words/周辺.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄛㄅㄝㄋ</rt></ruby>: the surroundings, vicinity, periphery — the area on all sides of a place or core, set apart from [[周囲]] by its sense of an outer margin (駅周辺 "around the station", 周辺機器 "peripheral devices").
- <ruby>[窮僻](../words/窮僻.md)<rt>ㄍㄨㄫㄆㄝㄎ</rt></ruby>: remote, out-of-the-way, backward — far from the centre and from main roads, and so poor and underdeveloped (the idiom 窮郷僻壌 "a remote, poor backwater"); a literary word, opposite in direction to [[周辺]]'s periphery around a core.
- <ruby>[於](../words/於.md)<rt>ㄛ</rt></ruby>: in, at, during — the general locative case particle.

**Not yet coined**: "gap" and "frame" (in the structural, bounding sense — as opposed to [[床]]'s furniture/chassis sense) all have no dedicated Dan'a'yo word yet. ("Space" as a general abstract noun is now [[空間]].)

## Intermediate (B1–B2)

### Position & Place

- <ruby>[処](../words/処.md)<rt>ㄑㄛ</rt></ruby>: a place — demonstrative in flavor ("this place"), not a general noun.
- <ruby>[所在](../words/所在.md)<rt>ㄙㄜㄐㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>: whereabouts, location — more formal and abstract than [[位置]], often used of where something *is to be found* rather than its bare coordinates.
- <ruby>[置](../words/置.md)<rt>ㄑㄧ</rt></ruby>: to put, place (something somewhere) — the verb, where [[位置]]/[[所在]]/[[処]] are all nouns.
- <ruby>[安置](../words/安置.md)<rt>ㄚㄋㄑㄧ</rt></ruby>: to settle, find a place for (lodging, enshrining).
- <ruby>[姿態](../words/姿態.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄜㄊㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>: a pose, position — of a body.
- <ruby>[定位](../words/定位.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄫ⼔ㄧ</rt></ruby>: a position — fixed or technical, the sense used for GPS-style positioning rather than a body's pose.

**Not yet coined**: "locate" (as a verb, "to find the position of") has no dedicated word — none of the position/place nouns above doubles as that verb.

### Occupying & Surrounding

- <ruby>[占](../words/占.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄇ</rt></ruby>: to occupy, possess — the plain, basic word; see [[Possession]] for its own citation there.
- <ruby>[占有](../words/占有.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄇ·⼜</rt></ruby>: to possess, occupy, hold — see [[Possession]] for its own citation there.
- <ruby>[占拠](../words/占拠.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄇㄍ⼄</rt></ruby>: to occupy, hold by force — see [[Conflict]] for its own citation there.
- <ruby>[占領](../words/占領.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄇㄌㄧㄫ</rt></ruby>: to capture, occupy, seize — the military-conquest sense; see [[Conflict]] for its own citation there.
- <ruby>[包囲](../words/包囲.md)<rt>ㄅ⼘ㄨ·ㄨㄧ</rt></ruby>: to encircle, surround — tactically, as of a siege; see [[Conflict]] for its own citation there.
- <ruby>[繞](../words/繞.md)<rt>ㄋㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: to entwine, surround, enclose — physically, as a vine or wrapping does, not tactically.

### Relational & Directional Particles

- <ruby>[之間](../words/之間.md)<rt>ㄊㄧㄍㄚㄋ</rt></ruby>: between, among.
- <ruby>[亙](../words/亙.md)<rt>ㄍㄨㄫ</rt></ruby>: across, athwart.
- <ruby>[傍](../words/傍.md)<rt>ㄆㄚㄫ</rt></ruby>: beside, close to.
- <ruby>[以](../words/以.md)<rt>ㄧ</rt></ruby>: by, using — the instrumental case particle.
- <ruby>[由](../words/由.md)<rt>⼜ㄛ</rt></ruby>: from, since.
- <ruby>[至](../words/至.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄜ</rt></ruby>: to, until.
- <ruby>[之](../words/之.md)<rt>ㄊㄧ</rt></ruby>: of — the genitive case particle; see [[Grammar]], its primary home as a function word.
- <ruby>[于](../words/于.md)<rt>ㄨ</rt></ruby>: to — the dative case particle; see [[Grammar]], its primary home as a function word.
- <ruby>[向](../words/向.md)<rt>ㄏ⼘ㄫ</rt></ruby>: toward, -ward; see [[Grammar]], its primary home as a function word.

**How Dan'a'yo says these, the CJKV way**: Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Vietnamese rarely have a separate preposition for each spatial relation. They put a *relational noun* after the landmark ("the top of the desk," "the inside of the box") and attach one case particle to the whole phrase, which is exactly Dan'a'yo's strategy: the relational noun is followed by [[於]], and since a noun phrase takes at most one case particle, the relation lives in the noun, not in a preposition. So "on," "upon," "onto," "overhead" and "upper" are all [[上]] (with [[表面]] "surface" when contact must be explicit: 机之表面於); "below" and "beneath" are [[下]]; "into" and "out" are the interior and exterior nouns [[内部]] and [[外部]] with [[向]] or [[至]]; "ahead" is [[前]]; "amid" is [[之間]]; "aside" is [[傍]]; "off," "away" and "apart" are [[離別]]'s idea of separation; "beyond" and "except" are [[以外]] and [[除外]]; "against" is [[反対]], "facing" in the sense of opposition; and "without" is [[無]], "lack." "Through" and "forth" are carried by the movement verb itself, as in Chinese and Japanese. These are strategies, not gaps.

## Semantic Range Notes

**Four "occupy" words graded by force and register, not meaning alone**: [[占]] is the plain, unmarked verb — the default. [[占有]] is more formal, closer to legal possession. [[占拠]] adds the sense of holding a place *against* something, by force or in defiance of a rightful claim. [[占領]] is specifically military: to capture and hold territory in conquest. All four are cross-cited on [[Possession]] and [[Conflict]] for their own angles (having vs. fighting for), while Locatives cites them for the shared core meaning both other domains build on: to be physically present in and command a place.

**Four "place" words split by part of speech and register, not just synonymy**: [[位置]] is the plain noun, a location or coordinate. [[所在]] is more formal and abstract — where something *is to be found*, closer to "whereabouts" than to a coordinate. [[処]] is demonstrative in flavor, closer to "this place" than to "a place" in the abstract. [[置]] alone is the verb, "to put" — the other three are all nouns. A learner reaching for "place" in Dan'a'yo needs to know which of these four angles is meant.

**包囲 and 繞 split tactical from physical surrounding**: 包囲 names a siege or encirclement — a deliberate, often military containment. 繞 names the physical fact of wrapping or enclosing, the way a vine surrounds a trellis, with no tactical dimension at all. [[周囲]], by contrast, is a noun — the area that surrounds something, not the act of surrounding it.

**之/于/向 are grammatical case particles first, locative vocabulary second**: all three are already housed as [[Grammar]]'s own function words (之 genitive "of," 于 dative "to," 向 directional "toward, -ward"). They appear here because their grammatical function *is* a spatial relation — the same particle that marks a genitive relation in a sentence is, semantically, doing locative work. This is not duplication so much as the same word doing double duty as both a piece of grammar and a piece of vocabulary, the way [[群衆]] does across [[Society]], [[Kinship]], and [[Government]].

**前's "front" is a different sense from its own "prior," not a coincidence**: [[前]] means both spatial "front" and temporal "prior, before" — [[Calendar]] cites it for the temporal sense (前日, "the day before"), while Locatives cites it here for the literal spatial sense. English keeps these apart with different words ("front" vs. "before"); Dan'a'yo, like Chinese, uses one.
