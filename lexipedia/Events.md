# Events

Events vocabulary covers happenings — beginning, continuing, ending, repeating, and the disasters, ceremonies, and crises that punctuate ordinary constancy. Several basic terms (again, always, common, forever, never, normal, occur, once, opportunity as a bare noun, pause, process, rare as a general adjective, stable, stop as a general verb, subject as a topic-marker sense, vary, role, victim) have no dedicated Dan'a'yo word yet, flagged inline below rather than skipped silently.

### Not Yet Coined

## Semantic Range Notes

**災害/災殃/禍事/禍害 are four near-synonyms without a clean split**: all four mean "disaster, calamity" with no sharp register or scope distinction documented between them — a genuine case of redundancy inherited from the source languages rather than a hidden nuance.

**絶後 only covers the narrow half of "never"**: it means "never to happen again" — a specific claim about non-recurrence — not the general adverb "never" (as in "I have never done this"), which remains uncoined.

**間諜 fills "agent" only by narrowing it**: Rosenfelder's "agent" means anyone who acts or is empowered to act on another's behalf; 間諜 specifically means a spy or intelligence operative. It's placed here because it's the closest existing word, not because the senses truly match.

## Cognitive Metaphors in Event Language

This domain's original planning notes flagged three conceptual metaphors (in the Lakoff/Johnson sense) to watch for while building it out. Checked against what's actually on this page and in the vault:

**TIME IS SPACE — strongly confirmed**: this is the clearest of the three, and this page's own vocabulary demonstrates it directly. [[際]] means "border" before it means "occasion" — a spatial edge extended to a temporal/circumstantial one (its own attested compounds 交際 "socialize" and 国際 "international" both build on the same "boundary between things" image, extended in yet another direction). [[頃]]'s Japanese reading ころ threads the same needle: this の頃, "these days," literalizes time as a nearby place. Outside this page, [[前]] ("front") is the textbook case — its own Notes already document the character heading "an enormous compound family for both spatial 'front' ... and temporal 'before, previous'" — exactly the same front/back-as-before/after mapping that produces English "look forward to" and "the week ahead."

**EVENTS ARE OBJECTS — present, but only partially evidenced here**: [[番]] ("a time, turn, occasion") already treats a single occurrence as a countable unit, grammatically parallel to counting physical objects — the same move English makes distinguishing "three times" from "three cups." A fuller confirmation (idioms like "seize an opportunity," treating 机会 as something graspable) would need independent verification against this vault's other event/opportunity words before being stated as established fact here.

**NON-VARIATION IS STANDING — checked, and it doesn't hold for this vault's own etymology**: the expected pattern (matching English "stable," from Latin *stare*, "to stand") would predict [[恒常]]'s root character deriving from an upright/standing image. It doesn't: 恒's own etymology is 心 ("heart") + 亙 ("the moon's waxing and waning phases") — constancy imagined through the *reliability of a recurring cycle*, not through fixed uprightness. [[立]] ("to stand") independently exists in this vault and is fully productive (獨立/独立, "independence"; 成立, "come into existence") — but "establishing, founding" is a different metaphor from "constancy," and neither of 立's own attested compounds means "constant, unchanging." This is worth recording as a real finding rather than silently forcing the expected pattern: this specific vocabulary doesn't confirm the metaphor, and a different one (constancy-as-cyclical-reliability) does the same job instead.
