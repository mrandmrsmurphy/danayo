# Clothing

Clothing vocabulary covers garments, the materials they're made from, and the textile production and care that turns raw fiber into wearable cloth — along with cosmetics and a few social-status metaphors ("white-collar," "blue-collar") built on garment vocabulary. A large share of basic garment terms (cloak, coat, fashion, rag, robe, shirt, blouse, skirt, glove, stocking, sandal, poncho, loincloth, veil, loom, lace, wool) have no dedicated Dan'a'yo word yet, flagged inline below rather than skipped silently — this domain's specific/historical vocabulary has grown faster than its basic-garment core.

## Semantic Range Notes

**裸 does double duty for "naked" and "to strip"**: the same word covers both the state (naked) and the verb (to undress) — Rosenfelder lists these as separate entries, but Dan'a'yo doesn't distinguish them.

**着 vs. 戴 — a real verb split by body zone**: 着 is the general verb "to wear" (clothing on the body), while 戴 specifically means to wear something *on top* — a hat, a crown, glasses, jewelry. English collapses both into "wear"; Dan'a'yo, like its source languages, keeps them apart.

**白領/藍領 are calques of the English metaphor, not native coinages**: both are 20th-century loan-translations of "white-collar"/"blue-collar," describing office/professional vs. manual labor — the collar becomes a synecdoche for the whole class of worker. See also [[Work]], which covers labor vocabulary more broadly.

**黼黻 is a genuine `#cranberry` pair**: both halves are independently bound characters legitimized only by this exact compound — neither 黼 nor 黻 stands alone anywhere else in the vault.
