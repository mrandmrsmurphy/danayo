# Water

Water vocabulary in Dan'a'yo covers the substance itself and its everyday states and uses, then broadens into the bodies of water and coastal geography it forms — lakes, rivers, seas, and the shapes coastlines take, overlapping with [[Geography]] — and finally into seafaring, the most specialized and least-developed layer of the domain.

## Core Vocabulary (A1–A2)

### Water & Its States

- <ruby>[水](../words/水.md)<rt>ㄙㄨ</rt></ruby>: water — in chemical notation $軽_二養$ (see [Periodic Table](../lexipedia/Periodic%20Table.md)).
- <ruby>[水中](../words/水中.md)<rt>ㄙㄨㄐㄨㄫ</rt></ruby>: underwater.
- <ruby>[水面](../words/水面.md)<rt>ㄙㄨ·ㄇ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby>: water surface.
- <ruby>[氷水](../words/氷水.md)<rt>ㄅㄧㄫㄙㄨ</rt></ruby>: ice.
  - **Note**: literally "ice water" — [[氷]] ("ice") is a bound character in Dan'a'yo, never appearing independently; 氷水 is its only legitimizing compound, and the closest thing Dan'a'yo has to a bare word for "ice" (confirmed by the same resolution already reached for Swadesh item #165).
- <ruby>[液体](../words/液体.md)<rt>⼶ㄎㄊㄝㄧ</rt></ruby>: liquid.
- <ruby>[液化](../words/液化.md)<rt>⼶ㄎㄏ⺢</rt></ruby>: to liquefy, liquefaction — to turn into a liquid, from [[液体]]; attested in Mandarin, Cantonese, Japanese and Korean, with Vietnamese using a descriptive phrase (sự hoá lỏng).
- <ruby>[気体](../words/気体.md)<rt>ㄎㄧㄜㄊㄝㄧ</rt></ruby>: gas — with [[液体]] and [[固体]], the three ordinary states of matter.
- <ruby>[固体](../words/固体.md)<rt>ㄍㄛ·ㄊㄝㄧ</rt></ruby>: solid.
- <ruby>[蒸汽](../words/蒸汽.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄫㄎㄧㄜ</rt></ruby>: steam, vapor.
- <ruby>[蒸](../words/蒸.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄫ</rt></ruby>: to steam — the verb counterpart of [[蒸汽]] above.
- <ruby>[湿](../words/湿.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄆ</rt></ruby>: wet, damp.
- <ruby>[湿度](../words/湿度.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄆㄉㄛ</rt></ruby>: humidity — the degree of wetness in the air.
- <ruby>[乾燥](../words/乾燥.md)<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄋㄙㄚㄨ</rt></ruby>: dry, arid.

### Waves & Movement of Water

- <ruby>[波浪](../words/波浪.md)<rt>ㄅㄚㄌㄚㄫ</rt></ruby>: wave(s), undulation.
- <ruby>[怒涛](../words/怒涛.md)<rt>ㄋㄛㄉㄚㄨ</rt></ruby>: surging waves.

- <ruby>[滴](../words/滴.md)<rt>ㄉㄝㄎ</rt></ruby>: to drip.
- <ruby>[洒](../words/洒.md)<rt>ㄙ⺢ㄧ</rt></ruby>: to splash, to sprinkle, to spray.
- <ruby>[注入](../words/注入.md)<rt>ㄐㄨ·ㄋㄧㄆ</rt></ruby>: to pour (into).
- <ruby>[汲](../words/汲.md)<rt>ㄎㄨㄆ</rt></ruby>: to draw water.
- <ruby>[没](../words/没.md)<rt>ㄇㄛㄊ</rt></ruby>: to drown, to sink.
- <ruby>[堰堤](../words/堰堤.md)<rt>ㄝㄋㄙㄝ</rt></ruby>: dam.

### Cleaning & Wiping

- <ruby>[洗](../words/洗.md)<rt>ㄙㄝㄋ</rt></ruby>: to wash, to rinse.
- <ruby>[洗濯](../words/洗濯.md)<rt>ㄙㄝㄋㄉㄚㄎ</rt></ruby>: to launder — specifically clothes-washing, narrower than [[洗]]'s general "to wash."
- <ruby>[沐浴](../words/沐浴.md)<rt>ㄇㄛㄎ·⼄ㄎ</rt></ruby>: to bathe, cleanse — the body-washing specialization, alongside [[洗濯]]'s clothes-washing one.
- <ruby>[清潔](../words/清潔.md)<rt>ㄑㄧㄫㄍㄝㄊ</rt></ruby>: clean, pure.
- <ruby>[擦拭](../words/擦拭.md)<rt>ㄑㄚㄊㄙㄧㄎ</rt></ruby>: to wipe.
- <ruby>[抹](../words/抹.md)<rt>ㄇㄚㄊ</rt></ruby>: to rub, to wipe — a shorter, more general near-synonym of [[擦拭]] above.
- <ruby>[掃除](../words/掃除.md)<rt>ㄙㄚㄨㄐㄝㄧ</rt></ruby>: to sweep, to clean up.

### Swimming & Floating

Cross-listed from [Movement](../lexipedia/Movement.md) and [Sin](../lexipedia/Sin.md), where these words are also placed.

- <ruby>[水泳](../words/水泳.md)<rt>ㄙㄨㄨㄧㄫ</rt></ruby>: swimming (the activity).
- <ruby>[泳](../words/泳.md)<rt>ㄨㄧㄫ</rt></ruby>: to swim.
- <ruby>[浮](../words/浮.md)<rt>ㄅㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>: to float, to waft.

## Intermediate (B1–B2)

- <ruby>[湖水](../words/湖水.md)<rt>ㄏㄛㄨㄙㄨ</rt></ruby>: lake.
- <ruby>[水位](../words/水位.md)<rt>ㄙㄨ⼔ㄧ</rt></ruby>: the water level — the height of water in a river, lake, reservoir or tide gauge against a fixed reference point (the basis of flood warnings); "position of the water", where [[湖水]] is the lake itself.
- <ruby>[川](../words/川.md)<rt>ㄑ⺢ㄋ</rt></ruby>: river.

- <ruby>[青海](../words/青海.md)<rt>ㄑㄝㄫㄏㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>: Qinghai Lake (Kokonor), the large salt lake of western China; the same spelling is the Chinese province Qinghai, listed on [China](China.md).

- <ruby>[湧](../words/湧.md)<rt>⼄ㄫ</rt></ruby>: to gush, rush up.
- <ruby>[流水](../words/流水.md)<rt>ㄌ⼜ㄙㄨ</rt></ruby>: flowing water; by extension, the inevitable passage of events (高山流水, "high mountains and flowing water," symbolizing deep mutual understanding between friends).
- <ruby>[流域](../words/流域.md)<rt>ㄌ⼜ㄨㄧㄎ</rt></ruby>: a river basin, watershed.
- <ruby>[小河](../words/小河.md)<rt>ㄙㄛㄏㄚ</rt></ruby>: stream.
- <ruby>[河川](../words/河川.md)<rt>ㄏㄚㄑ⺢ㄋ</rt></ruby>: rivers.
- <ruby>[小川](../words/小川.md)<rt>ㄙㄛㄑ⺢ㄋ</rt></ruby>: small stream, brook.
- <ruby>[川口](../words/川口.md)<rt>ㄑ⺢ㄋㄎㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: river mouth, estuary.
- <ruby>[渓流](../words/渓流.md)<rt>ㄎㄝㄧㄌ⼜</rt></ruby>: mountain stream.
- <ruby>[急流](../words/急流.md)<rt>ㄍㄧㄆㄌ⼜</rt></ruby>: rapids, torrent — fast, turbulent water; contrast the calm [[流水]].
- <ruby>[運河](../words/運河.md)<rt>ㄨㄋㄏㄚ</rt></ruby>: canal — an artificial waterway cut for transport or irrigation.
- <ruby>[噴水](../words/噴水.md)<rt>ㄆㄛㄋㄙㄨ</rt></ruby>: fountain.
- <ruby>[温泉](../words/温泉.md)<rt>ㄛㄆㄐ⼔ㄋ</rt></ruby>: hot spring — natural groundwater heated by the earth.
- <ruby>[湯池](../words/湯池.md)<rt>ㄊㄚㄫㄐㄨㄧ</rt></ruby>: city moat; hot springs (literary).
- <ruby>[池](../words/池.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄧ</rt></ruby>: pond, reservoir.
- <ruby>[渉](../words/渉.md)<rt>ㄙㄝㄆ</rt></ruby>: ford (a shallow river crossing).
- <ruby>[井堰](../words/井堰.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄫㄝㄋ</rt></ruby>: a weir — smaller-scale than [[堰堤]], associated with a well or irrigation channel rather than damming a river outright.
- <ruby>[井戸](../words/井戸.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄫㄏㄛ</rt></ruby>: a water well — a deliberately "very Japanese" entry (not a real Mandarin/Cantonese compound), most familiar in 井戸端会議, "well-side chat," gossip among neighbors.
- <ruby>[灌漑](../words/灌漑.md)<rt>ㄍ⺢ㄋㄍㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>: irrigation.
- <ruby>[溝](../words/溝.md)<rt>ㄍㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: a ditch, drain — a man-made water channel, distinct from [[井堰]]'s weir and the large municipal sewer ([[下水道]], also on [[Buildings]]).
- <ruby>[排水溝](../words/排水溝.md)<rt>ㄆㄚㄧㄙㄨㄍㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: a gutter, culvert.
- <ruby>[下水道](../words/下水道.md)<rt>ㄏㄚㄙㄨㄉㄚㄨ</rt></ruby>: sewer, drainage channel — the large, usually covered municipal system, contrast [[溝]] (a ditch) and [[排水溝]] (a gutter or culvert).

### Cleanliness & Flow

- <ruby>[乾浄](../words/乾浄.md)<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄋㄐㄝㄫ</rt></ruby>: clean, neat, tidy.
  - **Note**: near-synonym of [[清潔]] above — 乾浄 builds the sense of "clean" from "dry" (dryness implies the absence of residue), and skews more formal/literary in Japanese, dated in Korean, than its rival.
- <ruby>[澄清](../words/澄清.md)<rt>ㄑㄚㄫㄑㄧㄫ</rt></ruby>: clear, limpid; to clarify.
  - **Note**: also near [[清潔]], but about clarity/limpidity rather than general cleanliness — clear water, not necessarily clean water.
- <ruby>[清澈](../words/清澈.md)<rt>ㄑㄧㄫㄐㄝㄊ</rt></ruby>: clear, limpid (of water specifically).
  - **Note**: narrower than [[澄清]] — 澈's own radical 氵 marks it as a water-word specifically, describing water (or by extension sky, sound, a voice) so clear one can see straight through it.
- <ruby>[淋](../words/淋.md)<rt>ㄌㄧㄇ</rt></ruby>: to drain, to drip.
  - **Note**: near-synonym of [[滴]] above — 淋 leans toward draining/pouring over, 滴 toward a single drop.
- <ruby>[漏](../words/漏.md)<rt>ㄌㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: to leak.
- <ruby>[滲漏](../words/滲漏.md)<rt>ㄙㄛㄇㄌㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: to seep, to leak — specifically leaking through a barrier rather than an open leak.
- <ruby>[滲入](../words/滲入.md)<rt>ㄙㄛㄇㄋㄧㄆ</rt></ruby>: to seep into, soak in, infiltrate — liquid (or influence) working its way inward; the inward counterpart of [[滲出]]'s seeping out.
- <ruby>[滲出](../words/滲出.md)<rt>ㄙㄛㄇㄑㄨㄊ</rt></ruby>: to seep out, exude — liquid slowly working its way outward through a surface; the outward counterpart of [[滲入]].
- <ruby>[滲透](../words/滲透.md)<rt>ㄙㄛㄇㄊㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: to seep through, permeate; osmosis — liquid passing all the way through a membrane or material, and figuratively an idea or influence permeating a society.
- <ruby>[浸](../words/浸.md)<rt>ㄑㄧㄇ</rt></ruby>: to immerse, dunk, soak.
- <ruby>[泡沫](../words/泡沫.md)<rt>ㄆㄚㄨㄇㄚㄊ</rt></ruby>: foam, bubbles, froth — also the stand-in that legitimizes the bound character [[泡]]; figuratively, something showy that bursts without lasting.

### Coastal & Maritime

- <ruby>[灘](../words/灘.md)<rt>ㄊㄚㄋ</rt></ruby>: bank, shoal — specifically a sandbar or shallow river ford, narrower still than either [[岸]] or [[浜]].

- <ruby>[海上](../words/海上.md)<rt>ㄏㄚㄧㄙ⼘ㄫ</rt></ruby>: marine, maritime, on the sea — broader than [[沿海]]'s specifically coastal sense, covering the open sea as well.
- <ruby>[浦](../words/浦.md)<rt>ㄆㄛ</rt></ruby>: riverbank, shore — a near-duplicate of [[岸]]/[[浜]], but also extends to "river mouth, delta," resolving this page's own previously-flagged "delta" gap.
- <ruby>[畔](../words/畔.md)<rt>ㄅㄚㄋ</rt></ruby>: ridge, boundary — another near-duplicate of [[岸]]/[[浜]] in its common "waterside" extension (湖畔, "lakeside"; 河畔, "riverside"), though its root sense is a field-ridge boundary rather than a water's edge specifically.
- <ruby>[海口](../words/海口.md)<rt>ㄏㄚㄧ·ㄎㄛㄨ</rt></ruby>: river mouth, estuary; seaport — a Chinese common noun (literally "sea mouth"), also figuratively "to boast"; the same spelling is the city of Haikou, listed on [China](../lexipedia/China.md).
- <ruby>[海南](../words/海南.md)<rt>ㄏㄚㄧ·ㄋㄚㄇ</rt></ruby>: the southern sea and the coastal land beside it — a Japanese common noun (literally "sea south"); the same spelling is the Chinese island and province Hainan, listed on [China](China.md).
- <ruby>[港湾](../words/港湾.md)<rt>ㄏㄛㄫ⺢ㄇ</rt></ruby>: harbor.
- <ruby>[黄海](../words/黄海.md)<rt>ㄏ⺢ㄫㄏㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>: Yellow Sea.
- <ruby>[勃海](../words/勃海.md)<rt>ㄅㄛㄊㄏㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>: Bohai Sea, the inner gulf of the Yellow Sea.

## Semantic Range Notes

The clearest genuine split in this domain is between [[岸]] and [[浜]], both glossing "bank." 岸 is the general, unmarked word for a bank or shore of any kind — river, lake, or sea — free enough in Classical usage to stand as a Dan'a'yo word on its own. 浜 (a simplified form of 濱, "the water's edge") leans specifically toward the coast, the sea's edge, rather than an inland riverbank; the two overlap rather than cleanly dividing the space, since 浜's own stored glosses include "bank" too, but a learner reaching for a plain riverbank should default to 岸. A third word, [[灘]], narrows further still to a sandbar or shallow ford — a specific feature rather than a general term, and never a substitute for either of the other two.

The archipelago cluster shows a cleaner three-way split by scope and register. [[群島]] ("a group of islands") is the standard, unmarked cross-linguistic term — the one every source language reaches for by default. [[列島]] narrows to a specifically linear or elongated chain, the word used for Japan's own archipelago (日本列島) rather than islands scattered without a clear axis. [[島屿]] is the broadest of the three: not "archipelago" in the geographic sense at all, but a collective noun for islands and islets of every size, built from 島 (island, any size) paired with 屿 (specifically an islet) — a classical synonym-doubling pattern (連綿詞) that asserts completeness rather than adding new information. A learner should reach for 群島 by default, 列島 only for a genuinely chain-shaped group, and 島屿 when the point is "islands in general" rather than any one grouping.

Two words in this domain do real double duty rather than leaving a gap unfilled. [[船舶]] covers both "boat" and "ship" — Dan'a'yo draws no lexical line between vessel sizes the way English sometimes does — and is also the `stand_in` that legitimizes the otherwise-bound character [[船]]. [[航行]] likewise covers both "to sail" and "navigation," treating the two as one activity rather than two. This pattern of bound characters resolving into a single compound that then has to cover more semantic ground than its English counterparts recurs across the page: [[氷]] ("ice") and [[波]] ("wave") are both bound, each legitimized by exactly one compound ([[氷水]], [[波浪]]) that consequently has to stand in for the bare noun despite literally meaning something narrower ("ice water") or broader ("waves" plural) than the English gloss. [[小舟]] and [[艇]] show the same specificity difference on the small-boat end: 小舟 is tied to rivers and lakes specifically, while 艇 is unmarked and productive (the base of 潜水艇, "submarine"). [[沈没]] is a third instance of Dan'a'yo choosing emphatic redundancy over economy — it welds together two characters that already both mean "sink" ([[没]] is one of them, usable on its own) into the single, unambiguous word for a vessel going under, rather than reusing 没 alone for that specific disaster sense.

The most striking cluster on the page is the three-way flood variant on the Advanced tier: [[氾濫]], [[汎濫]], and [[泛濫]] all mean "flood, overflow," built on three near-synonymous characters (氾, 汎, 泛) that converge on the identical meaning in every daughter language. What keeps all three alive in Dan'a'yo rather than collapsing to one is a structural fact rather than a semantic one: each compound is the `stand_in` that legitimizes a different bound character — 氾濫 legitimizes 氾, 汎濫 legitimizes 濫 (not 汎, which is separately free), and 泛濫 legitimizes 泛. 氾 and 汎 happen to share an identical Dan'a'yo reading (ㄈㄚㄇ) despite being visually and etymologically distinct characters, a genuine coincidental homophone; 泛's own reading (ㄈㄧㄇ) diverges from both. [[洪水]] sits apart from this cluster entirely — it's the everyday, unmarked word every learner should default to, built on an unrelated character (洪) and carrying no orthographic-variant baggage.

The drainage vocabulary now forms a three-step scale by size and enclosure. A [[溝]] is a ditch, an open man-made channel; an [[排水溝]] is a gutter or culvert, a purpose-built drain along a road or under it; and [[下水道]] is the large, usually covered municipal sewer. Japanese uses 下水道 also for a rainwater drain, and Dan'a'yo takes that wider sense. Springs split the same way as the shore words: [[温泉]] is the literal hot spring, while [[源泉]] has drifted to "source, fountainhead," and a plain cold spring remains uncoined.

The cleaning and leaking vocabulary each split into a base word plus one or more register-specific near-synonyms, the same pattern seen elsewhere on the page. [[清潔]] is the everyday "clean," with [[乾浄]] (clean via the "dry implies spotless" logic, skewing literary in Japanese), [[澄清]] (clarity/limpidity in general), and [[清澈]] (clarity specifically of water, marked by 澈's own water-radical) as three progressively narrower near-synonyms. [[洗]] ("to wash," general) narrows to [[洗濯]] ("to launder," clothes specifically), the same base-plus-specialization shape. [[擦拭]] ("to wipe") has a shorter, more general counterpart in [[抹]]. On the leaking side, [[滴]] ("to drip," a single drop) contrasts with [[淋]] ("to drain," a continuous pour); [[漏]] ("to leak," general) contrasts with [[滲漏]] ("to seep," specifically through a barrier). A third leak-word, [[漏洩]], has drifted almost entirely away from liquids in modern usage — see See Also.

**Idiomatic uses**:
- [[波及]] — literally "the wave reaches," used for an effect rippling outward to affect something else (a financial crisis, a scandal, a policy change); the figurative extension of [[波浪]]'s own image is still fully alive rather than faded to abstraction.
- [[漏洩]] — literally "double leak" (pairing two characters that both independently mean "to leak"), used almost exclusively today for information rather than liquid: 情報漏洩 ("information leak"), 機密漏洩 ("leak of classified material") — the literal water sense survives mainly in [[漏]] and [[滲漏]] instead.
