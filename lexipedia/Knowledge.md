# Knowledge

Knowledge vocabulary spans cognition and truth (knowing, proving, doubting), the evidentiary and investigative apparatus built on top of it, and the whole institutional ladder of formal education from kindergarten through the doctorate. Because Dan'a'yo also functions as a working auxiliary language, this domain needed a small, deliberately cross-checked layer of epistemic-modality vocabulary — a real gradient from "necessary" down through "probable" to "merely possible" — rather than leaving certainty and probability to context the way conversational usage often does.

## Intermediate (B1–B2)


### Certainty, Possibility & Probability

A deliberately coined epistemic-modality gradient — see Semantic Range Notes for the full writeup, including a genuine homophone pair this cluster surfaced.

- <ruby>[確実](../words/確実.md)<rt>ㄎㄚㄎㄙㄧㄊ</rt></ruby>: certain, true, reliable; also "sure."
- <ruby>[必然](../words/必然.md)<rt>ㄅㄧㄊㄋ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby>: necessary, inevitable — the top of the epistemic gradient, paired with [[或然]].
- <ruby>[可能](../words/可能.md)<rt>ㄎㄜ·ㄋㄜㄫ</rt></ruby>: possible, may, might, perhaps.
- <ruby>[或然](../words/或然.md)<rt>ㄏㄛㄎㄋ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby>: probable, contingent — pairs with [[必然]] "necessary" the way classical logic contrasts the two.
- <ruby>[蓋然](../words/蓋然.md)<rt>ㄍㄚㄧㄋ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby>: highly probable, likely, plausible.
- <ruby>[概然](../words/概然.md)<rt>ㄍㄚㄧㄋ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby>: generally so, roughly true, broadly speaking — contrasts with "certain" above by degree of approximation, not likelihood.
- <ruby>[大略](../words/大略.md)<rt>ㄉㄚㄧㄌ⼘ㄎ</rt></ruby>: roughly, in broad outline — an approximating adverb, and as a noun a rough outline or plan (雄才大略, "heroic talent and grand strategy"); about the level of detail rather than [[概然]]'s degree of probability.
- <ruby>[大概](../words/大概.md)<rt>ㄉㄚㄧㄍㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>: for the most part, generally, approximately; also, loosely, "probably" — the everyday adverb in the same approximation register as [[概然]], not a graded-likelihood word.
- <ruby>[偶然](../words/偶然.md)<rt>ㄛㄨㄋ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby>: accidental, coincidental, random — chance itself, distinct from this cluster's own graded *likelihood* vocabulary (偶然性, "randomness, contingency").
- <ruby>[当然](../words/当然.md)<rt>ㄉㄚㄫㄋ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby>: of course, natural, inevitable — an epistemic-certainty adverb sitting near the top of this gradient, alongside [[必然]].

## Semantic Range Notes

**A deliberately built epistemic-modality gradient.** Dan'a'yo needed a clean scale from necessity down to bare possibility, and assembled it from real, independently attested CJKV vocabulary rather than inventing one: [[必然]] "necessary, inevitable" at the top, then [[蓋然]] "highly probable, likely" (the base of the real 蓋然性/がいぜんせい/개연성, the standard philosophy-and-statistics term for "not certain, but quite likely"), then [[或然]] "probable, contingent" (the base of 或然率/或然性, the classical-logic term paired against 必然 the way Aristotelian modality contrasts necessary and contingent), then bare [[可能]] "possible, may, might," with [[概然]] "generally so, roughly true" sitting to the side as an approximation register rather than a probability register. This last pair, 蓋然 and 概然, is a genuine four-way homophone: 蓋 and 概 share the identical reading in Mandarin (gài), Japanese on'yomi (GAI), Korean (개), and now Dan'a'yo (gai/개/ㄍㄚㄧ) — real, documented source of learner confusion in Japanese and Chinese. Vietnamese is the one daughter language that already keeps them apart (蓋 = *cái*, 概 = *khái*), which is what licenses Dan'a'yo to lean into the real ambiguity and cleanly split the two senses rather than collapsing them into true synonyms.

**士 keeps only its "scholar" sense here.** The character's fuller history runs from "war axe" to "soldier, officer" to "scholar, learned gentleman" (韩语/越南语/日本語 all still use its 士-suffix productively for both registers — 武士 "warrior," 博士 "doctor"), but Dan'a'yo's own stored word carries only the scholarly sense; the warrior-adjacent compounds are housed on [War](../lexipedia/War.md), not duplicated here.

**勉強 is the clearest cross-linguistic drift in this domain.** In Mandarin, Cantonese, and Vietnamese, 勉強/miễn cưỡng keeps its original, transparent sense — "to force oneself, reluctant, strained." Japanese べんきょう narrowed the same written compound to simply "to study" — the everyday word a Japanese schoolchild uses for homework — with "to force oneself" surviving only as a secondary sense (勉強する, "to give a discount," literally "to strain oneself to lower the price"). Dan'a'yo follows the Japanese narrowing for this domain's "study" slot precisely because it is the one attested sense that is actually about learning.

**単位 is a genuine dual-domain word.** Its everyday, most frequent sense is "a unit of measurement" (meters, kilograms, yen) — that belongs primarily to [Measurement](../lexipedia/Measurement.md), not duplicated here. Only its secondary, Japanese/Korean-specific academic sense ("a unit of credit earned toward a degree") is native to Knowledge, placed here alongside [[学位]] specifically for that reason. [[域]] (above) is a similar case, split between an abstract "field of knowledge" sense (native here) and a literal "territory" sense (reserved for Geography).

**経 is the bound root behind the Five Classics.** [[経]] itself covers "classic text," "channel" (経絡, a bodily meridian — [[Body]]'s domain, not duplicated here), and "warp" (the original loom-thread sense); the classic-text sense is what ties it to [[礼記]]/[[易経]]/[[書経]]/[[楽経]]/[[詩経]], already covered under [[Art]]'s own Language Arts section — cross-referenced rather than re-described here.

**知, 知識, 智慧 and 知性 divide knowing, knowledge and wisdom.** [[知]] is the verb "to know" and the root. [[知識]] is knowledge held, the stock of what is known, and [[常識]] is the common stock everyone is expected to share. [[智慧]] is wisdom, the judgment that comes of knowledge, and [[知性]] is intelligence as a quality of mind. [[上知]] and [[智者]] name the wise person. [[無知]] is the opposite of knowing, and [[無学]], without schooling, is a different lack.

**真実, 真理, 事実, 実際, 実情 and 真正 sort the kinds of truth.** [[事実]] is a fact, a thing that is the case. [[真実]] is truth or reality in the wider sense, and [[真理]] is truth in the high, ultimate sense. [[実際]] is the actual situation, "in fact", and [[実情]] is the actual state of affairs behind appearances. [[真正]] is genuine, as against false. [[虚偽]] is a falsehood and [[錯誤]] is an error, which are not the same: one is a lie, the other a mistake.

**Inquiry runs from looking into a thing to proving it.** [[調査]] is to look into, [[検査]] to inspect, [[考察]] to investigate and observe, and [[研究]] is research. [[検証]] is to check a claim against evidence, and [[証明]] is to prove it. [[推測]] is a guess, [[推定]] a reasoned presumption held until corrected, and [[推論]] is inference by rule from a [[前提]] to a [[結論]].

**演䋇 and 帰納 are the two directions of inference.** [[演䋇]] (演繹) reasons from a general principle down to the cases it covers, and [[帰納]], housed on Mind, reasons from particular cases up to a general rule. Both are kinds of [[推論]].

**目次, 目録 and 索引 divide the guides to a book.** [[目次]] is the table of contents in the order of the book, [[目録]] is a list, catalogue or inventory of things, and [[索引]] is the index to topics at the back. Mandarin 目錄 covers both the table of contents and the list, and Dan'a'yo splits the two senses between 目次 and 目録.

**仮説 and 仮定 are not the same.** [[仮説]] is a hypothesis, a proposal to be tested by [[実験]] and settled by [[検証]]. 仮定, housed on Mind, is an assumption made for the sake of argument. The first is a claim about the world waiting to be checked, and the second is a supposition one grants.

**理解, 了解 and 誤解 form a short path of understanding.** [[理解]] is to understand, [[了解]] is a near-synonym that in Japanese has narrowed to a stock "understood!" acknowledgment, and [[誤解]] is to take something wrongly. [[錯誤]] is an error of act, where 誤解 is an error of reading.

**分析 and 解析 differ in register.** [[分析]] is to analyze in general, and [[解析]] is the more technical, mathematical sense, as in analysis in mathematics. [[分類]] sorts into kinds, [[区分]] sets apart and [[区別]] distinguishes.

**The teacher and student words keep register apart.** [[先生]] is the general "teacher" and the polite title "Mr.", [[教師]] is a teacher as a role, [[教員]] a schoolteacher on the staff, and [[教授]] both to teach and a professor. [[夫子]] is the Master, Confucius. For the learner, [[学生]] is a student, [[生徒]] a pupil, [[学習者]] a learner and [[同学]] a schoolmate; [[同窓]] is the wider body of alumni.

**The school names are a ladder.** [[幼稚園]] is kindergarten; [[小学校]] elementary school; [[中学校]] middle school; [[高校]] secondary school; then [[大学]] (university) and [[大学院]] (graduate school). [[大学校]] is the full institutional form of "university", and the bare [[大学]] is the abbreviation. The classical names sit outside the ladder: [[太学]] the Imperial Academy and [[書院]] the regional academy.

**Exams end in a pass or a fail.** [[入試]] is the entrance exam, [[受験]] is sitting it, and [[試験]] is an exam in general. The result is [[合格]], to pass, or [[落第]], to fail; [[及第]] is the older word for passing the imperial examinations. [[採点]] is the grading, and the degree that follows is [[学士]], [[碩士]] or [[博士]], recorded on a [[卒業証書]]. Japanese says 修士 for the master's degree, where Dan'a'yo takes the form shared by Chinese, Korean and Vietnamese.

**無知, 無学 and 愚 are three different lacks.** [[無知]] is ignorance, a lack of knowledge. [[無学]] is being unlearned, a lack of schooling. [[愚]], housed on Valuation, is foolishness, a lack of judgment. A person can be learned and foolish, or unschooled and wise.
