# Grammar

Grammar vocabulary describes Dan'a'yo's own structure: the closed-class function words (conjunctions, modals, particles, pronouns, deictics, interrogatives) that don't fit a difficulty ladder the way content vocabulary does, alongside the metalanguage for talking about grammar, phonology, and the writing system itself. Rather than the usual Core/Intermediate/Advanced tiers, this page keeps the topic-based structure Rosenfelder's own grammar chapter uses — closed-class systems like the correlative pronoun grid or the 12-case system are inherently paradigmatic, not gradable by proficiency level.

- <ruby>[比較格](../words/比較格.md)<rt>ㄅㄧㄜㄍㄚㄎㄍㄚㄎ</rt></ruby>: comparative case

## Semantic Range Notes

**却 vs. 稍 — two senses of English "rather"**: 却 is the concessive "rather, on the contrary, all the more" (contrastive, like "he refused — rather, he laughed"), while 稍 is the degree modifier "rather, slightly, somewhat" (like "rather cold"). They are separate words.

**他 vs. 別 — two senses of English "other"**: 他 means "another" (an additional instance of the same kind), while 別 means "other, distinct" (a different kind entirely).

**Dan'a'yo has no grammatical gender and no accusative case**: the Grammatical Categories list above covers tense and aspect, animacy, mood and modality, prospective aspect, progressive aspect, and person. [[性別]], [[陰性]] and [[陽性]] are the words for describing gender in other languages, but Dan'a'yo does not mark it, and the 12-case system in the Case System section has no accusative slot. Direct objects aren't case-marked at all; they are identified by the topic-comment sentence structure that [[事詞]] ("eventive," the transitive verb class) is built around, with 也 marking the topic itself. This is a design choice, as in other isolating East Asian languages.

**若/苟 both mean "if," split by register**: 若 is the general conditional conjunction. 苟 is narrower and more formal and literary, closer to "if only" or "provided that". [[一旦]] sits beside both but is not a duplicate: it is temporal-conditional, "once/as soon as X happens," distinct from 若/苟's purely hypothetical "if."

**因為/除非/否則/然而/是否 — the Chinese-only conjunctions**: English Wiktionary has entries for these only in Chinese, so their Japanese and Korean fields are built from the characters. [[因為]] is the clause-linking "because" that [[為]], the co-verb "for, because of", was said to lack. [[除非]] is "unless", [[否則]] is "otherwise, or else", [[然而]] is "however, but, yet", beside the plain "but" [[但]] and the clausal "and, but" [[而]], and [[是否]] is "whether". "Although" and "even if" are already [[雖]].

**最/最少/至少/更少 — degree poles**: [[最]] is "most" and [[最少]] is "least, the fewest"; [[更]] is "more" and [[更少]] is "less, fewer". [[至少]] is "at least", a lower bound rather than a degree.

**爾 vs. 爾等 — "yes" and "y'all"**: 爾 is the word for "yes". [[爾等]] is not 爾 plus 等. It is a neologism of an anachronism, a new old word: the Dan'a'yo way to say "y'all" in olden speech, built like [[我等]] and [[君等]].

**熟 — which**: [[熟]] is the classical interrogative "which". It is spelled with 熟 because its original form 孰 is an alias of the character. The sense "ripe, cooked" belongs to compounds such as [[成熟]], and its sound ㄙㄨㄎ is shared with [[縮]]. Compare [[何]], "what", and [[誰]], "who".

**語根/語幹/語尾 — root, stem, ending**: [[語根]] is the root of a word, [[語幹]] is its stem, and [[語尾]] is its ending. [[活用]] and [[屈折]] both name inflection. These are terms of description; they do not claim that Dan'a'yo inflects.

**肯定/否定 — affirmation and negation**: [[肯定]] is affirmation and [[否定]] is negation, the grammatical counterparts of the answer words [[爾]], "yes", and [[否]], "no".
