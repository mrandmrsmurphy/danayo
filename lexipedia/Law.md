# Law

Law vocabulary spans the whole apparatus of legal order: the abstract concepts (law, justice, right and wrong), the institutions and people that administer it (courts, police, plaintiffs), the process a case moves through (suits, testimony, verdicts), and its consequences (punishment, fines, exile, execution). Dan'a'yo needed several deliberate three-way splits Rosenfelder's flat word list never asked for — a general/compelled/transitive distinction for exile, a building/institution distinction for "court" — cross-checked against real usage rather than left to a single catch-all word.

## Semantic Range Notes

**Exile is a deliberate three-way split, cross-checked against real usage rather than left to one catch-all word.** [[流亡]] is the general verb (Mandarin liúwáng, and especially Vietnamese lưu vong, well-attested in reference to the post-1975 Vietnamese diaspora — "chính phủ lưu vong," government in exile). [[亡命]] is the compelled, flee-for-one's-life register (Japanese ぼうめい, Korean 망명, both standard words for political defection). [[放逐]] is the transitive act done *by* an authority *to* someone else (Mandarin/Japanese 放逐, more literary, used figuratively too — 放逐される, "to be ousted"). The agentive -者 forms don't cleanly split the same way in every language: Vietnamese collapses both [[流亡者]] and [[亡命者]] to the same native phrase, người lưu vong ("exiled person," built on 流亡, not a Hán Việt 者-suffixed form) — noted on both words' own pages rather than papered over.

**無罪/有罪 surface a genuine markedness asymmetry.** [[無罪]]'s own Vietnamese field, vô tội, is the living, standard word for "innocent" — but Vietnamese does not mirror this with a parallel Sino-Vietnamese "hữu tội" for [[有罪]] "guilty." Living usage instead says có tội, pairing the Hán Việt tội with the *native* verb có ("to have") rather than Sino-Vietnamese hữu. The negative member of the pair stayed fully Sino-Vietnamese; the positive member didn't.

**天真 was a real, since-corrected false positive.** "Innocent, naive, artless" is a personality trait, not a legal verdict — it had sat in this domain's scratch bucket purely on an English-gloss keyword match until [[無罪]] was coined to properly fill the "not guilty" slot. [[縮]] ("shrink, contract"), [[阻止]], and [[阻碍]] ("thwart, obstruct, block") were caught the same way and rerouted out — none has a specifically legal sense in its own stored gloss.

**地下牢 keeps a fuller form than living Mandarin actually prefers.** Mandarin colloquially shortens "dungeon" to bare 地牢, but Dan'a'yo deliberately keeps the symmetric three-morpheme 地下牢, paralleling [[Buildings]]' own [[地下室]] "basement" (地+下+室). Japanese ちかろう uses the same fuller form, standard in fantasy/game/historical registers; Korean and Vietnamese don't independently attest a fixed compound at all here (Korean prefers the descriptive 지하 감옥; Vietnamese the native hầm tối).

**法/法律/法則 form a near-synonym cluster**, not three unrelated words: bare [[法]] is the root sense ("rule, law"), [[法律]] is the standard word for codified law/statute, and [[法則]] leans toward "a law, a rule, a code" in the more abstract/scientific sense (natural law, a code of rules) as much as the legal one.
