Christian theological, ecclesiastical, and scriptural vocabulary — not one of Rosenfelder's Lexipedia categories, but necessary given Christianity's historical and continuing presence across the CJKV cultural sphere. Names follow the translation principles of this vault: a name is rendered by its meaning where a meaning exists, from characters already in the vault, with the original sound kept in the ruby; the three divine names (上帝 for Elohim, 天主 for Adonai, 偕者 for YHWH) are kept distinct; and the theophoric element is 偕, placed wherever it makes a natural Chinese phrase. Where an older religion already has a word with the right feeling, the vocabulary adopts it, as Christianity itself absorbed pagan feasts and names (see 罪業, 業報, 天罰). The vocabulary sections up to Scripture are complete under the second-tier terms used on the other lexipedia pages; the Hebrew Scriptures, Greek Scriptures and Tribal-name sections are still a working draft, which is why this page is `partial`, and mostly record names awaiting proper word pages — see [[bible/聖経 (book)]] for the full source material and working notes.

## Sin, Guilt & Defilement

Hebrew has many words for what English calls "sin," and Dan'a'yo keeps them apart, using words that already exist where one fits. Several are old Buddhist words taken over for Christian use: Dan'a'yo's Christian vocabulary is glad to adopt another religion's term when it carries the right feeling, as Christianity itself took over feast days and names that nobody now remembers as pagan. Each word's own page says which Hebrew sense it best translates and where it differs from its neighbours; the kinds of sin are also listed on [[Sin]].

### Sin in Christian Teaching

Christian teaching treats sin as an act, as a condition, as a burden and as a consequence, and Dan'a'yo gives each its own word where English has only "sin."

**The act and the condition.** The general word is [[罪]]. A sin considered as a deed, with the weight it leaves on the sinner, is [[罪業]] (Hebrew *ḥēṭ*; "the soul who sins shall die," Ezekiel 18:20). The guilty, crooked condition of a person, and the guilt that stays after the deed, is [[罪悪]] (Hebrew *ʿāwōn*; "in iniquity I was brought forth," Psalm 51:5). That condition, inherited from Adam (Romans 5:12), is [[原罪]], original sin. A person who bears it is a [[罪人]], a sinner (Romans 3:23, "all have sinned"), who is not the same as the [[邪悪]] person, the settled evildoer of the Psalms.

**Gravity.** Not every sin weighs the same. A [[大罪]] is a grave sin, the "sin unto death" of 1 John 5:16, and the seven deadly sins on [[Sin]] are seven of these. At the other end is [[過失]], sin in error (Leviticus 4), for which the Law provides a remedy; against it stands [[反逆]], rebellion "with a high hand" (Numbers 15:30), for which it provides none.

**Against whom.** Some sins are aimed at God and some at a neighbour. [[背信]] (breach of faith) and [[不敬]] (irreverence, the lack of the fear of God) are sins against God, and so is the idol-worship named under [[偶像]]. [[不義]] (injustice) and [[暴力]] (violence, the sin that filled the earth before the Flood, Genesis 6:11) are sins against other people, though the Law treats these as wrong before God too.

**The consequence.** A sin brings something back. [[業報]] is the consequence that comes from the deed itself, "my punishment is greater than I can bear" (Genesis 4:13), and "the wages of sin is death" (Romans 6:23). [[天罰]] is the penalty God imposes, "vengeance is mine" (Deuteronomy 32:35). The final reckoning is [[審判]], the Last Judgment.

**Defilement is not sin.** The Law also knows [[汚穢]], ritual uncleanness (Leviticus 11–15), caused by what is [[禁忌]], an abomination. It is a state, not a wrongdoing: it is washed away and not forgiven, and its opposite is [[清潔]]. The New Testament moves the question inward: what defiles a person is what comes out of the heart (Mark 7:15–23).

**The remedy.** The way back runs through [[悔改]] (to turn around), [[懺悔]] (to confess and grieve) and [[告解]] (confession to a priest); then comes [[赦免]] (pardon) and [[容恕]] (forgiveness). The cost is borne by [[贖罪]] (atonement), the sin offerings and guilt offerings of Leviticus 4–5, fulfilled for Christians in the [[十字架]] (Hebrews 9–10), and by [[救贖]] (redemption). [[犠牲]] is the sacrifice itself.

## Semantic Range Notes

**偕者 is not a transliteration**: <ruby>[偕者](../words/偕者.md)<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄑㄚ</rt></ruby> is a meaning-based rendering of the name YHWH, "the one who is with (you)," read from Exodus 3:12 and 3:14, in place of the phonetic 耶和華. Dan'a'yo keeps three divine names apart: <ruby>[上帝](../words/上帝.md)<rt>ㄙ⼘ㄫㄊㄝㄧ</rt></ruby> for Elohim, <ruby>[天主](../words/天主.md)<rt>ㄊㄝㄋㄐㄨ</rt></ruby> for Adonai, and <ruby>[偕者](../words/偕者.md)<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄑㄚ</rt></ruby> for YHWH.

**偕 is the theophoric element, placed by how the Chinese reads**: in names such as <ruby>[救偕](../words/救偕.md)<rt>ㄍ⼜ㄍ⼶</rt></ruby> (Jesus, "YHWH saves"), 贈偕 (Matthew, "gift of YHWH"), 恵偕 (John) and 誰偕 (Micah), 偕 sits wherever it makes a natural Chinese phrase, not by copying the Hebrew order.

**Holy words split by what is holy**: <ruby>[神聖](../words/神聖.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄋㄙㄧㄫ</rt></ruby> is sacred, holy in itself; <ruby>[聖霊](../words/聖霊.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄫㄌㄝㄫ</rt></ruby> is the Holy Spirit; <ruby>[聖書](../words/聖書.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄫㄙ⼄</rt></ruby> is a holy book; <ruby>[聖像](../words/聖像.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄫㄙ⼘ㄫ</rt></ruby> is an icon; and <ruby>[聖人](../words/聖人.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄫㄋㄧㄋ</rt></ruby> is a saint.

**Church, hall and cathedral**: <ruby>[教会](../words/教会.md)<rt>ㄍ⼄ㄨㄏ⼔</rt></ruby> is the church as a community and a building, <ruby>[教堂](../words/教堂.md)<rt>ㄍ⼄ㄨㄉㄚㄫ</rt></ruby> is the church building, <ruby>[会堂](../words/会堂.md)<rt>ㄏ⼔ㄉㄚㄫ</rt></ruby> is a meeting hall, and <ruby>[坐堂](../words/坐堂.md)<rt>ㄐ⺢ㄉㄚㄫ</rt></ruby> is the cathedral, the church holding the bishop's seat, beside <ruby>[大教堂](../words/大教堂.md)<rt>ㄉㄚㄧㄍ⼄ㄨㄉㄚㄫ</rt></ruby>, the great church.

**Priest, pastor and father**: <ruby>[祭司](../words/祭司.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄙㄚ</rt></ruby> is the generic priest and <ruby>[司鈬](../words/司鈬.md)<rt>ㄙㄚㄉㄚㄎ</rt></ruby> the Catholic ordained priest; <ruby>[牧師](../words/牧師.md)<rt>ㄇㄨㄎㄙㄧㄜ</rt></ruby> is the Protestant pastor and <ruby>[神父](../words/神父.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄋㄅㄨ</rt></ruby> the Orthodox or Catholic father.

**Preaching has three registers**: <ruby>[説教](../words/説教.md)<rt>ㄙ⼔ㄊㄍ⼄ㄨ</rt></ruby> is the sermon to a congregation, <ruby>[宣教](../words/宣教.md)<rt>ㄙㄝㄋㄍ⼄ㄨ</rt></ruby> is the mission to those outside, and <ruby>[伝道](../words/伝道.md)<rt>ㄐ⼔ㄋㄉㄚㄨ</rt></ruby> is the everyday word for handing on the faith.

**Revelation and prophecy**: <ruby>[啓示](../words/啓示.md)<rt>ㄎㄝㄧㄍㄝ</rt></ruby> is open revelation, <ruby>[黙示](../words/黙示.md)<rt>ㄇㄨㄎㄍㄝ</rt></ruby> is veiled, apocalyptic revelation, and <ruby>[予言](../words/予言.md)<rt>⼄ㄝㄋ</rt></ruby> is prophecy.

**Heaven and its opposites**: <ruby>[天国](../words/天国.md)<rt>ㄊㄝㄋㄍㄛㄎ</rt></ruby> is the kingdom of heaven and <ruby>[楽園](../words/楽園.md)<rt>ㄌㄚㄎㄛㄋ</rt></ruby> is paradise; <ruby>[地獄](../words/地獄.md)<rt>ㄉㄧㄜ⼄ㄎ</rt></ruby> is hell, <ruby>[錬獄](../words/錬獄.md)<rt>ㄌㄝㄋ⼄ㄎ</rt></ruby> is purgatory, and <ruby>[審判](../words/審判.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄇㄆㄚㄋ</rt></ruby> is the judgment between them.
