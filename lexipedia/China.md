<ruby>[中国](../words/中国.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄫㄍㄛㄎ</rt></ruby>: China. This page gathers vocabulary specific to China: its famous people, places, provinces, historical states and ancient geography. Words shared across the Sinosphere stay in their own domains, such as [Geography](Geography.md) and [Government](Government.md).

## Places
### Cities
The four direct-administered municipalities, <ruby>直轄市<rt>ㄐㄧㄊㄏㄚㄊㄙㄧ</rt></ruby> ("municipality directly under the central government", that is, the <ruby>[中央](../words/中央.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄫ⼘ㄫ</rt></ruby><ruby>[政府](../words/政府.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄫㄈㄨ</rt></ruby>):
- <ruby>[北京](../words/北京.md)<rt>ㄅㄨㄎㄍ⼶ㄫ</rt></ruby>: Beijing, capital of the People's Republic of China.
- <ruby>[上海](../words/上海.md)<rt>ㄙ⼘ㄫㄏㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>: Shanghai.
- <ruby>[天津](../words/天津.md)<rt>ㄊㄝㄋㄐㄧㄋ</rt></ruby>: Tianjin.
- <ruby>[重慶](../words/重慶.md)<rt>ㄑㄛㄫㄎ⼶ㄫ</rt></ruby>: Chongqing.

### Peoples and names
- <ruby>[百越](../words/百越.md)<rt>ㄅㄚㄎ·⼔ㄊ</rt></ruby>: Baiyue, the "Hundred Yue" — the collective name of the many non-Han peoples of southern China and northern Vietnam in antiquity (Wu, Minyue, Nanyue, Ouyue, Luoyue and others), a historical designation, not one ethnic group.

### Historical states
- <ruby>[平城](../words/平城.md)<rt>ㄅ⼶ㄫㄙㄧㄫ</rt></ruby>: Pingcheng, the Northern Wei capital (now Datong); also Heijō in Japan and Pyongsong in North Korea, see Japan and Korea.
- <ruby>[斉国](../words/斉国.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄧㄍㄛㄎ</rt></ruby>: the state of Qi, a great power of the Zhou period in what is now Shandong, ended by Qin in 221 BC (from memory, not re-checked); not to be confused with the Southern Qi and Northern Qi dynasties.
- <ruby>[鄭国](../words/鄭国.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄫㄍㄛㄎ</rt></ruby>: Zheng, a minor state of the Zhou period in what is now Henan, whose "Airs of Zheng" survive in the <ruby>[詩経](../words/詩経.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄍㄝㄫ</rt></ruby> and whose name gave the surname <ruby>鄭<rt>ㄐㄝㄫ</rt></ruby>; a historical state, not a modern country.
- <ruby>[蜀国](../words/蜀国.md)<rt>ㄙㄛㄎㄍㄛㄎ</rt></ruby>: Shu, the kingdom centred on the Sichuan basin — the generic name for several historical states there (the early Shu conquered by Qin, Shu-Han of the Three Kingdoms, and the Five Dynasties Shu kingdoms); a historical state, not a modern country.
- <ruby>[魏国](../words/魏国.md)<rt>ㄝㄍㄛㄎ</rt></ruby>: Wei, one of the Seven Warring States, which carved itself out of Jin in the fifth century BC (from memory, not re-checked); also the Cao Wei kingdom of the Three Kingdoms (220–266), and the Northern Wei, which are different states with the same name.
- <ruby>[偪陽](../words/偪陽.md)<rt>ㄆㄧㄆ·⼘ㄫ</rt></ruby>: Fuyang (Bìyáng), a small state of the Spring and Autumn period, in what is now Shandong, captured by a coalition of Jin and its allies in 563 BC (from memory, not re-checked); not the Fuyang of Anhui, which is written differently.
- <ruby>[陳朝](../words/陳朝.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄋㄐㄚㄨ</rt></ruby>: the Chen dynasty (557–589), the last of the Southern Dynasties; the same spelling is the Trần dynasty of Vietnam, listed on Vietnam.
- <ruby>[安南](../words/安南.md)<rt>ㄚㄋㄋㄚㄇ</rt></ruby>: Annam, the Tang Protectorate General to Pacify the South in what is now northern Vietnam, and later the Chinese name for Vietnam; also Vietnamese, see Vietnam.
- <ruby>[交址](../words/交址.md)<rt>ㄍ⼄ㄨㄐㄧ</rt></ruby>: Jiaozhi, the Han-dynasty commandery in northern Vietnam and a generic term for the lands south of the Five Ridges; also Vietnamese, see Vietnam.
- <ruby>[鎬京](../words/鎬京.md)<rt>ㄏㄚㄨㄍ⼶ㄫ</rt></ruby>: Haojing, the ancient capital of the Western Zhou.

## Ancient China
In ancient East Asian thought, the division of the world followed a different structure. The <ruby>[天下](../words/天下.md)<rt>ㄊㄝㄋㄏㄚ</rt></ruby> was described as surrounded by the Four Seas (<ruby>[四海](../words/四海.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄜㄏㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>), namely 
1. <ruby>[東海](../words/東海.md)<rt>ㄉㄛㄫㄏㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>, 
2. <ruby>[西海](../words/西海.md)<rt>ㄙㄝㄧㄏㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>, 
3. <ruby>[南海](../words/南海.md)<rt>ㄋㄚㄇㄏㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>, 
4. and <ruby>[北海](../words/北海.md)<rt>ㄅㄨㄎㄏㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>

These were directional boundary waters (<ruby>[方位](../words/方位.md)<rt>ㄈㄚㄫ⼔ㄧ</rt></ruby><ruby>[界水](../words/界水.md)<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄙㄨ</rt></ruby>), not measured oceans. Within these boundaries lay the <ruby>[九州](../words/九州.md)<rt>ㄎ⼜ㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>, including:
1. Ji (<ruby>[希州](../words/希州.md)<rt>ㄏㄧㄜㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>),
2. Yan (<ruby>[兗州](../words/兗州.md)<rt>⼶ㄋㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>),
3. Qing (<ruby>[青州](../words/青州.md)<rt>ㄑㄝㄫㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>),
4. Xu (<ruby>[徐州](../words/徐州.md)<rt>ㄙ⼄ㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>),
5. Yang (<ruby>[揚州](../words/揚州.md)<rt>⼘ㄫㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>),
6. Jing (<ruby>[荊州](../words/荊州.md)<rt>ㄎ⼶ㄫㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>),
7. Yu (<ruby>[予州](../words/予州.md)<rt>⼄ㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>),
8. Liang (<ruby>[梁州](../words/梁州.md)<rt>ㄌ⼘ㄫㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>), and
9. Yong (<ruby>[雍州](../words/雍州.md)<rt>ㄛㄫㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>).

These provinces (<ruby>[州](../words/州.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>) were civilizational and administrative regions (<ruby>[行政](../words/行政.md)<rt>ㄏㄚㄫㄐㄧㄫ</rt></ruby><ruby>区域<rt>ㄎㄨ·ㄨㄧㄎ</rt></ruby>), not continents (<ruby>[洲](../words/洲.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄛ</rt></ruby>). Thus the ancient model was concentric (<ruby>[環状](../words/環状.md)<rt>ㄏ⺢ㄋㄐ⺢ㄫ</rt></ruby><ruby>[構造](../words/構造.md)<rt>ㄍㄛㄨㄑㄚㄨ</rt></ruby>), centered upon the royal domain (<ruby>[王畿](../words/王畿.md)<rt>⺢ㄫㄍㄧㄜ</rt></ruby>), while the modern model is global and comparative (<ruby>[全球](../words/全球.md)<rt>ㄐ⼔ㄋㄍ⼜</rt></ruby><ruby>[並列](../words/並列.md)<rt>ㄅㄝㄫㄌㄝㄊ</rt></ruby><ruby>構造<rt>ㄍㄛㄨㄑㄚㄨ</rt></ruby>), treating all continents as parallel divisions (<ruby>並列<rt>ㄅㄝㄫㄌㄝㄊ</rt></ruby><ruby>区域<rt>ㄎㄨ·ㄨㄧㄎ</rt></ruby>).

This concentric Nine-Provinces model eventually gave way to the historical reality of the Warring States, one of which — <ruby>[秦国](../words/秦国.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄋㄍㄛㄎ</rt></ruby> (the state of Qin) — unified the others into China's first empire.

Not every ancient polity survived to see that unification.  One of the earliest to vanish was <ruby>[鄂国](../words/鄂国.md)<rt>ㄚㄎㄍㄛㄎ</rt></ruby> (the State of E), destroyed in 863 BC, yet its name endures as the one-character abbreviation for Hubei.

## Language and Script
- <ruby>[中国語](../words/中国語.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄫㄍㄛㄎ·⼄</rt></ruby>: the Chinese language (primarily a Japanese/Korean coinage, country + <ruby>語<rt>⼄</rt></ruby>; Mandarin itself prefers <ruby>[中文](../words/中文.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄫㄇㄨㄋ</rt></ruby> / <ruby>[普通話](../words/普通話.md)<rt>ㄆㄛ·ㄊㄛㄫㄏ⺢ㄧ</rt></ruby> / <ruby>[漢語](../words/漢語.md)<rt>ㄏㄚㄋ·⼄</rt></ruby>).
- <ruby>普通話<rt>ㄆㄛ·ㄊㄛㄫㄏ⺢ㄧ</rt></ruby>: Mandarin, Standard Chinese (lit. "common speech," the PRC's official name for the standard language, narrower than <ruby>中国語<rt>ㄐㄨㄫㄍㄛㄎ·⼄</rt></ruby>).
- <ruby>中文<rt>ㄐㄨㄫㄇㄨㄋ</rt></ruby>: Chinese; the Chinese language/writing/culture (the everyday Mandarin/Cantonese term, emphasizing the cultural-written dimension over <ruby>中国語<rt>ㄐㄨㄫㄍㄛㄎ·⼄</rt></ruby>'s system-of-speech sense).
- <ruby>漢語<rt>ㄏㄚㄋ·⼄</rt></ruby>: Chinese language(s), Sino-Japanese vocabulary.
- <ruby>[広東語](../words/広東語.md)<rt>ㄍ⺢ㄫㄉㄛㄫ⼄</rt></ruby>: Cantonese, lit. "Guangdong language" (the Guangdong-based variety; compare <ruby>[粤語](../words/粤語.md)<rt>⼔ㄊ⼄</rt></ruby>, the Yue language as a whole).
- <ruby>粤語<rt>⼔ㄊ⼄</rt></ruby>: Yue language, Cantonese as a whole.
- <ruby>[国語](../words/国語.md)<rt>ㄍㄛㄎ·⼄</rt></ruby>: "national language" — in Taiwan (<ruby>國語<rt>ㄍㄛㄎ·⼄</rt></ruby>) Mandarin as the standard language, beside the PRC's <ruby>普通話<rt>ㄆㄛ·ㄊㄛㄫㄏ⺢ㄧ</rt></ruby>; the ambiguity of the term ("national language" vs a country's own modern language) is explained on its word page.
- <ruby>[注音符号](../words/注音符号.md)<rt>ㄐㄨㄨㄇㄅㄨㄏㄚㄨ</rt></ruby>: Bopomofo, the phonetic script devised in China in 1913 to write Mandarin syllables, and the notation Dan'a'yo adopted and extended for its own sounds; the page ruby text is written in it.

## Semantic Range Notes

**Shared spellings.** Dan'a'yo gives one word one spelling and one reading, so a Chinese place name that is also a place in Japan, Korea or Vietnam is one word with several senses. 河北, 湖南, 海南, 江西, 山西, 河南, 太原, 西寧, 広南, 平城 and 安南 each have a sense on another country page, and the other pages say which. For the Vietnamese cases it is the Vietnamese province named after the Chinese one.

**Written forms.** The vault uses one spelling per word, in the Japanese-style forms it has chosen (広東, 済南, 青海, 黒龍江), and other writings of a name are look-up aliases only, never used in running text. Characters outside Dan'a'yo, such as simplified forms, are not written on this page.

**深川 and 抗州 are stand-ins.** The Chinese cities of Shenzhen and Hangzhou are written 深川 and 抗州 because the character each would need has no page of its own in the vault, so a neighbouring character stands in. Both are deliberate vault decisions, not mistakes in the page.

**Language names.** 中国語 is the full country-plus-語 name, 中文 and 普通話 are the Mandarin-side names, and 漢語 is the cover term (homophone of 韓語). 国語 means different things in Taiwan and in Japan and Korea, which its word page sets out.

**Transliterated names.** The emperors and political figures are written only as ruby-transliterated characters, because this is a lexicon and personal names do not get words of their own.
