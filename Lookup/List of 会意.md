# 会意— Compound Ideographs
## Explained
### Definition
**会意** refers to characters formed by **combining two or more elements, each contributing meaning**, so that the whole expresses a new, often more abstract or relational idea. The interpretation arises from the interaction of parts, not from depiction (象形) or symbolic marking (指事), and not from a phonetic component (形声).

Common examples:
- 休 “rest” = 人 (person) + 木 (tree)
- 明 “bright” = 日 (sun) + 月 (moon)
- 武 “martial” (traditionally analyzed as 止 “foot/stop” + 戈 “halberd”)
- 信 “trust” = 人 (person) + 言 (speech)

In practice, 会意 is a productive way to build meaning transparently.

### Structural Characteristics
A character is best analyzed as 会意 when:
1. **Multiple components each contribute semantics**  
    Each part has an identifiable meaning relevant to the whole.
2. **The whole meaning is an interpretive combination**  
    Often metaphorical or relational (e.g., “person + tree” → “rest”).
3. **No component is present primarily for sound**  
    There is no dedicated phonetic element.
4. **The structure is compositional and interpretable**  
    A reader can plausibly recover the intended meaning from the parts.

### Contrast with Other Categories
**vs. 象形 (pictographs)**
- 会意 combines elements to form a concept
- 象形 is a single pictorial form

Example contrast:
- 林 (grove = 木 + 木) → 会意
- 木 (tree) → 象形

**vs. 指事 (simple indicatives)**
- 会意 uses multiple semantic units
- 指事 uses a single form with a symbolic mark

Example contrast:
- 本 (root: mark on 木) → 指事
- 休 (person + tree) → 会意

**vs. 形声 (phono-semantic compounds)**
- 会意: all parts are semantic
- 形声: one part is semantic, one part is phonetic

Example contrast:
- 信 (person + speech) → 会意
- 清 (water + phonetic 青) → 形声

### How to Classify a Character as 会意
Use the following decision process:
1. **Can the character be decomposed into recognizable components?**
    - No → consider 象形 or 指事
    - Yes → continue
2. **Do all components contribute meaning to the whole?**
    - Yes → continue
    - No → likely 形声
3. **Is any component primarily indicating pronunciation?**
    - Yes → 形声
    - No → continue
4. **Does the meaning arise from combining the parts (often metaphorically)?**
    - Yes → 会意
    - No → reconsider analysis

A useful diagnostic is substitution:  
If replacing one component would systematically change the meaning (not just the sound), the structure is likely 会意.

### On the “Lost Phonetic” Problem
Modern paleographic research often shows that some characters traditionally labeled 会意 were **originally 形声**, where the phonetic relationship has been obscured by sound change or graphic evolution.

Implications:
- **Diachronic analysis (historical origin)** may differ from
- **Synchronic analysis (how the character functions now)**

For a working system like Dan’a’yo, the synchronic perspective is typically more useful:

- If no phonetic role is recoverable or operative for users,  
    classify the character as 会意.

In other words:
> Treat “hidden” or obsolete phonetic links as irrelevant unless they are productive.

### Notes for Classification
- 会意 is the **second-largest category after 形声**.
- It is the most **useful category for deliberate character creation**, because it preserves semantic transparency.    

Guidelines:
1. **Prefer clear semantic contributions**  
    Each component should visibly support the intended meaning.
2. **Avoid accidental phonetic drift into 形声**  
    Do not rely on sound resemblance unless you intend a phonetic role.
3. **Favor interpretability over cleverness**  
    If a reader cannot plausibly infer the meaning, the composition is weak.
4. **Use it to reduce reliance on phonographic characters**  
    Especially for new vocabulary, 会意 provides a principled alternative to sound-based borrowing.

### Practical Boundary with 形声
A recurring ambiguity:
- If one component could be read as either semantic or phonetic, decide by **function**:
    - contributes meaning → 会意
    - contributes sound → 形声

When in doubt, ask:
> Would a learner use this component to guess the meaning, or the pronunciation?

That answer determines the classification.

### Summary
- 会意 builds meaning through **semantic composition**
- It contrasts with:
    - depiction (象形)
    - indication (指事)
    - phonetic structuring (形声)
- Historical reanalysis does not invalidate its usefulness
- In a constructed system, it is a **primary tool for controlled, transparent vocabulary expansion**.

69. <ruby>[尖](/characters/尖.md)<rt>ㄐㄝㄇ</rt></ruby> - [[小 (char)|小]] ("small") stacked over [[大 (char)|大]] ("large") — a shape narrowing gradually from small to large: "a tapering point, sharp." (Unlike in Japanese, this vault keeps 尖 distinct from 先 rather than blending them.)
70. <ruby>[戍](/characters/戍.md)<rt>ㄙㄨ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 009|人]] ("a person") bearing [[Radical 062|戈]] ("a dagger-axe") — a person standing watch with a weapon: "to garrison, a border guard."
71. <ruby>[戎](/characters/戎.md)<rt>ㄋㄨㄫ</rt></ruby> - [[甲 (char)|甲]] ("shield, armor") + [[Radical 062|戈]] ("a halberd, a weapon") — armor and weapon together standing for arms in general: "weaponry, military affairs" (also the name of the 西戎, an ancient frontier people, and later a surname).
72. <ruby>[早](/characters/早%20(char).md)<rt>ㄐㄚㄨ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 072|日]] ("the sun") risen above 十 (a stylized plant or marker) — the sun already above the horizon: "early."
73. <ruby>[旬](/characters/旬.md)<rt>ㄙ⼜ㄋ</rt></ruby> - 勹 ("to wrap, to enclose," no vault page) around [[日 (char)|日]] ("the sun, a day") — days wrapped into a complete cycle: the ancient ten-day period of the Chinese calendar (days named by the 十干 heavenly stems).
74. <ruby>[死](/characters/死.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄜ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 078|歹]] ("death, stripped bone remains") beside 人 ("a person," shown kneeling or bowing) — a mourner kneeling beside the dead: "to die, death."
75. <ruby>[老](/characters/老%20(char).md)<rt>ㄌㄚㄨ</rt></ruby> - [[人 (char)|人]] ("a man") with [[毛 (char)|毛]] ("hair," grown long) leaning on [[匕]] ("a cane") — an elderly man leaning on his cane: "old, aged." Cognate to [[考]] (OC *kʰluːʔ) — the classic textbook example of 転注 ("mutual annotation," where two characters gloss and derive from one another).
76. <ruby>[艮](/characters/艮.md)<rt>ㄍㄚㄋ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 109|目]] ("an eye") atop 人 ("a person") — a person turning to look back — extended to "to resist, to disobey," and to "a limit, a stopping point."
77. <ruby>[色](/characters/色.md)<rt>ㄙㄧㄎ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 087|爪]] ("a hand, a claw") pressing down on [[Radical 026|卩]] ("a kneeling person") — a hand shaping a kneeling person's expression: "complexion, expression," extended to "color, appearance," and to sensory phenomena generally.
78. <ruby>[血](/characters/血%20(char).md)<rt>ㄏ⼔ㄊ</rt></ruby> - [[一 (char)|一]] ("a drop") inside [[皿 (char)|皿]] ("a sacrificial vessel") — a drop of blood inside a ritual chalice, offered to the ancestors: "blood."
79. <ruby>[両](/characters/両%20(char).md)<rt>ㄌ⼘ㄫ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 001|一]] ("one") over 㒳 (an archaic glyph for a joined pair, no vault page) — two things bound together as one: "both, a pair." Bronze-script forms also resemble two [[丙 (char)|丙]] joined side by side (Baxter 1992), from which 丙 is sometimes cited as an incidental phonetic component, though this remains uncertain.
80. <ruby>[充](/characters/充.md)<rt>ㄑㄨㄫ</rt></ruby> - 𠫓 ("a newborn," an inverted 子, no vault page) growing up over [[Radical 010|儿]] ("legs, a standing person") — a newborn grown to maturity: "fully grown," extended metaphorically to "full, filled up," then "sufficient," and later "to serve, to act as (a role)."
81. <ruby>[光](/characters/光%20(char).md)<rt>ㄎ⺢ㄫ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 086|火]] ("fire") held above [[Radical 026|卩]] ("a kneeling person") — fire raised over a person's head: "radiance, brightness, light." Shuowen Jiezi: 明也，从火在人上 ("brightness; formed from fire above a person").
82. <ruby>[共](/characters/共%20(char).md)<rt>ㄍ⼄ㄫ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 055|廾]] ("two hands") holding up [[Radical 030|口]] (here "an object," not "a mouth") — two hands raising something together: "together, jointly, shared." The object component was eventually written 廿 (not "twenty"), which later fused with 廾 to form the modern 共.
83. <ruby>[再](/characters/再.md)<rt>ㄐㄚㄧ</rt></ruby> - genuinely uncertain and disputed. The modern leading theory reads [[一 (char)|一]] ("one") over an abbreviated 冓 ("a basket repeatedly rewoven from bamboo," no vault page), signifying the repetition of an act once done: "again, a second time." An older theory instead reads 魚 ("fish") + 二 ("two"): "to catch two fish at once."
84. <ruby>[卍](/characters/卍.md)<rt>ㄇㄛㄋ</rt></ruby> - not a depiction of any physical object but a purely geometric symbol built from interlocking 十-like segments: the Buddhist swastika, a mark of auspiciousness. Classified under [[Radical 024|十]] by convention, not because it decomposes into that glyph.
85. <ruby>[印](/characters/印.md)<rt>ㄧㄋ</rt></ruby> - [[爪 (char)|爪]] ("a hand") pressing down on [[Radical 026|卩]] ("a kneeling person") — a hand holding a person down: "to press down, to mark," extended to "a stamp, a seal, to print."
86. <ruby>[向](/characters/向%20(char).md)<rt>ㄏ⼘ㄫ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 040|宀]] ("a house") over [[口 (char)|口]] ("a mouth," here not "an opening" as in 高 or 谷, but a mouth proper) — sound echoing within a dwelling: "to face, to turn toward." The original form of [[響]] ("echo, sound," OC *qʰaŋʔ); later borrowed to also represent 𠨍 (嚮). Compare 冋.
87. <ruby>[妃](/characters/妃.md)<rt>ㄈㄧ</rt></ruby> - [[女 (char)|女]] ("a woman") beside 卩 ("a kneeling man," no vault page) — a woman paired with a man: "a spouse," specialized to "an imperial concubine, the wife of a prince."
88. <ruby>[尽](/characters/尽.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄋ</rt></ruby> - 聿 ("a hand holding a brush," no vault page) + 灬 ("bristles," no vault page) scrubbing [[皿 (char)|皿]] ("a dish") — cleaning a dish down to nothing: "to exhaust, to use up completely; empty." The character's own Kangxi indexing radical, 尸, is none of these three components — it reflects the modern simplified 尽 glyph's shape rather than 盡's traditional-form etymology.
89. <ruby>[旨](/characters/旨.md)<rt>ㄐㄧㄜ</rt></ruby> - [[匕]] ("a spoon," i.e. 匙) lifted to originally [[甘 (char)|甘]] ("delicious") — eating something delicious with a spoon: "delicious," extended to "intention, purpose, aim," and to "an imperial decree." The bottom component later drifted to resemble [[日 (char)|日]] ("the sun"), the character's modern but etymologically coincidental Kangxi radical. Shuowen instead reads this as 形声 (phonetic 匕 + semantic 甘), but 匕's own Old Chinese reading (a labial initial) does not support it as a valid phonetic donor for 旨's velar initial, so 会意 is preferred here.
90. <ruby>[此](/characters/此%20(char).md)<rt>ㄑㄜ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 077|止]] ("a foot, to stop") beside [[Radical 021|匕]] ("a person turned around") — the place where a person turns and stops: "here, this." Possibly the original form of 跐 ("to stamp, to stand").
91. <ruby>[灰](/characters/灰%20(char).md)<rt>ㄏㄛㄧ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 086|火]] ("fire") held by [[又 (char)|又]] ("a hand") — fire cooled enough to be touched by hand: "ash, ashes."
92. <ruby>[考](/characters/考.md)<rt>ㄎㄚㄨ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 125|耂]] ("an old man with long hair, bent over" — the abbreviated form of [[老 (char)|老]]) leaning on 丂 ("a walking cane," an alias of this very character, no separate page) — an elderly person leaning on a cane: "aged; to examine, to test" (from checking on one's elders). Cognate to [[老 (char)|老]] (OC *ruːʔ) — the classic textbook example of 転注 ("mutual annotation").
93. <ruby>[好](/characters/好%20(char).md)<rt>ㄏㄚㄨ</rt></ruby> - [[Radical 038|女]] ("a woman") beside [[子]] ("a child") — it was good for a woman to have a child: "good," extended from the mutual affection between mother and child.
