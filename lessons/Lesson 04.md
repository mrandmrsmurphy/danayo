## Learning objectives
By the end of this lesson you will be able to:
- Say what a topic and a comment are, and find both in a sentence.
- Explain why a Dan'a'yo sentence is built on a topic and a comment, not on a subject and a predicate.
- Read and write a sentence whose comment is a quality.
- Read and write a sentence whose comment is a verb with its object.
- Recognise a topic that has its own subject inside the comment.
- Put a place phrase before the comment.
- Know 30 new words and 20 new characters, and use every one in a sentence.

You can now read 200 characters and 55 words.  This lesson teaches the one idea that shapes every Dan'a'yo sentence, and gives you 30 new words to practise it with.  The words come in five small batches, and each batch is used in sentences straight away.  The sentences in this lesson were written for the course from the words you know; they are constructed, not quoted.  The rules are in [[文法 - 04句法.en]] and the word classes in [[文法 - 97品詞]].

Dan'a'yo is written in the Literary style (書面体), the standard, with ruby over every word.

## Topic and comment
An English sentence starts with a subject: "The mountain is big."  A Dan'a'yo sentence starts with what you are talking about, the **topic** (主題), and then says something about it, the **comment** (述語).  So the same idea is closer to "As for the mountain, (it) is big."


- <ruby>山<rt>ㄙㄚㄋ</rt></ruby> <ruby>大<rt>ㄉㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>。  As for the mountain, (it) is big.  Topic: 山.  Comment: 大.

A topic is not the same as a subject.  The topic is what the sentence is about; a subject is a part of the comment, and a sentence may have both.  You will see this in Batch 3.

Three things to notice already:
- Nothing marks the topic or the comment; position does the work.  A space follows the topic.
- A comment that is a quality (a **stative**, 性詞) needs no "is".  The quality 大 is the whole comment.
- Time is not marked.  <ruby>山<rt>ㄙㄚㄋ</rt></ruby> <ruby>大<rt>ㄉㄚㄧ</rt></ruby> can be about now or about then, and the context decides.

- <ruby>犬<rt>ㄎ⼔ㄋ</rt></ruby> <ruby>耳<rt>ㄋㄧ</rt></ruby> <ruby>長<rt>ㄐㄚㄫ</rt></ruby>。  As for dogs, (their) ears are long.
- <ruby>姉<rt>ㄐㄚ</rt></ruby> <ruby>手<rt>ㄙ⼜</rt></ruby> <ruby>大<rt>ㄉㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>。  As for (my) sister, (her) hands are big.
- <ruby>先生<rt>ㄙㄝㄋㄙㄚㄫ</rt></ruby> <ruby>心<rt>ㄙㄧㄇ</rt></ruby> <ruby>大<rt>ㄉㄚㄧ</rt></ruby>。  As for the teacher, (his) heart is big (he is generous).
- <ruby>学校<rt>ㄏㄚㄎㄏ⼘ㄨ</rt></ruby> <ruby>草花<rt>ㄑㄚㄨㄏ⺢</rt></ruby> <ruby>多<rt>ㄉㄜ</rt></ruby>。  As for the school, flowers are many.

The topic and the subject are different parts of the sentence.  Nothing marks the subject: it is simply the word before the quality.

A verb that does something to a thing is an **eventive** (事詞).  It is not complete without that thing, its object.  The object comes after the verb, and the two are written together: <ruby>見<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby><ruby>雪<rt>ㄙ⼔ㄊ</rt></ruby>.  The topic is usually the one who does the action.

- <ruby>我<rt>ㄚ</rt></ruby> <ruby>見<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby><ruby>雪<rt>ㄙ⼔ㄊ</rt></ruby>。  As for me, (I) see snow.
- <ruby>姉<rt>ㄐㄚ</rt></ruby> <ruby>持<rt>ㄉㄧ</rt></ruby><ruby>紙<rt>ㄐㄝ</rt></ruby>。  (My) sister holds paper.
- <ruby>男人<rt>ㄋㄚㄇㄋㄧㄋ</rt></ruby> <ruby>持<rt>ㄉㄧ</rt></ruby><ruby>筆<rt>ㄆㄨㄊ</rt></ruby>。  The man holds a pen.
- <ruby>君<rt>ㄍㄨㄋ</rt></ruby> <ruby>持<rt>ㄉㄧ</rt></ruby><ruby>薬<rt>⼘ㄎ</rt></ruby>。  You hold medicine.
- <ruby>先生<rt>ㄙㄝㄋㄙㄚㄫ</rt></ruby> <ruby>開<rt>ㄎㄚㄧ</rt></ruby><ruby>学校<rt>ㄏㄚㄎㄏ⼘ㄨ</rt></ruby>。  The teacher opens a school.
- <ruby>姉<rt>ㄐㄚ</rt></ruby> <ruby>開<rt>ㄎㄚㄧ</rt></ruby><ruby>市場<rt>ㄙㄧㄐㄚㄫ</rt></ruby>。  (My) sister opens a market.
- <ruby>先生<rt>ㄙㄝㄋㄙㄚㄫ</rt></ruby> <ruby>話<rt>ㄏ⺢ㄧ</rt></ruby><ruby>雪<rt>ㄙ⼔ㄊ</rt></ruby>。  The teacher talks about snow.
- <ruby>男人<rt>ㄋㄚㄇㄋㄧㄋ</rt></ruby> <ruby>持<rt>ㄉㄧ</rt></ruby><ruby>草花<rt>ㄑㄚㄨㄏ⺢</rt></ruby>。  The man holds flowers.
- <ruby>姉<rt>ㄐㄚ</rt></ruby> <ruby>送<rt>ㄙㄛㄫ</rt></ruby><ruby>薬<rt>⼘ㄎ</rt></ruby>。  (My) sister gives medicine.
- <ruby>我<rt>ㄚ</rt></ruby> <ruby>入<rt>ㄋㄧㄆ</rt></ruby><ruby>市場<rt>ㄙㄧㄐㄚㄫ</rt></ruby>。  I enter the market.

Nouns need no word for "a" or "the", and no plural: <ruby>持<rt>ㄉㄧ</rt></ruby><ruby>紙<rt>ㄐㄝ</rt></ruby> can be "holds a paper" or "holds papers", and the context decides.  A comment that is a noun, such as "I am a fish", needs a word for "is".  That comes in Lesson 05.

## Batch 5: A place before the comment
One word.

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| <ruby>於<rt>ㄛ</rt></ruby> | in, at |

A place noun followed by <ruby>於<rt>ㄛ</rt></ruby> means "at that place".  Such a phrase is a **frame** (域): it sets the scene, and it goes after the topic and before the comment.  The word 於 is called a postposition (関詞) because it comes after the noun.  Case marking gets its own lesson, Lesson 08; for now 於 simply means "at".

- <ruby>我<rt>ㄚ</rt></ruby> <ruby>学校<rt>ㄏㄚㄎㄏ⼘ㄨ</rt></ruby><ruby>於<rt>ㄛ</rt></ruby> <ruby>見<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby><ruby>先生<rt>ㄙㄝㄋㄙㄚㄫ</rt></ruby>。  As for me, at school, (I) see the teacher.
- <ruby>男人<rt>ㄋㄚㄇㄋㄧㄋ</rt></ruby> <ruby>市場<rt>ㄙㄧㄐㄚㄫ</rt></ruby><ruby>於<rt>ㄛ</rt></ruby> <ruby>見<rt>ㄍ⼶ㄋ</rt></ruby><ruby>草花<rt>ㄑㄚㄨㄏ⺢</rt></ruby>。  As for the man, at the market, (he) sees flowers.
